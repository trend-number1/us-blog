Damian Lillard’s Stunning Comeback: 3-Point King Returns

Here’s a fact almost no one saw coming: a 35-year-old guard, more than a year removed from a torn Achilles, walked onto an All-Star floor and beat two of the league’s deadliest shooters. Damian Lillard didn’t just compete in the 2026 3-Point Contest — he won it.

That single night reminded everyone why “Dame Time” still echoes around the NBA. It also raised a bigger question: what does Lillard’s recovery mean for a Portland Trail Blazers team that is suddenly reshaping its entire roster?

Key Takeaways

  • Lillard won the 2026 3-Point Contest, his third career title — tying Larry Bird and Craig Hodges for the most all-time.
  • He has been sidelined since April 2025 after tearing his left Achilles, and is roughly 10 months into recovery.
  • Lillard returned to Portland in the offseason, reuniting with the franchise that drafted him sixth overall in 2012.
  • The Blazers landed Ja Morant in a blockbuster trade with the Grizzlies, dramatically changing the backcourt picture.

A Record-Tying Night Despite the Injury

Damian Lillard wasn’t expected to see an NBA floor for any kind of competition this season. The recovery timeline simply didn’t point that way.

Then, on a Saturday night, he changed the script. Lillard took down Devin Booker and Kon Knueppel in the final round to claim the crown.

It marked his third all-time victory in the event. That total ties him with two legends — Larry Bird and Craig Hodges — for the most 3-Point Contest wins in history.

The performance wasn’t a full return to action. But it gave fans a glimpse of the shooter who built his reputation on logo-range threes and ice-cold clutch moments. So how did he end up here in the first place?

The Long Road Back From an Achilles Tear

Lillard has been sidelined since April 2025, when he tore his left Achilles in Game 4 of a first-round playoff series between the Milwaukee Bucks and the Indiana Pacers.

At the time, he was wrapping up his second season with the Bucks. The injury came shortly after he had returned from a deep vein thrombosis diagnosis that had already cost him multiple weeks.

Milwaukee was eliminated soon afterward, and Lillard entered the 2025 offseason facing a grueling rehab. Given his age, it was widely expected that he would miss most — if not all — of the 2025-26 season.

Here’s the insight that makes his story remarkable: at roughly 10 months into recovery, he is still not back in regular game action, yet he was sharp enough to out-shoot the league’s best in a high-pressure contest. That gap between “competition-ready shooting” and “full basketball recovery” is exactly what Portland fans are watching now.

A common misconception is that an Achilles tear ends elite explosiveness for good. While the rehab is long and uncertain, Lillard’s contest win suggests his signature skill — shooting — may return well before his timeline is complete.

Back Home in Rip City

Lillard’s return to Portland in the offseason was warmly received. It closed a loop that began more than a decade ago.

The Blazers drafted him sixth overall in 2012. He wasn’t a highly recruited prospect out of high school — instead, he earned a scholarship at Weber State, where he developed into a star before the NBA took notice.

His connection to the franchise and the city runs deep. That loyalty is part of why teammates speak about him the way they do.

Carmelo Anthony recently named Lillard as the best leader he ever played with, pointing to how genuinely Lillard cares for his teammates. That leadership may matter more than ever, because the team around him is changing fast.

A Reshaped Blazers Roster

While Lillard rehabs, Portland’s front office has been busy. The Blazers acquired Ja Morant in a blockbuster trade with the Memphis Grizzlies, a move that instantly altered the backcourt outlook.

That deal puts intriguing pieces around Lillard, including young guard Shaedon Sharpe, and raises real questions about how the rotation fits together. New head coach Micah Nori inherits the puzzle of blending Morant’s downhill attacking with Lillard’s perimeter shooting once Dame is fully healthy.

Here’s a list of what makes this Blazers situation so compelling:

  1. A franchise icon returning home at the tail end of his prime.
  2. A new star guard in Ja Morant reshaping the offense.
  3. A new head coach in Micah Nori building chemistry from scratch.
  4. A recovery timeline that keeps fans guessing week to week.

More Than Basketball: The Scholars Program

Lillard’s impact this period hasn’t been limited to the court. In February, he launched the Damian Lillard Scholars Program to help high school students from the Bay Area get into Portland State University.

The first recipient, Kayla Sisavat, is — like Lillard — from Oakland. She will receive $25,000 annually for her four-year college education.

Lillard, who grew up in a rough Oakland neighborhood, framed it through his own life. “Earning a scholarship to attend college out of state was a life-changing experience for me,” he wrote, adding that he’s honored to give deserving students from his hometown similar opportunities.

It’s worth noting he practices what he preaches: after leaving Weber State early for the draft, he returned for several summers and completed his bachelor’s degree in professional sales in 2015.

What This Means For You

If you’re a basketball fan, Lillard’s season is a live case study in modern sports medicine and longevity. Watching how — and when — he returns to full action will tell you a lot about whether elite guards can extend their primes past 35 after a major injury.

If you’re a Blazers fan, the takeaway is patience. The pieces are bold, but chemistry between Lillard, Morant, and a new coaching staff won’t form overnight.

And if you simply love a good comeback story, this one has it all: a hometown kid, a long rehab, a record-tying shooting night, and a city that never stopped rooting for him.

So here’s the question worth pondering: when Damian Lillard finally returns to full speed, will he prove that a torn Achilles is just another chapter — or the final one? Keep watching. With Dame, the clutch moment usually comes when you least expect it.
