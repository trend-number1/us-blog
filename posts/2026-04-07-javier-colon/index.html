Javier Colon Returns: The Voice's First Champion Heads Back to the Stage

April 7, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

Javier Colón made television history in 2011 when all four coaches turned their chairs for his Blind Audition — and he went on to become the very first winner of The Voice. Now, 15 years later, the soulful singer-songwriter is heading back to the NBC stage for a showdown that fans never saw coming.

Key Takeaways:

  • Javier Colón, The Voice Season 1 champion, is returning for the All-Star Showdown during Season 29’s “Battle of Champions.”
  • After winning in 2011, Colón released two albums but parted ways with Universal Republic Records on difficult terms.
  • Season 29 features coaches Kelly Clarkson, Adam Levine, and John Legend, with CeeLo Green returning as the All-Star judge.
  • Colón continues to perform live and recently visited a Connecticut high school for the arts to mentor students.

Javier Colón’s Iconic Start on The Voice

When The Voice debuted in 2011, it was still an experiment. The concept of blind auditions, spinning chairs, and coach battles was untested on American television. But one performer helped define what the show could be.

Colón stepped onto the stage and performed “Time After Time” in front of the original coaching panel: Adam Levine, Blake Shelton, CeeLo Green, and Christina Aguilera. All four chairs turned — an instant, unanimous recognition that something special was happening.

He chose Team Adam, a decision that proved pivotal. With Levine’s mentorship, Colón’s gentle tone and emotional depth carried him through the competition as a consistent frontrunner. When he was crowned the Season 1 champion, he set the standard for what winning The Voice could mean.

As NBC recently noted, Colón “carved out a lane in the Season 1 competition that was entirely his own.” And the show’s official Instagram reminded fans in April 2026 that his four-chair Blind Audition “still gives chills.”

So what happened after that triumphant moment?

Life After Winning: Albums, Label Struggles, and Staying True

Unlike some reality competition winners whose careers skyrocketed overnight, Colón’s post-show trajectory was more complicated — and more honest.

After his victory, Colón signed with Universal Republic Records and released his album Come Through for You in November 2011. The album featured a collaboration with Natasha Bedingfield and was accompanied by a Christmas EP that same year.

But the relationship with the label soured quickly. According to TV Insider, Colón “did not part ways with Universal Republic on the best of terms.” The creative tensions that often surface between artists and major labels took their toll, and Colón moved on.

In 2014, he signed with Concord Music Group and released his album Gravity in 2016 — his most recent full-length project to date.

Here’s a quick look at his discography:

Despite the lack of a blockbuster post-show career, Colón never stopped making music. His Instagram is filled with covers of other artists’ songs, and he performs live shows regularly. In his own words, he’s a “Singer, Songwriter, Family man, Golfer, Billiards Enthusiast, Vegan, Dreamer.”

That authenticity is exactly what made him resonate with audiences in the first place — and it’s what makes his return so compelling.

The Voice Season 29: Battle of Champions

Season 29 of The Voice is branded as the “Battle of Champions,” and it’s pulling out all the stops for nostalgia and high-stakes competition.

The coaching lineup alone is stacked: Kelly Clarkson, Adam Levine, and John Legend sit in the chairs. During a special “In-Season All-Star Competition” in the Knockouts Round, each coach brings back two fan-favorite artists from their previous teams.

Here’s how NBC describes the format:

  • Each coach selects two returning artists from past seasons
  • These all-stars compete in head-to-head sing-offs representing their former coaches
  • The coach with the most sing-off wins earns a second finalist in the finale
  • CeeLo Green returns to determine the winner of each All-Star Showdown

Colón is confirmed as one of the returning artists, making him one of six former contestants announced for the All-Star Showdown. While the returning artists won’t be eligible to win the main competition, they play a critical strategic role in helping their coach advance an additional artist to the finale.

For Colón, this is more than a cameo — it’s a chance to perform on the stage where it all began, this time with 15 years of experience behind him.

Beyond The Voice: Mentoring and Live Performances

Colón hasn’t just been preparing for his television return. He’s been actively giving back and staying connected to the music community.

In February 2026, he visited a Connecticut high school for the arts, offering advice and mentorship to students pursuing creative careers. The visit, covered by the New Haven Register, highlighted Colón’s commitment to nurturing the next generation of musicians — a fitting role for someone who knows firsthand the highs and lows of the industry.

He also performed live at Zimmer Theater in early April 2026, just days before his All-Star Showdown return was set to air. These intimate live performances are a reminder that Colón’s artistry thrives beyond the competition format.

What This Means for The Voice Franchise

Colón’s return signals something important about where The Voice is headed. By bringing back its very first champion, the show is leaning heavily into its legacy while introducing a competitive twist that rewards long-term fan loyalty.

The “Battle of Champions” format also raises the stakes for current contestants. Knowing that proven winners are stepping onto the stage to fight for their coaches adds a layer of pressure — and excitement — that the standard format doesn’t offer.

Consider how the show has evolved:

  • Season 1 (2011): Four coaches, a brand-new format, and Colón setting the tone
  • Season 29 (2026): Three coaches, an All-Star format, and Colón returning to close the circle
  • 15 years: Over two dozen winners, hundreds of memorable performances, and a cultural institution

The fact that Colón is still relevant enough to headline this return speaks to the lasting impact of that first season.

Looking Ahead

Javier Colón’s story isn’t one of overnight fame and fortune. It’s a story of an artist who won the biggest singing competition on television, navigated the messy realities of the music industry, and kept making music on his own terms.

His return to The Voice for the All-Star Showdown is the kind of moment that reminds long-time fans why they started watching in the first place. Whether he wins his sing-off or not, his presence on that stage carries weight — the weight of being first.

The Voice airs Mondays at 9/8c on NBC, with episodes streaming the next day on Peacock. Will Colón’s return live up to the legacy of that unforgettable four-chair turn? There’s only one way to find out.
