Patriots Trade Shocker: Bold Move to No. 28 Lands Lomu

Would you hand your biggest AFC East rival a first-round pick? The Patriots trade story of draft night says yes — and the payoff is a 6-foot-6 tackle who could anchor Drake Maye’s blind side for the next decade.

Quick Summary: New England traded up from No. 31 to No. 28 with the Buffalo Bills, sending pick No. 125 along with their first-rounder. With the new slot, the Patriots drafted Utah offensive tackle Caleb Lomu. It’s the second straight year the team has invested a Round 1 selection in the offensive line.

The Patriots Trade That Lit Up Round 1

At Acrisure Stadium in Pittsburgh, the opening night of the 2026 NFL Draft delivered a surprise. The Bills had already traded back once, and when they traded back a second time, they sent No. 28 to New England in exchange for Nos. 31 and 125, according to ESPN’s Field Yates.

For a middling cost — a late fourth-round pick — the Patriots jumped three spots in a run on offensive linemen. That’s significant because Buffalo, New England’s divisional rival, now gets extra draft capital. The Bills later moved No. 31 again to the Titans, who selected Auburn edge Keldric Faulk.

  • Patriots sent: picks No. 31 and No. 125
  • Patriots received: pick No. 28
  • Original trade partner: Buffalo Bills
  • Selection: Caleb Lomu, OT, Utah

The Patriots trade up move reflected real urgency to beat a wave of tackles off the board. The fact that they were willing to do business with an in-division team underscores just how much they liked the player.

Who Is Caleb Lomu? The New Blind-Side Bet

Lomu is a 6-foot-6, 304-pound sophomore out of Utah who primarily played left tackle in college. Selecting him at No. 28 signals that New England is building its offensive line of the future around youth and length.

It’s the second year in a row that the Patriots have used a first-round pick on a tackle. Last year, they took Will Campbell at No. 4 overall. Campbell struggled in the playoffs after returning from an MCL injury that sidelined him for weeks.

Veteran right tackle Morgan Moses was solid last season, but at age 35, his runway is short. Pairing Campbell and Lomu — two recent first-rounders — gives head coach Mike Vrabel the kind of bookend talent that protects a young quarterback long-term.

Why Two Tackles in Two Years?

The pattern is deliberate. When your quarterback is Drake Maye, protecting him is a non-negotiable. Campbell’s rookie-year injury exposed how thin the room was. Moses’ age compounds the risk. Lomu answers both problems at once.

Transitioning from this line of thinking raises an even bigger question: what’s next on Vrabel’s to-do list?

The A.J. Brown Question Hangs Over Everything

The draft-night Patriots trade doesn’t happen in a vacuum. ESPN’s Adam Schefter has reported that New England is expected to trade for Philadelphia Eagles star receiver A.J. Brown on or after June 1. No binding agreement is in place, however, which means the Patriots could still change course.

Brown’s personal relationship with Vrabel makes the fit obvious. But Pro Football Talk’s Mike Florio reported that the Jaguars may be laying the foundation for a trade into the bottom of Round 1, with Brian Thomas Jr. as potential bait. Thomas caught just 48 passes for 707 yards and two touchdowns in 2025 — a steep drop from his 87-catch, 1,282-yard, 10-touchdown 2024 season.

For a team chasing a No. 1 receiver to support Drake Maye, both paths matter:

  1. The A.J. Brown route — proven All-Pro, costs veteran compensation, adds immediate star power.
  2. The Brian Thomas Jr. route — rookie-contract bounce-back candidate, adds long-term cost control.
  3. Do neither — bank the picks, keep flexibility for 2027.

Pro Football Rumors also reported that Kayshon Boutte is available in trade, and MassLive noted Boutte has been absent from Patriots workouts amid the rumors. The receiver room, in other words, is still being reshaped.

Here’s the thought worth sitting with: the draft-night trade solved one problem, but the biggest roster decisions are still ahead.

What This Means for You as a Fan

If you’re a Patriots fan, the signal from this draft is clear: the front office is prioritizing the trenches before the skill positions. That’s a long-view approach that tends to age well.

A few things to watch in the coming weeks:

  • June 1: The earliest reasonable window for the expected A.J. Brown trade to formalize.
  • Boutte’s status: Whether he’s traded, benched, or returns to workouts.
  • Vrabel situation: The Patriots have released a statement on the controversy involving Vrabel and former NFL reporter Dianna Russini. Vrabel was also reported to miss the final day of the draft to seek counseling.

Common misconception: Trading within the division is always a mistake. Reality: when the cost is a late fourth-rounder and you land a premium position, the math usually favors the team that got the player. The Patriots got the player.

The Bottom Line on the Patriots Trade

New England’s decision to swap picks with Buffalo to secure Caleb Lomu tells you everything about their 2026 priorities: protect the quarterback, build the front, then figure out the receiver room. The patriots trade at No. 28 wasn’t splashy in the fantasy-football sense — but it may quietly be the most foundational move of the team’s night.

Will this offensive-line-first approach pay off when the Eagles phone finally rings for A.J. Brown? Or should the Patriots pivot toward the Brian Thomas Jr. conversation instead? The next six weeks will tell us more about Vrabel’s vision than any press conference could.

What would you do if you ran New England’s war room right now — go get Brown, or stay patient?
