TSA Wait Times PHL: Essential Guide to Surviving Philadelphia Airport Security

March 25, 2026 18:03 Trend Insights

Half of Philadelphia International Airport’s security checkpoints are closed, TSA workers are going without pay, and wait times have stretched past 44 minutes — and that’s on a good day. If you’re flying out of PHL anytime soon, here’s everything you need to know to avoid missing your flight.

Key Takeaways

  • Three of six TSA checkpoints at PHL are closed (Terminals A-West, C, and F) since March 18
  • Arrive 2.5 hours early for domestic and 3.5 hours early for international flights
  • TSA PreCheck is available at Terminals A-East and D/E only
  • ICE agents have been deployed to PHL to help ease security lines
  • About 50,000 TSA workers nationwide have been working without pay for over a month

TSA Wait Times at PHL: What’s Happening Right Now

The partial government shutdown over DHS funding has pushed Philadelphia International Airport into crisis mode. Since March 18, 2026, three of the airport’s six main security checkpoints have been temporarily closed in what the airport describes as an effort to “help optimize operations across other checkpoints.”

The closed checkpoints are at Terminals A-West, C, and F. That leaves only half of PHL’s screening capacity open to handle the full volume of passengers.

The impact has been immediate and painful. According to an airport spokesperson, the longest recorded wait time hit 44 minutes at the D/E checkpoint — and that was at 3 a.m., the first checkpoint to open each day. During peak hours, the situation can be significantly worse.

Footage from PHL captured early on a Thursday morning showed hundreds of passengers backed up on elevators and escalators, all trying to funnel through the remaining open checkpoints. Airport staff were deployed to help direct the crowds, but the scene painted a stark picture of an airport under strain.

So how does PHL compare to other major airports dealing with the same shutdown?

How PHL Compares to Other Major U.S. Airports

Philadelphia isn’t alone in this mess. Airports across the country are grappling with the fallout from the partial shutdown, which has left roughly 50,000 TSA personnel working without pay for more than a month. Hundreds have quit or called out of work, compounding the staffing crisis.

On Monday, March 23, the situation at Atlanta, LaGuardia, and Newark airports became so severe that all three removed wait time estimates from their websites entirely. Atlanta’s airport went a step further, advising passengers to allow at least four hours for security screenings alone.

By comparison, PHL has managed to avoid the absolute worst-case scenarios — at least for now. A report from 6abc Philadelphia noted that the airport had so far avoided the longest delays seen at other major airports, though the situation remains volatile.

But a new development on March 23 added another layer of complexity to the already chaotic scene.

ICE Agents Deployed to Philadelphia Airport

On Monday, March 23, President Trump announced the deployment of Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents to airports across the country, including Philadelphia International Airport, stating they would help ease the long security lines.

According to NBC10 Philadelphia, the deployment came amid the TSA worker shortage and growing lines at PHL. The Inquirer also reported that ICE agents were expected at the Philadelphia airport as part of a broader nationwide deployment.

The move has sparked debate. While the stated goal is to reduce wait times, questions remain about how immigration enforcement personnel will integrate into airport security operations traditionally handled by TSA officers.

What’s clear is that the underlying problem — TSA workers going unpaid — has not been resolved. Until the partial government shutdown ends and TSA personnel receive their paychecks, airports like PHL will continue to operate under significant strain.

For travelers, the practical question remains: how do you navigate this chaos?

Your Survival Guide: Flying Out of PHL Right Now

If you have a flight out of Philadelphia in the coming days or weeks, here’s your action plan based on the latest information from the airport and travel experts.

Know Which Checkpoints Are Open

As of March 24, the following checkpoints remain open:

  • Terminal A-East (TSA PreCheck available)
  • Terminal B
  • Terminal D/E (TSA PreCheck available)

The following checkpoints remain closed:

  • Terminal A-West
  • Terminal C
  • Terminal F

All terminals are still accessible. Even if your gate is in Terminal C or F, you can reach it by passing through security at one of the open checkpoints and walking to your terminal.

Arrive Earlier Than You Think

The Philadelphia International Airport’s official recommendation:

  • Domestic flights: Arrive 2.5 hours before departure
  • International flights: Arrive 3.5 hours before departure
  • Peak hours: Add an extra 30 minutes on top of those estimates

These are significantly longer than the usual 1.5- to 2-hour recommendations for domestic travel. Don’t gamble with your departure — the checkpoint closures mean longer walks to your gate in addition to longer security lines.

Use TSA PreCheck If You Have It

TSA PreCheck lanes are available at Terminals A-East and D/E only. If you’re enrolled, this can save significant time, though even PreCheck lines have been affected by the overall congestion.

If you don’t have PreCheck, now might be the time to consider enrolling for future travel. It typically costs $78 for five years and can dramatically reduce your time in security lines.

Check Live Wait Times Before You Leave

The airport publishes live wait time updates on its checkpoint hours page, updated roughly every 10 minutes. Check these before leaving for the airport so you can plan accordingly.

Unlike Atlanta, LaGuardia, and Newark — which have pulled their wait time data offline — PHL is still publishing real-time estimates, giving travelers a critical advantage in planning their arrival.

What This Means for You

The situation at PHL is a direct consequence of the ongoing partial government shutdown. Until that shutdown ends and TSA workers are paid, checkpoint closures and extended wait times are likely to continue — and could potentially get worse.

Here’s what you should do right now:

  1. Check your flight status and the PHL checkpoint hours page before every trip
  2. Build in extra buffer time — treat the 2.5-hour recommendation as a minimum, not a maximum
  3. Pack smart — have your ID and boarding pass ready, remove laptops and liquids before reaching the front of the line
  4. Stay calm and patient — TSA officers on the front lines are working without pay and doing their best under extraordinary circumstances
  5. Consider alternative airports if your schedule allows and wait times become unmanageable

The reality is that 50,000 TSA workers across the country are showing up to work without paychecks, and the ripple effects are being felt by millions of travelers. PHL has managed to hold things together better than some airports, but no one should assume that will last indefinitely.

Are you flying out of PHL soon? The single best thing you can do is leave early, stay informed, and prepare for the unexpected. What’s your experience been with airport security during the shutdown?
