Marcus Smart's Stunning Lakers Revival Rewrites 2026 Playoffs

April 25, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

Nine games in street clothes. Nine games of doubt. Then Marcus Smart walked into Game 2 and shredded the Houston Rockets for 25 points, five threes, seven assists, and five steals.

If you thought the Lakers’ 2026 playoff run would hinge on LeBron James and Deandre Ayton alone, Smart just changed your mind.

Quick Summary: Marcus Smart signed a two-year, $11 million deal with the Lakers in July 2025. After battling injuries that cost him nine late-season games, he erupted in Game 2 of the first round, leading Los Angeles to a 101-94 win and a 2-0 series lead over Houston.

From Wizards Buyout to Lakers Starter

Smart’s path to this moment wasn’t obvious. The 2022 NBA Defensive Player of the Year had played just 54 games over two seasons with Memphis and Washington after Boston traded him in 2023.

He appeared in only 15 games for the Wizards before a buyout freed him to join Los Angeles. When the Lakers signed him in July 2025, the hope was simple: stay healthy, anchor the defense.

He’s done far more. Smart has started 62 regular-season games for Los Angeles this year, becoming a cornerstone next to LeBron James and Luka Doncic.

The bigger story? His mere presence has filled a leadership gap that widened when Doncic and Austin Reaves went down with injuries late in the regular season.

The Game That Defined His Lakers Turn

Tuesday night at Crypto.com Arena was the proof of concept. Smart didn’t just score — he set the tone from the opening minute.

On one sequence, he stepped up three-quarters court to meet Kevin Durant, poked the ball loose, then dove on the floor to secure it before the 7-foot Durant could recover. The crowd jumped to its feet. Smart’s defensive aggression forced Durant into nine turnovers that night.

That contrast is the entire reason the Lakers are up 2-0.

The Leadership Factor Nobody Saw Coming

Coach JJ Redick has been candid about what Smart brings beyond the box score. After the three-game skid that followed the Doncic and Reaves injuries, Redick pulled Smart aside in San Francisco.

“The biggest thing was just, because he has the voice he has, he can help create the belief and the confidence in our group,” Redick said after Game 2. “And I think he’s done that.”

Smart is now a finalist for the NBA’s Teammate of the Year award. Teammates describe him as the coach on the court and the unofficial coach on the bench.

Consider what he told reporters after the Game 2 win:

  • “I’m very grateful to be doing this.”
  • “I thank God every day because I could have been out the league.”
  • “To be back on this stage making the plays that I’m making… I’m just grateful.”

That’s not standard postgame filler. That’s a 12-year veteran who knows how close he came to being done.

Why This Playoff Run Means More to Smart

Playoff basketball was the default for Smart in Boston. He played in four Eastern Conference Finals and one NBA Finals with the Celtics across nine seasons.

Then came the bitter twist. Smart was traded to Memphis in 2023 — and the Celtics won the title the very next year. Instead of hoisting Banner 18, Smart was rehabbing in Memphis.

Two injury-marred seasons later, he’s finally back where he belongs. Friday’s Game 3 in Houston is the test: the Lakers have dropped nine consecutive road playoff games, a streak they desperately need to end.

Here’s the common misconception worth clearing up: many fans assumed Smart’s best defensive years were behind him after the injuries. Game 2 suggested otherwise. Forcing Durant into nine turnovers isn’t a fluke — it’s a reminder that defensive IQ doesn’t expire at 31.

What This Means For the Lakers’ Title Odds

The Lakers’ roster is older than most contenders. LeBron is in his record-tying 19th postseason. Ayton is a recent Finals participant. Five Lakers have played in the NBA Finals — including Smart.

That depth of playoff experience is why the Lakers are surviving without Doncic and Reaves. Smart’s two-year, $11 million contract is looking like one of the steals of the 2025 offseason.

For bettors, fans, and fantasy managers, the takeaway is straightforward:

  1. Don’t fade Smart’s minutes — he’s a starter, not a role piece.
  2. Watch his three-point volume — five triples in Game 2 wasn’t a one-off if defenses keep sagging off.
  3. Track his defensive matchups — Redick is deploying him on elite scorers like Durant.

Lakers Nation also notes a growing on-court chemistry between LeBron and Smart that’s translating to smoother pick-and-roll reads and faster transition offense.

The Free Agency Question Lurking Beneath It All

There’s a storyline running under the surface. Reports indicate the Lakers are already weighing free-agency decisions on Smart and Luke Kennard for the summer.

Every playoff minute Smart plays at this level raises his price tag. And every Lakers win makes it harder for the front office to let him walk.

The question the Lakers brass has to answer: is Smart’s current form sustainable, or is this a hot streak juiced by playoff adrenaline?

If Friday’s Game 3 in Houston produces another performance like Tuesday’s, that question may answer itself.

The Bigger Lesson for NBA Followers

Smart’s arc — from Defensive Player of the Year, to trade casualty, to buyout candidate, to playoff hero — is a reminder that NBA narratives shift faster than we think.

A year ago, many expected Smart to quietly fade. Instead, he’s the emotional engine of a 2-0 Lakers team in the 2026 playoffs.

So here’s the question for you: if Marcus Smart can rewrite his story at 31 after two lost seasons, which other “fading” veterans are one roster change away from their own redemption arc?

Keep watching Friday night. The answer might already be unfolding in Houston.
