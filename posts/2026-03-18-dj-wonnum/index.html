DJ Wonnum Signs With Detroit Lions: Why This $6 Million Bet Could Transform Their Pass Rush

March 18, 2026 09:07 Trend Insights

The Detroit Lions just made one of the quieter but most strategically important moves of free agency so far — signing edge rusher DJ Wonnum to a one-year deal worth up to $6 million. In a league where elite pass rushers command $25 million annually, this looks like a bargain. But is it a steal or a gamble?

Key Takeaway:

  • DJ Wonnum signed a one-year deal worth up to $6 million with the Detroit Lions, confirmed by ESPN’s Adam Schefter
  • The 28-year-old brings 30 career sacks across 6 NFL seasons, including two 8-sack campaigns
  • Detroit desperately needed edge depth behind star Aidan Hutchinson, who had only Ahmed Hassanein and Tyler Lacy behind him on the roster
  • Wonnum was a fourth-round pick out of South Carolina by the Minnesota Vikings in 2020
  • His 2025 season with Carolina produced 42 total tackles, 3 sacks, and 1 interception in 16 games with 15 starts
  • The Panthers ranked near the bottom of the NFL in sacks and quarterback pressures last season

From Viking to Panther to Lion: Wonnum’s Journey to Detroit

DJ Wonnum’s career arc tells the story of a player who has always shown flashes of dominance without fully breaking through to stardom. Selected in the fourth round of the 2020 NFL Draft by the Minnesota Vikings, Wonnum immediately proved he belonged in the league. His initial rookie contract was a modest four-year, $4.04 million deal — a fraction of what first-rounders command.

The peak years came early. Wonnum registered 8 sacks in both 2021 and 2023 with Minnesota, establishing himself as a legitimate pass-rushing threat. Those numbers put him in the conversation among the league’s better rotational edge players, even if he never quite reached the tier of a perennial Pro Bowler.

Then came the move to Carolina. The Panthers, desperate for any help on the defensive line, brought Wonnum in on a two-year stint. The results were mixed at best. While he started 15 of 16 games during the 2025 season, his production dipped to just 3 sacks — a far cry from his Minnesota highs.

Part of the problem wasn’t Wonnum himself. The Panthers’ defense as a whole was a disaster, ranking near the bottom of the NFL in both sacks and quarterback pressures. It’s difficult for any edge rusher to thrive when the surrounding talent can’t generate complementary pressure. Offensive lines could afford to slide protection toward Wonnum because nobody else on Carolina’s front was beating their man.

So what changed? The Panthers would have owed $825,000 in dead money by moving on from Wonnum, a minimal price to pay for cap flexibility. And Wonnum, now 28 and entering his seventh NFL season, needed a fresh start on a team where he could actually succeed.

Why Detroit Makes Perfect Sense

The Lions’ need at edge rusher has been one of the worst-kept secrets in the NFL this offseason. Aidan Hutchinson is a certified star — his 14.5 sacks earned him Pro Bowl honors and cemented his status as one of the league’s premier pass rushers. But behind Hutchinson, Detroit’s depth chart looked alarmingly thin.

Here’s what the Lions’ edge rusher room looked like before the Wonnum signing:

The gap between Hutchinson and everyone else was enormous. In today’s NFL, where teams routinely deploy four-man edge rotations and defensive coordinators scheme complex pressure packages, having only one reliable pass rusher is a recipe for disaster. If Hutchinson missed time due to injury or fatigue, Detroit had almost nothing behind him.

Wonnum doesn’t need to be a superstar to make this signing work. He needs to be a reliable second option who can generate pressure when Hutchinson draws double teams, spell Hutchinson on obvious passing downs, and hold up in the run game on early downs.

His 30 career sacks suggest he can do exactly that. And the one-year, incentive-laden structure of the deal means both sides carry minimal risk.

The $6 Million Question: Can Wonnum Bounce Back?

The skeptics have a fair point. Wonnum’s Pro Football Focus ratings ranked him near the bottom tier among qualifying edge players during his Panthers tenure. Three sacks in 16 games isn’t going to scare any offensive coordinator. And at 28, he’s no longer a young player with untapped upside — he is who he is.

But context matters enormously in the NFL. Consider these factors:

  • Scheme fit: Carolina’s defense was a mess from top to bottom. Detroit’s defense is built around elite talent and aggressive play-calling. Wonnum will face fewer double teams and benefit from better surrounding pieces.
  • Motivation: This is a one-year, prove-it deal. Wonnum knows a strong season in Detroit could set up a massive payday in 2027 free agency. Nothing motivates a 28-year-old veteran like contract-year pressure.
  • Role clarity: In Carolina, Wonnum was asked to be a primary pass rusher on a bad team. In Detroit, he’s the clear number two behind a dominant Hutchinson. Less pressure, better support, more favorable matchups.
  • Historical precedent: Wonnum has already shown he can produce at a high level when the conditions are right. His twin 8-sack seasons in Minnesota weren’t flukes — they were the result of playing in a competent defensive system with complementary talent around him.

The $6 million price tag, especially with incentives built in, represents almost zero risk for Detroit. If Wonnum produces 6-8 sacks and provides solid depth, the Lions got a bargain. If he struggles, they move on after one year with minimal cap consequences.

What This Means for the Lions’ Super Bowl Push

Detroit’s front office clearly believes this roster is built to win now. Hutchinson anchors one of the league’s best defensive lines, and the offense has the firepower to compete with anyone in the NFC. But championship teams don’t just have stars — they have depth that prevents a single injury from derailing an entire season.

That’s precisely what Wonnum provides. He’s not the flashy signing that generates headlines. He’s the kind of signing that matters in Week 14 when Hutchinson is nursing a sore shoulder and the Lions need someone who can line up on the edge and win one-on-one battles against a right tackle.

The one-year structure also reveals something about Detroit’s long-term thinking. If Wonnum thrives in this system and recaptures his Minnesota-era form, the Lions could extend him at a reasonable price. If not, they haven’t mortgaged any future capital.

The Bigger Picture: Value Hunting in Free Agency

DJ Wonnum’s signing highlights a broader trend across the NFL — contending teams finding high-upside, low-risk veterans on short deals to fill specific roster holes. Not every free-agency win involves a $100 million contract. Sometimes the smartest moves are the ones that cost $6 million and solve a problem that could have cost you a playoff game.

For Wonnum, this is a chance to revive a career that was fading in Carolina. For Detroit, this is insurance on their most important defensive asset. And for the rest of the NFC, it’s another reminder that the Lions aren’t just building a contender — they’re building a roster with no weak links.

The real test comes in September. Can a change of scenery and a star teammate turn DJ Wonnum back into the 8-sack pass rusher Minnesota once knew? Or will the Panthers’ version prove to be the more accurate reflection of his talent? Either way, at this price, Detroit can afford to find out.
