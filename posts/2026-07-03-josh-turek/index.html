Josh Turek: The Surprising Iowa Senate Contender

July 3, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

Iowa has not sent a Democrat to the U.S. Senate in nearly two decades. Josh Turek wants to change that — and the early polling suggests he has a real shot.

Turek, a two-time Paralympic gold medalist turned Iowa state representative, has jumped into one of 2026’s most closely watched Senate contests. A recent Fox News survey put him ahead of his Republican opponent, a result almost no one predicted a year ago.

Key Takeaways

  • Josh Turek won Iowa’s Democratic Senate primary, defeating state Sen. Zach Wahls.
  • He faces Republican Rep. Ashley Hinson for retiring Sen. Joni Ernst’s open seat.
  • A Fox News poll shows Turek leading Hinson 50% to 46%, within the margin of error.
  • Iowa last elected a Democratic senator in 2008 (Tom Harkin).

Who Is Josh Turek?

Josh Turek is a 47-year-old Iowa state representative who holds what has been described as the reddest legislative seat controlled by a Democrat in the entire state. That alone makes him an unusual figure in Iowa politics.

Before entering the statehouse, Turek built a career as an elite adaptive athlete. He is a two-time Paralympic gold medalist in wheelchair basketball, a background that has become central to how voters see him.

His personal story is inseparable from his politics. Turek was born with spina bifida after his father, John, a Vietnam War veteran, was exposed to Agent Orange during the war. By the age of 12, Turek had already undergone 21 surgeries.

He was raised in a working-class family alongside four siblings, all of whom competed in collegiate or professional basketball. His brother John and sister Elisha both played at the college level. His mother, Luellen, is credited with introducing him to adaptive sports.

Today Turek lives in Council Bluffs with his wife, Jarolin — an immigrant who is now a U.S. citizen — and their children. That biography, blending disability, military legacy, and immigrant family life, is exactly what he is putting at the center of his campaign.

So how did a state legislator from a deep-red district become a statewide contender? The primary tells the story.

Winning the Primary

Turek secured the Democratic nomination by defeating state Sen. Zach Wahls, according to NBC News projections. The victory set up a general-election showdown for the seat being vacated by retiring two-term Republican Sen. Joni Ernst.

His Republican opponent is U.S. Rep. Ashley Hinson, who won the GOP primary over former state Sen. Jim Carlin. Hinson entered the race endorsed by President Donald Trump and Republican leaders, and the GOP considers the seat pivotal to holding its Senate majority.

The primary season was competitive across Iowa. In a notable result, Trump-backed U.S. Rep. Randy Feenstra lost the Republican gubernatorial primary to Zach Lahn — a rare setback for a candidate the president had endorsed.

Turek framed his own launch around economic frustration. In a campaign ad that showed him pushing his wheelchair up an incline, he said, “Iowans feel like they’re pushing uphill, and I know exactly what that feels like.”

With the nomination locked up, the real question became whether the numbers could actually add up in November.

What the Polls Actually Say

The headline number is striking. A statewide Fox News poll found Turek with an early 4-point advantage over Hinson, 50% to 46% — an edge that sits within the poll’s margin of error.

The favorability gap is even wider. Turek posts a net positive rating of 18 points (51% favorable, 33% unfavorable). Hinson runs at net negative 7 points (42%–49%), and Trump is underwater by 13 points in the state (42%–55%).

That last figure represents a dramatic shift. Trump’s Iowa rating swung 19 points from his +6 (52%–46%) in the November 2024 Fox News Voter Analysis.

Enthusiasm may matter most. Among voters who say they are extremely motivated to vote, Turek leads by 15 points, powered by higher Democratic intensity (67% extremely motivated) than Republican (53%).

Not every survey agrees. A New York Times poll showed Hinson narrowly leading Turek, a reminder that this race remains genuinely close. Here is how the recent public numbers compare:

These numbers are especially surprising when you look at how red Iowa has become.

Why Iowa Was Supposed to Be Out of Reach

Iowa has trended sharply Republican. Sen. Ernst won reelection by 6.5 points in 2020, the same year Trump carried the state by 8 points. In 2024, Trump’s Iowa margin grew to 13 points.

The last time Iowa elected a Democratic senator was Tom Harkin, nearly 20 years ago in 2008. Democrats currently hold no seats in the state’s federal delegation and face a Republican statehouse trifecta.

So why do Democrats feel hopeful now? Party strategists point to concrete pressures:

  1. High prices squeezing household budgets
  2. Lost manufacturing jobs across the state
  3. Shuttered healthcare facilities in rural areas
  4. A struggling agricultural economy

Turek has leaned directly into those issues, vowing to bring down the cost of health care, housing, and prescription drugs, and to fight for farmers and small businesses. He has also campaigned against cuts to Medicaid and pledged to fight corruption, recently launching a statewide “Team Iowa Tour” that kicked off in Cedar Rapids.

A common misconception is that a red state’s presidential margin locks in its Senate races — but split-ticket voting and open seats often tell a different story.

What This Means For You

If you follow national politics, the Iowa race is worth watching regardless of your party. Control of the U.S. Senate could hinge on a handful of contests like this one, and a seat the GOP treated as safe is now competitive.

For Iowa voters specifically, the practical stakes are concrete: the candidates are drawing sharp contrasts on health care access, Medicaid, prescription drug costs, and support for farmers and small businesses.

Here is actionable advice: don’t rely on a single poll. As the Fox News, Hill, and New York Times results show, surveys can disagree even weeks apart. Check the margin of error, and track whether favorability and voter enthusiasm are moving over time — those trends often signal more than a single top-line number.

Josh Turek has already defied expectations by winning a red district and leading in an early statewide survey. Whether that translates into flipping a Senate seat in November remains an open question.

Could a Paralympic gold medalist from Council Bluffs be the candidate who finally breaks Iowa’s Democratic drought — or will the state’s rightward shift hold? What would it take to change your own vote in a race this close?
