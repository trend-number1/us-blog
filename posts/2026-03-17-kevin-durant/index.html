Kevin Durant MVP Push: Rockets Star Dominates at 37 With Historic Scoring Streak

March 17, 2026 13:43 Trend Insights

At 37 years old, most NBA players are winding down their careers, accepting reduced roles, and mentoring younger teammates from the bench. Kevin Durant didn’t get that memo. The Houston Rockets forward is in the middle of one of the most remarkable late-career surges in league history, climbing into the top 10 of the MVP ladder and reminding everyone why he’s considered one of the greatest scorers to ever touch a basketball.

Key Takeaways:

  • Durant has entered the top 10 of the 2025-26 MVP ladder, averaging 26.2 points, 5.4 rebounds, and 4.6 assists
  • He’s shooting an elite 51.0% from the field and 40.5% from three — a combination almost no player in history has matched at his age
  • Durant earned his 16th All-Star selection, becoming the oldest All-Star in Rockets franchise history
  • The Rockets sit at 37-22 and are positioned as legitimate title contenders in the Western Conference
  • His recent eight-game stretch saw him average 27.3 points on 55.8% shooting, including a 32-point outburst against the Pelicans

From Phoenix to Houston: The Trade That Changed Everything

The basketball world was rocked in the summer of 2025 when the Phoenix Suns dealt Durant to the Houston Rockets in a blockbuster seven-team trade involving 13 players. The Suns received Jalen Green, Dillon Brooks, the No. 10 pick in the 2025 draft (Khaman Maluach), and five second-round picks in return.

Many questioned whether acquiring a 36-year-old superstar aligned with Houston’s timeline. The Rockets had been building around their young core of Alperen Sengun and Amen Thompson, and pairing them with a veteran nearing the end of his career seemed like a gamble.

Durant silenced the skeptics almost immediately. He signed a two-year, $90 million extension — taking less than the $122 million he was eligible for to give the team financial flexibility. “It was a no-brainer,” Durant told Houston Public Media. His willingness to sacrifice money for winning sent a powerful message to the locker room and the rest of the league.

A Season for the Record Books

The numbers Durant is putting up this season are staggering, especially when you factor in his age. He’s averaging 26.2 points, 5.4 rebounds, and 4.6 assists in 36.8 minutes per game. His shooting splits — 51.0% from the field, 40.5% from three, and 88.6% from the line — represent the kind of efficiency that most players can only dream of in their prime, let alone at 37.

What makes this even more extraordinary is the historical context. Durant has now averaged at least 25 points per game while shooting 50% from the floor and 40% from three in seven different seasons. No other player in NBA history has accomplished that feat more than three times. He’s not just defying age — he’s rewriting what’s possible.

His recent hot streak has been especially dominant. Over his last eight games, Durant averaged 27.3 points, 6.4 rebounds, 3.9 assists, 1.1 blocks, and 1.0 steals while shooting a scorching 55.8% from the field. The stretch was punctuated by a 32-point performance in a nail-biting 107-105 victory over the Pelicans, where he went 13-for-24 from the floor and played all 40 minutes.

16th All-Star, Fourth-Most in NBA History

In February, Durant claimed his 16th NBA All-Star selection, making him the oldest player in Rockets franchise history to receive the honor. He now trails only LeBron James, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, and Kobe Bryant for the most All-Star appearances in league history.

Combined with his two NBA championships, two Finals MVPs, and an MVP award, Durant’s resume continues to grow in ways that cement his place among basketball’s all-time greats. What separates him from other aging superstars is that he’s not coasting on reputation — he’s earning every accolade through nightly dominance.

“The wide-open playoff race is fun,” Durant told reporters in early March. His competitiveness hasn’t dimmed one bit. When asked about Houston’s chances as the postseason approaches, he offered a characteristically bold declaration: “We can go to the moon.”

Beyond the Court: Building a Legacy at Texas

Durant’s impact extends well beyond the basketball court. On March 10, he and the University of Texas announced a comprehensive NIL program in partnership with Nike, designed to celebrate and support Longhorn student-athletes in their basketball journeys.

The initiative reflects Durant’s deep connection to his college roots — he spent one legendary season at Texas before being selected second overall in the 2007 NBA Draft — and his commitment to empowering the next generation of athletes. It’s a reminder that even as he chases another ring, Durant is thinking about the future of the sport.

Can the Rockets Make a Championship Run?

With a 37-22 record through 59 games, the Rockets are firmly positioned as one of the top teams in the Western Conference. The combination of Durant’s veteran leadership and scoring prowess with the youthful energy and athleticism of Sengun and Thompson has created something genuinely dangerous.

Durant has emphasized that defensive commitment will be the key to Houston’s postseason success. “If everyone will be committed to max effort on the defensive end, we can go a long way,” he said. It’s the kind of message that resonates differently coming from a player with two championship rings.

The NBA has rarely seen a player perform at this level this deep into his career. At 37, Kevin Durant isn’t just surviving — he’s thriving, and the Houston Rockets are reaping the benefits. Whether the season ends with a championship or not, the 2025-26 campaign has already added another remarkable chapter to one of basketball’s greatest careers.

The question isn’t whether Durant belongs in the MVP conversation. It’s whether anyone counted him out in the first place.
