Hotel Trends 2026: The Surprising Shifts Reshaping Travel

June 30, 2026 18:03 Trend Insights

Would you believe that nearly three out of every four hotel deals in the U.S. this year involved high-end properties? The hotel industry is not just recovering — it is reinventing itself, and the change is happening at the top of the market.

From artificial intelligence quietly redesigning how you book a room to workers walking off the job in major cities, 2026 has become a defining year for hospitality. Whether you travel for business or pleasure, these shifts will shape your next stay.

Quick Summary: Luxury and upscale hotels now dominate U.S. transactions, AI is reshaping how guests discover and book rooms, and labor strikes are rippling through major cities. Meanwhile, new brands are racing into Asia Pacific and travel demand remains strong.

Let’s unpack what’s really driving the headlines — and what it means for your wallet.

The Luxury Boom Is Real

The biggest story in hotels right now is where the money is going. According to PwC’s U.S. midyear outlook, upscale, upper upscale, and luxury assets made up 73% of hotel deals over the last six months.

That’s a striking concentration. Investors are betting heavily on the premium end of the market, not the budget tier.

One of the clearest signals came from Blackstone. Funds affiliated with Blackstone Real Estate are set to acquire a hotel from Sunstone, a deal that aligns with Sunstone’s ongoing stock repurchase strategy, according to CEO Bryan Giglia.

Design is also becoming a competitive weapon. Industry reports note that small but carefully considered design details now represent a valuable way for owners to differentiate their hotel and drive a tangible return. The design-forward Palisociety brand, for example, recently added 16 hotels to its portfolio as it expands across the Americas.

So why does this premium surge matter to you? Because it sets the tone for pricing and innovation everywhere — and the next wave of that innovation is already arriving through your phone.

AI Is Quietly Taking Over the Front Desk

If you’ve searched for a hotel recently, an algorithm may have shaped your choices more than you realize.

AI discovery is reshaping hospitality, and early movers are building advantages that competitors will struggle to match. The shift is happening fast, and it touches everything from how rooms surface in search to how guests are served on-site.

Choice Hotels is among the companies leaning in. Anna Scozzafava, Choice’s Chief Data, AI and Technology Officer, has described how the company is deploying artificial intelligence to create value for both owners and guests — not just back-office efficiency, but the guest experience itself.

Here’s the insight worth pausing on: hotels that adopt AI now are not simply cutting costs. They are learning your preferences, anticipating demand, and personalizing offers in ways that compound over time. The gap between early adopters and laggards may soon be impossible to close.

What this means for you: expect more tailored recommendations, dynamic pricing, and faster service — but also less anonymity as your travel patterns feed the system.

Technology may be transforming the guest experience, but the people who actually run these hotels are pushing back hard.

Workers Are Walking Off the Job

Behind the polished lobbies, tension is mounting. A wave of strikes has hit major U.S. hotels in 2026.

After employees at a Hilton hotel in Seattle went on strike, union workers at the Sheraton Philadelphia Downtown walked off the job the following Sunday. Separately, employees at the Embassy Suites Pioneer Square hotel in Seattle launched a strike calling for fair wages, enhanced healthcare coverage, and other workplace improvements.

The common threads across these actions:

  • Fair wages that keep pace with rising costs
  • Better healthcare coverage for hospitality workers
  • Improved workplace conditions and protections

These disputes aren’t just labor news. They can affect your stay through reduced services, picket lines, or rebooking headaches if you’ve reserved a room at an affected property. Checking for active labor actions before you travel is now a smart pre-trip habit.

Labor unrest is a domestic pressure point — but globally, hotel brands are sprinting in the opposite direction: rapid expansion.

Brands Are Racing Into New Markets

While U.S. operators navigate strikes, the world’s biggest hotel brands are planting flags abroad.

Hyatt Hotels Corporation announced the official opening of Hyatt Place Jiaxing Nanhu, marking the first Hyatt Place property in Jiaxing, China. It’s a small dot on the map with a big strategic message: secondary cities are the new frontier.

The luxury push is even more dramatic. NoMad Hilton Singapore is scheduled to open in late 2026, marking the brand’s debut in Asia Pacific with multiple new dining and bar concepts. For a design-led brand like NoMad, entering Asia Pacific is a statement of where future demand lies.

Domestically, the expansion continues too. Hilton opened its second Tempo hotel in Nashville, reinforcing how mid-scale lifestyle brands are filling the space between budget and luxury.

This global build-out only makes sense if travelers keep showing up. And the data says they are.

Travel Demand Stays Strong — With New Patterns

Despite economic uncertainty, consumers remain eager to travel. New Priceline research shows that consumers are “deeply committed” to travel this season — but they want more from their trips, and brands like Choice Hotels are leaning into that demand.

Booking flows are shifting in measurable ways. Consider these figures:

  • American flight bookings to Canada are up 44% year-on-year, while bookings to Mexico are down 12%.
  • Intra-European flight bookings are up 37%, signaling robust regional travel.
  • For upcoming World Cup host cities, Japan and South Korea rank fourth and seventh among the top international origin markets, with the United Kingdom leading the way.

A common misconception is that travelers are trading down to save money. The reality is more nuanced: people are still spending, but they’re being selective — choosing destinations, experiences, and properties that feel worth it.

What this means for you: if you’re eyeing a popular destination or a major event city, book early. Demand concentration drives prices up fast, especially at the upscale properties investors are flocking toward.

The Bottom Line

The 2026 hotel landscape is a study in contrasts. Luxury investment is booming while frontline workers strike. AI is personalizing the guest experience while brands chase scale across continents. Travelers are committed but increasingly choosy.

For you, the smart play is awareness. Know that premium properties are where the innovation — and the price hikes — are concentrated. Watch for labor actions at your booked hotel. And book early for high-demand destinations, because the data shows travelers aren’t slowing down.

So here’s the question worth asking before your next trip: in a world where hotels know more about you than ever, are you choosing the property — or is the algorithm choosing it for you?
