Ali Mohammad Naeini: The New Voice of Iran's Revolutionary Guard Explained

March 20, 2026 18:03 Trend Insights

Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) has a new public face — and he’s already making waves. Brigadier General Ali Mohammad Naeini, a veteran of 94 months on the Iran-Iraq War frontlines and a scholar of “soft warfare,” was recently appointed as the IRGC’s official spokesperson. His first major public statements have drawn global attention, particularly his claims about Iran’s performance in what he calls the “12-day war” against Israel.

Key Takeaways

  • Ali Mohammad Naeini is the newly appointed IRGC spokesperson, replacing Brigadier General Ramezan Sharif.
  • Born in Kashan in 1955, he holds a PhD in strategic management with a focus on American soft-war models against Iran.
  • He spent 94 months on Iran-Iraq War frontlines and authored 10 books on defense and soft warfare.
  • Naeini has claimed Iran achieved “absolute victory” in a 12-day conflict with Israel, citing a 40% increase in missile capability after “True Promise 2.”

Ali Mohammad Naeini’s Background: Scholar, Soldier, Strategist

Naeini is no newcomer to Iran’s security establishment. Born in the city of Kashan in 1955, he built a career that bridges the battlefield and the lecture hall.

His academic credentials are extensive: a bachelor’s degree in educational sciences, a master’s in defense management, and a PhD in strategic management. His doctoral dissertation focused specifically on what he describes as “the American model of soft war against Iran” — a topic that has defined much of his professional focus.

On the military side, Naeini served 94 months on the frontlines of the Iran-Iraq War (1980–1988), one of the longest and deadliest conventional conflicts of the 20th century. That nearly eight-year combat experience formed the foundation of his later work in military documentation and strategic communications.

He went on to serve as head of the IRGC’s Documentation and Research Center for the Iran-Iraq War and as a former advisor to the IRGC commander-in-chief. He is the author of 10 books and 20 articles, with his most notable publication being Principles and Foundations of Soft War.

Before his current role, Naeini held the position of deputy for cultural and social affairs in Tehran’s municipality until October 2017. He was also known to be closely associated with Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, the speaker of Iran’s parliament.

So what prompted his appointment to the IRGC’s most public-facing role?

Why Naeini Was Appointed: A Strategic Shift in Messaging

Naeini’s appointment was issued by the representative of the Supreme Leader within the IRGC, with the approval of the Guard’s commander-in-chief. He replaces Brigadier General Ramezan Sharif, who had long served as the IRGC spokesperson and deputy for public relations.

Sharif, meanwhile, was reassigned to lead the IRGC’s Documentation and Research Center for the Iran-Iraq War — a role Naeini himself previously held.

The IRGC’s leadership framed the appointment as reflecting the organization’s “commitment to strengthening the revolutionary and defense discourse in the face of contemporary media and cultural challenges.” In other words, Iran sees the information battlefield as increasingly critical — and Naeini, with his academic focus on soft warfare, is their chosen strategist for it.

Naeini’s Bold Claims About the “12-Day War”

Naeini’s most attention-grabbing statements have centered on what he refers to as a “12-day war” between Iran and Israel. In a televised interview, he made several specific assertions:

  • Iran was not caught off guard. Naeini stated that Iranian armed forces had executed military deployment before the Israeli attack began, in anticipation of the offensive.
  • Missile capability increased by 40%. He quoted the late General Hussein Salami as saying, just two days before the conflict, that Iran’s “missile and technical capability had risen by at least 40%” after the “True Promise 2” operation.
  • Rapid operational response. After Iranian commanders were targeted at 4 AM, Naeini said drone operations began that same morning, with missile launches following that night.
  • The Supreme Leader personally intervened. According to Naeini, in the first hours and days of the war, the Supreme Leader “led the situation and rebuilt the command cycle” after senior commanders were killed.
  • A single missile as a message. On the eighth day of the conflict, Iran reportedly launched one missile as a deliberate signal to Israel.

Naeini declared that Iran achieved an “absolute victory” over Israel in this conflict. He also emphasized that Israeli expectations of an internal uprising in Iran proved to be “completely wrong calculations.”

These claims remain Iran’s own characterization of events and have not been independently verified. But they offer a window into how the IRGC wants this conflict to be perceived domestically and internationally.

Warnings for the Future: “We May Not Let Them Survive”

Beyond recounting the recent conflict, Naeini has issued pointed warnings. Speaking at a memorial ceremony for IRGC aerospace martyrs, he stated bluntly: “The enemies have tested our response before. If the Zionist entity commits another folly, it is not certain that we will let it survive this time.”

He also argued that over the past 46 years, adversaries have “never understood the true essence of the Islamic Republic of Iran’s power.” According to Naeini, Iran’s deterrent capability — including missiles, its nuclear program, air defense systems, and other military assets — was demonstrated clearly in the recent conflict.

Naeini drew a parallel to the funeral of General Qasem Soleimani’s father in Kerman, recalling the massive public turnout. He used this comparison to argue that popular support for Iran’s military leadership remains deeply rooted, undermining any external strategy that relies on separating the Iranian public from its armed forces.

What This Means for Regional Dynamics

Ali Mohammad Naeini’s appointment and rhetoric matter for several reasons:

  1. Soft-war expertise at the microphone. Having a spokesperson whose PhD is literally about information warfare suggests the IRGC will pursue a more sophisticated media strategy.
  2. Escalatory rhetoric with precision. Naeini’s language is calibrated — specific enough to project strength, vague enough to maintain strategic ambiguity.
  3. Internal audience is primary. Many of his statements appear designed to reassure the Iranian public that the country’s military leadership remained effective even after losing senior commanders.

The IRGC’s choice of Naeini reflects a broader trend across militaries worldwide: the spokesperson role is no longer just about press conferences — it’s about controlling the narrative in real time.

As tensions in the Middle East continue to evolve, Naeini’s words will be closely parsed by analysts, governments, and media organizations alike. The question now is whether his expertise in “soft warfare” will translate into a measurably different communication strategy for one of the world’s most watched military organizations — or whether the messaging will remain fundamentally unchanged beneath a more polished surface.
