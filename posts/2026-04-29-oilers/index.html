Oilers' Stunning Game 5 Comeback Bid Against Ducks

April 29, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

The Edmonton Oilers, two-time reigning Western Conference champions, are one loss away from the most shocking first-round exit in recent NHL memory. With captain Connor McDavid limping and the team trailing 3-1 in the series, Game 5 against the Anaheim Ducks may define a generation of Oilers hockey.

Quick Summary: The Oilers entered Game 5 facing elimination against the Ducks. McDavid was a game-time decision with a lower-body injury, Connor Ingram got the start in net, and Edmonton jumped out to an early lead behind goals from Vasily Podkolzin and Zach Hyman.

After back-to-back trips to the Stanley Cup Final, Edmonton suddenly looks mortal. The team that once seemed destined for a championship coronation is now scrambling just to extend its season.

Why the Oilers Are on the Brink

The Oilers entered the playoffs as heavy favorites against an Anaheim team many considered overmatched. Through four games, that script has been violently flipped.

McDavid, the most dominant player of his generation, did not record his first point of the series until Game 3. That stat alone tells the story of a captain operating at less than full capacity.

Head coach Kris Knoblauch confirmed before Game 5 that both McDavid and forward Jason Dickinson were game-time decisions. McDavid appeared to injure his ankle in Game 2 after getting tangled with teammate Mattias Ekholm.

When asked about the moment post-game, McDavid downplayed it: “I just rolled up on it a little bit. It’s fine.” The numbers tell a different story.

So how did a Stanley Cup contender end up here? Let’s break it down.

The Series So Far: A Game-by-Game Breakdown

The Ducks have controlled this series in ways few predicted. From timely scoring to disciplined defensive play, Anaheim has neutralized Edmonton’s biggest weapons.

The Game 4 overtime call has become a flashpoint. The Ducks won the game after a lengthy review, and according to The New York Times, league sources called it “a clear-cut decision from the NHL’s perspective.” That hasn’t stopped the debate from raging.

Anaheim defenseman Olen Zellweger and 25-year-old Jackson LaCombe have been revelations. LaCombe in particular is having an eye-opening series against the Oilers.

So what’s been wrong with Edmonton’s goaltending? It’s a question that has dominated the series.

The Goaltending Carousel

The Oilers’ crease has become a revolving door. Connor Ingram started the first three games before being yanked after his Game 3 implosion.

His series numbers are brutal:

  • 4.70 goals-against average
  • .849 save percentage

Tristan Jarry got the call in Game 4 but couldn’t steal the win. For Game 5, Knoblauch turned back to Ingram with the season on the line — a sign of how thin Edmonton’s options have become.

This is the third straight spring the Oilers have publicly considered a goaltending change. As one observer put it, it’s “become a sign of springtime in Southern California” when these teams meet.

The bigger question: can a hobbled superstar still drag this team back from the dead?

McDavid’s Quiet Series and What It Means

Connor McDavid has 154 points in 100 career playoff games — historic territory. But through four games of this series, he’s posted just one goal and three assists.

That’s not McDavid hockey. And the eye test backs up the numbers: he hasn’t looked like himself since the Game 2 collision.

Despite the injury, McDavid was still named a Ted Lindsay Award finalist alongside Nikita Kucherov and Macklin Celebrini. The award honors the most outstanding player as voted by the players themselves.

The Oilers’ first-period response in Game 5 offered hope. Vasily Podkolzin opened the scoring with what TSN called a “wicked snipe,” and Zach Hyman followed with a five-hole finish past Lukas Dostal to make it 2-0.

Leon Draisaitl — fresh off his 1,000-point celebration earlier this season — also got into the action, ending Dostal’s night with a through-the-legs finish.

But early leads mean nothing if Edmonton can’t close. Which brings us to the bigger picture.

What This Means for the Oilers’ Future

A first-round exit would trigger serious evaluations across the organization. After two Stanley Cup Final appearances in a row, expectations were a championship — not a quick exit.

Here’s what’s potentially at stake if Edmonton can’t extend the series:

  1. Roster overhaul: Deadline acquisitions Dickinson, Murphy, and Dach were brought in for a deep run
  2. Coaching scrutiny: Knoblauch’s seat could warm up after a third straight spring of goaltending issues
  3. McDavid contract pressure: Every postseason adds urgency to keeping the captain happy long-term
  4. Cap space decisions: Veterans like Adam Henrique (out for Game 5) face uncertain futures

Common misconception: Many fans assume that two straight Cup Final runs guarantee the same core another shot. NHL history shows otherwise — windows close fast when goaltending and depth scoring fail simultaneously.

For Oilers fans, the next 60 minutes of hockey may matter more than any regular-season game in years.

Key Takeaways

  • The Oilers trail the Ducks 3-1 and face elimination in Game 5
  • McDavid was a game-time decision with a lower-body injury from Game 2
  • Connor Ingram got the start over Tristan Jarry despite a .849 save percentage in the series
  • Edmonton jumped to an early Game 5 lead with goals from Podkolzin and Hyman
  • A first-round exit would force major organizational evaluations after two straight Cup Final appearances

The Bigger Question

Can a banged-up McDavid and a shaky goaltending tandem really come back from 3-1 against a hungry Ducks team? Or are we watching the end of an era in Edmonton?

History says only four teams in NHL playoff history have come back from 3-1 down to win the Stanley Cup. The Oilers don’t need to win the Cup tonight — they just need to win one game. But the road from here is steeper than anything they faced the past two springs.

What do you think — is this Edmonton’s last stand, or the start of an unforgettable comeback story?
