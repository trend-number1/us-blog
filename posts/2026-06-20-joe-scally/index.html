Joe Scally: The Surprising USMNT Star Powering America’s World Cup Run

What if the player anchoring the United States’ right flank at the 2026 FIFA World Cup is also the guy who pulls espresso shots between training sessions? That player exists, and his name is Joe Scally.

Quick Summary: Joe Scally is a 23-year-old defender from Lake Grove, Long Island, playing his fifth season at Borussia Mönchengladbach in the Bundesliga. He’s on his second straight USMNT World Cup roster, helped beat Paraguay 4-1 in the opener, and faces Australia in Seattle on June 19. He recently became the youngest non-German to play 150 Bundesliga matches.

The story of Joe Scally is the kind of feel-good narrative every World Cup needs — a hometown kid who never forgot his first coach, a Bundesliga regular before he could legally drink in the U.S., and now a key piece for Mauricio Pochettino’s American side on home soil.

Who Is Joe Scally? From Lake Grove to the Bundesliga

Joe Scally was born and raised in Lake Grove, New York, on Long Island. He graduated from Sachem North High School in 2020 — though, interestingly, he never played soccer for the Arrows. Instead, he came up entirely through the New York City FC Academy system.

Scally signed his first professional contract with NYCFC as a 15-year-old Homegrown signing in 2018, fast-tracking a path most American kids only dream about. By 19, he was the youngest player on the United States’ 2022 World Cup roster in Qatar.

His soccer DNA runs deep:

  • His grandfather Nick ran a local soccer shop on Long Island for decades.
  • His mother Margaret was a standout player in an Olympic development program before an ACL injury ended her playing days as a teen.
  • His first coach, Frank Schmidt, won two state championships and 11 Suffolk County titles over a 31-year career at Sachem High School.

That foundation matters. After the U.S. lost 2-1 to Germany in a June 6 World Cup warm-up at Soldier Field, Scally could have swapped his American jersey with any Bundesliga peer on the German side. Instead, he gave it to Schmidt.

“He was my first coach,” Scally told Newsday. “He coached with my mom, so he means a lot to me. He’s the one who built my foundation as a player.”

That mix of loyalty and quiet ambition is exactly what’s defined his European career, too.

A Bundesliga Cornerstone at 23

Most American fans still file Scally under “young prospect.” That label is starting to feel outdated.

Scally has just completed his fifth consecutive season with Borussia Mönchengladbach, primarily as a right back. According to Front Row Soccer, he made 32 appearances in 2025-26 with two goals and three assists. Greater Long Island reports a slightly different cut of starts at 31 of 35 games with the same goal and assist totals — either way, he’s a fixture in the XI.

His durability is the headline. The New York Post reports he recently became the youngest non-German to play 150 Bundesliga matches, and over his five-season run he’s logged 153 league appearances.

That body of work is why ESPN reported in May that Scally is expected to decide his club future after the World Cup — a signal that bigger clubs are watching closely.

So how does all that translate to a tournament being played in his backyard?

Inside the 2026 World Cup: USMNT’s Group D Path

The United States opened the 2026 FIFA World Cup at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California, on June 12, beating Paraguay 4-1 in a performance that drew widespread praise.

“We know what to do, and we hope to perform,” Scally told ABC News before the Australia match.

The opening win wasn’t just a result — it was a referendum on belief.

“We’ve constantly asked the question, ‘Can this team even do it? Can this group of players actually put together a performance that galvanizes the nation?’” former USMNT player Taylor Twellman told ABC News. “They did exactly that.”

Here’s how the U.S. Group D slate breaks down:

  1. June 12 — USA vs. Paraguay at SoFi Stadium, Inglewood (Won 4-1)
  2. June 19 — USA vs. Australia in Seattle
  3. June 25 — USA vs. Türkiye, back at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood

Scally, who was named to Pochettino’s 26-player roster announced May 26 in Lower Manhattan, is no longer the wide-eyed teenager from Qatar. He’s now one of the experienced voices in the room.

“I think we have one of the best games out of all the teams who performed so far,” Scally said of the Paraguay win. “So we definitely have a lot of confidence to us.”

And confidence at home, in front of American crowds, isn’t a small thing.

What Makes Scally Different (And Why It Matters For You)

Halfway into this article, here’s the insight worth lingering on: Joe Scally’s value isn’t only in what he does on the pitch — it’s in his versatility and temperament.

According to Greater Long Island, he’s grown into “a more experienced and versatile option capable of playing multiple positions along the back line.” For a national team historically thin at full back, a 23-year-old with 153 Bundesliga matches and the ability to slide across the defense is gold.

There’s also the home-field psychology. Scally described the LA crowd at the Paraguay match as a “12th player on the field.”

“It really feels like we have the whole stadium kind of with us, which is very special,” he told ABC News.

Common Misconceptions About Scally

  • “He’s still just a prospect.” He’s a five-season Bundesliga regular with 150+ top-flight matches.
  • “He’s an NYCFC academy product, so MLS owns the story.” True he came through NYCFC, but his professional development has been in Germany since his teens.
  • “He’s the same player from the 2022 World Cup.” In 2022 he was the youngest player on the squad at 19. In 2026, he’s a starter-caliber, multi-position defender.

That re-framing matters whether you’re a casual fan filling out a bracket or a soccer parent watching your own kid grind through academy tryouts.

What This Means For You

If you’re following the USMNT this summer, don’t overlook Joe Scally just because he doesn’t post highlight-reel goals. Watch the right side of the American defense. Watch how often he’s involved in build-up. Watch how he handles speed on the counter — that’s where Pochettino is asking the most of him.

If you’re a young player or parent, the Scally arc is also a quietly radical case study:

  • He skipped high school soccer entirely for an MLS academy.
  • He signed his first pro deal at 15.
  • He moved to Europe in his teens and committed to the grind of the Bundesliga — not a flashy mid-table flirtation, but five full seasons of consistent minutes.

That path isn’t for everyone. But it’s a real, mapped route for an American kid who wants to be a starting full back at a World Cup before turning 24.

Conclusion: A Long Island Kid on Home Soil

Joe Scally’s story is the kind of dual-identity narrative the 2026 World Cup was made for: a Lake Grove native who’s spent half a decade in Germany, returning to American stadiums to defend the right flank in front of family, friends, and his first coach.

He’s already lived the youngest-on-the-roster experience in 2022. Now he gets to live the home-tournament experience in 2026 — with 150+ Bundesliga appearances behind him and a clear-eyed sense of who he is.

So here’s the question worth sitting with: Will the U.S. men’s team’s deepest run in a generation be remembered for its star midfielders — or for the quiet, ultra-reliable defender from Long Island who turned the right back position into a strength?

Watch Seattle on June 19. The answer is probably already taking shape.
