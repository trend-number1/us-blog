Palantir Stock: The Surprising Truth Behind Its 2026 Slump

July 14, 2026 00:04 Trend Insights

Here’s a fact that stumps most investors: Palantir stock grew its business by roughly 85% over the past year — and the share price still went nowhere. In fact, it fell.

How can a company crush its own growth targets while its stock treads water? That paradox is the whole story of PLTR in 2026, and it holds a lesson every AI investor needs to hear.

Quick Summary

  • PLTR traded around $132, down about 5% over the trailing year and off roughly 21% year to date (as of early July 2026).
  • Q1 2026 revenue hit $1.63 billion, up 84.7% year over year.
  • Shares plunged 34% in the first half of 2026, versus a 10% gain for the S&P 500.
  • The core debate: an elite business trapped inside a sky-high valuation.

Why Palantir Stock Fell While the Business Boomed

Let’s start with the number that matters most. According to data from S&P Global Market Intelligence, Palantir stock dropped 34% in the first half of 2026 — a stunning gap from the S&P 500’s 10% gain over the same stretch.

Yet the business itself was firing on all cylinders. This is the disconnect that has divided Wall Street.

The company reported two quarters during that period, and each topped the last. In Q4 (reported in early February), revenue surged 70% year over year and 19% quarter over quarter to $1.4 billion — its 10th consecutive quarter of accelerating growth. Adjusted earnings came in at $0.25 per share.

Demand for the Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP) drove the results. U.S. commercial revenue soared 137% to $507 million, while remaining performance obligation (backlog) jumped 143% to $4.21 billion — a strong signal of future revenue already under contract.

So if the results were this good, why did the stock crater? The answer lies in one word: valuation. And that’s where the most famous skeptic on Wall Street enters the picture.

The Michael Burry Bet and the Valuation Problem

Michael Burry — the investor made famous by The Big Short — began betting against Palantir in the fall of 2025, when it was one of the loudest momentum trades on the market.

His argument was blunt. At north of 180 times earnings, the multiple was carrying the valuation, not the fundamentals. And when an AI narrative cools, the multiple compresses first.

Nine months later, the scoreboard was mixed. PLTR sat around $132 — down about 5% over the trailing year — while the business behind it grew 85%. Burry wasn’t fully vindicated, but a stock that goes flat for a year while its revenue nearly doubles is telling you something.

Here’s the insight most retail investors miss: a great company and a great stock are not the same thing. Price you pay determines return. When you buy growth at 180x earnings, years of future success can already be baked in.

Retail sentiment whipsawed violently. In mid-May, r/wallstreetbets posted sentiment scores of 88 with nearly 3,000 upvotes in a single window. Two weeks later, after a viral Reddit post called Palantir “a sand castle supported only by AI applications narrative,” sentiment cratered into deeply bearish territory.

That mood flip — from euphoria to fear in 14 days — is a textbook example of how narrative, not numbers, moves high-multiple stocks in the short term. So has the selloff finally made PLTR a bargain? Let’s look at the other side.

The Bull Case: Why Analysts Still Say Buy

Despite the slump, professional analysts remain constructive. According to 32 analysts tracked by StockAnalysis, the average rating on PLTR is “Buy,” with a 12-month price target of $183.12 — roughly 42.5% above recent levels.

The full-year 2025 picture backs up the optimism:

Metric20242025Change
Revenue$2.87B$4.48B+56.2%
Earnings—$1.63B+251.6%

Momentum has continued into 2026. In Q1, U.S. commercial revenue rose 133% to $595 million, and GAAP operating income reached $754 million — a 46% margin. Software companies rarely produce margins that fat at this growth rate.

The company is also expanding globally. In July 2026, Palantir landed its first Latin America commercial customer, partnering with GNP and Rackspace — a sign that AIP demand is spreading beyond U.S. borders.

Some bulls go further still. One analysis argued Palantir could reach a $400 billion market cap by the end of 2027 if commercial adoption keeps compounding.

Which side is right? That depends on how you frame the decision — and that’s where you come in.

What This Means For You

Common misconception: “The stock is down 34%, so it must be cheap now.” Not necessarily. A falling price only becomes a bargain relative to value. After years at extreme multiples, even a big drop can leave a stock richly priced.

Here are three actionable ways to think about Palantir stock right now:

  1. Separate the company from the entry price. Palantir is unquestionably a strong business. Whether it’s a strong investment depends entirely on what multiple you’re paying today.
  2. Watch the backlog, not the headlines. The $4.21 billion RPO and accelerating commercial growth are the real tells. If those decelerate, the bull case weakens fast.
  3. Size your position for volatility. A stock that swings from +88 to deeply bearish sentiment in two weeks is not a place for money you can’t stomach seeing cut in half.

The healthiest reframe is this: the 2026 decline didn’t break Palantir — it partly normalized a valuation that had run far ahead of even a fantastic business.

So here’s the question worth sitting with: if a company can grow revenue 85% and still see its stock fall, what does that tell you about the price you’re willing to pay for growth? Answer that honestly, and you’ll make better decisions on every AI stock you touch — not just this one.
