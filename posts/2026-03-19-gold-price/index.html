Gold Price Drops 13% Since January: Why Experts Still See $6,000 Ahead

March 19, 2026 22:32 Trend Insights

Gold just hit a one-month low — and some analysts say that’s exactly when you should be paying attention.

Despite an ongoing conflict involving Iran and persistent geopolitical uncertainty, the gold price dropped to approximately $5,010 per ounce on March 17, 2026, driven by a strengthening U.S. dollar and hawkish signals from the Federal Reserve. Yet top forecasters still see a clear path to $6,000 and beyond.

So what’s really going on with gold — and should you be buying the dip?

Key Takeaways

  • Gold is down roughly 13% since January 2026, hitting a one-month low near $5,010/oz
  • A strong dollar and hawkish Fed policy are the primary headwinds
  • Despite the pullback, CRU Group sees gold reaching $6,000 — with five-digit potential in extreme scenarios
  • The Iran conflict has not triggered the expected safe-haven rally
  • Analysts recommend using the dip as a strategic entry point

Gold Price Today: What’s Behind the Decline

The gold market in mid-March 2026 presents a paradox. On one hand, the world is dealing with a war involving Iran that has disrupted the Strait of Hormuz and triggered a global oil supply shock. On the other hand, gold prices are falling.

As of March 19, gold rose slightly off its one-month low on a softer dollar, but hawkish Fed commentary continued to cap gains. The precious metal has shed roughly 13% from its January highs, a correction that has caught many investors off guard.

The primary culprits are straightforward:

  • Dollar strength: A rising greenback makes gold more expensive for international buyers, dampening demand
  • Hawkish Federal Reserve: The Fed’s commitment to keeping interest rates elevated has increased the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like gold
  • Profit-taking: After a massive run-up, institutional investors have locked in gains

The combination has created what CBS News called a compelling entry point for new investors — a view shared by several market analysts.

But why isn’t geopolitical risk pushing gold higher? The answer reveals something important about how modern gold markets actually work.

Why the Iran War Isn’t Pushing Gold Higher

This is the question that has puzzled many market watchers. Traditionally, armed conflicts drive gold prices up as investors seek safe-haven assets. The Iran situation — which has disrupted the Strait of Hormuz and caused a global supply shock — seems like it should be a textbook catalyst for a gold rally.

According to DW’s analysis, the explanation lies in the competing forces at play. While geopolitical risk normally supports gold, the dollar strength and Fed hawkishness are currently exerting even stronger downward pressure.

Here’s how the key drivers stack up:

FactorImpact on GoldCurrent Strength
Iran conflict / geopolitical riskBullish ↑Moderate
U.S. dollar strengthBearish ↓Strong
Hawkish Fed policyBearish ↓Strong
Central bank buyingBullish ↑Moderate
Inflation hedging demandBullish ↑Moderate

The bearish factors are simply overwhelming the bullish ones — for now. But as analysts point out, this dynamic can shift rapidly if the Fed changes its tone or the dollar weakens.

This sets up an interesting scenario for forward-looking investors.

The Case for $6,000 Gold — and Beyond

Despite the near-term pullback, long-term gold forecasts remain remarkably bullish.

CRU Group has outlined a clear path to $6,000 per ounce, arguing that the fundamental drivers of gold’s multi-year bull run remain intact. Even more striking, CRU suggests gold has “five-digit price potential” — meaning $10,000 or more per ounce — if belief in the global financial system breaks down.

Alex Ebkarian, a prominent precious metals analyst, highlighted several key drivers to watch for the remainder of 2026:

  1. Central bank accumulation: Global central banks continue to diversify reserves away from the dollar
  2. Debt levels: U.S. national debt trajectory remains unsustainable long-term
  3. De-dollarization trends: BRICS nations continue exploring dollar alternatives
  4. Supply constraints: Mining output has struggled to keep pace with demand
  5. Retail investment demand: Physical gold and ETF inflows remain robust despite the correction

GoldSilver’s March 2026 forecast framed it as “the $6,000 question” — noting that the interplay between Fed policy, the Iran situation, and broader macroeconomic conditions will determine whether gold reaches that level this year or next.

The key insight here is that most analysts view the current dip as a correction within a larger bull market, not the beginning of a sustained decline.

What This Means for Your Portfolio

If you’re wondering whether now is the time to buy gold, here’s what the data and expert consensus suggest.

The Bull Case for Buying Now

CBS News reported that gold’s 13% decline since January actually creates an attractive entry point. The logic: if the long-term trajectory is still toward $6,000+, then buying at $5,000 offers significant upside.

Common Misconceptions

Many investors make the mistake of assuming gold only goes up during crises. In reality, gold’s price action is driven by a complex mix of factors:

  • Misconception: “War always makes gold go up” — Reality: Dollar strength can override geopolitical risk
  • Misconception: “Gold is a short-term trade” — Reality: Gold performs best as a long-term portfolio hedge
  • Misconception: “Gold doesn’t generate income” — Reality: While gold pays no dividends, its appreciation potential and portfolio protection value compensate

Practical Steps

  • Dollar-cost averaging: Rather than timing the bottom, consider buying in increments
  • Portfolio allocation: Most financial advisors recommend 5-15% of a portfolio in precious metals
  • Choose your vehicle: Physical gold, ETFs (like GLD), or mining stocks each offer different risk-reward profiles
  • Watch the Fed: Any dovish pivot would likely be a strong catalyst for gold prices

Gold Price Forecast: Where Do We Go From Here

The gold price story for 2026 is far from over. The current correction has created a tension between short-term bearish technicals and long-term bullish fundamentals.

The consensus among analysts is that gold’s path to $6,000 remains intact, but the timeline depends heavily on three factors: Federal Reserve policy direction, the resolution (or escalation) of the Iran conflict, and the trajectory of the U.S. dollar.

For individual investors, the 13% pullback may represent one of the better buying opportunities of the year. As the old market saying goes, the best time to buy is when others are fearful.

Are you positioned for what comes next in the gold market — or are you waiting on the sidelines while the long-term trend passes you by?
