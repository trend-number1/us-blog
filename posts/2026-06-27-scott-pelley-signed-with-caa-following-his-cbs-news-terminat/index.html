Scott Pelley Signs With CAA After Shocking ‘60 Minutes’ Firing

What does a 51-time Emmy winner do after getting fired with no severance and 37 years on the line? He signs with one of Hollywood’s most powerful talent agencies — and makes it clear he is not done.

Scott Pelley has officially signed with Creative Artists Agency (CAA), just weeks after his abrupt and bitter departure from CBS News and “60 Minutes.” The agency confirmed the deal in an Instagram post on Thursday, June 25, 2026, marking the veteran journalist’s first major career move since his exit.

Quick Summary:

  • Scott Pelley signed with CAA on June 25, 2026, weeks after being fired from “60 Minutes.”
  • The 68-year-old was removed on June 2 following a confrontation with new CBS News leadership.
  • Pelley has won 51 national Emmy Awards, 3 Peabody Awards, and 6 Edward R. Murrow Awards.
  • He joins former “60 Minutes” colleagues including Lesley Stahl at the agency.

This is more than a routine signing. It is a statement from a journalist who spent nearly four decades at one network — and left it engulfed in controversy.

The Firing That Stunned the Industry

Pelley, 68, was abruptly fired from “60 Minutes” in early June after more than two decades on the program and 37 years total with CBS News. According to USA TODAY, his termination left him without severance or other benefits, effective immediately.

The dispute was anything but quiet. His exit stemmed from an internal clash with CBS leadership over the direction of the storied newsmagazine.

Reports point to a verbal confrontation with newly appointed “60 Minutes” executive producer Nick Bilton during a staff meeting. Bilton reportedly cited an inability to find a path forward together.

The broader backdrop matters here. Pelley’s departure reflects sweeping changes at CBS News under new leadership — including David Ellison and editor-in-chief Bari Weiss. Multiple correspondents have exited the program in the past year, raising questions about its editorial direction.

So what exactly did Pelley say that lit the fuse? That’s where the story gets explosive.

“Murdering 60 Minutes”: The Words That Went Public

During a heated June 1 staff meeting, Pelley made accusations that quickly went public — and they were not subtle.

He described Bari Weiss’s early leadership as “murdering 60 Minutes,” according to a recording obtained by reporters. He went further, saying she “does not love this place” and was “brought in to kill it.”

Pelley also questioned Bilton’s qualifications to lead the flagship program, citing his background at Vanity Fair and The New York Times rather than traditional broadcast journalism.

The response from leadership was equally pointed. Bilton called Pelley’s actions a “performative display of hostility.”

The fallout rippled across the industry. Both SAG-AFTRA and the Writers Guild of America East condemned the CBS News firings, while FCC Chair Brendan Carr publicly slammed Pelley as “completely out of touch.”

Here’s the surprising part: rather than retreat, Pelley used the moment to signal ambition. And his choice of agency tells you everything about his next chapter.

Why CAA Is a Power Move

Signing with CAA is not a soft landing — it’s a launchpad. Founded in 1975, Creative Artists Agency is one of the top talent agencies in Hollywood, representing A-listers across film, television, and music.

The agency’s roster includes Oscar winner Jamie Lee Curtis, “Friends” stars Jennifer Aniston and Lisa Kudrow, comedian Tracy Morgan, and pop stars Ariana Grande and P!nk.

For a broadcast journalist, that’s rarefied air. As Mediaite noted, the move “suggests that he’s not planning to sit on the sidelines, and is looking to make a big splash.”

Notably, the signing reunites Pelley with familiar faces. He joins former “60 Minutes” colleagues now at CAA, including:

  • Lesley Stahl — longtime “60 Minutes” correspondent
  • Bill Owens — former executive producer
  • Cecilia Vega — correspondent
  • Tanya Simon — producer
  • Anderson Cooper — CNN anchor who recently stepped away from the program

By choosing CAA, Pelley took a different path from many correspondents represented by rival agency UTA. The agency said it will represent him “in all areas” as he evaluates his options.

But what does a journalist of Pelley’s stature actually bring to the table? His résumé answers that question.

So what should you take away from all of this?

What This Means for You

You might wonder why a media executive’s exit matters to anyone outside the industry. The answer is bigger than one journalist.

This is a window into how legacy news is changing. When a 51-time Emmy winner is fired without severance amid a leadership overhaul, it signals real turbulence at one of America’s most respected news institutions.

A common misconception is that talent agencies only represent actors and musicians. In reality, agencies like CAA increasingly represent journalists, authors, and on-camera experts, helping them build careers across streaming, podcasts, books, and live events.

Here’s the actionable insight: in today’s media landscape, your personal brand can outlast any single employer. Pelley’s swift pivot shows that reputation, credibility, and a strong network are portable assets — for journalists and professionals alike.

The lesson applies far beyond broadcasting. When institutions shift, the people who thrive are those who have built trust and relationships that travel with them.

The Bottom Line

Scott Pelley’s signing with CAA closes one chapter and opens another. After 37 years and a firing that turned into a public battle over the soul of “60 Minutes,” he is not fading away — he is regrouping with one of the most powerful agencies in entertainment.

What comes next remains genuinely open. Will he land at a competing network, launch an independent project, or reinvent himself entirely?
