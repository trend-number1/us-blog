Jerami Grant Trade Talk: Surprising Truth Behind Portland's $160M Star

April 27, 2026 06:03 Trend Insights

What if the Portland Trail Blazers’ highest-paid player is also the one most likely to be moved this summer? That’s the uncomfortable question swirling around Jerami Grant as the 2026 NBA playoffs unfold and Portland’s front office begins shaping the roster around a returning Damian Lillard.

Quick Summary: Jerami Grant is averaging 18.6 points per game on 38.9% from three this season, but his playoff minutes have shrunk to 20 or fewer per game following a calf strain. With a contract reportedly worth around $160 million, he is now at the center of trade speculation as Portland searches for a third star to pair with Lillard and Deni Avdija.

Jerami Grant’s 2026 Playoff Run So Far

Grant returned from an extended absence with a calf strain just in time for the postseason, but he hasn’t yet looked like the featured scorer Blazers fans are used to. In Game 2 of the first round against the San Antonio Spurs, Portland pulled off a 106-103 upset win, and Grant’s stat line was unusually muted.

According to ESPN and RotoWire, Grant logged:

  • 5 points on 1-of-8 shooting (0-of-2 from three, 3-of-3 from the line)
  • 1 rebound and 1 steal
  • 20 minutes of action

That’s now three straight games at 20 minutes or fewer since his return. For a player who has anchored Portland’s wing rotation all season, the reduced workload is striking — and it raises real questions about whether his role expands later in the series or whether the Blazers are quietly recalibrating around him.

The contrast with regular-season Grant is stark, and it sets the stage for an even bigger storyline: the offseason.

The $160 Million Question Hanging Over Portland

Grant is, by a wide margin, the highest-paid player on the Trail Blazers’ current roster. Sports Illustrated’s coverage of the situation frames the dilemma bluntly: Portland is a “middle of the pack” Western Conference team that needs to climb into the tier of legitimate playoff hopefuls — and Grant’s contract is the biggest lever they have to pull.

Bleacher Report’s Greg Swartz captured the nuance well, writing that “Jerami Grant is quietly having a nice season for the Portland Trail Blazers, averaging 18.6 points and shooting 38.9 percent from three while splitting his time between starter and reserve.”

Quietly nice, however, isn’t the same as franchise-altering. And that’s the heart of the issue.

Why Trading Grant Is Even on the Table

A few factors are converging at once:

  1. Lillard’s return. Damian Lillard is expected to be back as Portland’s starting point guard next season.
  2. Avdija’s emergence. Deni Avdija has solidified himself as a long-term building block alongside Lillard.
  3. A missing third star. Per SI, the Blazers “will likely need a third solidified star” to compete — and Grant, despite his scoring, is viewed more as a complementary piece than a true co-star.
  4. Cap flexibility. Moving Grant’s contract would unlock significant financial room for a bigger swing.

Put together, the math starts to look uncomfortable for a player who has otherwise done his job well.

What Grant Brings — And What He Doesn’t

Here’s where context matters. Grant is a legitimate NBA scorer and a respected veteran. But the case for trading him isn’t about him being bad — it’s about fit and ceiling.

StrengthsAreas of Concern
Three-level scoring (18.6 PPG)Limited playmaking
Strong perimeter shooting (38.9% 3PT)Calf injury history
Veteran presence in locker roomReduced playoff minutes (≤20)
Flexible starter/reserve roleHeavy contract (~$160M)

The pattern is clear: Grant is a high-end scorer who doesn’t dramatically tilt a game in other ways. For a team trying to build around a Lillard-Avdija core, that scoring is valuable — but possibly more valuable as a trade chip than as a long-term piece.

This is the exact kind of “good player, wrong fit” scenario that defines NBA roster building. It’s also why his name keeps showing up in offseason rumor mills.

The Common Misconception About “Quiet” Stars

There’s a temptation to look at Grant’s reduced playoff role and assume he’s slumping or fading. That misreads the situation.

Grant isn’t underperforming because of decline — he’s coming off a calf strain that kept him out for an extended stretch, including questions around his availability for Portland’s Play-In game versus the Suns. ESPN, BasketNews, and USA Today all tracked his injury status closely heading into the postseason.

The misconception worth correcting:

  • Myth: “Grant has lost a step and that’s why his minutes are down.”
  • Reality: He’s still ramping back up from injury, and the Blazers are managing his workload during a deep playoff push.

That distinction matters for trade value. Teams evaluating Grant this summer aren’t looking at his playoff minutes — they’re looking at the full-season 18.6 PPG / 38.9% 3PT profile from a 6'8" forward who can defend multiple positions.

In other words: his trade market is probably stronger than his current box scores suggest.

What This Means For You as a Fan

Whether you root for Portland, follow fantasy basketball, or just track NBA trade chatter, here’s how to read the next few months:

  • If you’re a Blazers fan: Watch how Grant’s minutes evolve as the playoffs continue. A bigger role late in the series would signal Portland still views him as core. A continued limited role could be a quiet preview of an offseason move.
  • If you’re a fantasy manager: Treat Grant’s playoff sample as noise, not signal. His regular-season averages remain the better baseline for next year’s projections — assuming he stays in a similar role.
  • If you follow trade rumors: Keep an eye on which contending teams need a high-volume scoring forward. Grant’s contract is large but movable in the right multi-team framework.

The bigger question for Portland isn’t whether Grant is a good player. He clearly is. It’s whether a “good but not great” $160M forward is the best use of a roster slot when a championship window is starting to open.

The Road Ahead for Portland — and Grant

Right now, Grant is doing what veterans do: playing through a recent calf injury, accepting a reduced role, and helping his team steal playoff games. The 106-103 Game 2 win over San Antonio is exactly the kind of result that buys a front office time to think clearly.

But the offseason will arrive quickly. Lillard’s return, Avdija’s growth, and Portland’s hunt for a third star all point toward a summer of difficult decisions. Grant’s contract makes him both the most valuable trade asset on the roster and the hardest one to replace.

So here’s the question worth sitting with: If you were running the Blazers, would you cash in Grant’s $160M contract for a swing at a true co-star — or bet that one more year alongside a healthy Lillard finally unlocks the version of him that takes Portland from good to great?

That answer may define the next era of Trail Blazers basketball.
