McDonald’s Menu Shakeup: Surprising New 2026 Strategy

What if the world’s largest burger chain decided it wasn’t really about burgers anymore? That’s the question hanging over the McDonald’s menu this week, as the Golden Arches unveiled a sweeping new growth strategy that puts chicken, beverages, and value front and center.

Key Takeaways

  • McDonald’s announced “McDonald’s Next,” a multi-pillar strategy spanning menu, technology, restaurants, and hospitality
  • The chain is testing wings and bigger chicken tenders to compete with specialists like Dave’s Hot Chicken
  • McValue 2.0 brings back $3 items and $4 breakfast meal deals to address affordability concerns
  • Six new beverages — including Strawberry Watermelon Refreshers and Sprite Berry Blast — are now on menus
  • AI drive-thrus and store remodels are part of the technology rollout

A New Era: What “McDonald’s Next” Actually Means

On June 1, 2026, McDonald’s CEO Chris Kempczinski sent a system-wide message announcing the chain’s next chapter. The strategy, dubbed McDonald’s Next, is built on four pillars: menu, the consumer, the restaurant, and people.

“We can’t ask our customers to choose,” Kempczinski wrote. “Hospitality or speed. Great taste or convenience. Value or quality.”

The framing matters. For years, McDonald’s leaned on its drive-thru speed and dollar-menu DNA. But with fast-casual specialists chipping away at every category — coffee, chicken, premium burgers — sticking to the old playbook is no longer enough.

The company has reportedly formed dedicated teams to develop products that can compete directly with chains like 7 Brew, Dave’s Hot Chicken, and Five Guys. That’s a notable shift for a brand that historically defined its own categories rather than chasing them.

So what does that look like on the tray? Let’s start with the most surprising pivot.

Chicken Is the New Beef

Here’s a fact that might rewrite how you think about McDonald’s: the Big Mac isn’t even close to its top seller. According to recent reporting, Chicken McNuggets outsell the Big Mac by a wide margin — and the company is leaning hard into that reality.

McDonald’s is currently testing:

  • Chicken wings — a category it has never seriously competed in
  • Bigger chicken tenders designed to take on Raising Cane’s and Dave’s Hot Chicken
  • New chicken sandwich variations

This isn’t a one-off LTO experiment. In a public message earlier this year, McDonald’s USA President Joe Erlinger acknowledged that fans “have made one thing clear: they want more chicken. But not just any chicken.”

The pattern is clear: rather than ceding entire categories to specialists, McDonald’s is building targeted answers for each one. It’s a more aggressive posture than the brand has taken in years.

But menu innovation alone won’t fix the problem that has dogged the chain since 2022 — sticker shock.

McValue 2.0: The Affordability Reset

For all the chicken news, the most consequential change for everyday customers is probably the value reset. McDonald’s has openly admitted that years of post-pandemic price hikes pushed many lower-income consumers away. The fix has a name: McValue 2.0.

Launched in April 2026, the program features:

  • $3 items — including 4-piece Chicken McNuggets and a Sausage Biscuit
  • $4 breakfast meal deal — a Sausage McMuffin or Sausage Biscuit with Hash Browns and a small Premium Roast Coffee
  • A new Under $3 Menu for everyday low-priced options
  • Continued $5 Meal Deal and $8 Big Mac meal as extra-value promotions

In an internal memo to franchisees, McDonald’s described “unanimous alignment” between corporate and franchise operators on closing the affordability gap. That’s significant because franchisees set their own prices and have historically resisted aggressive value pushes that pressure margins.

“From the $5 Meal Deal to McValue and now Extra Value Meals, we’re sending a clear message: we’re here for our customers,” Erlinger said in a public statement. “McDonald’s will always be a place where you can get the food you love at a price that fits your life.”

The strategy is already showing signs of working — McDonald’s remains the most popular fast food chain in the United States. But the chain isn’t betting on price alone. Halfway through this transformation, the most futuristic pieces are still on the way.

Beverages, Squishmallows, and the Drive-Thru of the Future

If chicken is the menu pivot and McValue is the price pivot, beverages are the margin pivot. Drinks are among the most profitable items on any fast-food menu, and McDonald’s has dramatically expanded its lineup.

The current drink rollout includes six new beverages:

  1. Strawberry Watermelon Refresher
  2. Mango Pineapple Refresher
  3. Sprite Berry Blast (crafted soda)
  4. Additional Refreshers (caffeinated)
  5. Dirty sodas
  6. Energy drinks (planned for later this year)

This puts McDonald’s directly in the lane of customizable-drink chains like 7 Brew and Swig.

Collectibles and Culture

The chain is also leaning harder into pop-culture drops. This week McDonald’s debuted World Cup collectibles, Squishmallows, and a specialty McFlurry — a clear nod to the kind of viral, limited-edition energy that fueled the Adult Happy Meal phenomenon.

AI in the Drive-Thru

The technology pillar of McDonald’s Next includes AI-powered drive-thrus currently in testing. After an earlier voice-AI pilot was scaled back in 2024, the company is taking another swing — this time integrated with store remodels and new restaurant prototypes that will roll out in upcoming locations.

What does all of this add up to for you, the customer?

What This Means For You

If you’re a regular McDonald’s customer, expect three concrete changes over the next 12 months:

  • More chicken options, including wings and larger tenders, likely launching in test markets first
  • Lower entry-level prices thanks to McValue 2.0, especially at breakfast
  • A more drink-forward menu, with energy drinks arriving later in 2026

There’s also a common misconception worth addressing: many people assume McDonald’s value menus are loss leaders designed to upsell you. In reality, the $4 breakfast deal and $3 items are part of a coordinated strategy to rebuild visit frequency from lower-income customers — a segment the company has openly said it lost ground with. That means the deals are likely to stick around rather than vanish in 90 days.

And one final detail worth knowing: if you download the McDonald’s app, first-time users get free large fries with any purchase of $1 or more, plus 100 reward points for every $1 spent. It’s the cheapest way to test-drive the new menu.

The Bottom Line

The 2026 McDonald’s menu isn’t just getting new items — it’s getting a new identity. Chicken is moving from supporting role to headliner. Beverages are becoming a destination category. Value is being rebuilt from the ground up. And the drive-thru itself may soon be run by an AI.

The bigger question is whether McDonald’s can pull off all four pillars at once. Specialists got big by being great at one thing. McDonald’s is betting it can be great at everything — what Ray Kroc called “the total of everything.”

Which part of the new menu would actually pull you back into the drive-thru — the chicken, the $4 breakfast, or the new drinks?
