Anya Taylor-Joy's Surprising Career Year, Explained

June 28, 2026 06:03 Trend Insights

What if one actress headlined the first billion-dollar movie of the year, fought a legendary director to a standstill, and signed on to Middle-earth — all in a single 12-month stretch? That actress is Anya Taylor-Joy, and 2026 is shaping up to be the defining year of her career.

Quick Summary: Anya Taylor-Joy, 29, voiced Princess Peach in The Super Mario Galaxy Movie — the first film to cross $1 billion globally in 2026. She’s now joining The Lord of the Rings: The Hunt for Gollum, returning for Dune: Part Three, and leading the Apple TV crime thriller Lucky. She also revealed she fought director George Miller to change the ending of Furiosa.

From a record-breaking animated blockbuster to a “suffocating” action shoot she’s still processing, the Anya Taylor-Joy story right now is one of range, leverage, and hard-won creative control. Let’s break down what’s actually happening.

The Billion-Dollar Breakthrough

Taylor-Joy’s first feature of 2026 wasn’t a prestige drama — it was a video game adaptation. She reprised her role as Princess Peach in The Super Mario Galaxy Movie, the follow-up to 2023’s The Super Mario Bros. Movie.

The result was historic. The Super Mario Galaxy Movie became the first film to cross the $1 billion mark at the global box office in 2026.

That’s not a footnote. It cements Taylor-Joy as a bankable name attached to one of animation’s most valuable franchises — a status few “indie darling” actors ever convert into.

Her path here was anything but conventional. She broke through with The Queen’s Gambit, one of the biggest Netflix limited series of all time, then stacked roles in genre films like last year’s The Gorge opposite Miles Teller.

So how does an actress go from a Netflix chess drama to a billion-dollar mascot movie? The answer is the role she still won’t fully discuss.

The Furiosa Fight She Finally Won

In 2024, Taylor-Joy starred as the title character in Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga, George Miller’s prequel to Mad Max: Fury Road (2015), where Charlize Theron originated the role. The shoot meant six months isolated in Australia.

It was, by her own account, brutal. Speaking to The Hollywood Reporter in June 2026, she described a “suffocating” experience. Miller, she said, directed her to be still and stoic — even instructing her not to breathe, to close her mouth, and to show no emotion to build the icon he envisioned.

That clashed with her instincts. “It was my dream to be in these Mad Max movies and as this crazy feminist icon,” she told the outlet, adding, “I knew that I would enter Australia and leave changed.”

Here’s the insight most fans missed: she fought the ending — and won.

Taylor-Joy went to Miller every day, advocating that Furiosa shouldn’t give Chris Hemsworth’s Dementus a “clean kill.” She wanted “something that required Furiosa to be the one who decides.” The final film ends with Furiosa planting a peach pit into Dementus’ body, which grows into a peach tree as he slowly rots into nature.

“I just advocated and advocated and advocated for her to live up to her name,” she said. “That was my mountain on that movie, and I got it, but it was hard, hard won.”

She remains careful about saying more. “It’s a very difficult conversation to have,” she admitted. “If I were to be completely honest about my experience, it would hurt nobody but myself.”

That restraint reveals something important about where she is now — and where she’s headed next.

What’s Next: Middle-earth, Dune, and a Crime Thriller

The headline-grabber is fantasy. Taylor-Joy is joining The Lord of the Rings: The Hunt for Gollum, from New Line Cinema and Warner Bros. Pictures, set for worldwide release on Dec. 17, 2027.

She’ll play Seren, described as “a Sindar Elf of the Woodland Realm, a trusted and lethal agent of King Thranduil.” Andy Serkis directs and returns as Gollum, with Ian McKellen, Elijah Wood, and Lee Pace (Thranduil) also returning.

She joins a stacked cast of newcomers to the franchise:

  • Kate Winslet as Marigol
  • Jamie Dornan as Strider
  • Leo Woodall as Halvard

The Oscar-winning team of Peter Jackson, Fran Walsh, and Philippa Boyens is producing, with Walsh and Boyens writing the screenplay alongside Phoebe Gittins and Arty Papageorgiou.

Before Middle-earth, two major projects land sooner. Dune: Part Three arrives in theaters on Dec. 18, 2026 — the same day as Avengers: Doomsday. After a brief role in 2024’s Dune: Part Two, the saga’s 17-year time jump gives her a far larger part.

And next month, she returns to television in Lucky, an Apple TV crime thriller based on the book by Marissa Stapley, co-starring Timothy Olyphant.

Here’s how her near-term slate stacks up:

Lucky premieres its first two episodes at once on July 15, then drops weekly across a seven-episode season until the finale on August 19. It isn’t billed as a limited series — leaving room for more seasons if it lands.

She’s also turned up in unexpected places, including the Rolling Stones’ “Jealous Lover” music video alongside Charles Melton.

What This Means For You

If you’re deciding what to watch this year, Taylor-Joy is an unusually reliable signal. Her slate spans animation, sci-fi spectacle, prestige TV, and fantasy — so there’s an entry point regardless of your taste.

A few actionable takeaways:

  1. Start with Lucky (July 15) if you want her newest work the soonest.
  2. Catch up on Dune: Part Two before Part Three hits theaters in December.
  3. Revisit Furiosa with the ending context above — the peach-pit finale reads very differently once you know she fought for it.

A common misconception is that Taylor-Joy is purely an arthouse actress. The data says otherwise: a billion-dollar animated hit, a Dune sequel, and a Lord of the Rings film say she’s now operating at full blockbuster scale.

The deeper story is about leverage. An actress who can quietly win a months-long battle over a film’s ending — and headline a billion-dollar release — has earned the right to choose her next mountain.

So here’s the question worth sitting with: when The Hunt for Gollum arrives in 2027, will a “lethal elf assassin” be the role that finally lets Anya Taylor-Joy stop holding back? Which of her 2026 projects are you watching first?
