TSA PreCheck Amid the Shutdown: Essential Guide to Surviving Airport Chaos

March 19, 2026 21:08 Trend Insights

Three-hour security lines. Travelers missing flights. Checkpoints shutting down without warning. Welcome to air travel during the partial government shutdown — now in its fourth week.

Quick Summary: TSA PreCheck remains operational during the DHS funding lapse, though Global Entry has been suspended. Airport security wait times have soared past three hours at major hubs due to TSA staffing shortages. Here’s what you need to know to navigate the chaos.

TSA PreCheck During the Shutdown: What Actually Happened

The confusion started on the morning of February 22, 2026, when the Department of Homeland Security implemented emergency measures to conserve resources. TSA PreCheck was initially included in the suspension — and for a few hours, airports like Los Angeles International and St. Louis Lambert International reported PreCheck lane closures.

Then came the reversal. By that same morning, TSA issued a clear statement: “At this time, TSA PreCheck remains operational with no change for the traveling public.”

The back-and-forth was swift. DHS Secretary Kristi Noem had announced the suspension, but criticism from elected leaders and travelers forced an abrupt course correction. The result is a split situation that many travelers still find confusing:

ProgramStatus
TSA PreCheckOperational — no changes for travelers
Global EntrySuspended at all participating airports
Courtesy EscortsSuspended — resources redirected to security
New EnrollmentsProcessing delays expected

The key distinction: airports can keep PreCheck lanes open if they have enough staffing. That’s a significant caveat, and it explains why some travelers are still seeing inconsistent experiences.

So if PreCheck is technically open, why are lines still a nightmare?

Why Airport Security Lines Are Breaking Records

The partial government shutdown affecting DHS began on February 14, 2026, when Congress failed to pass a funding bill. Unlike a full government shutdown, this funding lapse only affects DHS agencies — but that includes the TSA.

TSA officers are classified as essential federal employees, meaning they must continue reporting to work. The catch? They’re doing it without a paycheck. These same officers went more than a month without pay during the much larger shutdown last fall.

The consequences are hitting travelers hard:

  • Houston Hobby Airport (HOU): Security lines snaked for two consecutive days, with wait times topping three hours by late morning
  • Philadelphia International Airport: Additional security checkpoints closed as staffing shortages worsened
  • Atlanta, New Orleans, Charlotte: Travelers encountered similar hourslong backups over the weekend
  • Sunday travelers: Some missed their flights entirely — on what was the busiest air travel day since early January

Short staffing has forced the TSA to close some lanes entirely and slow processing in others. The result is a cascading effect: fewer lanes mean longer lines, which mean more missed flights, which mean more frustrated travelers flooding customer service desks.

The timing couldn’t be worse — this is happening during spring break rush, when airport volumes are already elevated.

TSA PreCheck Touchless ID: Your Best Weapon Right Now

If there’s a silver lining in this chaos, it’s the accelerated adoption of TSA PreCheck Touchless ID. This feature uses facial recognition to verify your identity, meaning you don’t need to show your boarding pass or ID at the checkpoint.

Here’s what makes it different from standard PreCheck:

  • No physical documents needed at the security checkpoint
  • Faster processing — the system matches your face to your ID in seconds
  • Already available at a growing number of airports
  • Works with your existing PreCheck membership

To sign up, you’ll need to opt in through the TSA’s enrollment process. It’s a straightforward addition if you already have PreCheck, and in the current environment, those saved seconds can mean the difference between making your flight and watching it leave without you.

But Touchless ID only helps if there are enough lanes open to process travelers — which brings us back to the staffing problem.

What This Means for Your Next Trip

If you’re flying in the coming weeks, here’s actionable advice based on current conditions:

  1. Arrive earlier than usual — add at least 90 minutes to your normal buffer time
  2. Check your airport’s wait times before leaving home. Several airports now publish real-time TSA wait data
  3. If you have PreCheck, use it — lanes are open, but expect some variability
  4. If you have Global Entry, note that arrival processing is suspended. You’ll go through standard customs lines
  5. Consider enrolling in PreCheck if you haven’t — even with current delays, PreCheck lanes consistently move faster than standard screening
  6. Sign up for Touchless ID to maximize your time savings at participating airports

One common misconception: many travelers believe PreCheck guarantees a short wait. It doesn’t. PreCheck reduces wait times by letting you keep shoes, belts, and laptops in place, but if a checkpoint is short-staffed, even PreCheck lines can back up.

The Philadelphia situation illustrates this perfectly. Travelers who paid for PreCheck are finding that checkpoint closures affect everyone, regardless of their trusted traveler status.

The Bigger Picture: When Does This End?

The partial government shutdown shows no immediate signs of resolution. TSA officers continue working without pay, and the longer this drags on, the greater the risk of increased call-outs and further staffing declines.

What’s particularly striking is the contrast between the two programs. TSA reversed its PreCheck suspension within hours, recognizing the backlash. But Global Entry remains down, leaving international travelers without their expedited arrival processing at a time when airports are already overwhelmed.

For everyday travelers, the message is clear: plan for disruption, use every tool available to you, and build in extra time. TSA PreCheck and Touchless ID won’t eliminate the delays, but they remain your best options for getting through security with your sanity intact.

Will Congress act before the spring travel season peaks? That’s the multi-billion-dollar question every traveler should be asking their representatives.
