Jamie Raskin Introduces 25th Amendment Bill to Assess Trump's Mental Fitness

April 15, 2026 06:03 Trend Insights

Fifty House Democrats just filed a bill that could force an evaluation of whether the President of the United States is mentally fit to serve. It’s a long shot — but the fact that it exists at all tells you something about the moment we’re in.

Key Takeaways

  • Rep. Jamie Raskin and 50 House Democrats introduced a bill to create a 17-member bipartisan commission under the 25th Amendment
  • The commission would conduct a medical examination of President Trump’s mental and physical fitness
  • The move follows Trump’s profane Easter threats toward Iran and alarming public remarks in front of children
  • Republicans control both chambers, making passage extremely unlikely — but Democrats are making a political statement
  • Raskin previously demanded the White House physician conduct a comprehensive cognitive assessment of Trump

Jamie Raskin’s Bold Legislative Move

Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD), the ranking member of the House Judiciary Committee, introduced a 10-page bill on Tuesday, April 14, 2026, that would establish a commission authorized by the 25th Amendment to evaluate whether President Donald Trump can continue to discharge the powers and duties of his office.

The bill has drawn support from 50 House Democrats — a significant show of force, even if the legislation faces near-impossible odds in a Republican-controlled Congress.

According to the bill’s text, “the Commission shall carry out a medical examination of the President to determine whether the President is mentally or physically unable to discharge the powers and duties of the office.”

This isn’t the first time Raskin has raised concerns about presidential fitness. Earlier this month, on April 10, he sent a formal letter to White House Physician Captain Sean Barbabella demanding an immediate and comprehensive cognitive and neurological evaluation of the 79-year-old president, along with full public disclosure of the findings.

So what exactly triggered this escalation?

The Easter Incidents That Sparked Bipartisan Alarm

The catalyst was a series of public statements by President Trump that alarmed lawmakers on both sides of the aisle.

On Easter Sunday, Trump posted a profane and threatening message on Truth Social directed at Iran, demanding the country “open the f*****’ Strait, you crazy bastards, or you’ll be living in Hell.” The following day, he wrote that “a whole civilization will die tonight” if Iran did not agree to a conditional ceasefire in the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict.

The alarm didn’t stop there. At the White House Easter Egg Roll, the president delivered what Raskin described as a “disjointed and graphic account of military operations — including bombing missions and hostage rescues” — to an audience of young children.

In his letter to the White House physician, Raskin wrote that Trump “has been exhibiting signs consistent with dementia and cognitive decline” with outbursts that have turned “increasingly incoherent, volatile, profane, deranged, and threatening.”

What made this particularly notable was that criticism came from within Trump’s own party. Former Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) publicly stated that “the president has gone insane” after the Easter Sunday Truth Social post — a striking rebuke from one of Trump’s most loyal former allies.

How the 25th Amendment Commission Would Work

The proposed commission would consist of 17 members selected through a bipartisan process:

  • 2 members appointed by the Senate Majority Leader
  • 2 members appointed by the Senate Minority Leader
  • 2 members appointed by the House Speaker
  • 2 members appointed by the House Minority Leader
  • 4 physicians selected by congressional leaders from both parties
  • 4 psychiatrists selected by congressional leaders from both parties
  • 1 chair elected by the 16 appointed members
  • 4 former high-ranking executive branch officials

The commission would then have the authority to conduct a medical examination and report its findings to Congress.

The 25th Amendment’s Section 4 allows Congress to establish a body — other than the Cabinet — to assess presidential fitness. It’s a provision that has never been invoked in American history, making this bill both historically significant and practically symbolic.

The White House Response and Political Reality

The White House wasted no time pushing back. Spokesperson Davis Ingle called Raskin “a stupid person’s idea of a smart person” and countered that “President Trump’s sharpness, unmatched energy, and historic accessibility stand in stark contrast to what we saw during the past four years when Democrats like Raskin intentionally covered up Joe Biden’s serious mental and physical decline from the American people.”

Raskin addressed the Biden comparison head-on in his letter, arguing that the questions raised about former President Biden’s fitness were all the more reason for the current White House to take stock of Trump’s cognitive state.

The political math, however, is stark. As Axios Congress reporter Andrew Solender pointed out, the bill is a “long shot” because Republicans still hold majority control of both the House and Senate — and Trump could simply veto it.

Democrats appear to be weighing a broader strategic debate. According to NOTUS reporting from April 13, party leaders are deliberating between pursuing impeachment or the 25th Amendment route, with some worried about what has been described as an “impeachment containment breach” — the risk that calls for removal could spin beyond leadership’s control.

What This Means for You

Even if this bill never becomes law — and virtually no political analyst expects it to — the episode matters for several reasons:

  1. It sets a precedent for how Congress can formalize concerns about presidential fitness
  2. It creates a legislative record of specific incidents and expert assessments
  3. It forces a public debate about what standard of behavior Americans should expect from their commander-in-chief
  4. It tests bipartisan boundaries — with even some Republicans expressing alarm about Trump’s recent statements

The question of presidential mental fitness isn’t going away. As presidents serve at older ages and public scrutiny intensifies, the mechanisms outlined in the 25th Amendment will likely face more real-world testing.

Whether you see Raskin’s bill as a legitimate constitutional exercise or political theater, one thing is clear: the conversation about presidential cognitive fitness has moved from cable news talking points to formal legislative action.

The deeper question isn’t whether this particular bill will pass. It’s whether the American political system has adequate tools to address genuine concerns about a sitting president’s capacity — and whether either party is willing to use them when it matters most.
