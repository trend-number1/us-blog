Stock Market News Today: Surprising Records Amid War

April 17, 2026 18:03 Trend Insights

Wall Street just did something almost nobody expected: it shrugged off a war. The stock market news today is dominated by a counterintuitive story — the S&P 500 and Nasdaq keep minting fresh record highs even as the Iran conflict continues, with the Dow joining the rally on hopes of a resolution.

Key Takeaways

  • The S&P 500 and Nasdaq closed at fresh records on Thursday, April 16, 2026, with the Dow rising on war-resolution hopes.
  • The Nasdaq extended its winning streak to 12 straight sessions — its longest run in nearly two decades.
  • Netflix tumbled after hours, while Abbott fell about 5% and Schwab dropped 10% despite record revenue.
  • A 10-day Israel-Lebanon ceasefire lifted global futures heading into Friday.
  • Investors are rotating back into tech and semiconductors as AI-disruption fears fade.

Why the Stock Market Is Hitting Records Despite the Iran War

The headline looks strange at first glance: stock market news today shows equities at all-time highs while a shooting war is still underway. Yet that is exactly what CNBC, Yahoo Finance, and Kiplinger reported on Thursday.

According to Yardeni Research President Ed Yardeni, investors are “looking past the war in the Middle East,” treating the conflict as if it is effectively over and focusing instead on market fundamentals. Peter Oppenheimer, chief global equity strategist at Goldman Sachs, reinforced that view — predicting another stellar quarter of underlying double-digit profit growth, led by tech.

The mood brightened further overnight. Yahoo Finance UK reported that S&P 500, Nasdaq, and Dow futures climbed after Israel and Lebanon agreed to a 10-day ceasefire, giving bulls another reason to press higher into Friday.

That said, not everyone is convinced the all-clear has been sounded. Bloomberg flagged that Asian stocks may fall on concern the war will drag on, and some strategists note that oil prices remain an obstacle even as equities rally.

If the market can climb this kind of wall of worry, the next question is obvious: which stocks are actually doing the heavy lifting?

Tech Leadership: The Nasdaq’s 12-Session Winning Streak

The single most striking stat in stock market news today comes from Kiplinger: the Nasdaq has now extended its winning streak to 12 straight sessions, the tech-heavy index’s longest run in nearly two decades.

Investors are piling back into tech in a hurry. Business Insider reports that tech valuations have receded to pre-ChatGPT levels, and analysts argue the sector could be about to reclaim market leadership after a tough first quarter. As a fragile Middle East ceasefire holds and AI-disruption fears fade, money is flowing back into everything from semiconductors to large-cap software.

A few specific catalysts worth noting:

  • Chip giant breakout: Investor’s Business Daily flagged a major chip leader breaking out to new highs as “bulls roll on.”
  • Quantum rally: Quantum computing stocks rallied on Tuesday after Nvidia announced a new AI advancement utilizing quantum technology.
  • Space is back: Morgan Stanley equity analyst Adam Jonas wrote in an April 12 client note that “Space is back in a big way,” pointing to renewed institutional interest in the sector.

Here’s how the three major US indexes stacked up in Thursday’s session, based on reporting from Yahoo Finance, WSJ, and Kiplinger:

IndexThursday MoveNotable Detail
Dow JonesRoseLifted by war-resolution hopes
S&P 500Fresh record closeBroad-based strength

The rally is clearly tech-led — but as always, not every story under the surface is bullish.

The Losers: Netflix, Abbott, and Schwab

Even on a record day, there are losers — and stock market news today features some notable ones.

Netflix tumbled after hours on Thursday, according to both the Wall Street Journal and Investor’s Business Daily. The streaming giant’s late-session drop was one of the most talked-about moves of the day, though specific earnings details were still being digested by investors overnight.

Elsewhere, The Motley Fool’s live blog highlighted two more painful moves:

  • Abbott fell about 5% after what the publication described as “a flu-season miss.”
  • Charles Schwab dropped roughly 10% despite reporting record revenue — a reminder that in this tape, even strong top-line numbers are not enough if the guidance or mix disappoints.

What This Means For You: Earnings season is separating leaders from laggards quickly, and reactions are sharp in both directions. If you own individual names heading into their reports, be prepared for outsized moves — and don’t assume a “beat” will automatically lift the stock. Schwab’s 10% drop on record revenue is the clearest warning of that.

Ready for the angle most retail investors miss? It’s happening in the plumbing of the market, not on the ticker.

Under the Hood: Private Credit, CDS, and the TACO Trade

One of the most important threads in today’s financial news has almost nothing to do with the Nasdaq’s record close. According to stockanalysis.com, JPMorgan Chase, Barclays, and other Wall Street banks have started trading credit default swaps against flagship private credit funds run by Blackstone, Apollo Global, and Ares Management.

That is a meaningful shift. CDS trading effectively lets the market price the risk of default on these giant private credit vehicles — a sector that a top JPMorgan executive said institutional investors are still adding exposure to despite swirling concerns.

A few other under-the-radar storylines from Business Insider worth watching:

  1. Anthropic’s “Mythos” project has drawn a warning letter to the US Treasury from a trade group, which argued it could lead to “dire consequences for financial stability.”
  2. Allbirds is pivoting into AI, sending the stock climbing — though investing pros are openly skeptical of the bold plan.
  3. Andrew Left praised a newly public quantum company as a big winner thanks to its partnerships with Nvidia.
  4. BCA Research argued that former President Trump would likely hit his “breaking point” with the Iran war once the combined decline in stocks and bonds reached a key level — a framework traders are now openly modeling.

The common misconception here is that record highs mean the market is uniformly healthy. In reality, stock market news today shows a tape where the indexes print records while banks quietly buy insurance on private credit and trade groups warn regulators about AI-driven systemic risk.

That gap between the headline and the plumbing is where the most interesting ideas for the next 12 months are hiding.

What Smart Investors Are Actually Doing Right Now

So how are pros positioning as stock market news today flashes record highs? A few patterns stand out from the reporting:

  • Leaning back into tech, carefully. With tech valuations at pre-ChatGPT levels and AI-disruption fears cooling, selective exposure to semiconductors, quantum, and large-cap software is back in style.
  • Watching the oil tape. Even bulls acknowledge oil prices remain an obstacle. A spike there could flip the narrative faster than any earnings report.
  • Respecting earnings reactions. Netflix’s after-hours tumble, Abbott’s flu-season miss, and Schwab’s 10% drop on record revenue all argue for tight risk management around reports.
  • Not ignoring the plumbing. CDS on private credit funds and warnings about Anthropic’s Mythos suggest the next market shock may come from a corner most retail investors are not watching.

Jim Cramer, host of CNBC’s “Mad Money,” weighed in on the markets reaching new highs and published his usual top-10 watchlist for Thursday — a useful reminder that even in a record-setting tape, discipline and a watchlist beat FOMO every time.

The Bottom Line

The stock market news today is a case study in how markets price the future, not the present. Wars, earnings misses, and private-credit anxiety all exist in the same tape as fresh all-time highs on the S&P 500 and a 12-session Nasdaq winning streak. That is not irrational — it is the market telling you it believes earnings growth, a cooling conflict, and fading AI-disruption fears will matter more than this week’s headlines.

So here is the question worth sitting with: if the indexes can set records through a war, what would it actually take to knock them off course — and is your portfolio built for that scenario, or just for more of the same?
