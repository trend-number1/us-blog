2027 All-Star Game Returns to Wrigley Field

July 14, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

Here is a number that no other ballpark in baseball can claim right now: four. When the 2027 All-Star Game is played at Wrigley Field, the Chicago Cubs will become the only active Major League Baseball franchise to host the Midsummer Classic four separate times.

That is a distinction shared historically only by stadiums that no longer exist. And it turns the ivy-covered walls on Chicago’s North Side into the center of the baseball universe for one week in July 2027.

Quick Summary: The 2027 MLB All-Star Game will be played at Wrigley Field on Tuesday, July 13, 2027. It marks the 97th All-Star Game, the Cubs’ fourth time hosting, and the game will be televised nationally by Fox. It is Chicago’s first All-Star Game since 2003.

Why the 2027 All-Star Game Is Historic

MLB Commissioner Rob Manfred made it official from a podium near home plate at Wrigley Field. “It is my honor to award the 2027 All-Star Game to the Chicago Cubs and Wrigley Field,” he announced.

The 2027 All-Star Game will be the 97th edition of the annual showcase between the American League and the National League. It is scheduled for July 13, 2027, and will be presented by Mastercard.

For the Cubs, this is more than a scheduling win. It is a homecoming decades in the making.

Wrigley Field last hosted the All-Star Game in 1990. Before that, the Friendly Confines welcomed the event in 1947 and 1962 (the second game of a two-game format used in that era). That history is exactly why 2027 carries such weight.

Here is the fact that makes the record books sit up: Wrigley Field now becomes the only active MLB stadium to host the All-Star Game four times. The only other venues to reach four are Cleveland’s Municipal Stadium (closed in 1995) and the old Yankee Stadium (closed in 2008) — both gone.

So how did a 100-plus-year-old ballpark earn this honor again? The answer is in the transformation surrounding it.

The Wrigleyville Transformation

Over the past decade-plus, the Cubs have worked to reshape not just the team, but the entire neighborhood around the ballpark.

The franchise ended its 108-year World Series drought in 2016, a moment that redefined the club. Bringing the All-Star Game back to Wrigley Field has been a consistent organizational goal ever since.

“This is an honor for our team, our city and our state,” Cubs chairman Tom Ricketts said in the announcement. He called Wrigley Field “a baseball cathedral and one of Illinois’ top tourist destinations.”

Manfred pointed directly to the renovations as the deciding factor. “The amazing transformation that has taken place at Wrigley Field and, indeed, Wrigleyville will provide a great setting for one of our most important events,” he said.

The rollout reflected that civic pride. On hand for the ceremony were Cubs executive chairman Tom Ricketts, Illinois Governor JB Pritzker, and Chicago Mayor Brandon Johnson.

There is a poetic touch to the timing, too: the news landed on a Friday morning, ahead of one of the Cubs’ famous 1:20 p.m. afternoon games — a Wrigley tradition unlike anywhere else in the sport.

But 2027 does not arrive in a vacuum. To understand its place, you have to look at the game right before it.

2026 vs. 2027: A Tale of Two Host Cities

The 2027 event follows a milestone edition. The Philadelphia Phillies are set to host the 2026 All-Star Game in recognition of the 250th anniversary of American independence — a deliberately symbolic choice.

That back-to-back sequencing gives MLB two very different but equally resonant story lines: a national celebration in Philadelphia, then a return to one of baseball’s most iconic cathedrals in Chicago.

For Chicago specifically, the drought has been notable. The city last hosted an All-Star Game in 2003, but that one was played at the Chicago White Sox’s stadium, then known as U.S. Cellular Field. The 2027 All-Star Game will be the eighth All-Star Game hosted in Chicago overall — but the first on the North Side in 37 years.

Now for the detail casual fans often overlook — and it changes how you should read the record entirely.

A Common Misconception About “Four-Time” Hosts

Many fans assume that hosting four All-Star Games is a routine milestone for legacy stadiums. It is not.

The reality: no currently operating MLB stadium had reached four hosting duties until Wrigley earned 2027. The other two four-time venues are both demolished. That makes Wrigley’s achievement genuinely singular in today’s game, not just a footnote.

It also underscores a broader point about the ballpark itself. Wrigley Field, which opened long before most of its peers, keeps proving that preservation and modernization can coexist — the exact quality Manfred cited when awarding the game.

So what does all of this actually mean if you are a fan planning ahead?

What This Means For You

Whether you are a Cubs die-hard or a neutral baseball fan, the 2027 All-Star Game at Wrigley Field is worth marking on your calendar now. Here is how to prepare:

  • Save the date: The game is set for Tuesday, July 13, 2027.
  • Know the broadcast: The 2027 game will be televised nationally by Fox, so you can watch from anywhere.
  • Plan travel early: All-Star Week draws fans from around the world, and Chicago summers are a marquee draw. Hotels near Wrigleyville historically book fast for marquee events.
  • Expect a full week: The All-Star Game anchors a week of festivities and community events, not just a single night.
  • Watch for roster news: As with every year, lineups and rosters will be confirmed closer to the event.

The takeaway is simple. This is not just another All-Star Game — it is a record-setting return to one of the sport’s most beloved venues, in a city that has waited nearly four decades to host baseball’s best on the North Side.

Here is the question worth pondering: in an era of gleaming new ballparks, is it fitting that baseball’s most exclusive hosting record belongs to a stadium that opened more than a century ago? Wrigley Field, it seems, still has stories left to write.
