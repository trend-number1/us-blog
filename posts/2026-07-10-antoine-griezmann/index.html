Antoine Griezmann Joins Orlando City: The Surprising MLS Move

A World Cup winner and Atletico Madrid’s all-time leading scorer just chose purple over the Spanish spotlight. Antoine Griezmann has officially traded La Liga for Major League Soccer, and his very first appearance in an Orlando City shirt could not have gone any better.

Key Takeaways

  • Griezmann joins Orlando City from Atletico Madrid as a Designated Player, signed through the 2027-28 season with an option for 2028-29.
  • He scored a goal and added an assist in a 6-0 friendly win over the Tampa Bay Rowdies during his first 48 hours with the club.
  • The 35-year-old is a 2018 World Cup champion and Atletico’s record scorer with 211 goals in 488 games.
  • His MLS debut is expected later in July, setting up a much-anticipated showdown with Lionel Messi.

This is not a routine signing. It is a statement that MLS continues to attract some of the biggest names in world football at the peak of their reputations.

Who Is Antoine Griezmann?

For more than a decade, Antoine Griezmann has been one of the most complete attackers in European football. He can score and create in equal measure, a rare double threat that made him a fixture at the very top of the game.

His resume speaks for itself:

  • 2018 World Cup champion with France
  • Atletico Madrid’s record scorer: 211 goals from 488 games
  • Two seasons at Barcelona playing alongside Lionel Messi
  • Third in the Ballon d’Or twice, in 2016 and 2018

He also spent 17 years in Spain’s La Liga, scoring more than 200 goals across a career that established him as a proven winner on the biggest stages. Nicknamed “Le Petit Prince,” Griezmann built his legend on intelligence, movement, and clinical finishing rather than raw pace.

There is also an American side to his story that makes this move feel almost inevitable. So what drew him across the Atlantic?

Why the Move to MLS Made Sense

Griezmann has never hidden his affinity for American sports and culture. He frequently holidays in the United States and has attended NBA and NFL games over the years.

A committed Kansas City Chiefs fan, he even plays fantasy American football and admires star quarterback Patrick Mahomes. In November of last year, he featured in the official broadcast when the Miami Dolphins beat the Washington Commanders at Madrid’s Santiago Bernabeu, the first NFL game ever played in Spain.

His MLS ambition was long-signaled. When news broke that he was flying to Orlando to finalize the deal, few in the football world were caught off guard.

Here is the insight that ties it all together: for Griezmann, this was never a retirement fade-out. It was a lifelong dream finally realized on his own terms, at a club actively building for silverware.

Orlando City announced the deal in March, with Griezmann arriving as a Designated Player in July after completing the season in Spain. He signed a contract running through the 2027-28 season, with an option for 2028-29.

Club owner Mark Wilf called it a landmark moment. “Bringing Antoine to Orlando is a landmark moment not only for our club, but for our city, our supporters and for Major League Soccer,” he said. GM and sporting director Ricardo Moreira described it as “a statement signing.”

But statements mean little without performance. Griezmann answered that question almost immediately.

A Dream Debut in Purple

Less than 24 hours after Orlando City introduced the French superstar at his official presentation at Full Sail Live on July 7 — a fitting date for the club’s new No. 7 — Griezmann delivered exactly the kind of performance fans had hoped for.

Wearing Orlando City’s colors for the first time on July 8, he scored a goal and added an assist in just over 60 minutes as the Lions defeated the Tampa Bay Rowdies 6-0 in a Season Ticket Member exclusive friendly at Inter&Co Stadium.

The first goal, naturally, came from the man himself. Weaving through the defense in the 32nd minute, Griezmann finished across the face of goal, celebrating in front of The Wall and the supporters who had welcomed him the night before. Moments before leaving the match, he pressured the Rowdies’ goalkeeper into a turnover and set up Iván Angulo for the club’s third goal.

Nearly 11 years after Orlando City introduced Kaká as its first Designated Player, the Lions once again welcomed a global soccer icon to the City Beautiful.

Griezmann on His Ambitions

Griezmann was clear about his priorities. “My goal is to win a trophy,” he said at his unveiling. Asked what he would bring to the club, he emphasized work ethic and example.

“Training hard, playing hard and knowing the offense,” he said. “Helping my team defensively. I think it’s important for me to show the example.”

Notably, Griezmann is not playing for France at the 2026 World Cup, freeing him to focus entirely on his new chapter. So how does his arrival stack up against the club’s ambitions?

Moreira highlighted the intangibles: “He connects with other players on the field, off the field. It shows our ambition to win trophies, to compete for trophies. He can be the face of this club for hopefully many years from now.”

That combination of on-field quality and off-field leadership is exactly what separates a marketing signing from a roster-defining one.

What This Means for You

If you are a soccer fan in the United States, Griezmann’s arrival is a gift. His MLS debut is expected later in July, and he has openly relished the prospect of a showdown with Lionel Messi — the former Barcelona teammate now starring for Inter Miami.

Here is the actionable angle: keep an eye on Orlando City’s schedule this summer, especially any Leagues Cup fixtures and the eventual Orlando-Miami meetings. Those matches are likely to be among the hottest tickets in North American soccer.

Griezmann himself framed the moment simply. “I’ve been waiting for this moment for a long time,” he said of his upcoming debut. “I hope I can score one goal and do some celebration with my teammates and the fans.”

A World Cup winner in his prime years chose Orlando to chase one more trophy. The question now is not whether he can perform — his debut already answered that. The real question is: can one of France’s greatest ever attackers turn a statement signing into silverware?

What do you think — will Griezmann lift a trophy in purple before his contract is up? The answer may define the next chapter of MLS.
