Aces vs Valkyries: The Ultimate WNBA Showdown Breakdown

What happens when a reigning champion stumbles into a matchup with the league’s hottest expansion story? You get Aces vs Valkyries — and on Sunday, the answer was a 24-point statement.

The Las Vegas Aces, fresh off a humiliating 53-point loss, walked into Chase Center carrying pride and a text message from A’ja Wilson. By the final buzzer, they had tied two franchise records and reminded the WNBA that the defending champions still have teeth.

Quick Summary: The Las Vegas Aces routed the Golden State Valkyries 101-77, tying franchise records for 3-pointers made (18) and attempts (40). Jewell Loyd led the way with a season-high 27 points, hitting 7-of-11 from deep. A’ja Wilson added another double-double after privately texting her teammates that another collapse “could never happen again.”

Why This Aces vs Valkyries Matchup Mattered

Coming into Sunday, both teams were in very different places. The Valkyries had stunned the league by climbing to a 5-2 record, including a 90-88 win over the Indiana Fever on Thursday night. They held the second-best record in the Western Conference and were 3-1 at home.

The Aces, by contrast, were wobbling. After dropping the season opener to the Phoenix Mercury — the team they beat in the 2025 Finals — Las Vegas rattled off four straight wins. Then came back-to-back losses to the Los Angeles Sparks and Dallas Wings, and finally that crushing 53-point defeat to the Minnesota Lynx.

The Aces entered at 4-3. Golden State’s expansion-era Cinderella run had everyone wondering whether Las Vegas’s window was actually closing.

Spoiler: it isn’t.

The Message That Set the Tone

Coach Becky Hammon had hours of film notes ready. She had pulled statistics, prepared a pregame speech, and walked into the locker room loaded for a sermon.

Then she said almost nothing.

“I changed my mind,” Hammon explained. “My only comment was, ‘Do I need to say anything?’ and they said no.”

Why? Because A’ja Wilson had already handled it. After the Minnesota loss, the reigning WNBA MVP texted every teammate individually with one message: nothing like that could ever happen again.

“It sat with us,” guard Jewell Loyd said. The team carried that message onto the floor at Chase Center — and then onto the scoreboard.

Eighteen made threes on 40 attempts. That is volume and efficiency in the same breath — and it ties the Aces’ single-game franchise record on both ends.

Jewell Loyd’s Record-Tying Night

Loyd’s 27 points were a season high, but the story is the shape of those points. Seven made 3-pointers on 11 attempts ties the Aces franchise record for triples made by a single player in one game.

That is the kind of performance that bends a season’s narrative.

A few things stand out:

  • Loyd shot 63.6% from beyond the arc in a single game — elite by any guard’s standard.
  • Her output came on the heels of Wilson’s challenge, suggesting the message landed loudest with the team’s veterans.
  • The Aces’ offensive identity, often built around Wilson’s interior dominance, looked completely different — and equally lethal — when the perimeter caught fire.

This is where the “Aces are done” narrative gets complicated. A roster that can shoot 18-for-40 from three is not a roster running on fumes.

What the Valkyries Showed (and Didn’t)

Golden State’s net rating coming in was a modest plus-2.6. Over their previous four games before this matchup, they had actually been outscored by six points despite a 2-2 record. The cracks were there.

Still, this season has been a massive overachievement for an expansion franchise that exceeded expectations in 2025 by reaching the playoffs and now looks bound for another postseason berth.

The misconception worth busting: a 24-point loss to a wounded champion doesn’t undo a 5-2 start. The Valkyries are not pretenders. They are a young team that just learned what a real championship-level shooting night looks like from the receiving end.

What This Means For You (the WNBA Fan)

If you’ve been tracking the WNBA’s Western Conference race, this game reset the board. Here’s the practical takeaway:

  1. Don’t count the Aces out. This is the same franchise that started slow in 2025 and won the title anyway.
  2. The Valkyries are real — but young. Expect growing pains in marquee matchups.
  3. A’ja Wilson’s leadership is now a measurable factor. When the MVP sends a text, the team responds.

Common Misconceptions About Aces vs Valkyries

“The Valkyries’ record means they’re better than the Aces.” Record alone is misleading early in the WNBA season. Net rating, strength of schedule, and championship pedigree all matter — and the Aces still own all three categories.

“The Aces’ slow start is panic-worthy.” They had a slow start last year too. Then they won the championship. History matters.

“This was an upset.” It wasn’t. Oddsmakers favored Las Vegas on Sunday despite the Aces’ rough patch. The market priced in exactly what happened.

So the question heading into the next Aces vs Valkyries chapter isn’t whether Las Vegas can still compete. It’s whether Golden State can grow up fast enough to actually challenge them when it counts.

Final Thoughts

The 2026 WNBA season was supposed to be about parity. Through one Sunday in late May, it might still be about a dynasty refusing to die.

The Aces vs Valkyries rematch — and there will be a rematch — now carries weight it didn’t have 48 hours ago. Golden State has the energy of an upstart. Las Vegas has the muscle memory of a champion and a leader who clearly isn’t done.

So here’s the question worth asking: When a champion gets embarrassed and responds with a franchise-record shooting night, is that a blip — or a warning shot to the rest of the league?

Watch the standings. The answer is coming.
