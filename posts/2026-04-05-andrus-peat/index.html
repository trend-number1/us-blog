Andrus Peat: The NFL Veteran Who Paved the Way for Arizona's Koa Peat

April 5, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

As the Arizona Wildcats stormed into the 2026 Final Four, all eyes turned to freshman sensation Koa Peat. But behind every great athlete, there’s often a trailblazer who came first. For Koa, that trailblazer is his eldest brother Andrus Peat — a first-round NFL draft pick who spent over a decade protecting some of football’s biggest names.

Key Takeaways

  • Andrus Peat was the 13th overall pick in the 2015 NFL Draft by the New Orleans Saints
  • He played 11 seasons in the NFL across three teams: Saints, Raiders, and Steelers
  • The Peat family has seven siblings, all with athletic backgrounds
  • Father Todd Peat Sr. also played in the NFL for seven years
  • Koa Peat, the youngest sibling, is now a standout on Arizona’s Final Four basketball team

Andrus Peat’s NFL Journey: From Stanford to the Pros

Andrus Peat’s path to professional football started at Stanford University, where he developed into one of the top offensive line prospects in college football. He chose to forego his final season of eligibility to enter the 2015 NFL Draft — a decision that paid off handsomely.

The New Orleans Saints selected Peat with the 13th overall pick in the first round. He immediately stepped into a high-pressure role, tasked with protecting future Hall of Fame quarterback Drew Brees during the twilight of Brees’ legendary career.

Peat spent nine seasons in New Orleans, establishing himself as a reliable starter on the offensive line. The Saints clearly valued his contributions, making what The New York Times described as “a significant investment” in keeping him on the roster.

But his time in the Big Easy wasn’t without challenges — and those challenges would define a career marked by both resilience and frustration.

A Career Defined by Talent and Injury

For all his talent, Andrus Peat’s career has been punctuated by a frustrating series of injuries. Here’s a look at some of the key setbacks he faced:

  • Rookie season: Missed games due to a leg injury
  • 2017: Suffered a broken fibula and high ankle sprain
  • 2021: Torn pectoral muscle that sidelined him significantly
  • 2025: Dealt with a concussion while playing for the Steelers, missing the Week 15 game against Miami

Despite these setbacks, Peat consistently fought his way back onto the field. After clearing the NFL’s concussion protocol in December 2025, he returned to start at left tackle for the Steelers’ Week 16 matchup against the Detroit Lions.

His ability to play multiple positions on the offensive line — both guard and tackle — made him a valuable asset throughout his career. That versatility kept him employed even as injuries mounted.

Life After New Orleans: Raiders and Steelers

After nine years with the Saints, Peat entered free agency and embarked on a new chapter. He signed with the Las Vegas Raiders for the 2024 season, then moved to the Pittsburgh Steelers for 2025.

In Pittsburgh, Peat’s versatility was on full display. According to PennLive.com, he emerged as a “surprising new starter at left tackle” for the Steelers, winning the competition for that position. TribLIVE.com reported that Peat was “prepared to step in at tackle,” showcasing the adaptability that had served him well throughout his career.

As of April 2026, the 32-year-old Peat is not currently signed to a team’s roster. His 11-year career appears to be winding down, but his legacy in the NFL is already secure.

So what happens when one Peat’s career fades? Another one rises to the spotlight.

The Peat Family: An Athletic Dynasty

The Peat family story is one of the most remarkable athletic dynasties in American sports. It all started with patriarch Todd Peat Sr., who played as an offensive lineman in the NFL for seven years. Andrus followed directly in his father’s footsteps, choosing the same position and building an even longer professional career.

But the athletic genes didn’t stop with football. The Peat family has seven children, and all of them have some form of athletic background. The youngest, Koa Peat, chose a different sport entirely — basketball — and has quickly become one of the brightest young stars in college hoops.

Koa is a standout player for the Arizona Wildcats’ 2025-2026 basketball team, which earned a spot in the Final Four as one of the best teams in the country. His rise to national prominence during March Madness is precisely why Andrus Peat’s name is trending again.

The family’s success across multiple sports and generations highlights something powerful: the role of family support and athletic development in producing elite competitors. Koa’s journey to the Final Four was almost certainly shaped by watching his older brother navigate the highs and lows of professional sports.

What Andrus Peat’s Story Means for Young Athletes

There’s a common misconception that elite athletes simply “have it” from birth — that raw talent alone carries them to professional careers. The Peat family’s story tells a more nuanced tale.

Andrus Peat’s career teaches several important lessons:

  1. Versatility matters. Playing both guard and tackle kept Peat in the league for over a decade, even when injuries threatened to derail his career.
  2. Resilience is non-negotiable. From a broken fibula to a torn pectoral to concussions, Peat repeatedly came back and competed at the highest level.
  3. Family environment shapes careers. Growing up watching his father play in the NFL gave Andrus a blueprint. He, in turn, provided that same blueprint for Koa and his other siblings.
  4. Career longevity requires adaptation. Moving from the Saints to the Raiders to the Steelers, Peat adapted to new systems, new teammates, and new roles each time.

Sustaining an 11-year NFL career is a remarkable achievement by any measure, especially for an offensive lineman who battled through multiple serious injuries along the way.

The Spotlight Shifts to Koa

As Andrus Peat’s playing days appear to be nearing their end, the family spotlight has firmly shifted to Koa Peat and the Arizona Wildcats’ Final Four run. But every time a commentator mentions Koa’s athletic pedigree, Andrus’s name comes up — a testament to the path he carved.

The elder Peat brother may not have the flashiest highlight reel or the most Pro Bowl appearances, but his 11 years of professional football, his ability to overcome repeated injuries, and his role in fostering one of America’s most athletic families make his story worth telling.

What does the Peat family’s multi-sport, multi-generational success say about the nature of athletic greatness? Is it genetics, environment, or something else entirely? As Koa continues his run through the NCAA tournament, we may be watching the next chapter of a family legacy that shows no signs of slowing down.
