SpaceX Launch Schedule April 2026: Starship Delays, Starlink Missions, and What's Next

April 6, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

SpaceX is juggling an unprecedented launch cadence in April 2026 — firing off Falcon 9 rockets nearly every other day while quietly pushing back its most ambitious Starship test yet. Meanwhile, NASA’s Artemis II crew is circling the Moon for the first time since 1972, and China’s attempt to rival Falcon 9 just ended in failure.

Here’s everything happening in the world of SpaceX launches right now.

Key Takeaways

  • SpaceX delayed the next Starship test launch by a month, according to Elon Musk
  • Multiple Falcon 9 Starlink missions are scheduled from both coasts throughout April
  • Artemis II launched successfully, sending astronauts on a historic Moon flyby
  • SpaceX won a $178.5 million Space Force contract for missile tracking satellites
  • China’s heavyweight rocket designed to challenge Falcon 9 failed on launch

SpaceX Starship Test Pushed Back — What Happened?

The biggest SpaceX news this week isn’t a launch — it’s a delay. SpaceX has pushed back its crucial Starship test launch by approximately one month, Elon Musk confirmed on April 3, 2026.

The delay follows Flight 11, which took place on October 13, 2025, and capped off a busy testing campaign last year. Since that flight, SpaceX has shifted its focus to ground-based testing and infrastructure upgrades at its Starbase facility in South Texas.

While the specific reasons for the postponement haven’t been detailed publicly, this pattern is consistent with SpaceX’s iterative approach. Each Starship flight feeds data back into hardware and software improvements, and rushing a test before the vehicle is ready has never been Musk’s style — at least not when it comes to Starship’s full-stack flights.

What this means for you: If you were planning to watch the next Starship test, you’ll need to wait until approximately May 2026. Keep an eye on official SpaceX channels for updated timing.

So while Starship waits, what’s actually flying right now?

SpaceX’s bread-and-butter Falcon 9 rocket is keeping an extraordinary pace this month. Here’s the confirmed launch lineup for early-to-mid April 2026:

That’s five launches in nine days — a tempo that would have seemed impossible just a few years ago.

The April 6 mission from Vandenberg Space Force Base is particularly notable, carrying 25 Starlink satellites into orbit. SpaceX prepared the Falcon 9 rocket over the weekend, with the launch window opening at 2:56 AM UTC.

This relentless cadence is driven by Starlink’s expanding global broadband network. Each batch of satellites extends coverage and capacity, supporting SpaceX’s position as the dominant commercial launch provider worldwide.

But SpaceX isn’t just launching internet satellites. A major military contract is adding a new dimension to the company’s mission profile.

SpaceX Wins $178.5M Space Force Contract

SpaceX secured a $178.5 million contract with the U.S. Space Force to launch missile tracking satellites, with missions beginning in 2027. The contract, announced in early April 2026, underscores the military’s deepening reliance on commercial launch providers.

This deal fits into the broader Space Development Agency architecture, which aims to create a layered network of satellites in low Earth orbit for:

  • Missile detection and tracking in real time
  • Data relay between military assets
  • Resilient communications that can survive potential adversary interference

For SpaceX, the contract diversifies its revenue beyond Starlink and commercial payloads, cementing its role as a critical national security partner.

Speaking of milestones, there’s also the financial side of the story — and it’s massive.

SpaceX IPO and Record-Breaking Valuation

While rockets grab headlines, SpaceX’s financial trajectory is equally staggering. The company has confidentially filed for an initial public offering targeting a valuation of approximately $1.75 trillion with an $80 billion raise — figures that would make it the largest IPO in history.

Key financial highlights:

  • Current valuation: $1.25 trillion, as noted by Time Magazine in its profile of SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell
  • IPO driver: Starlink’s explosive revenue growth and the proposed xAI merger
  • Retail access: Elon Musk swiftly dismissed reports that popular brokerages like Robinhood and SoFi would be excluded from the offering

Musk has hinted for years that an IPO was inevitable but has consistently stressed the need to maintain operational focus first. With Starlink approaching profitability and the Starship program progressing, the timing appears deliberate.

The IPO news comes alongside the unveiling of TERAFAB, a joint Tesla-SpaceX-xAI initiative to build a $25 billion chip factory targeting one terawatt of AI compute annually — signaling just how intertwined Musk’s companies have become.

Now, beyond SpaceX itself, a historic mission is unfolding right now that has the entire space community watching.

Artemis II: Humans Return to the Moon’s Neighborhood

While not a SpaceX mission, NASA’s Artemis II is arguably the biggest space story of 2026. The mission launched successfully, sending a crew of astronauts on a flyby of the Moon — the first crewed lunar mission since Apollo 17 in 1972.

The crew is currently en route, with live coverage tracking their progress. Photographer Steven Madow captured the launch using 14 cameras, producing stunning imagery that has gone viral across social media.

Artemis II is a critical stepping stone toward Artemis III, which will land astronauts on the lunar surface using — notably — a SpaceX Starship variant as the Human Landing System. That’s another reason the Starship test delays matter: NASA’s timeline depends partly on SpaceX’s vehicle readiness.

China’s Falcon 9 Rival Fails on Launch

In a development that highlights just how difficult rocket engineering remains, China’s heavyweight rocket designed to challenge SpaceX’s Falcon 9 failed during launch on April 3, 2026, according to the South China Morning Post.

The failure is a setback for China’s ambitions to compete in the commercial launch market, where SpaceX currently dominates with its reusable Falcon 9 booster technology. While Chinese aerospace companies have made significant strides in recent years, achieving the reliability and reusability that SpaceX has perfected remains an enormous engineering challenge.

This contrast is striking: on the same weekend that a Chinese competitor failed, SpaceX was preparing yet another routine Falcon 9 launch from Vandenberg — its 200-plus successful mission with the platform.

What This All Means for the Space Industry

April 2026 encapsulates the current state of spaceflight perfectly:

  • SpaceX’s dominance is undeniable — military contracts, commercial launches, Starlink expansion, and a potential record-breaking IPO
  • Starship’s timeline remains fluid — ambitious technology takes time, even for the world’s most prolific launch company
  • Competition is intensifying but struggling — China’s failed launch shows the gap remains wide
  • Human exploration is back — Artemis II represents a genuine return to deep space

Whether you’re an investor watching the IPO, a space enthusiast tracking Starlink launches, or simply someone who looks up when a rocket crosses the sky, this is one of the most dynamic periods in space history.
