Hormiga González: The Rejected Kid Now Worth €15 Million and Wanted by Barcelona

March 29, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

What happens when a 12-year-old is told he’s too short to be a striker — twice? If you’re Armando “Hormiga” González, you come back and break every record in sight. Now 22, the Chivas forward has become Liga MX’s most valuable player at €15 million, and FC Barcelona has him on their priority scouting list.

Key Takeaways

  • Hormiga González was rejected by Chivas at ages 12 and 13 for being “too short”
  • He has scored 23 goals in 33 matches in the 2025-2026 season, tying as the world’s top-scoring striker under 23
  • His market value doubled to €15 million, making him the most valuable player in Liga MX
  • Barcelona, Borussia Dortmund, West Ham, Feyenoord, and Celtic are all tracking him
  • He broke Chicharito Hernández’s historic scoring record with Chivas

Hormiga González: From Rejection to Record-Breaker

The nickname “Hormiga” — Spanish for “ant” — fits a player who was once dismissed for his small stature. In a recent appearance on the Chivas TV podcast, González revealed the painful rejections that shaped his career.

“My brother was already at Chivas, and they invited me to try out at 12 for the Sub-13 team. They rejected me and said I was too short, that I couldn’t be a striker at that height and weight,” González recalled. “I left very sad because I had scored many goals. I thought, ‘What does height matter? I scored a lot of goals.’”

At 13, the same thing happened again. He was invited back to Chivas San Rafael for a week-long trial — and received the same answer. Today, González stands at 1.80 meters (5'11"), a far cry from the undersized kid who was turned away. But even before the growth spurt, his finishing instinct was already elite.

That instinct has now made him impossible to ignore on the world stage.

That last stat is the one that turns heads in European boardrooms. According to Transfermarkt data reported in March 2026, González is tied with Palmeiras’ Vitor Roque as the top-scoring striker under 23 in the world this season, both with 23 goals. Behind them sit Lamine Yamal (18, Barcelona) with 21 goals and Joaquín Panichelli (23, Strasbourg) with 18.

He didn’t just top Liga MX’s scoring chart — he surpassed a legend. In March 2026, González broke Chicharito Hernández’s historic scoring record with Chivas, a milestone that cements his place in the club’s storied history.

His market value tells the rest of the story. The Transfermarkt update for the Clausura 2026 placed González at €15 million, ahead of Erik Lira (Cruz Azul) at €12 million and a group at €10 million that includes Alejandro Zendejas (América), Gilberto Mora (Xolos), Marcel Ruiz (Toluca), and Raphael Veiga (América). Just months ago, 18-year-old Gilberto Mora held the top spot — González’s surge has been that rapid.

So who exactly is lining up to sign him?

Barcelona and Europe’s Elite Join the Race

According to Spanish outlet Sport, Barcelona has placed Hormiga González on their priority scouting list, describing his 2025-2026 performance as “excellent.” For a club that already has Lamine Yamal, the interest signals just how highly they rate the Mexican forward.

The fit makes sense on paper. Barcelona scouts have noted González’s mobility in the box and his ability to combine with teammates — qualities that align with the club’s attacking philosophy. At €15 million, he would also be one of the more affordable options compared to strikers already proven at the elite European level.

But Barcelona isn’t alone. Multiple reports indicate that Borussia Dortmund, Feyenoord, West Ham United, and Celtic are all monitoring the situation. The 2026 World Cup in the United States, Mexico, and Canada adds another layer of urgency — a standout performance on home soil could send his price even higher.

González has already earned his call-up to the Mexican National Team for the March international window, and his first steps onto the Azteca Stadium pitch were captured on camera, a moment that went viral among Mexican soccer fans.

What does all of this mean for the player himself?

The Mentality Behind the Goals

What separates González from other young strikers isn’t just talent — it’s discipline. In an interview on former Chivas legend Fernando “Sheriff” Quirarte’s podcast, González was asked about his personal life. His answer surprised many.

“I don’t have a girlfriend. There are candidates, but I’m very much about my routines,” he said, signaling a focus that prioritizes his career above everything else at this stage.

That single-mindedness has been evident since his youth. Despite being rejected twice by his dream club, he persisted through the lower divisions, growing from roughly 1.70 meters to his current 1.80 meters — but more importantly, growing into a lethal finisher who has made Chivas one of the most exciting teams in Liga MX.

Chivas recognized his trajectory early enough to lock him down. In December 2025, the club renewed González’s contract, ensuring the player who was once told he was too small would remain at the Estadio Akron for the foreseeable future.

But with Barcelona and half of Europe circling, that “foreseeable future” may be shorter than Chivas fans hope.

What This Means for the 2026 World Cup

The timing of González’s breakout couldn’t be better — or more significant. The 2026 FIFA World Cup will be co-hosted by Mexico, the United States, and Canada, meaning González could play in front of a home crowd on the biggest stage in soccer.

Here’s why his emergence matters beyond club football:

  • Mexico’s striker problem is solved. For years, El Tri has searched for a reliable goal scorer to replace the aging Chicharito era. González has answered emphatically.
  • European interest validates his level. A player tracked by Barcelona, Dortmund, and West Ham is no longer a Liga MX curiosity — he’s a genuine prospect.
  • His story resonates. A kid rejected for being too short who becomes his country’s top striker before a home World Cup? That’s the kind of narrative that transcends soccer.

Whether González moves to Europe before or after the World Cup remains the biggest question. A strong tournament could push his value well beyond €15 million. A move beforehand would give him time to adapt to a new league but could disrupt his rhythm.

The Road Ahead for Hormiga González

From being told he was too small at 12 to standing as the most valuable player in Liga MX at 22, Armando “Hormiga” González has written one of Mexican soccer’s most compelling underdog stories — and it’s far from over.

With 23 goals this season, a broken Chicharito record, Barcelona’s scouts in the stands, and a home World Cup on the horizon, every match González plays now carries enormous weight. The ant who was too small has become the player no one can stop watching.

The question isn’t whether González will make the leap to Europe — it’s when, and for how much. Will Barcelona pull the trigger before the World Cup drives his price even higher? Keep an eye on the summer transfer window. This story is just getting started.
