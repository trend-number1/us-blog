Celtic vs Motherwell: Scottish Premiership Title Race Heats Up in Crucial Clash

March 15, 2026 00:45 Trend Insights

Can Celtic afford another slip-up? With nine games left and a five-point deficit to league leaders Hearts, every match is now a must-win for the Bhoys.

Quick Summary: Celtic host Motherwell at Celtic Park in a Scottish Premiership clash with major title implications. Martin O’Neill’s side sit second, five points behind Hearts, while Motherwell — managed by Celtic-linked Jens Berthel Askou — are fourth, 10 points off the pace. Celtic are unbeaten in eight of their last nine league games under O’Neill’s second interim spell, but Motherwell already beat them 2-0 in December.

A Season of Turbulence for Celtic

Celtic’s 2025-26 campaign has been anything but straightforward. The Bhoys have gone through three managers this season — a remarkable level of instability for one of Scotland’s biggest clubs.

Brendan Rodgers departed in late October after a breakdown in relations with the board. His final two league games both ended in defeat, leaving Celtic eight points behind Hearts.

Legendary manager Martin O’Neill stepped in on an interim basis and immediately transformed the team’s fortunes, winning all five of his matches to bring Celtic level with the leaders.

Then came the ill-fated appointment of Wilfried Nancy, whose tenure lasted just 33 days — the shortest in the club’s history. Nancy managed just two wins from eight matches, losing six, before being dismissed.

O’Neill returned for a second interim spell and has since guided Celtic to six wins, two draws, and one loss in nine league games, while also reaching the Scottish FA Cup semi-finals by beating Rangers on penalties in the quarter-finals.

Despite the turbulence, Celtic remain firmly in the title fight. But can they sustain their challenge with the pressure mounting?

Motherwell: The Dark Horse Under Askou

Don’t underestimate Motherwell. Under Danish manager Jens Berthel Askou, the Steelmen have become one of the most entertaining sides in the Scottish Premiership this season.

Their fast and fluid football has won admirers across the league, and Askou is considered by many Celtic fans as a strong candidate to become the club’s next permanent manager.

Key facts about Motherwell’s season:

  • Sitting 4th in the Scottish Premiership, 10 points behind leaders Hearts
  • Already beat Celtic 2-0 in December, when Wilfried Nancy’s side were “chasing shadows”
  • Known for their attacking style even after a recent defeat at Dundee
  • Well-drilled defensively despite their adventurous approach

Team News: Key Absences and Returns

Celtic have several selection headaches heading into this crucial fixture.

Out for Celtic:

  • Auston Trusty — Serving the final game of his league suspension following a red card against Hibs
  • Callum McGregor — Supporters face an anxious wait regarding the extent of his injury
  • Araujo — Not included in the starting XI

Celtic Starting XI (confirmed by Martin O’Neill):

  • Kieran Tierney starts, with O’Neill confirming a “double boost” in fitness news ahead of the match
  • Viljami Sinisalo in goal, appearing to be the new first-choice after a reassuring performance in the cup quarter-final
  • Adam Cvancara is tipped to start up front after the penalty win, having impressed recently

O’Neill made three changes from the previous lineup, looking to keep things fresh with the congested schedule ahead.

For Motherwell, Askou is expected to stick with the same attacking philosophy that has defined their season, despite last week’s setback at Dundee.

Why This Match Matters for the Title Race

The mathematics are simple. Celtic trail Hearts by five points with nine games remaining. Every dropped point makes their task exponentially harder.

What makes this fixture particularly tricky:

  1. Motherwell’s motivation — They’ve already shown they can beat Celtic this season
  2. Celtic’s recent wobble — Three losses in their last six games across all competitions, including the Milan-style derby defeat mentioned in broader context
  3. Psychological factor — The off-pitch headlines around managerial uncertainty could be a distraction
  4. Askou’s audition — If the Motherwell boss is indeed a target for the permanent Celtic job, he’ll want to showcase his credentials

O’Neill’s decision to skip his pre-match press conference suggests a deliberate focus on preparation over media management — a veteran move from the experienced manager.

What This Means for Scottish Football Fans

This Celtic vs Motherwell clash is more than just three points. It’s a litmus test for Celtic’s title credentials under extraordinary circumstances.

If Celtic win, they maintain pressure on Hearts and keep the five-point gap manageable. If Motherwell pull off another upset, the title race could effectively be over.

For neutral fans, the subplot of Askou potentially managing against his future employer adds an extra layer of intrigue. Could a Motherwell victory actually help Celtic in the long run by convincing the board to appoint the Dane permanently?

One thing is certain: Celtic Park will be rocking on Saturday afternoon. In a season that has delivered three managers, dramatic cup wins, and unexpected defeats, this Scottish Premiership clash promises to be another unforgettable chapter.
