University of Arizona's Historic Final Four Run: Everything You Need to Know

April 3, 2026 18:03 Trend Insights

The University of Arizona is heading to the Final Four for the first time in 25 years — and the entire city of Tucson is losing its mind.

After a dominant March Madness run that saw the No. 1 seed Wildcats win every game by double digits, Arizona punched its ticket to Indianapolis with a 79-64 comeback victory over No. 2 seed Purdue in the Elite Eight. It’s a moment a quarter-century in the making.

Key Takeaways:

  • Arizona advances to its first Final Four since 2001 after beating Purdue 79-64
  • The Wildcats outscored Purdue 48-26 in the second half after trailing at halftime
  • Koa Peat led with 20 points and was named West Region Most Outstanding Player
  • All four NCAA Tournament wins came by more than 10 points
  • Tucson is preparing massive watch parties, with free student tickets and heightened police presence for game day

Arizona Wildcats Break the March Curse

For years, the narrative around Arizona basketball was one of March heartbreak. Despite consistently strong regular seasons, the program hadn’t reached the Final Four since 2001 — a drought that spanned the better part of two decades.

That curse is now officially shattered.

Playing in San Jose, California, the Wildcats found themselves in unfamiliar territory against Purdue. The Boilermakers went on a run just before halftime to build a seven-point lead, marking the first time Arizona had trailed in the entire 2026 NCAA Tournament.

But this team doesn’t flinch. Entering the game, the Wildcats were 5-0 when trailing at halftime this season — and they made it 6-0 in emphatic fashion.

Arizona stormed out of the locker room with a 16-3 run to seize the lead, then never looked back. The second-half statistics told the whole story:

Purdue, which had hit seven three-pointers in the first half, managed just one in the final 20 minutes — and that came when the game was already decided. The defensive adjustment by head coach Tommy Lloyd was nothing short of masterful.

So what’s fueling Arizona’s dominance heading into the biggest weekend of the season?

Koa Peat and a Balanced Scoring Attack

The individual star of the Elite Eight was Koa Peat, who poured in 20 points and earned West Region Most Outstanding Player honors. But what makes this Arizona squad so dangerous is its depth.

Four starters finished in double figures against Purdue, making it nearly impossible for opponents to key in on a single player. That balanced attack has been a recurring theme throughout the tournament, where Arizona won all four games by more than 10 points — one of the most impressive runs of the entire bracket.

Coach Tommy Lloyd, who made headlines for an inadvertent fashion choice — wearing UNC colors before the game, which he laughed off saying “I must have a blind spot when it comes to fashion” — has built a team that thrives under pressure.

The combination of elite scoring, suffocating defense, and a roster that elevates in the second half makes Arizona a legitimate title contender heading into Indianapolis. But the excitement isn’t limited to the court.

Tucson Goes All In: Watch Parties, Free Tickets, and Final Four Frenzy

Back in Tucson, the city is rallying behind its Wildcats like it’s 1997 all over again — the last time Arizona won a national championship.

McKale Center at ALKEME Arena is hosting an official watch party for students and fans, transforming the iconic arena into a sea of red and blue. The university also scored a major win for its student body: Arizona students received free tickets to the Final Four in Indianapolis, giving the next generation of fans a chance to witness history in person.

The energy has spilled beyond campus. Local businesses are bracing for the biggest weekend in recent memory:

  • Tucson bars and restaurants are expecting wall-to-wall crowds throughout the weekend
  • Police presence and traffic control measures are being deployed across the city for game day
  • A Tucson Mexican snack shop even created special Wildcat-themed candies for March Madness
  • The university issued a warning about counterfeit merchandise, urging fans to buy only from authorized retailers

Perhaps the most heartwarming story: a former Arizona mascot from the 1997 championship team traveled to Indianapolis to watch the Wildcats in the Final Four, bridging the gap between Arizona’s last golden era and what could become its next one.

The atmosphere proves that this isn’t just a basketball story — it’s a community event that has unified an entire region. And there’s more happening at the university than just sports.

Beyond Basketball: A University on the Rise

While the Final Four dominance grabs headlines, the University of Arizona has been making significant strides off the court as well.

Under President Suresh Garimella, who is completing his first full academic year, the university launched several major initiatives for 2025–26:

  • No tuition increase for in-state undergraduate students
  • Guaranteed admission for every eligible Arizona student to one of the UA’s campuses — the main campus in Tucson, Arizona Online, or UA Global Campus
  • Expanded partnerships with K-12 schools and community colleges to create more pathways to a degree
  • Increased investment in wellness services, career preparation, internships, and study-abroad opportunities

“Student success is our North Star, excellence is the standard,” Garimella said when announcing the new direction.

The university has also been active in research, with a newly announced study investigating growth hormones and cognitive aging in dogs — adding to Arizona’s reputation as a research powerhouse.

Meanwhile, discussions continue around integrating the UA Global Campus (formerly Ashford University, acquired in 2020 for $1) more fully into the main university, including academic reviews of programs and potential faculty integration.

These academic and institutional moves signal that Arizona is positioning itself for growth on every front — not just on the hardwood.

What This Means for Arizona Fans and Students

The Final Four appearance is more than a sporting event — it’s a brand-building moment for the entire university. National television exposure during March Madness historically drives application increases, boosts alumni donations, and raises the profile of academic programs.

For current students, this is a once-in-a-generation experience. Many weren’t alive the last time Arizona made the Final Four, making this weekend in Indianapolis genuinely historic.

For Tucson residents and local businesses, the economic impact of a deep tournament run is substantial — from packed restaurants to merchandise sales to national media attention on the city.

And for the program itself, breaking the 25-year Final Four drought removes the psychological weight that had hung over Arizona basketball for decades. Win or lose this weekend, the trajectory of the program has fundamentally shifted.

Looking Ahead: Can Arizona Win It All?

With a dominant run that saw every opponent beaten by double digits, the Arizona Wildcats enter the Final Four as one of the most complete teams in the tournament. A balanced scoring attack led by Koa Peat, stifling second-half defense, and a coach who knows how to make halftime adjustments — it’s a formula built for a championship run.

The last time Arizona won a national title was 1997. Nearly three decades later, the pieces are in place for history to repeat itself.

Whether you’re watching from McKale Center, a packed Tucson bar, or your living room, one thing is clear: the University of Arizona is back on college basketball’s biggest stage — and this time, they look like they belong.

Will Arizona complete the journey and bring home a national championship? The Final Four tips off this weekend in Indianapolis.
