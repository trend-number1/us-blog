T. Rowe Price AUM Drops to $1.71 Trillion: What Investors Need to Know

April 15, 2026 18:03 Trend Insights

$3.2 billion walked out the door in a single month. That’s the reality T. Rowe Price Group (NASDAQ: TROW) is facing as the Baltimore-based asset management giant reported its March 2026 assets under management fell to $1.71 trillion, down from the previous month amid persistent market headwinds and investor outflows.

Key Takeaways

  • T. Rowe Price reported $1.71 trillion in AUM for March 2026, a month-over-month decline
  • $3.2 billion in net outflows hit the firm in March alone
  • Goldman Sachs set a new price target of $85.00 — well below the current trading price
  • The stock closed at $95.84, up 1.56% on the day and 8% over the past month
  • 2025 revenue reached $7.31 billion (+3.12% YoY), but earnings dipped slightly to $2.04 billion (-0.38%)

So what’s really happening at one of America’s oldest independent asset managers, and should investors be concerned? Let’s break it down.

T. Rowe Price March AUM: The Numbers Behind the Decline

On April 13, 2026, T. Rowe Price officially announced its month-end assets under management for March at $1.71 trillion. While that figure still places the firm among the world’s largest asset managers, the direction of the trend is what has analysts paying close attention.

The decline was driven by two factors:

  • Net client outflows of $3.2 billion — money actively leaving the firm’s funds
  • Broader market weakness that dragged down portfolio valuations across the industry

This isn’t an isolated event. The asset management industry has been grappling with a prolonged shift as investors increasingly move toward passive index funds and lower-cost alternatives. For an active management firm like T. Rowe Price, which built its reputation on 85+ years of fundamental, bottom-up research, the competitive pressure is real.

Still, $1.71 trillion is a massive base. The question is whether the outflow trend accelerates or stabilizes in the coming quarters.

Stock Performance: A Mixed Picture for TROW

Despite the AUM headwinds, TROW stock has shown surprising resilience in the short term. The stock closed at $95.84 on April 14, gaining 1.56% on the day — outpacing the S&P 500’s 1.18% gain and the Dow’s 0.66% rise.

Over the past month, shares have climbed 8%, beating broader market returns. But zoom out, and the picture gets more complicated.

Here’s the tension: Goldman Sachs recently set a price target of $85.00 — roughly 11% below the current trading price. And the consensus among 12 analysts is a “Sell” rating, even though the average 12-month target of $99.17 implies a modest 3.48% upside from current levels.

That disconnect between short-term momentum and analyst skepticism is worth watching closely.

Revenue Up, Earnings Down: The 2025 Financial Snapshot

T. Rowe Price’s 2025 full-year results tell a nuanced story. Revenue grew 3.12% year-over-year to $7.31 billion, up from $7.09 billion in 2024. On the surface, that’s healthy top-line growth for a mature financial services firm.

But earnings slipped 0.38% to $2.04 billion, suggesting that rising costs or margin pressure offset the revenue gains. For a company that prides itself on disciplined management, even a slight earnings decline draws scrutiny — especially when competitors are finding ways to grow profitably.

The firm has been investing in new areas to diversify its business. Notably, T. Rowe Price announced its entrance into the private credit market in early April 2026, with a first deal closing at $403.59 million. This move signals the company’s ambition to compete beyond traditional equity and fixed-income mutual funds.

However, as the firm itself noted, most private credit funds launched recently were already in development before enthusiasm cooled for the category — raising questions about timing.

Institutional Investors Are Repositioning

Major institutional shareholders are making notable moves. Baillie Gifford & Co., the Edinburgh-based asset manager with over $450 billion in AUM, trimmed its T. Rowe Price stake by 2.3% in Q4 2025, selling 11,166 shares. The fund now holds 482,941 shares worth approximately $49.4 million.

Baillie Gifford isn’t alone. Other significant holders — including Northern Trust Corp, Dimensional Fund Advisors, and Caisse de depot et placement du Quebec — maintain large positions, but shifts in institutional ownership often signal changing sentiment.

When institutions reduce exposure, retail investors should at least ask: what do they see that the broader market might be missing?

The AI Factor: LLMs and Legacy Financial Firms

One of the most interesting recent developments came from T. Rowe Price’s Tony Wang, who discussed how large language model (LLM) advancement is creating a competitive dynamic for legacy software firms. While his comments were focused on the tech sector’s investment landscape, the implications extend to T. Rowe Price itself.

Asset management firms that leverage AI for research, portfolio optimization, and client service stand to gain significant advantages. T. Rowe Price’s recognition of AI’s disruptive potential — both as an investment theme and an operational tool — suggests the firm is at least thinking about how technology reshapes its industry.

The question for investors is whether T. Rowe Price can move fast enough to integrate these technologies while managing the structural challenges of active management in a passive-dominated world.

What This Means for You

If you’re a current T. Rowe Price investor, the March AUM report isn’t cause for panic, but it deserves attention. The $3.2 billion outflow represents less than 0.2% of total assets — manageable in isolation, but concerning if it becomes a pattern.

Here’s what to watch:

  1. April and May AUM reports — Do outflows accelerate or reverse?
  2. Private credit expansion — Can this new business line generate meaningful revenue?
  3. Dividend sustainability — T. Rowe Price has long been known for its strong dividend, which remains a key draw for income investors
  4. Q1 2026 earnings — Expected soon, these will reveal whether the earnings dip from 2025 is a trend or a one-time event
  5. Analyst revisions — Goldman’s $85 target is bearish; will others follow or push back?

For prospective investors, the current environment presents a classic value question. At $95.84, TROW trades at a level where the dividend yield becomes increasingly attractive — but only if the firm can stabilize its asset base and find new growth engines.

The Road Ahead for T. Rowe Price

T. Rowe Price stands at a crossroads. With 85 years of investment heritage, a $1.71 trillion asset base, and a freshly launched private credit platform, the firm has real strengths. But $3.2 billion in monthly outflows, a consensus “Sell” rating, and an industry-wide shift toward passive investing create headwinds that can’t be ignored.

The firm’s ability to adapt — whether through AI integration, alternative asset classes, or fee structure innovation — will determine whether this is a temporary rough patch or the start of a longer structural decline.

What would it take for T. Rowe Price to change your mind — as a buyer or a seller?
