Timberwolves Stunning 3-1 Lead Despite Crushing Injuries

April 28, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

How does a team lose both starting guards in a single playoff game and still walk away with a commanding series lead? Just ask the Minnesota Timberwolves.

Quick Summary: The MN Timberwolves lead the Denver Nuggets 3-1 in their first-round playoff series despite losing Anthony Edwards (knee hyperextension, week-to-week) and Donte DiVincenzo (torn right Achilles tendon) in Game 4. Backup guard Ayo Dosunmu erupted for a career-high 43 points to deliver a 112-96 win.

This isn’t just a basketball story — it’s a story about resilience, depth, and a franchise refusing to let circumstances dictate the outcome. Here’s everything you need to know about one of the most dramatic series in the 2026 NBA Playoffs.

The Edwards Injury That Stunned Minnesota

Late in the second quarter of Game 4, Anthony Edwards leapt to contest a layup attempt by Nuggets forward Cameron Johnson. Johnson’s forward momentum pushed Edwards backward in midair. He landed awkwardly, his left knee buckled, and the entire Target Center held its breath.

The team announced Monday that Edwards is week-to-week with a left knee hyperextension and bone bruise. An MRI conducted at the Mayo Clinic confirmed there was no serious ligament damage — a small mercy in an otherwise nightmarish moment.

Before the injury, Edwards was already battling a nagging right knee issue that had cost him extended time at the end of the regular season. Through four playoff games, he averaged 18.5 points, 6.8 rebounds, 3.0 assists, and 1.3 blocks, though his shooting slash line of .358/.258/.857 reflected just how much he was grinding through pain.

So how did Minnesota take Game 4 anyway? That answer arrived in the form of a relatively unknown guard.

Ayo Dosunmu’s Career Night Saved the Series

When Donte DiVincenzo went down with a torn right Achilles tendon early in the first quarter — and then Edwards followed him to the locker room minutes later — the Timberwolves were looking at a bench-driven playoff game against the defending Western Conference contenders.

Enter Ayo Dosunmu.

The breakout guard scored a career-high 43 points to lead Minnesota to a 112-96 victory. It was the kind of performance that turns role players into folk heroes — the type of game opposing teams will study tape on for the rest of the series.

Key contributors filling the void:

  • Ayo Dosunmu — career-high 43 points, primary scoring catalyst
  • Mike Conley — veteran floor general, steadying the offense
  • Bones Hyland — bench scoring punch
  • Terrence Shannon Jr. — rotation minutes in critical moments

This is what depth looks like when it actually matters. Most teams lose two starters and quietly surrender. Minnesota turned it into a statement win.

The Nuggets are on the brink of elimination in the first round for the first time in the last four years. That’s a remarkable shift in the Western Conference power dynamic — and it’s happening with Minnesota’s superstar mostly watching from the bench.

What’s Really Going Wrong for Denver

The Nuggets entered this series as a battle-tested core that had won at the highest level. Yet Minnesota has methodically dismantled their rhythm. Friction has boiled over too — the NBA fined Nikola Jokić and Julius Randle following an altercation in Game 4.

When tempers flare in a series you’re losing, it usually signals something deeper. Denver is also without forward Peyton Watson, compounding their depth issues just as Minnesota’s reserves are catching fire.

Here’s the surprising insight: the Wolves’ internal bond, not their star talent, has powered this run. Reports out of the locker room describe a team rallying around adversity rather than fracturing under it. That intangible quality — chemistry under duress — is something analytics can’t measure but every playoff veteran recognizes immediately.

So what does this mean if Edwards remains sidelined?

What Comes Next for the Timberwolves

Minnesota now needs to close out the series with just one win in their next three games. That sounds simple — but closing out an experienced playoff team without your franchise star is anything but routine.

Three things to watch heading into Game 5:

  1. Can Dosunmu repeat? A 43-point game is rarely repeatable. Denver will tighten its defensive scheme around him.
  2. Will Edwards return? “Week-to-week” leaves the door open — but rushing a hyperextended knee is risky territory.
  3. Conley’s veteran calm. With young guards thrust into bigger roles, Mike Conley’s experience becomes priceless.

If Minnesota wins Game 5 in Denver, they advance regardless of Edwards’ availability. If they lose, this becomes a much more complicated conversation about whether to risk their superstar in a Game 6 or 7 scenario.

What This Means For You as a Fan

Whether you’re a Timberwolves loyalist, a casual NBA viewer, or someone tracking playoff betting markets, the takeaway is the same: don’t write off teams based on star availability alone. The 2026 playoffs are reminding everyone that depth, coaching, and heart still decide series — not just box score names.

A Reminder That Playoffs Belong to the Brave

The common misconception is that playoff basketball is purely a star-driven enterprise. The 2026 Timberwolves are rewriting that narrative in real time. Edwards on the bench. DiVincenzo in a walking boot. And yet — a 3-1 lead, a stunned defending contender, and a city believing again.

Game 5 is the inflection point. If Dosunmu, Conley, Hyland, and Shannon Jr. can deliver one more road win, this becomes one of the most improbable first-round closeouts in recent memory.

The question worth asking: When your best player goes down, do you fold — or do you find out who you really are? The Minnesota Timberwolves are answering that question one possession at a time.

Will the MN Timberwolves finish what they started, or will Denver’s experience finally claw the series back? Game 5 has the answer.
