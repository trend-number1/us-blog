Devils vs Red Wings: Detroit's Do-or-Die Playoff Push in Home Finale

April 12, 2026 06:03 Trend Insights

The Devils vs Red Wings matchup on April 11, 2026 wasn’t just another late-season game — it was the most consequential home game Detroit has played all year. With only three games remaining and a three-point deficit to the Ottawa Senators for the final Eastern Conference Wild Card spot, every shift carried elimination-level stakes.

Key Takeaways

  • Detroit (41-29-9, 91 points) needed a win to keep playoff hopes alive against New Jersey (40-36-3, 83 points)
  • A Red Wings loss combined with an Ottawa win would officially end Detroit’s postseason push
  • Patrick Kane has been scorching hot: 23 points in his last 17 games
  • Dylan Larkin delivered a hat trick and an assist in Detroit’s previous outing against Philadelphia
  • Devils lost defenseman Luke Hughes for the rest of the season to an undisclosed procedure

Detroit Red Wings Fight for Survival in Wild Card Race

The math is brutal and simple for the Red Wings. Sitting at 91 points with three games left, Detroit trails the Ottawa Senators (42-27-10, 94 points) by three points in the battle for the second Eastern Conference Wild Card berth. The Boston Bruins (43-26-10, 96 points) hold the first Wild Card spot.

A loss to New Jersey, combined with any Ottawa win over the New York Islanders, would clinch a playoff berth for the Senators and end Detroit’s season.

“It’s do-or-die now for the rest of the way until the end, and we need to win every single game because every point really matters,” forward Carter Mazur said ahead of the contest.

Head coach Todd McLellan kept his team focused on what they can control. “Ottawa is going to do their own thing, and whatever the outcome is, the outcome is,” McLellan said. “We have to play at 5 p.m., and that’s what we have to do. The tiebreakers and all that type of stuff, if we’re even lucky enough to get involved in any of that, we’ll know those scenarios in the last game.”

Detroit’s recent form hasn’t inspired confidence — the club posted a 5-8-2 record over its last 15 games. But momentum can shift quickly in the NHL, and the Red Wings are hoping Thursday’s emphatic 6-3 win over Philadelphia was the spark they needed.

Patrick Kane and Dylan Larkin Lead Detroit’s Late-Season Surge

If the Red Wings are going to pull off a miracle finish, it will be because their veteran stars dragged them there.

Patrick Kane, the 37-year-old future Hall of Famer, has turned back the clock at exactly the right time. Over his last 17 games, Kane has racked up seven goals and 16 assists for 23 points — a pace that would put him among the league’s elite over a full season. His ability to elevate in high-pressure moments is precisely what Detroit needs.

Dylan Larkin provided a masterclass on Thursday night against the Flyers, recording a hat trick and an assist in the 6-3 victory. Two of his goals came in highlight fashion — a power-play goal and a shorthanded marker within a span of just 3 minutes and 6 seconds early in the second period, blowing the game open at 4-1.

Alex DeBrincat leads the team with a remarkable 40 goals and 83 points on the season, cementing himself as Detroit’s most dangerous offensive weapon.

Defenseman Moritz Seider contributed a five-point night against Philadelphia (one goal, four assists), showing the kind of two-way dominance that has made him one of the league’s rising blue-line stars.

Between the pipes, John Gibson has been a reliable presence with a 29-20-4 record, four shutouts, a .904 save percentage, and a 2.64 goals-against average. He was expected to be available for the Devils matchup after recent uncertainty around his status.

New Jersey Devils Enter with Nothing to Prove — and That’s Dangerous

The Devils sit at 83 points with a 40-36-3 record, effectively eliminated from playoff contention. But don’t mistake a team with nothing to lose for a team that won’t compete.

New Jersey has gone 12-7-1 over its past 20 games, a stretch of play that suggests far more talent than their overall record indicates. That said, they have dropped four of their last six, including a 5-2 loss to the Pittsburgh Penguins on Thursday night.

Jack Hughes and Nico Hischier share the team lead with 26 goals each, giving New Jersey a formidable one-two punch up the middle. Hughes also leads the squad in total points, and his dynamic skating ability can change a game in a single shift.

However, the Devils received devastating news on Thursday: defenseman Luke Hughes will miss the remainder of the season after undergoing an undisclosed procedure. The younger Hughes brother had already missed time in January with an upper-body injury. His absence strips New Jersey of a key blue-line talent and could limit their defensive flexibility.

This Saturday clash is the third and deciding matchup between the two teams this season, with each side having won one game apiece. The rubber match adds an extra layer of intrigue — even for a Devils team playing out the string.

What This Means for the Eastern Conference Playoff Picture

The Eastern Conference Wild Card race heading into the final weekend of the regular season is one of the tightest in recent memory. Here’s where things stand:

  • Boston Bruins: 96 points (43-26-10) — hold WC1
  • Ottawa Senators: 94 points (42-27-10) — hold WC2
  • Detroit Red Wings: 91 points (41-29-9) — chasing, 3 games left

Detroit needs to win out and hope for Ottawa to stumble. There’s no margin for error and no room for dropped points. Even a single overtime loss could prove fatal to their chances.

The Red Wings’ home finale — branded “Eras Night: People of Hockeytown” in partnership with Coca-Cola and Meijer — carries emotional weight beyond the standings. This fanbase has endured years of rebuilding and finally has a team worth believing in again. A loss would end the dream; a win keeps it alive for at least one more game.

Looking Ahead: Can Detroit Complete the Impossible?

The Devils vs Red Wings game is more than a single result — it’s a referendum on whether this Detroit roster has the character to finish what it started. A team that has gone 5-8-2 over its last 15 games doesn’t inspire playoff confidence, but the 6-3 demolition of Philadelphia suggests the Red Wings can still summon their best when it matters most.

With Kane rolling, Larkin scoring in bunches, DeBrincat sitting at 40 goals, and Gibson steady in net, the talent is there. The question is whether Detroit can string together three consecutive wins against teams that have nothing to lose.

Can the Red Wings defy the odds and earn a Wild Card spot in the final days of the season, or will this promising season end just short of the postseason? With the way this team fights, counting them out would be a mistake.
