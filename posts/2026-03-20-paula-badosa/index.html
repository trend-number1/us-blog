Paula Badosa’s Relentless Comeback: Inside the Spanish Star’s 2026 Tennis Journey

Few stories in professional tennis are as dramatic as Paula Badosa’s ongoing battle to reclaim her spot among the sport’s elite. Once ranked World No. 2 in 2022, the 28-year-old Spaniard has endured a punishing cycle of injuries, retirements, and emotional setbacks — yet she keeps coming back.

Key Takeaways

  • Paula Badosa, the former World No. 2, is competing at the 2026 Miami Open after a grueling stretch of injury-plagued seasons.
  • She was eliminated in the Indian Wells 2026 opener by Yulia Putintseva but bounced back to reach a WTA 125 semifinal in Austin.
  • Recurring back, spine, and muscular injuries have forced multiple retirements and long absences since 2023.
  • Off the court, Badosa confirmed her split from Stefanos Tsitsipas and is navigating a fresh chapter in her personal life.

From World No. 2 to Starting Over

Paula Badosa burst onto the global stage when she won the Indian Wells Masters in 2021, one of the most prestigious titles outside the Grand Slams. By 2022, her powerful baseline game and mental toughness had propelled her to the No. 2 ranking in the world, making her one of the most feared competitors on the WTA Tour.

But the climb came at a physical cost. In May 2023, while competing in the Italian Open quarterfinals, Badosa sustained a stress fracture in her spine — an injury that would alter the trajectory of her career. She was forced to miss the entire clay court season, including the French Open.

Her attempted comeback at Wimbledon 2023 ended in heartbreak when she had to withdraw from her second-round match against Marta Kostyuk. Further withdrawals followed: the Canadian Open, and then the US Open, where she would have faced seven-time Grand Slam winner Venus Williams in the first round.

What was supposed to be a brief recovery turned into a prolonged absence that tested her resolve like never before.

The Injury Cycle That Won’t Quit

If there’s one word that has defined Badosa’s recent career, it’s resilience. The list of setbacks she’s overcome reads like a medical textbook:

  • Stress fracture in the spine (May 2023) — missed the entire clay season
  • Psoas tear — forced a two-month hiatus between the Miami Open and Strasbourg Open
  • Opening-round loss at Wimbledon as the No. 9 seed to Katie Boulter, followed by months on the sideline
  • Left thigh and groin injury at the China Open, where she retired in tears against Karolina Muchova trailing 4-2

Each time, the pattern has been the same: Badosa shows flashes of her best tennis, builds momentum, and then her body fails her at a critical moment. At the China Open, she was playing her first WTA event since Wimbledon when the latest setback struck. She left the court visibly heartbroken.

“Coming back from injury, sometimes you feel a little alone,” Badosa said during the Billie Jean King Cup Finals in Shenzhen, where she represented Spain and picked up a straight-sets win over Antonia Ruzic. “You have ups and downs. Having these people by your side always helps.”

That team environment provided a spark — but the road ahead remained steep.

Paula Badosa in 2026: Indian Wells, Austin, and Miami

The 2026 season has been a microcosm of Badosa’s career: flashes of brilliance interrupted by tough losses.

At Indian Wells 2026, the tournament she won in 2021, Badosa was ousted in the opener by Yulia Putintseva. For a former champion, an early exit at her signature event stung. But rather than retreat, Badosa entered the WTA 125 event in Austin, where she and fellow former Indian Wells champion Bianca Andreescu set up a rare semifinal clash. Badosa ultimately fell in that semifinal to a former Grand Slam champion who went on to reach her biggest final in two years.

Now at the 2026 Miami Open, Badosa faces Aliaksandra Sasnovich in the first round. Ranked around No. 100, Badosa is far from the seeded position she once held, but the match represents another chance to string together wins and rebuild confidence.

Her ranking may have dropped, but anyone who watched Badosa reach the 2025 Australian Open semifinal — a career-defining run that rekindled her World No. 2 form — knows the talent is still there.

Life Beyond the Court

Badosa’s personal life has also undergone major changes. Her high-profile relationship with Greek tennis star Stefanos Tsitsipas captivated the tennis world when they went public in 2023, earning the couple nickname “Tsitsidosa.” The pair described each other as soulmates and frequently shared affectionate moments on social media.

However, in late 2025, the couple confirmed their split. Tsitsipas’s sister Julia noted that both entered the relationship “traumatized” by injuries and struggled with the long-distance nature of their union. Badosa embraced the new chapter with characteristic directness, celebrating her 28th birthday holding a sash that read “Sexy and single.”

She has also drawn attention for her modeling work, posing for MARCA in a recent bikini shoot — a reminder that Badosa’s brand extends well beyond the tennis court.

What This Means for Tennis Fans

Badosa’s journey dispels a common misconception in professional sports: that a player who drops in the rankings is “finished.” At 28, she is still in her competitive prime. The 2025 Australian Open semifinal proved she can still compete with the best when healthy. The challenge has never been talent — it’s been staying on the court long enough to let that talent shine.

For fans following the 2026 Miami Open, Badosa’s first-round match is worth watching closely. A win could be the first step toward another momentum-building run. A loss would simply mean regrouping for the clay season ahead.

Her story also offers a valuable lesson that she herself articulated to a fan who questioned her fight: “It’s not a lack of respect, but an excess of resilience.” Badosa doesn’t lack belief or ability. She lacks only the cooperation of her own body.

The Road Ahead

With the clay season approaching — historically her strongest surface — Badosa has every reason to believe the best is still ahead. The WTA Tour is filled with comeback stories, from Kim Clijsters to Victoria Azarenka, and Badosa has the game and the grit to write her own.

The question isn’t whether Paula Badosa can return to the top of women’s tennis. It’s whether her body will let her. And if the last three years have taught us anything, it’s that counting her out would be a mistake.

Will 2026 be the year Paula Badosa finally stays healthy long enough to fulfill her enormous potential? The tennis world is watching — and rooting for her.
