Dbacks vs Mets: Mauricio’s Walk-Off Caps Thrilling Extra-Inning Battle at Citi Field

What happens when a rookie gets called up to replace one of baseball’s biggest stars and delivers in his very first at-bat? That’s exactly the scene that unfolded at Citi Field on Tuesday, April 7, as the Mets edged the Diamondbacks 4-3 in 10 innings on a frigid, wind-whipped afternoon in New York.

Key Takeaways

  • Ronny Mauricio delivered a pinch-hit walk-off RBI single in his first at-bat after being called up to replace the injured Juan Soto.
  • The Mets extended their win streak to four games, improving to 7-4 on the season.
  • Extreme wind at Citi Field caused a Brooks Raley sweeper to move 35 inches horizontally — nearly double its usual break.
  • Start times for the series were shifted to 4 p.m. ET due to brutal cold conditions.
  • Merrill Kelly is set to throw 85 pitches in extended spring training on April 9 as part of his comeback.

Mauricio Makes an Instant Impact

Ronny Mauricio didn’t need a warm-up. Called up to fill the roster spot left by an injured Juan Soto, the young infielder stepped to the plate as a pinch-hitter in the bottom of the 10th inning and laced a walk-off RBI single to clinch the victory.

It was a storybook moment — first at-bat, biggest stage, game on the line.

The win pushed the Mets’ record to 7-4 and extended a hot streak that began with three consecutive victories over the Giants in San Francisco. The Diamondbacks, meanwhile, continued to search for consistency on the road.

But before Mauricio’s heroics, the game itself was a battle shaped by weather, bullpen chess, and late-inning drama.

A Game Defined by Wind and Cold

If you didn’t watch this game, you missed one of the most bizarre weather-influenced performances of the young season. Players from both teams were still buzzing about the conditions a day later.

Center fielder Jorge Barrosa said his sliding catch on the warning track in right-center during the ninth inning came on a ball he initially thought was hit directly at right fielder Corbin Carroll. The wind turned routine plays into adventures.

The most jaw-dropping moment came in the top of the eighth. Mets lefty Brooks Raley threw a sweeper to Diamondbacks slugger Luken Baker that moved a staggering 35 inches of horizontal movement. For context, Raley’s sweeper averages 20 inches of break, according to Baseball Savant.

Baker swung through it — a pitch that nearly hit him.

“It just moved a ton,” Baker said. “I don’t know if I’ve seen a slider move that much. It wasn’t fun.”

He added: “The wind plus guys who are really good at their job makes it a little more difficult.”

The conditions were so harsh that MLB moved the start times for the Tuesday and Wednesday games to 4 p.m. ET, a rare early-season scheduling adjustment to avoid the worst of the evening chill.

How the Scoring Unfolded

The Mets struck first, plating a run early to take a 2-0 lead. But the Diamondbacks answered emphatically in the top of the fifth.

With two outs and starter Freddy Peralta’s pitch count ballooning, the D-backs strung together a rally:

  1. Corbin Carroll singled.
  2. Geraldo Perdomo drew a six-pitch walk.
  3. Peralta hit Gabriel Moreno to load the bases, ending his outing at 101 pitches.
  4. Reliever Huascar Brazoban entered and promptly surrendered back-to-back bloop singles to Adrian Castillo and Nolan Arenado, plating all three runners.

Just like that, the Diamondbacks led 3-2.

But the Mets weren’t done. In the bottom of the eighth, they mounted a classic comeback:

  • Jorge Polanco led off with a single.
  • Tyrone Taylor replaced him on the bases and advanced to third on a Brett Baty single.
  • Jared Young came off the bench and tied the game with a sacrifice fly to right field.

The score sat locked at 3-3 heading into extras.

In the top of the 10th, closer Luke Weaver delivered a masterful frame, retiring the Diamondbacks’ automatic runner at second with a pop-up, lineout, and strikeout of Tim Tawa. That set the stage for Mauricio’s walk-off.

Merrill Kelly’s Comeback Watch

Beyond the on-field drama, Diamondbacks fans received an encouraging update on Merrill Kelly. Manager Torey Lovullo confirmed that Kelly was scheduled to throw 85 pitches in an extended spring training game on Thursday, April 9.

Kelly’s progress is a significant development for a D-backs rotation that could use reinforcements. If he continues to build up without setbacks, his return to the big league rotation could provide a much-needed boost during the grind of the early season.

What This Means for Both Teams

For the Mets, this win is about more than the standings. It’s about depth and culture. Losing Juan Soto would derail most lineups, but the Mets responded by calling up Mauricio and getting immediate production. The bullpen, anchored by Weaver’s dominant 10th inning, showed it can hold leads when it matters.

For the Diamondbacks, the loss stings but isn’t catastrophic. The starting pitching was competitive, and Nolan Arenado’s two-run single showed the lineup can produce in clutch moments. But the bullpen’s inability to hold the lead in the eighth — combined with Weaver’s shutdown of their extras opportunity — highlights areas that need tightening.

Looking Ahead: Eduardo Rodriguez Takes the Mound

The series continued on Wednesday, April 8, with Eduardo Rodriguez slated to start for the Diamondbacks against the Mets. With temperatures still frigid and wind still a factor, both teams faced another challenging day of early-spring baseball at Citi Field.

The Mets’ ability to grind out wins in tough conditions has been a hallmark of their early-season success. Whether they can sustain this momentum — especially without Soto — will be one of the most compelling storylines of the National League’s first month.

Can Mauricio keep producing in Soto’s absence, or was Tuesday’s walk-off a one-time spark? Either way, the Mets have shown that their roster runs deeper than any single star — and in April, that depth might matter more than anything.
