Spurs Game Tonight: San Antonio's Dominant 57-Win Season and What Comes Next

April 3, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

The Spurs Are the NBA’s Most Surprising Powerhouse — and They’re Not Slowing Down

Just two seasons removed from the league’s worst record, the San Antonio Spurs have stormed to a 57–18 record and the No. 2 seed in the Western Conference. With a nine-game winning streak and the Southwest Division already clinched, this team has gone from rebuild to contender faster than anyone predicted.

Key Takeaways

  • The Spurs sit at 57–18, No. 2 in the West, with a 9-game winning streak
  • San Antonio has clinched the Southwest Division title
  • Victor Wembanyama’s health has been a storyline heading into late-season matchups
  • The Warriors, at 36–39, have struggled without Stephen Curry (out 26 straight games)
  • Curry is targeting a return for Houston on Sunday after clearing five-on-five scrimmage work

If you haven’t been paying attention to San Antonio this season, now is the time. Here’s everything you need to know about the Spurs’ remarkable campaign, their recent stretch of games, and what it all means heading into the playoffs.

San Antonio’s Historic Turnaround

The Spurs’ rise from lottery team to Western Conference powerhouse is one of the most dramatic turnarounds in recent NBA history. A 57–18 record through 75 games puts them on pace for roughly 62 wins — a mark that would rival some of the franchise’s best seasons under Gregg Popovich.

What’s driving the surge?

  • Victor Wembanyama’s sophomore leap: The 7-foot-4 phenom has been the centerpiece of San Antonio’s transformation, drawing comparisons to generational talents
  • Elite depth: The Spurs aren’t a one-man show — their roster construction has allowed them to sustain winning even when managing Wembanyama’s workload
  • Defensive identity: True to Popovich’s legacy, San Antonio has built a defensive system that smothers opponents

The nine-game winning streak heading into their recent slate of games underscored just how locked in this team is. And with the postseason approaching, the Spurs have positioned themselves for homecourt advantage through at least the conference semifinals.

So how did they fare in their latest tests against Western Conference opponents?

Spurs vs. Warriors: A Tale of Two Trajectories

The Spurs’ April 1 matchup against the Golden State Warriors perfectly illustrated the gap between a contender and a team fighting for survival.

The fourth and final regular-season meeting between these teams carried different stakes for each side. For San Antonio, it was about maintaining momentum and seeding. For Golden State, it was a desperate push to improve their play-in positioning.

The Warriors entered the game severely shorthanded. Their injury report read like a medical journal:

  • Stephen Curry — Out (right patellofemoral pain syndrome, 26th consecutive missed game)
  • Jimmy Butler III — Out (ACL surgery)
  • Moses Moody — Out (knee surgery)
  • De’Anthony Melton — Out (thumb)
  • Quinten Post — Out (foot)
  • Al Horford — Out (soleus strain)
  • Kristaps Porzingis — Out (illness management)
  • Gary Payton II — Questionable (knee)

That’s a staggering amount of talent sitting on the sideline. The Warriors have gone 9–16 without Curry this season, a record that highlights just how much the franchise still depends on its 38-year-old cornerstone.

But there’s a light at the end of the tunnel for Golden State fans.

Stephen Curry’s Return Timeline

Perhaps the biggest storyline surrounding the Warriors’ late-season push is Curry’s potential return. After missing 26 consecutive games due to right patellofemoral pain syndrome — his last appearance came against the Detroit Pistons on January 30 — the two-time MVP is finally making progress.

According to Sam Amick and Nick Friedell of The Athletic, Curry is targeting Sunday’s matchup against the Houston Rockets as a potential return date. He was recently cleared for full five-on-five scrimmage work, a critical milestone in his rehabilitation.

When healthy this season, Curry has been vintage Steph:

  • 27.2 points per game
  • 4.8 assists per game
  • 3.5 rebounds per game
  • 1.1 steals per game
  • Efficient shooting from the field and beyond the arc — in just 39 games

His return would be a massive boost for a Warriors team clinging to the 10th seed. But with only a handful of regular-season games remaining, Golden State needs Curry back immediately to have any realistic shot at making noise in the play-in tournament.

The question is whether even a healthy Curry can make up for the roster’s other significant absences. With Butler, Moody, Porzingis, and Horford all sidelined, the Warriors are running thin on options.

Spurs vs. Clippers: Keeping the Foot on the Gas

Following the Warriors game, the Spurs traveled to face the LA Clippers on April 2. The matchup drew attention largely because of questions surrounding Victor Wembanyama’s availability, with multiple outlets tracking his injury status heading into the contest.

Both Sports Illustrated and TWSN Sports published updates on Wembanyama’s status for the Clippers game, reflecting just how much the league revolves around the young star’s presence. When Wembanyama plays, the Spurs are a different team — and opponents know it.

The Spurs’ late-season schedule has been about fine-tuning for the playoffs while managing their stars’ health. With the No. 2 seed all but locked up, Popovich has the luxury of being strategic about rest and rotation — a stark contrast to teams like the Warriors who are fighting for every game.

What This Means for the NBA Playoffs

The Spurs’ dominant regular season sets up fascinating postseason possibilities. Here’s what to watch:

For San Antonio:

  • Homecourt advantage through at least the second round is virtually guaranteed
  • The nine-game winning streak suggests the team is peaking at the right time
  • Managing Wembanyama’s health and workload will be the top priority down the stretch

For Golden State:

  • Curry’s return could transform the play-in race — but timing is everything
  • The sheer number of injuries makes a deep postseason run unlikely regardless
  • This season may be about building toward next year rather than chasing a title

For the Western Conference at large:

  • The Spurs have announced themselves as legitimate title contenders alongside the Thunder and other top seeds
  • San Antonio’s combination of youth, coaching, and defensive identity makes them a nightmare matchup for anyone

The wider NBA landscape is shifting. The Spurs’ rebuild didn’t just work — it produced a potential dynasty in the making. And with Wembanyama still just scratching the surface of his potential, the scariest part for the rest of the league is that this might be the floor, not the ceiling.

Looking Ahead: Can Anyone Stop San Antonio?

As the regular season winds down, the Spurs game tonight — and every night — carries the weight of expectation. This is a franchise that knows how to win championships, guided by the longest-tenured coach in major American professional sports.

The 57–18 record speaks for itself. The nine-game winning streak speaks even louder. And with the playoffs just around the corner, San Antonio has positioned itself as the team nobody in the West wants to face.

The real question isn’t whether the Spurs will make the playoffs — it’s whether anyone can beat them once they get there. With Wembanyama leading the charge and Popovich pulling the strings, the silver and black are playing their best basketball at exactly the right time.

What do you think — are the Spurs legitimate title favorites, or will a more experienced contender take them down in the postseason? The next few weeks will tell us everything.
