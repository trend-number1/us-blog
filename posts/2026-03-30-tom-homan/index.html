Tom Homan's Bold Moves: ICE at Airports, DHS Shutdown, and the Battles Ahead

March 30, 2026 00:03 Trend Insights

What happens when the agency responsible for airport security runs out of money — and the government’s top immigration enforcer steps in to fill the gap? That’s exactly the scenario playing out across the United States right now, and Tom Homan is at the center of it all.

Key Takeaways

  • Border czar Tom Homan confirmed ICE officers are being deployed to U.S. airports to assist TSA during the ongoing DHS shutdown.
  • TSA workers have gone unpaid since DHS funding lapsed on January 31, causing massive staffing shortages and long lines.
  • Homan rejected Democratic demands for ICE agents to remove masks, citing an 8,000% increase in death threats.
  • Democrats and Republicans remain deadlocked over DHS funding, with no resolution in sight after more than 40 days.

Tom Homan and the ICE Airport Deployment

On March 23, 2026, Tom Homan confirmed on CNN’s State of the Union that Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents would be deployed to U.S. airports starting the following day. The move came after President Donald Trump announced plans to send ICE officers to help ease the chaos caused by severe TSA staffing shortages.

Homan emphasized that the deployment would be limited in scope. ICE officers would not replace TSA agents or take over specialized duties like operating X-ray machines. Instead, they would handle support tasks such as guarding exits and managing passenger flow.

“There’s TSA agents covering exits… certainly a highly trained ICE law enforcement officer could cover an exit,” Homan told CNN. He added, “I don’t see an ICE agent looking at an X-ray machine because you’re not trained in that.”

When pressed by CNN host Dana Bash on whether a plan assembled in 24 hours could be “well-thought-out,” Homan pushed back firmly. “ICE has been at airports across the country for a long time. It’s just expanding those things,” he said. “How much of a plan does it mean to guard an exit to make sure no one comes through that exit?”

The deployment has drawn sharp criticism from unions and Democrats, who argue it blurs the line between immigration enforcement and transportation security — but for Homan, it’s a practical response to a crisis.

The DHS Shutdown: Why TSA Workers Aren’t Getting Paid

The root cause of the airport chaos is the partial Department of Homeland Security shutdown that began on January 31, 2026, after Congress failed to pass legislation to keep the department funded. The standoff has now stretched beyond 40 days, leaving TSA workers — who fall under DHS — without paychecks.

The consequences have been immediate and visible:

  • Long security lines at major airports nationwide
  • Staffing shortages as unpaid TSA agents call out or quit
  • Passenger frustration and travel disruptions
  • Safety concerns from aviation security experts

On March 29, Homan told media outlets that TSA agents could expect their paychecks “tomorrow or Tuesday,” following an executive order from President Trump. But the broader funding dispute remains unresolved.

Democrats, led by House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, have demanded reforms including prohibiting masks and requiring judicial warrants before agreeing to reopen DHS. Republicans and the White House have refused those conditions, creating a standoff with no clear end.

The Mask Controversy: Homan Refuses to Back Down

One of the most contentious flashpoints in the shutdown negotiations has been the question of whether ICE agents should be allowed to wear masks while conducting operations. Democrats argue the masks create an atmosphere of intimidation. Homan sees it differently.

“You want us to take off the mask? Well, you know what, no, we’re not,” Homan told WABC’s Cats & Cosby on March 26. “That’s one of the things the Hill keeps pushing for in negotiations.”

Homan backed up his position with striking numbers. According to him and DHS, death threats against ICE officers have increased 8,000%, while actual threats have risen 1,400%. He attributed the surge directly to what he called “hateful rhetoric” from Democratic lawmakers.

“They do it out of choice because of threats, because of doxing of them and their families,” Homan said. “So you know what, stop your hateful rhetoric and maybe if threats go down and decrease, we’ll think about it.”

The mask debate has become a symbol of the deeper ideological divide over immigration enforcement in the Trump administration. For Homan, officer safety is non-negotiable. For Democrats, transparency and accountability in federal law enforcement are equally essential.

Homan Fires Back at Critics Comparing ICE to Nazis

Beyond the policy disputes, Homan has also been fighting a public relations battle. On March 29, he appeared on CNN to directly address critics who have compared ICE officers to Nazis — a comparison he called deeply offensive.

“Stop calling ICE Nazis,” Homan said bluntly, arguing that such rhetoric puts officers and their families at greater risk. He has framed ICE’s operations under the Trump administration as lawful enforcement of existing immigration statutes, rejecting characterizations that the agency is acting as a rogue force.

The rhetorical war extends to Congress, where Homan has clashed repeatedly with Democratic lawmakers over the scope and conduct of immigration enforcement operations. From the Minnesota immigration crackdown in February — which AP News reported ended after angry protests and two fatal shootings — to the airport deployments, Homan has been the administration’s most visible and outspoken defender of aggressive enforcement.

What This Means for Travelers and Everyday Americans

If you’re flying in the coming weeks, here’s what you should know:

  1. Expect ICE officers at airports — they will be assisting with security but not conducting passenger screening
  2. Lines may still be long — the core staffing shortage won’t be fully resolved until TSA workers receive consistent pay
  3. TSA paychecks are expected soon — Homan confirmed payments should arrive by late March or early April
  4. The shutdown could continue — without a deal between Democrats and Republicans, DHS remains partially unfunded
  5. Arrive early — major airports are still experiencing significant delays

The broader implications extend well beyond airport security. The DHS shutdown affects Coast Guard operations, FEMA disaster response, Secret Service protection, and other critical functions. Every day the standoff continues, the ripple effects grow.

The Road Ahead for Tom Homan

Tom Homan sits at the intersection of the country’s most heated political battles — immigration, government funding, and the balance between security and civil liberties. His refusal to bend on ICE masks, his rapid deployment of agents to airports, and his combative responses to critics have made him one of the most polarizing figures in the Trump administration.

With the DHS shutdown showing no signs of resolution and ICE operations expanding into new domains like airport security, Homan’s influence is only growing. Whether you see him as a pragmatic enforcer keeping the country safe or an overreaching official pushing boundaries, one thing is clear: Tom Homan is shaping the debate on immigration and security in ways that will echo for years.

The question that remains: Can Washington find a compromise to reopen DHS, or will the shutdown — and its consequences for millions of travelers and federal workers — drag on indefinitely?
