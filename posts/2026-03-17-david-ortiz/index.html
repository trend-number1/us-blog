David Ortiz WBC 2026: Big Papi Steals the Show as Broadcaster and Red Sox Mentor

March 17, 2026 13:44 Trend Insights

David Ortiz hasn’t swung a bat in a major league game since 2016, but somehow Big Papi is still the biggest personality in baseball. At the 2026 World Baseball Classic in Miami, the Hall of Famer has been everywhere — breaking down games on the FOX Sports desk, embracing young stars, and doing what he’s always done best: making everyone around him feel like they belong on the biggest stage.

Key Takeaways:

  • Ortiz is headlining FOX Sports’ 2026 WBC broadcast alongside Derek Jeter and Alex Rodriguez
  • He gifted Red Sox phenom Roman Anthony his trademark “MY DAWG” shirt after Team USA’s semifinal win over the Dominican Republic
  • Ortiz has been mentoring Red Sox prospects including 19-year-old Justin Gonzales, who calls him a “father figure”
  • Despite his Dominican team’s elimination, Ortiz showed pure class by celebrating Anthony’s performance
  • The 2026 WBC Championship game airs today, March 17, with Ortiz on the broadcast

The Biggest Voice in the Broadcast Booth

When FOX Sports assembled its dream team for the 2026 World Baseball Classic, the network knew exactly who to build the show around. David Ortiz, Derek Jeter, and Alex Rodriguez — three of baseball’s most iconic figures — headline the studio coverage from loanDepot Park in Miami for the semifinal round and championship game.

Ortiz has been a full-time analyst for FOX Sports since 2017, and his broadcasting career has been everything fans hoped it would be. He brings the same larger-than-life energy to the desk that he brought to the batter’s box. He’s loud, he’s honest, he’s funny — and he genuinely knows the game inside and out.

The WBC coverage has showcased Ortiz at his most compelling. After Team USA defeated his beloved Dominican Republic 2-1 in a controversial semifinal on March 16, a lesser personality might have sulked. Instead, Ortiz was on camera praising the opposition and breaking down the game with the same passion he’d show if the DR had won. He did, however, make it clear he didn’t agree with the final call — insisting the decisive pitch was not a strike.

The “MY DAWG” Moment That Went Viral

The most memorable scene of the entire tournament may have happened after the semifinal buzzer. As Roman Anthony — the 21-year-old Red Sox outfielder who powered Team USA’s victory with a towering 421-foot solo home run — finished his postgame interviews, Ortiz appeared on the FOX Sports set with a surprise.

Big Papi handed Anthony the first “MY DAWG” shirt of the year, his personal stamp of approval reserved for players who earn his highest respect. The moment was pure Ortiz — spontaneous, warm, and electric.

“You are a great athlete, a good listener, your work ethic is on another level,” Ortiz told Anthony on live television. Then he added the words that every young player dreams of hearing from a Hall of Famer: “That kid is a student of the game. We are so proud to have him in our organization, because he does nothing but get better and better and better.”

Anthony has been spectacular throughout the tournament, posting a .318/.423/.591 slash line with seven hits, five runs, two home runs, and seven RBI across six games. His semifinal blast off Dominican pitcher Gregory Soto gave Team USA the lead they would never relinquish.

Mentoring the Next Generation of Red Sox

Ortiz’s influence on the Red Sox organization goes far beyond broadcast moments. At spring training in 2026, he’s been a constant presence, working with prospects and offering the kind of wisdom that only comes from 541 career home runs and three World Series rings.

One particularly striking story involves Justin Gonzales, a 19-year-old Red Sox prospect who stands 6-foot-6 and weighs 277 pounds. The massive young player, ranked as the organization’s No. 6 prospect by MLB Pipeline, has called Ortiz “a father figure” and credits Big Papi’s mentorship with helping him develop both on and off the field.

It’s a role Ortiz has embraced with the same intensity he brought to October at-bats. He towers over active players — figuratively, at least — and they seek his advice willingly. The sight of the legendary designated hitter holding court in the Red Sox clubhouse has become one of the defining images of spring training.

Born on November 18, 1975, in Santo Domingo, Dominican Republic, Ortiz transformed from a cast-off by the Minnesota Twins into the most clutch hitter in Red Sox history. His role in ending Boston’s 86-year championship drought in 2004 — when he was named ALCS MVP after delivering multiple walk-off hits — elevated him from star to legend.

His first-ballot Hall of Fame induction in 2022 confirmed what Boston fans already knew: Big Papi was one of the greatest to ever do it.

Why Ortiz Still Matters in 2026

What makes David Ortiz remarkable in 2026 isn’t just nostalgia. It’s that he remains genuinely relevant to the current state of baseball. He’s not a retired legend offering empty platitudes from the broadcast booth. He’s an active mentor shaping the next wave of Red Sox talent, a passionate analyst who isn’t afraid to disagree with umpires on national television, and a personality so magnetic that his approval — a t-shirt, a hug, a few words of praise — can become the defining moment of an international tournament.

The 2026 World Baseball Classic Championship game takes place today, March 17, at loanDepot Park in Miami. David Ortiz will be on the FOX Sports set, doing exactly what he’s been doing for a decade since retirement: being the heartbeat of baseball.

Whether Team USA hoists the trophy or falls short, the 2026 WBC will be remembered in part for the image of a Hall of Famer from the Dominican Republic crossing competitive lines to celebrate a young American player he helped raise in the game. That’s not just sportsmanship. That’s David Ortiz being David Ortiz.
