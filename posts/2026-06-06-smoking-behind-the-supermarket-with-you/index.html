Smoking Behind the Supermarket: The Surprising Anime Hit

What if the most talked-about anime romance of Summer 2026 isn’t about teenagers in a high school classroom, but about a 45-year-old salaryman and a goth cashier sharing a cigarette break? That’s exactly the premise Crunchyroll is betting on — and the bet is already paying off in unexpected ways.

Quick Summary: Crunchyroll dropped 12 mini-episodes of Smoking Behind the Supermarket with You on June 3, 2026, ahead of the show’s official July premiere. The slow-burn adult romance, animated by Asahi Production, adapts Jinushi’s hit manga and pairs an exhausted office worker with a mysterious convenience store clerk hiding a dual identity.

The early release strategy is rare. The premise is rarer. And the buzz around this adult romance anime suggests audiences are hungry for stories that grow up alongside them.

What Is “Smoking Behind the Supermarket with You”?

Smoking Behind the Supermarket with You — known in Japan as Super no Ura de Yani Suu Futari — began as a webcomic posted by author Jinushi on Twitter (now X) in March 2022. Square Enix picked it up just five months later, serializing the series in Monthly Big Gangan magazine from August 2022. Six volumes have been published to date.

The plot is deceptively simple. Sasaki, a forty-five-year-old office worker, is worn down by the corporate grind. His one daily comfort is smoking on his break — and the warm smile of Yamada, the cheerful cashier at his local supermarket. When Yamada disappears one evening, a striking, sharp-tongued stranger named Tayama invites him to smoke with her behind the store. Sasaki thinks he’s made a new smoking buddy. He doesn’t realize he already knows her.

The Square Enix synopsis sums it up best: “An overworked salaryman and a mischievous cashier with a dual personality form an unexpected bond in this sweet-and-sour comedy.”

It’s a setup that sounds slight on paper. The execution is anything but — and the staff list explains why.

The Talent Behind the Smoke Break

Asahi Production is animating the series, and the creative team is stacked with veterans. Here’s the core staff confirmed across press releases:

  • Directors: Tadato Suzuki and Aoi Mori (some outlets credit them as Masato Suzuki and Aori Mori)
  • Series composition: Mio Inoue
  • Character designer: Yasuka Otaki
  • Music: Shin Kono and Sayaka Aoki
  • Opening theme: “Kuzuri Nen” by Zutomayo
  • Ending theme: “NIGHT DANCER” by imase (also reported as “Fiction” by imase)

Pairing Zutomayo’s “Kuzuri Nen” with imase’s nostalgia-soaked vocals is a deliberate mood signal: this is an anime that wants to feel like a late-night walk home, not a shounen battle. And that mood is exactly what Crunchyroll’s marketing team chose to lean into.

Why Crunchyroll’s Early Release Strategy Matters

Here’s the move that turned heads across the industry: on June 3, 2026, Crunchyroll quietly released the equivalent of the anime’s first six episodes — sliced into 12 mini-episodes of roughly 10 minutes each — ahead of the official simulcast premiere in July. The mini-episodes are presented in chronological order and include the special OP and ED videos.

The same day, the anime premiered in Japan, with ABEMA also handling early streaming domestically. Crunchyroll’s subtitle options at launch include English and Spanish, broadening the reach immediately.

Why does this matter? Because the standard anime release model is rigid: simulcast at a fixed Japanese broadcast date, one episode a week, take it or leave it. Splitting six episodes into 12 bite-sized chunks does three things at once:

  1. Lowers the barrier to entry for viewers unfamiliar with the source material
  2. Builds organic word-of-mouth in the weeks before the official premiere
  3. Targets adult viewers who scroll between tasks rather than block-watch

It’s a deliberate experiment, and it’s working — coverage has spread across Anime News Network, Animation Magazine, Polygon, and dozens of niche outlets in a matter of days.

So why is this particular show getting that treatment?

The Adult Romance Revival Nobody Saw Coming

For years, the dominant romance subgenre in anime has been the high school love story. Smoking Behind the Supermarket with You lands in a different lane entirely — and that’s the point.

Sasaki is forty-five. He smokes. He’s tired. He’s not chasing a destiny. His emotional arc is built on small kindnesses, mistaken identities, and the slow recognition that someone has been quietly seeing him all along.

This is part of a broader shift. Adult-protagonist anime — Sweat and Soap, I Want to Eat Your Pancreas, Tonari no Seki-kun in spirit — have steadily expanded their audience over the last decade. The Smoking Behind the Supermarket manga’s six-volume run since 2022 is part of that wave. Polygon has already flagged the title as a sneak peek aimed at romance anime fans hungry for something more grown-up.

Common misconception: A lot of viewers assume an anime about smoking is glamorizing it. The series isn’t. The cigarettes are a narrative device — a shared, slightly forbidden pause that gives two emotionally guarded adults an excuse to talk. The story is about loneliness and connection, not vice.

That distinction matters more than ever as audiences look for stories that respect their age and emotional vocabulary.

What This Means For You

If you’ve been waiting for an anime romance you don’t have to apologize for recommending to a coworker, this is it. Here’s how to jump in without spoiling the experience:

  • Watch the 12 mini-episodes on Crunchyroll first — they’re already live and total roughly two hours of content
  • Read the manga in English via Square Enix Manga & Books (or BOOK WALKER) if you want to get ahead of the July premiere
  • Don’t skip the OP and ED — Zutomayo and imase aren’t background; they’re tonal anchors
  • Set a reminder for July 2026 for the full TV simulcast

The bigger takeaway is about the format itself. If Crunchyroll’s mini-episode experiment drives strong engagement, expect more shows — especially slow-burn, character-driven ones — to get this kind of staggered, snackable release in 2027.

So here’s the question worth sitting with: when an industry built on weekly cliffhangers starts releasing adult romance anime in 10-minute bites, who is it really designed for — the viewers who don’t have time, or the stories that finally have room to breathe?

Either way, Smoking Behind the Supermarket with You is the first show this year that feels like the answer might be both.
