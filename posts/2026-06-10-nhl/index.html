NHL Shockwaves: Babcock Drama, Landeskog's Triumph, and a Wild Cup Final

June 10, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

What if the most explosive NHL story of June 2026 wasn’t happening on the ice? Between a controversial coaching candidacy in Edmonton, a fairytale comeback in Colorado, and a Stanley Cup Final no one saw coming, the league is generating headlines faster than fans can refresh their feeds.

Quick Summary

  • The Edmonton Oilers are reportedly nearing a deal to hire Mike Babcock as head coach — and the NHLPA wants a full investigation first.
  • Gabriel Landeskog of the Colorado Avalanche won both the Masterton Trophy and the Mark Messier Leadership Award after his injury comeback.
  • The Carolina Hurricanes trail the Vegas Golden Knights 2-1 in the Stanley Cup Final, with Brandon Bussi now the starting goalie for Game 4.
  • The 2026 NHL Draft could see Gavin McKenna go No. 1 overall to the Toronto Maple Leafs.

The Babcock Bombshell: Why Edmonton Is Walking Into a Storm

According to ESPN, the Edmonton Oilers are nearing a deal with Mike Babcock to become their next head coach. For general manager Stan Bowman, the logic is straightforward: the Oilers’ core is in win-now mode, and Babcock owns a Stanley Cup ring and an Olympic gold medal.

But the move is already drawing fire. The NHLPA has formally asked the NHL to conduct a full investigation of Babcock before the Oilers can hire him, according to The Athletic and Sportsnet. The union’s concerns center on prior conduct allegations that have followed Babcock through multiple coaching cycles.

Critics aren’t subtle. As reported by Yardbarker, the sentiment in hockey circles is that “Stan Bowman is out of ideas” if he’s seriously considering Babcock for the job. For a franchise built around superstar talent, the optics of a controversial hire could overshadow whatever tactical edge Babcock brings.

So why pursue him anyway? Because championship windows close fast — and Edmonton’s is starting to creak.

Landeskog’s Historic Night: The Comeback That Defined a Season

While Edmonton wrestled with controversy, Colorado celebrated one of the most emotional storylines in recent NHL history. Gabriel Landeskog, the Avalanche captain, made history Tuesday night by winning both the Bill Masterton Memorial Trophy and the Mark Messier NHL Leadership Award.

Per ESPN, KUSA, and The Athletic, the dual honor recognizes Landeskog’s return to the ice after a grueling injury layoff. The Masterton honors perseverance and dedication to hockey, while the Messier Award recognizes on-ice leadership — and Landeskog became the rare player to sweep both in the same year.

What makes this moment resonate? It’s not just stats. It’s the reminder that elite athletes can lose years of their career to injury and still claw their way back to the league’s biggest stage. For Avs fans watching their captain hoist not one but two trophies, the emotional payoff was years in the making.

But while individual hardware was being handed out in Colorado, an entirely different drama was playing out under the Vegas lights.

Stanley Cup Final 2026: A Series No Expert Predicted

The 2026 Stanley Cup Final features the Carolina Hurricanes versus the Vegas Golden Knights, and through three games, Vegas leads 2-1. According to USA Today and NHLRumors, the storylines have been wilder than anyone forecasted.

Heading into Game 4, head coach Rod Brind’Amour faces a critical decision: Brandon Bussi will start in net for Carolina, USA Today reports. It’s a high-stakes goalie change at the worst possible time — or, depending on how Game 4 unfolds, the masterstroke of the postseason.

Mitch Marner, Shea Theodore, and Bussi himself have become unlikely centerpieces of the series narrative. Here’s a quick look at who’s driving the storylines:

Three things to watch in Game 4:

  1. Whether Bussi can steal momentum back for Carolina
  2. How Vegas adjusts if Brind’Amour shuffles his forward lines
  3. Whether Marner continues his Conn Smythe-caliber Cup Final run

If Carolina can’t even the series, the Cup could be heading to Vegas faster than anyone expected. And just when you think there’s no more news to absorb, the 2026 NHL Draft enters the chat.

2026 NHL Mock Draft: McKenna to Toronto?

Bleacher Report and The Athletic’s latest mock drafts both point to Gavin McKenna as the projected No. 1 overall pick, with Toronto reportedly tipping its hand at the combine that he’s their guy, per Yahoo Sports.

For the Maple Leafs, this would be a franchise-altering pick. Toronto holding the No. 1 selection is itself a rarity — and pairing a generational prospect with their existing core could redefine the Atlantic Division.

The draft also collides with a busy rumor cycle:

  • The Pittsburgh Penguins are reportedly hunting for an impactful trade (NHLRumors)
  • The Vancouver Canucks are targeting leadership-type players
  • The Chicago Blackhawks want a veteran defenseman
  • The Detroit Red Wings are dealing with captain Dylan Larkin’s trade request, per Yardbarker

That last one stings. Larkin has been the face of Detroit through years of rebuilding, and a request to move on signals just how deep the frustration runs in Hockeytown.

What This Means for You as a Fan

Whether you root for an Original Six franchise or a recent expansion team, the next six weeks of NHL news will reshape rosters across the league. Here’s how to stay ahead:

  • If you’re an Oilers fan: Watch the NHLPA’s response closely. A formal investigation could delay or kill the Babcock hire entirely.
  • If you’re betting on the Cup Final: Goalie changes in Game 4 historically swing series — Bussi’s start could shift the entire market.
  • If you’re a draft watcher: Toronto at No. 1 with McKenna would dramatically reshape both fantasy hockey and real-world Eastern Conference dynamics.
  • If you’re a Red Wings fan: A Larkin trade would mark the end of an era — and likely accelerate a full rebuild.

A common misconception is that the NHL “slows down” after the Cup Final ends. The opposite is true. The draft, free agency, and coaching carousel turn June and July into the league’s loudest months.

Final Whistle

The NHL in June 2026 is a perfect storm of legacy storylines and franchise-defining decisions. Babcock’s potential return could fracture player-management trust league-wide. Landeskog’s awards remind us why we love this sport. And the Stanley Cup Final is still anyone’s to win.

So the question is: which storyline matters most to your team’s future — the coach you hire, the captain you keep, or the prospect you draft? Drop your prediction in the comments and tell us which Game 4 outcome you’re banking on.
