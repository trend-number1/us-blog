Sylvester Stallone Joins Quentin Tarantino for Surprising New Gangster Series

March 21, 2026 00:03 Trend Insights

At 79 years old, most actors would be content with a quiet retirement. Sylvester Stallone is doing the exact opposite — teaming up with one of cinema’s most legendary directors for what could be the most unexpected collaboration in entertainment history.

Key Takeaways

  • Stallone and Quentin Tarantino are co-creating a 1930s gangster series filmed in black and white with period-authentic cameras
  • Lionsgate has greenlit a “John Rambo” prequel with Stallone as executive producer
  • Stallone revealed Hollywood abandoned him for nearly a decade after Cop Land — agents and managers dropped him
  • At 79, Stallone was honored at the 48th Kennedy Center Honors by President Trump
  • He continues a rigorous workout routine despite admitting it “gets harder and harder”

Stallone and Tarantino: A 1930s Gangster Series Nobody Saw Coming

The biggest headline rocking Hollywood this week is a collaboration that reads like fan fiction: Quentin Tarantino and Sylvester Stallone are teaming up to write and direct a 1930s-set gangster series.

According to multiple reports from TMZ and World of Reel, the series will be filmed entirely in black and white using 1930s-era cameras, giving the project an authentically vintage aesthetic. Tarantino, known for his meticulous attention to period detail, is reportedly co-writing and directing the project alongside Stallone.

This marks a fascinating creative pairing. Tarantino has long been vocal about his admiration for classic Hollywood, while Stallone brings decades of screen presence and storytelling instinct. The combination of Tarantino’s sharp dialogue and Stallone’s raw charisma could produce something genuinely remarkable.

Details about the plot remain scarce, but the 1930s gangster setting opens the door to Prohibition-era crime stories — a genre both men have circled throughout their careers. The project is still in its early stages, but the sheer star power behind it has already generated massive buzz.

So how did Stallone go from being told his career was over to landing one of the most exciting projects in years?

The Decade Hollywood Forgot Sylvester Stallone

In a candid interview with AARP, Stallone opened up about what might be the darkest chapter of his career — a period of nearly ten years where he couldn’t find work.

The trouble started after Cop Land (1997). Despite Stallone delivering one of the most critically praised performances of his career, the film flopped at the box office. The fallout was swift and brutal.

“Nobody wanted me after ‘Cop Land.’ Even my agents,” Stallone told AARP. “I was fired from CAA. My personal manager at the time let me go. He said, ‘I can’t do anything for you. Nobody really wants you anymore.’”

Stallone recalled approaching a former agent who was then running Universal Studios, practically begging: “Please, I’ll take anything.” The response was lukewarm at best: “I’ll try to help you, but it’s not up to me.”

The industry’s message was clear: his time had passed, his genre was over.

But Stallone refused to accept that verdict. He turned back to the character that made him a star.

“I wanted to go back to Rocky,” Stallone recalled, calling the franchise his “safe place.” The problem? He was 60 years old, and the previous installment — Rocky V — had been what he himself called “an abject failure.” The original producers wanted nothing to do with a sixth film.

What followed was Rocky Balboa (2006), a film that defied every expectation and proved that Stallone’s storytelling instincts were sharper than Hollywood gave him credit for. That comeback laid the groundwork for everything that followed — The Expendables franchise ($855 million globally, now streaming on Netflix), the Creed series, and Tulsa King.

The lesson from Stallone’s wilderness years is one he summarized himself: “Every setback can be the first step to something better.”

The Rambo Universe Expands: “John Rambo” Prequel

Stallone’s resurgence isn’t limited to the small screen. In early March, Deadline reported that Lionsgate has greenlit a prequel titled “John Rambo,” with Stallone serving as executive producer.

The project will explore the origins of the iconic Vietnam War veteran before the events of First Blood (1982). While Stallone won’t be starring — the prequel necessarily requires a younger actor — his involvement as executive producer signals that the franchise’s DNA will remain intact.

This is a smart strategic move. The Rambo franchise remains one of the most recognizable action brands in cinema history, and a prequel allows Lionsgate to build on that legacy while introducing the character to a new generation of audiences.

Between the Tarantino series, the Rambo prequel, and his ongoing work on Tulsa King, Stallone has arguably the busiest slate of any actor his age in Hollywood history.

Stallone at 79: Fitness, Health, and the Kennedy Center Honor

Stallone’s public appearance at the 48th Kennedy Center Honors in Washington, D.C. drew attention for several reasons. He was among a select group — alongside Gloria Gaynor, Michael Crawford, George Strait, and Kiss — handpicked by President Donald Trump for recognition of lifetime contributions to American culture.

Stallone attended the black-tie gala in a classic tuxedo, wearing an American flag pin and the Kennedy Center Medal. His wife, Jennifer Flavin Stallone, 57, accompanied him throughout the evening.

What caught the most attention, however, was the black walking cane with a gold handle that Stallone relied on throughout the event. For fans who grew up watching him sprint up the Philadelphia Museum of Art steps, the image was a stark reminder that even action heroes age.

Stallone has been open about the physical toll of his career. Decades of performing his own stunts, taking real hits in boxing sequences, and pushing through injuries have left their mark. As he told Fox News Digital, receiving the Kennedy Center Honor was something he “never expected” and still finds shocking.

Despite the physical challenges, Stallone hasn’t abandoned his fitness regimen. In a March 2026 interview with Men’s Health, he revealed that he still works out religiously, though he acknowledges the reality of aging.

“It gets harder and harder,” Stallone told People magazine earlier this year. “That’s why you gotta push.”

His approach to fitness at 79 reflects the same philosophy that drove his career comeback: refuse to accept limitations, but be honest about the struggle.

What This Means for Hollywood — and for You

Stallone’s story is more than just celebrity news. It’s a masterclass in reinvention.

Consider the facts:

  • Fired by his agents in his 50s — now collaborating with Tarantino in his late 70s
  • Told his genre was dead — then revived two of its biggest franchises
  • Written off as finished — now busier than actors half his age
  • Physically slowing down — but showing up and pushing through

The Tarantino collaboration, in particular, signals something important about where Hollywood is heading. Streaming platforms and limited series are creating opportunities that didn’t exist during Stallone’s exile years. A-list talent that once would have considered television beneath them is now flocking to premium series formats.

For anyone who’s been told their best days are behind them — professionally, physically, or creatively — Stallone’s trajectory offers a powerful counter-argument. The man who was literally begging for roles two decades ago is now at the center of one of the most talked-about projects in the industry.

What would you attempt if you refused to believe your time had passed? Stallone answered that question at 60 with Rocky Balboa. At 79, he’s still answering it — this time with Tarantino by his side.
