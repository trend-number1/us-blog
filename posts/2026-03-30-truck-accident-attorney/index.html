Staged Truck Accidents: A Federal Crackdown Shakes the Industry

The trucking litigation landscape was rocked in March 2026 when a federal jury in New Orleans found two personal injury attorneys guilty of orchestrating a scheme to stage collisions with tractor-trailers. Attorneys Jason Giles and former Hollywood stuntwoman Vanessa Motta were convicted of fraud, obstruction of justice, and witness tampering. Their law firms also faced related charges.

A third defendant, Diaminike Stalbert, was convicted of making false statements but acquitted of a conspiracy charge.

Prosecutors alleged that Giles and Motta paid so-called “slammers” to intentionally crash into 18-wheelers and then file fraudulent lawsuits seeking larger settlement payouts. While defense attorneys argued the conduct amounted to professional negligence at worst, the jury disagreed.

This case is not an isolated incident. The sentencing for attorneys involved in a broader Louisiana staged truck accident scheme has drawn national attention, with multiple lawyers facing significant jail time. Experts estimate that car accident fraud in Louisiana has inflated insurance premiums statewide, costing everyday drivers hundreds of dollars per year.

So how do you separate legitimate legal help from bad actors? It starts with understanding what a qualified truck accident attorney actually does.

What a Truck Accident Attorney Does — and Why It Matters

Accidents involving semi-trucks, also known as big rigs or 18-wheelers, are among the most complex types of motor vehicle cases. The sheer size and weight of commercial trucks mean that collisions often result in severe injuries, fatalities, and extensive property damage.

A skilled truck accident attorney handles several critical tasks:

  • Investigating the crash — preserving black box data, driver logs, maintenance records, and surveillance footage before evidence is lost
  • Identifying all liable parties — the driver, trucking company, cargo loader, or vehicle manufacturer may share fault
  • Navigating federal regulations — the trucking industry is governed by FMCSA rules on hours of service, weight limits, and CDL requirements
  • Negotiating with insurance companies — trucking firms carry large policies and deploy aggressive legal teams
  • Filing within strict deadlines — statutes of limitations vary by state, and missing the deadline can permanently bar your claim

Nationally recognized firms like Pritzker Hageman have built reputations by conducting thorough investigations that uncover critical evidence and achieve substantial settlements and verdicts for victims and their families. Their approach emphasizes holding negligent trucking companies and drivers accountable while guiding families through the legal process.

Understanding the causes behind these crashes is the first step toward building a strong case.

Driver fatigue remains one of the most common causes. Truck drivers often work long hours, sometimes exceeding federally mandated limits, which leads to drowsy driving that impairs reaction times and judgment.

Driver distraction is a growing concern. With the prevalence of mobile devices and in-cab technologies, drivers can easily become distracted from the road — whether from texting, adjusting GPS systems, or other activities.

Recent reports from the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) show a troubling increase in truck-related crashes across the United States, underscoring the urgency of both regulatory enforcement and legal accountability.

But knowing the cause is only half the battle — timing matters just as much.

Critical Deadlines: Why Filing on Time Can Make or Break Your Case

One of the most overlooked aspects of truck accident litigation is the statute of limitations — the legal deadline for filing a claim. These deadlines vary by state:

  • Georgia: Two years from the date of the accident
  • California: Two years for personal injury claims
  • Texas: Two years for most personal injury cases
  • Louisiana: One year — one of the shortest in the nation

Legal professionals in Duluth, Georgia, have recently emphasized how missing the deadline can permanently impact your truck accident case. Once the statute of limitations expires, courts will almost certainly dismiss your claim, regardless of how strong your evidence may be.

This is why consulting a truck accident attorney as soon as possible after a collision is critical. Early engagement allows attorneys to:

  1. Preserve time-sensitive evidence like dashcam footage and electronic logging device (ELD) data
  2. Interview witnesses while memories are fresh
  3. File necessary paperwork well before any deadline
  4. Begin negotiations with insurance companies from a position of strength

The difference between acting quickly and waiting too long can be the difference between full compensation and getting nothing.

How to Choose the Right Truck Accident Attorney

Not all personal injury lawyers have the expertise to handle complex trucking cases. Here is what to look for when selecting a truck accident attorney:

Experience in trucking litigation specifically. Truck accident cases differ significantly from standard car accident claims. Look for attorneys who understand FMCSA regulations, electronic logging requirements, and the multi-party liability structures common in commercial trucking.

A track record of substantial results. Firms like Pritzker Hageman have built national reputations on achieving meaningful settlements and verdicts specifically in semi-truck accident cases. Ask prospective attorneys about their case outcomes.

Resources to investigate thoroughly. Truck accident cases require accident reconstruction experts, medical professionals, and sometimes forensic accountants. A well-resourced firm can pursue every avenue of evidence.

Transparency about fees. Most truck accident attorneys work on a contingency fee basis — meaning they only get paid if you win. Confirm this arrangement upfront and understand the percentage structure.

Red flags to watch for:

  • Attorneys who contact you unsolicited at the hospital or accident scene
  • Firms that guarantee specific dollar amounts before reviewing your case
  • Any suggestion of exaggerating or fabricating injuries — as the New Orleans convictions show, fraud carries severe criminal consequences

What This Means for You

Whether you are a truck accident victim, a commercial driver, or simply someone sharing the road with 18-wheelers, the legal landscape around trucking accidents is evolving rapidly. The 2026 federal convictions in New Orleans have put the industry on notice that staged accidents and fraudulent claims will be prosecuted aggressively.

For legitimate victims, this scrutiny actually works in your favor. Insurance companies and juries take real claims more seriously when the legal system demonstrates its commitment to weeding out fraud.

If you or a loved one has been involved in a truck accident, the most important step you can take is to consult a qualified truck accident attorney immediately. Document everything — photos of the scene, medical records, police reports, and witness contact information. Do not accept any early settlement offers from insurance companies without legal counsel.

The stakes in truck accident cases are too high to navigate alone. With the right attorney, victims can focus on healing while their legal team pursues the justice and compensation they deserve. The question is not whether you can afford a truck accident attorney — it is whether you can afford not to have one.
