Jeremy Fears Jr. All-American: Michigan State's Record-Chasing Point Guard

March 14, 2026 12:38 Trend Insights

Jeremy Fears Jr. is 27 assists away from rewriting Michigan State basketball history. The redshirt sophomore point guard has turned a season that began with questions into one that has produced All-American recognition, a Big Ten First Team selection, and a legitimate shot at breaking Cassius Winston’s single-season assist record – one of the most revered marks in Spartan lore.

At 15.5 points and 9.1 assists per game, Fears has established himself as one of the most productive floor generals in all of college basketball. His 264 assists on the season lead the Big Ten, and the record of 291 set by Winston – now an NBA veteran – is firmly within reach.

But this story is not without complications. A kicking controversy, a Big Ten Tournament loss to UCLA, and the intensity of March have all added layers to a season that refuses to be simple.

Key Takeaways:

  • Jeremy Fears Jr. was named Sporting News Third Team All-American and All-Big Ten First Team by both coaches and media
  • He leads Michigan State with 15.5 PPG and 9.1 APG, leading the entire Big Ten in assists
  • Fears has 264 assists this season, just 27 shy of Cassius Winston’s MSU single-season record of 291
  • A kicking controversy drew criticism from head coach Tom Izzo, who called the behavior “stupid” but did not suspend Fears
  • Michigan State’s season ended in the Big Ten Tournament with a loss to UCLA

From Illinois to East Lansing: The Fears Jr. Origin Story

Jeremy Fears Jr. arrived at Michigan State as one of the most heralded recruits out of Illinois, a state with a rich tradition of producing elite point guards. His path to becoming the Spartans’ engine, however, was not immediate.

After taking a redshirt season to develop and adjust to the pace of Big Ten basketball, Fears emerged this year as a transformed player. The burst, the court vision, the confidence to run a Tom Izzo offense at full speed – all of it came together in a way that even optimistic Michigan State fans did not fully anticipate.

The Illinois-born guard brought with him a style that blends traditional point guard playmaking with modern scoring ability. He is not merely a distributor who racks up assists by swinging the ball to open shooters. Fears creates advantages off the dribble, collapses defenses, and makes reads that most college guards simply cannot process at game speed.

That combination is why his numbers – 15.5 points and 9.1 assists per game – are not just impressive in isolation. They represent a level of dual-threat production that very few players in the country can match.

The assists number is the headline. At 264 on the season, Fears needs just 27 more to surpass Cassius Winston’s Michigan State single-season record of 291. Winston, who went on to play in the NBA after leading the Spartans to a Final Four, set that mark during one of the most decorated individual seasons in program history.

For Fears to approach and potentially break that record as a redshirt sophomore speaks to the extraordinary pace at which he has developed. Winston was a senior when he posted his record-setting total. Fears is doing this two years ahead of that timeline.

All-American and All-Big Ten: The Accolades Arrive

The awards have come in rapid succession. Fears was named Sporting News Third Team All-American, a distinction that places him among the top 15 players in college basketball as recognized by one of the sport’s most established publications.

At the conference level, the recognition was even more emphatic. Fears earned All-Big Ten First Team honors from both the coaches and the media – a dual selection that reflects universal respect for his impact. When both the people who game-plan against him and the people who watch him every night agree that he is one of the best five players in the conference, there is no room for debate.

These honors are particularly significant given the depth of talent in the Big Ten this season. The conference has featured multiple NBA-caliber players, and for Fears to rise above that competition reinforces just how special his season has been.

Chasing Cassius Winston’s Ghost

The Cassius Winston assist record carries enormous weight at Michigan State. Winston was not merely a productive point guard – he was the heartbeat of a Spartan program that reached the 2019 Final Four and won a Big Ten championship. His 291 assists in a single season represented the apex of his college career and became a benchmark for every Michigan State point guard who followed.

Fears has spoken about the record with a mix of respect and determination. He understands what Winston meant to the program and what surpassing his mark would symbolize.

With 27 assists needed, the math is straightforward but the path is not guaranteed. If Michigan State receives an NCAA Tournament bid – which remains likely despite the Big Ten Tournament exit – Fears would need to maintain his average of 9.1 assists per game over approximately three tournament games to reach the record.

That is achievable but far from certain. Tournament games are played at a different intensity, with opponents specifically scheming to limit a team’s primary playmaker. Every assist Fears records from this point forward will carry the weight of history.

The Kicking Controversy

No discussion of Fears’s season is complete without addressing the kicking incidents that drew national attention and internal criticism. On multiple occasions during games, Fears was seen kicking at opponents – a dangerous act that could result in injury and ejection.

Head coach Tom Izzo did not mince words. The Hall of Fame coach publicly called the behavior “stupid”, a blunt assessment that reflected both his frustration and his high standards for Spartan basketball.

However, Izzo opted not to suspend Fears, a decision that itself generated debate. Critics argued that a suspension would have sent a stronger message about sportsmanship and accountability. Supporters countered that Izzo’s public rebuke, combined with internal discipline, was sufficient to address the issue.

The incidents raised important questions:

  • Does the behavior reflect a pattern, or were these isolated moments of frustration during competitive games?
  • Should a player of Fears’s caliber be held to a higher standard precisely because of his visibility and influence?
  • Did the lack of a formal suspension undermine the message Izzo was trying to send?

What is clear is that the kicking controversy added an unwelcome distraction to an otherwise brilliant season. Fears’s talent is undeniable, but the ability to channel competitive fire without crossing the line into recklessness is part of what separates good players from great ones.

Big Ten Tournament: The UCLA Loss

Michigan State’s Big Ten Tournament run ended with a loss to UCLA, a result that stung given the Spartans’ aspirations for a deep postseason run. The defeat highlighted both the challenges of tournament basketball and the specific areas where Michigan State remains vulnerable.

For Fears personally, the tournament exit meant fewer guaranteed opportunities to chase Winston’s record. Every game from this point forward could be his last of the season, adding urgency to an already compelling statistical pursuit.

The UCLA loss also raised questions about Michigan State’s NCAA Tournament seeding and draw. A team with a player of Fears’s caliber is dangerous in any matchup, but the Spartans will need contributions from the entire roster – not just their star point guard – to make a meaningful March run.

What Makes Fears Jr. Special: The Eye Test

Beyond the statistics, what stands out about Jeremy Fears Jr. is how he makes everyone around him better. His 9.1 assists per game are not accumulated through simple entry passes or kick-outs. They come from:

  • Pick-and-roll mastery: Fears reads the defense’s coverage and delivers the ball to the roller or the open shooter with equal precision
  • Transition vision: He pushes the pace and finds streaking teammates before the defense can set
  • Drive-and-dish creativity: His ability to draw two defenders and hit the open man has been Michigan State’s most reliable offensive weapon
  • Late-game composure: In close games, Fears has consistently made the right play under pressure, whether that means scoring himself or setting up a teammate

Tom Izzo has coached some of the finest point guards in Big Ten history – Mateen Cleaves, Drew Neitzel, Cassius Winston. For Fears to be mentioned in that lineage as a redshirt sophomore is a testament to both his talent and his accelerated development.

The Road Ahead: NCAA Tournament and Beyond

Michigan State’s season is not over. The NCAA Tournament looms, and with it comes the opportunity for Fears to add to his legacy in the most visible setting college basketball offers.

If the Spartans receive a tournament bid, each game becomes a stage:

  • A first-round win with 9+ assists puts Fears within striking distance of Winston’s record
  • A second-round appearance could be the game where history is made
  • A deep run would cement Fears’s season as one of the greatest individual campaigns in Michigan State history

The stakes extend beyond the record. NBA scouts are watching closely, and Fears’s tournament performance will significantly influence his draft stock. A point guard who can run a team at this level, produce at this rate, and perform on the biggest stages is exactly what NBA front offices covet.

A Season That Demands Attention

Jeremy Fears Jr. has done something remarkable this season. He has taken a Michigan State program with championship expectations, placed it squarely on his shoulders, and produced at a level that has earned national recognition. All-American. All-Big Ten First Team. Big Ten assists leader. Record chase.

The kicking controversy is a blemish, and the Big Ten Tournament loss is a disappointment. But neither diminishes the overall arc of what Fears has accomplished. He is 27 assists away from etching his name above Cassius Winston’s in the Michigan State record book – a sentence that would have seemed improbable at the start of the season.

Whether the record falls or not, Jeremy Fears Jr. has already secured his place in the conversation about the best point guards in Michigan State history. At just a redshirt sophomore, the most remarkable part is that he is only getting started.
