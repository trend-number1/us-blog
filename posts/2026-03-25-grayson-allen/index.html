Grayson Allen Returns: Suns Get Essential Boost for Nuggets Showdown

March 25, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

Can a single player’s return flip a team’s fortunes? The Phoenix Suns are betting on it. Grayson Allen has been cleared to play Tuesday night against the Denver Nuggets after missing the Suns’ previous four games due to left knee injury management — and his comeback couldn’t come at a more critical time.

Key Takeaways

  • Grayson Allen is officially available for Tuesday’s Suns-Nuggets matchup after a four-game absence
  • Allen is posting career-high numbers this season: 17.2 points per game on 35.4% from three
  • Royce O’Neale also returns after missing three games, giving Phoenix two key rotation pieces back
  • The Suns have been battling a mounting injury list that has sidelined Devin Booker, Dillon Brooks, and others at various points

Grayson Allen’s Return Changes the Equation

Allen appeared to be a true game-time decision heading into Tuesday, but the Suns officially cleared him to play against Denver. According to ESPN, he was averaging 15.2 points, 2.2 rebounds, 5.5 assists, and 2.7 made three-pointers per game through six March appearances before being temporarily shut down.

His absence was felt immediately. The Suns dropped into a five-game losing streak at one point, including a tough 92-77 loss to the Portland Trail Blazers. While Phoenix managed to claw back with some gritty defensive performances, the offense clearly missed Allen’s shooting and playmaking.

The knee issues have been a recurring theme this season. Allen missed three games before the All-Star break with a right knee sprain, then sprained his ankle during shootaround before a loss to the San Antonio Spurs. A right quad contusion sidelined him further in early March. Head coach Jordan Ott initially labeled the quad issue as “day-to-day,” but it lingered longer than expected.

Now healthy, Allen’s return alongside Royce O’Neale — who was listed as probable and had missed three games with his own knee injury — gives the Suns a desperately needed roster boost.

A Career-Best Season by Every Measure

This isn’t the same Grayson Allen who bounced around the league as a role player. The 2025-26 season has been a legitimate breakout campaign.

Allen’s season has featured genuine star-level performances. In early November, he erupted for a 42-point game against the New Orleans Pelicans — a scoring explosion that announced his arrival as more than just a complementary piece. His previous career high in scoring average was 13.5 points per game during his first season in Phoenix, making this year’s 17.2 PPG a massive leap.

What makes Allen’s production even more impressive is the context. He’s been doing this while navigating multiple injury setbacks and adapting to a Suns roster that has dealt with significant absences all season long.

The Suns’ Injury Battle: Who’s In, Who’s Out

Phoenix’s injury report has read like a medical textbook this season. Allen’s return helps, but the Suns are still navigating a minefield of health concerns.

Key absences the Suns have dealt with this season:

  • Devin Booker — right hip strain
  • Dillon Brooks — left hand fracture
  • Jalen Green — hamstring strain (missed 13 games, not at full force upon return)
  • Jordan Goodwin — left calf strain
  • Haywood Highsmith — right knee injury management
  • Cole Anthony — not with team

The good news beyond Allen’s return: Royce O’Neale is also back in the lineup. O’Neale has appeared in 69 games this season with 64 starts, averaging 9.9 points, 4.8 rebounds, 2.8 assists, and 1.1 steals on an efficient 41.6% from the field and 40.3% from beyond the arc. His floor-spacing and defensive versatility have made him a key cog in Phoenix’s rotation.

Getting both players back simultaneously gives coach Ott more flexibility in his rotations — something the Suns have sorely lacked during their recent skid.

What Allen Means for the Suns’ Playoff Push

The Western Conference standings leave no room for error. The Suns entered the week with a record that puts them squarely in the playoff picture, but the margin between seeding positions in the West is razor-thin.

Allen’s value extends beyond his scoring numbers. Here’s why his return matters so much:

  1. Three-point shooting: At 35.4% from deep with 2.7 made threes per game in March, Allen spaces the floor for Phoenix’s other creators
  2. Playmaking: His 4.1 assists per game represent a career best and give the Suns another ball-handler when Booker is out
  3. Defensive activity: Allen’s 1.4 steals per game contribute to a Suns defense that forced 19 steals in their recent win over Portland
  4. Veteran presence: In a season of constant roster shuffling, Allen provides stability and experience

The Nuggets matchup Tuesday is particularly significant. Denver enters with a 44-28 record after defeating the Trail Blazers on Sunday night, making this a critical Western Conference showdown with potential seeding implications.

What This Means for You

If you’re a Suns fan, Allen’s return is the kind of news that shifts the conversation from “surviving” to “competing.” Phoenix has shown it can win ugly — that 19-steal defensive masterclass against Portland proved the team’s resilience. But winning in the playoffs requires more than grit; it requires shot-making, and Allen is one of the best shooters on this roster.

Common misconception: Some fans have written off Allen as injury-prone this season. The reality is that he’s dealt with a series of separate, mostly minor issues — a knee sprain, an ankle tweak, a quad contusion — rather than one chronic problem. When healthy, he’s been one of the most productive guards in the league.

For fantasy basketball managers, Allen is a must-start when active. His career-high 17.2 points per game combined with his assists and steals production makes him a multi-category contributor.

The bigger question now: can the Suns keep Allen and their other key players healthy long enough to make a deep postseason run? With the trade deadline behind them and the playoff picture crystallizing, the next few weeks will determine whether Phoenix is a true contender — or just a team that showed flashes of what could have been.

One thing is clear: Grayson Allen is no longer a supporting character. He’s a lead performer in this Suns story.
