Grizzlies vs Bulls: Essential Breakdown of a Pivotal Lottery Showdown

March 17, 2026 08:43 Trend Insights

When the Memphis Grizzlies rolled into the United Center to face the Chicago Bulls on March 16, neither team was playing for playoff glory. They were playing for something arguably just as valuable — lottery positioning.

Key Takeaways

  • Memphis (23-43) entered riding a brutal seven-game losing streak, the longest of their season.
  • Chicago (27-40) went winless in February and traded key role players at the deadline.
  • Only 3.5 games separated these two teams in the tank race, making every outcome critical for draft lottery odds.
  • Josh Giddey recorded three triple-doubles in his last four games, while Matas Buzelis scored 20+ points in six of his last eight contests.
  • Memphis listed six players as out with additional doubtful and questionable designations.

Grizzlies vs Bulls: Two Franchises at a Crossroads

This was the first meeting between the Grizzlies and Bulls all season, and the circumstances could not have been more dramatic. Memphis came in with the eighth-worst record in the league at 23-43, while Chicago sat at 27-40 — close enough to make every game a swing in the draft lottery calculus.

The Grizzlies’ seven-game losing streak had sent them spiraling from play-in contention to near the top of draft boards. For a franchise that reached the Western Conference Semifinals just a few seasons ago, the freefall has been stunning.

Chicago’s story is equally jarring. The Bulls were comfortably battling for a playoff spot until February happened. An 11-game losing streak forced the front office’s hand, leading them to sell off several key role players to contenders and officially pivot toward the future.

So what happens when two teams with tanking incentives meet on the hardwood? The answer is more complicated than you might think.

Chicago’s Young Core Is Making a Statement

Despite the franchise being in rebuild mode, the Bulls’ young players have been turning heads. Josh Giddey has evolved into a legitimate triple-double machine, averaging 18 points per game and recording three triple-doubles in his last four outings. For a player who arrived in Chicago amid questions about his scoring ability, the numbers speak for themselves.

Then there is Matas Buzelis, the second-year forward having a breakout campaign. Buzelis is putting up 15.9 points per game and has been on a tear lately, dropping 20+ points in six of his last eight contests. At just 20 years old, he is looking like a cornerstone piece for Chicago’s future.

Head coach Billy Donovan has continued to preach a winning mindset, refusing to embrace the tank publicly. Both Giddey and Buzelis were listed as full go for this matchup, and the Bulls’ expected starting lineup featured:

  1. Josh Giddey
  2. Tre Jones
  3. Matas Buzelis
  4. Leonard Miller

With their best young players healthy and firing on all cylinders, the Bulls appeared well-positioned to compete. But as their own beat writers noted — that is also what everyone thought before the Sacramento Kings loss.

Memphis Injury Woes Paint a Grim Picture

While Chicago had the advantage of relatively healthy key players, the Grizzlies entered the matchup decimated by injuries. Six Memphis players were listed as out, and the situation only got worse from there.

Ty Jerome was listed as doubtful with a left shoulder contusion. GG Jackson was also doubtful due to right foot soreness. Walter Clayton Jr. was questionable with a right ankle sprain. The Memphis lineup was essentially held together with duct tape.

The projected Grizzlies starters reflected the depth of the roster crisis:

  1. Cam Spencer
  2. Jaylen Wells
  3. Cedric Coward
  4. Taylor Hendricks
  5. Olivier-Maxence Prosper

For casual NBA fans, this lineup might look unfamiliar — and that is precisely the point. Memphis has been forced to rely on deep bench pieces and developmental players as injuries have gutted the roster throughout the season.

The injury situation has been the defining story of Memphis’s 2025-26 campaign, turning what was supposed to be a competitive season into a lottery-bound year.

The Tank Race: Why This Game Mattered More Than It Seemed

Here is where things get fascinating. With only 3.5 games separating Memphis and Chicago in the standings, every win and loss carries enormous implications for the 2026 NBA Draft lottery.

The modern NBA draft lottery is weighted, meaning teams with worse records have better odds at landing a top pick. For two franchises clearly in rebuild mode, the math is straightforward — losing is winning.

But there is a tension at play. Billy Donovan’s insistence on competing means the Bulls risk diminishing their lottery odds if they keep winning. This became especially relevant as the Milwaukee Bucks struggled in the standings, potentially opening up a higher lottery position for teams willing to lose.

Here is the uncomfortable truth both fan bases must grapple with: the team that loses this game might actually benefit more in the long run. A higher draft pick in 2026 could define the next decade for either franchise.

  • Memphis’s incentive: Continue the losing streak to solidify a top-8 pick
  • Chicago’s incentive: Losses improve their lottery position, but the coaching staff refuses to throw games
  • The wildcard: Young players like Buzelis and Giddey are too competitive to coast

This dynamic creates the most watchable kind of NBA game — one where the outcome matters on multiple levels, even if neither team is sniffing the postseason.

What This Means for Fans Going Forward

For Grizzlies fans, the remainder of the season is about two things: monitoring injury recoveries and watching the draft lottery odds shift game by game. The seven-game losing streak is painful, but it could pay dividends on draft night. Each remaining game is pivotal in determining where Memphis lands.

For Bulls fans, the story is more nuanced. The front office clearly made a choice at the trade deadline to prioritize the future. But with Giddey and Buzelis playing at this level, there is a genuine foundation to build around. The question becomes whether Chicago can land another impact player in the draft to accelerate the rebuild.

Both teams have 15 games remaining to determine their final positioning. Every matchup between lottery-bound teams now carries outsized importance.

The Bigger Picture

The Grizzlies vs Bulls matchup encapsulates everything fascinating and frustrating about the modern NBA’s late-season landscape. Two proud franchises, both with legitimate young talent, caught between the desire to compete and the mathematical reality that losing serves their long-term interests.

Giddey’s triple-double prowess and Buzelis’s scoring explosions suggest the Bulls are closer to contention than their record indicates. Meanwhile, Memphis’s injury-ravaged roster masks a team that could look dramatically different — and better — next season with a healthy squad and a high draft pick.

As the final stretch of the 2025-26 season unfolds, one question looms over both organizations: Will tonight’s result look like a footnote, or a turning point, when we look back five years from now?

The answer might depend entirely on a few ping pong balls at the lottery drawing.
