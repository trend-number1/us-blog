Demond Claiborne: Vikings' Electric New 4.37 Speedster

April 26, 2026 06:03 Trend Insights

What does it take to make the Minnesota Vikings trade up in the sixth round of the NFL Draft? Apparently, a 5-foot-9 running back who clocked a 4.37 in the 40-yard dash and treats open-field tackles like optional suggestions.

That’s the headline on Demond Claiborne, the Wake Forest speedster Minnesota selected with the 198th overall pick in the 2026 NFL Draft on Saturday. And the Vikings didn’t just sit and wait for him — they paid up to make sure he wasn’t snatched away.

Quick Summary

  • Selected 198th overall by the Minnesota Vikings in Round 6 of the 2026 NFL Draft
  • Vikings traded pick No. 234 plus a 2027 sixth-rounder to New England to move up
  • 4.37-second 40-yard dash at the NFL Scouting Combine — third-fastest among RBs in the class
  • Three-year starter at Wake Forest with 2,599 career rushing yards and 26 touchdowns
  • Joins a backfield led by Aaron Jones, Jordan Mason, and Zavier Scott

Who Is Demond Claiborne?

Born October 9, 2003, Demond Claiborne is a 22-year-old running back who spent four years at Wake Forest, the last three as the program’s lead rusher. He measured in at 5-foot-9 3/4 and 188 pounds at the NFL Scouting Combine — undersized by NFL standards, but the kind of compact frame that lets him cut on a dime.

He’s a two-time All-ACC honoree: Second-Team All-ACC in 2025 and Third-Team All-ACC in 2024. That kind of consistency in the ACC’s Power Four trenches matters more than highlight clips on a Tuesday night.

His college production tells the story:

  • 2024 season: 1,049 rushing yards, 13 total touchdowns, 1,300 yards from scrimmage
  • 2025 season: 907 rushing yards, 10 touchdowns, 5.1 yards per carry
  • Career totals: 558 carries, 2,599 yards, 26 rushing TDs
  • Receiving: 55 catches for 424 yards and 2 scores
  • Special teams: 22 kickoff returns averaging 26.3 yards, with one return TD in each of his last two seasons

He’s not just a runner. He’s a three-phase player, and that’s the kind of profile teams chase late on Day 3.

Up next: why the Vikings were willing to give up future capital to land him.

Why the Vikings Traded Up

Here’s the move in plain terms: Minnesota sent pick No. 234 and a 2027 sixth-round selection to the New England Patriots in exchange for No. 198, and used it on Claiborne. Trading up in the sixth round is unusual. Trading up to grab a running back at that point of the draft is rarer still.

But the Vikings did their homework. Claiborne was hosted on a top-30 visit at TCO Performance Center, meaning Minnesota’s front office had already flagged him as a target weeks before the draft. When he slid further than expected, they pounced.

The fit makes sense. The Vikings entered the draft with 31-year-old Aaron Jones atop the depth chart, Jordan Mason as the power complement, and Zavier Scott rounding out the room. What was missing was youth and elite burst — exactly what Claiborne brings.

This offseason, Minnesota also hired former Dolphins offensive coordinator Frank Smith to its staff. A scheme that values speed-in-space backs and a roster gap at that exact archetype made Claiborne a logical bet.

So what does Minnesota actually have in him? Let’s break down the tape.

The Skill Set: Speed, Vision, and a Few Caveats

Claiborne’s calling card is breakaway acceleration. His 4.37 40-yard dash ranked third among running backs in the entire 2026 class — behind only Arkansas’ Mike Washington Jr. (4.33) and Notre Dame’s Jeremiyah Love (4.36, drafted 3rd overall by Arizona). He also posted a 10-foot-2 broad jump.

But raw speed alone doesn’t get you drafted. Scouts at Daily Norseman describe him as a “slashing type of back with outstanding vision and very good patience in setting up his blocks,” with elite acceleration to the second level. Sports Illustrated called him “an electric playmaker” who “wins with his elite burst and agility on the edge.”

Here’s a quick comparison to put his profile in context:

The downside? It’s the same one you see with every sub-200-pound back. He’s not a consistent between-the-tackles bruiser, his pass protection still needs work, and he probably won’t be the guy you call on 3rd-and-1 from your own goal line.

But that’s not what Minnesota drafted him to be. The Vikings drafted a chess piece — a back who can take a screen 60 yards, return a kickoff for six, or punch in a touchdown from 20 yards out when a hole opens up.

That brings us to the most interesting question: what’s his actual role going to be?

What This Means for Vikings Fans

If you’re a Minnesota fan staring at the depth chart, here’s the realistic picture for 2026:

  1. Aaron Jones remains the lead back, but he turns 32 in December
  2. Jordan Mason absorbs the early-down power work
  3. Claiborne likely starts as a third-down change-of-pace back, kick returner, and home-run threat on screens and tosses
  4. By 2027, with Jones potentially moving on, Claiborne could compete for a much larger role

The pass-blocking question is the swing factor. RotoWire noted he “could earn his keep in a third-down role if he can tidy up his pass-blocking.” Translation: if he proves trustworthy in protection, his snap count balloons. If not, he’s a designed-touch specialist and a return man.

For fantasy football managers, that’s a nuanced bet. He’s not a Week 1 starter, but he’s exactly the kind of late-round handcuff who could become a monster value pick if Aaron Jones misses time.

A common misconception worth addressing: people assume Day 3 picks are throwaways. They’re not. Last year’s draft alone produced multiple sixth-round running backs who became fantasy-relevant by midseason. Burst and contact balance translate to the NFL at any draft slot.

The Bottom Line

Demond Claiborne isn’t a finished product. He’s an undersized back with a big-play gear, a willingness to contribute on special teams, and just enough question marks to fall to pick 198. But the Vikings clearly saw something the rest of the league missed — enough that they paid future capital to make sure he ended up in purple.

The bet is simple: in a league trending toward speed and space, a 4.37 back with three-down upside at pick 198 is a steal. If even half of the projection hits, Minnesota will look brilliant.

So here’s the question for you: which sixth-round pick from this draft class do you think will outproduce the running backs taken in Round 1 by 2027? Claiborne might just be the answer.
