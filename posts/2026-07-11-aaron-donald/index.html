Aaron Donald Comeback: The Surprising Rams Reunion Buzz

July 11, 2026 06:03 Trend Insights

What if one of the most dominant defensive players in NFL history decided that two years of retirement was enough?

That’s the question gripping the league right now. An Aaron Donald comeback has gone from wishful thinking to what one insider calls “close to a done deal” — and the Los Angeles Rams are the team at the center of it.

Quick Summary

  • NFL insider Jason La Canfora reports Donald’s return to the Rams is “close to a done deal.”
  • Donald has been retired since 2024 and hasn’t played a snap since 2023.
  • The trigger: the Rams traded for star edge rusher Myles Garrett, and Donald reportedly wants to play alongside him.
  • The biggest obstacle isn’t skill — it’s injury risk and whether his body is ready for a 17-game grind.

Why the Aaron Donald Comeback Suddenly Feels Real

For most of 2026, the idea of an Aaron Donald return was “more an alluring fantasy than a legitimate opportunity,” as Sporting News put it. Then the buzz shifted.

NFL insider Jason La Canfora wrote that “with training camp now less than a month away, the NFL is widely anticipating the return of future Hall of Fame defensive tackle Aaron Donald to the Los Angeles Rams.”

His words were blunt: “What began as speculation and then became very well-informed buzz is now being viewed as close to a done deal.”

The reason? Donald reportedly wants to play with Myles Garrett, and he’s told people within the Rams organization how hard he’s leaning toward ending his retirement.

There’s still no firm timetable for Donald to formalize a decision or report to the team. But the Rams have made clear they’ll keep the door open for as long as he wants. So what changed the equation? It starts with a blockbuster trade.

The Myles Garrett Trade That Started It All

The Rams are in a clear win-now window. With franchise quarterback Matthew Stafford only a few years from retirement, Los Angeles is pushing its chips in.

On June 1, the 2022 Super Bowl champions made their statement move. They traded for Cleveland Browns star defender Myles Garrett to drastically elevate their pass rush.

The price was steep. The Rams sent the following to Cleveland:

  1. Defensive standout Jared Verse
  2. A 2027 first-round pick
  3. A 2028 second-round pick
  4. A 2029 third-round pick

That’s an aggressive package for one player — and it signals exactly how serious Los Angeles is about another Super Bowl run. Pairing Garrett with a returning Donald would give the Rams one of the most fearsome pass-rushing duos imaginable.

ESPN’s Adam Schefter said he believes the Rams could make a run at Donald this offseason. Sports Illustrated’s Albert Breer went further, laying out exactly what a return would demand. And that’s where the story gets complicated.

The One Factor That Could Halt Everything

Here’s the insight that separates hype from reality: the obstacle to an Aaron Donald comeback isn’t talent. It’s the toll of the game itself.

Donald has been retired since 2024 and hasn’t played since 2023. Coming back means a massive physical commitment.

As Breer wrote: “It’s a big commitment, returning to go through the whole grind after two years on the shelf. He’d have to get bowed up for training camp, battle to get into football shape, and then endure the gauntlet of a 17-game regular season, plus the playoffs.”

Breer even floated a lighter alternative — a late-season, playoff-only role similar to what veteran safety Eric Weddle did, coming off the bench for a handful of snaps rather than grinding through a full year.

Former Rams left tackle Andrew Whitworth framed the real decision around risk. “I think it’s going to be how comfortable he is with going back out there and understanding not the positive, but the negative,” Whitworth said. “And that is, you get injured. Injuries, things that affect you past football.”

Whitworth added that the way Donald’s body feels will make the final determination. “Aaron’s a guy like me. He’s training all the time, no matter what… but only he will know that.”

That last point matters. Because if you’ve seen Donald lately, he doesn’t look like a man who’s slowed down.

Still Elite: The Steelers Training Session

Even in retirement, Donald reminded everyone why the buzz exists.

In a video that spread across social media, Donald — wearing an LA shirt — gave a pass-rushing lesson to Jaquan Brisker, the Pittsburgh Steelers’ new safety. He showed Brisker the ins and outs of beating blockers.

Brisker spent his first four NFL seasons with the Chicago Bears before signing a one-year, $5.5 million contract with Pittsburgh for the 2026 season. Learning technique directly from Donald — a player whose attention to detail is widely considered second to none — is a rare opportunity for any defender.

The clip did more than help Brisker. It “opened eyes” about how sharp Donald still looks, adding fuel to the unretirement fire.

A Résumé That Speaks for Itself

Why does the football world care this much? Because Donald’s career numbers are staggering.

A player who earned a Pro Bowl nod in every year he played doesn’t just walk back onto the field casually. And a return would put that perfect streak — and his legacy — on the line.

What This Means For You

If you’re a Rams fan, the calculus is thrilling: a Garrett-Donald pairing could be the difference in a wide-open NFC. If you follow the NFL more broadly, this is a case study in how modern contenders chase championships with bold, expensive gambles.

A word of caution for the hype, though: as Yahoo Sports noted, Donald unretiring doesn’t guarantee the Rams a Super Bowl. Football is a team game, and even a legend can’t win one alone.

Here’s the honest bottom line. The buzz is real, the insiders are serious, and the timing — right before training camp — is telling. But the final call rests entirely with one man weighing his body against his ambition.

So here’s the question worth sitting with: If you were Aaron Donald — a first-ballot Hall of Famer with nothing left to prove — would you risk a flawless legacy for one more shot at glory?
