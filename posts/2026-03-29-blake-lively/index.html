Blake Lively vs Justin Baldoni: Essential Guide to the It Ends With Us Trial

March 29, 2026 00:03 Trend Insights

Has a film set feud ever escalated this far? The legal war between Blake Lively and Justin Baldoni over the 2024 film It Ends With Us is barreling toward a May 18 trial — and the pretrial developments alone are making headlines.

Key Takeaways

  • Blake Lively and Justin Baldoni failed to reach a settlement after six hours of courtroom talks.
  • Baldoni secured a one-week pretrial delay after accusing Lively’s team of a massive “document dump.”
  • A federal judge ruled Baldoni’s company Wayfarer can proceed with a defamation lawsuit against former publicist Stephanie Jones.
  • The trial is still expected to begin May 18 in Manhattan federal court.
  • Discovery could expose private messages involving Lively, Ryan Reynolds, and publicist Leslie Sloane.

Blake Lively’s Lawsuit: How It All Started

The roots of this legal battle trace back to December 2024, when Lively filed a complaint against Baldoni with the California Civil Rights Department. She accused the actor-director of sexual harassment on the set of It Ends With Us and alleged that both Baldoni and his production company, Wayfarer Studios, engaged in a “social manipulation” campaign designed to “destroy” her reputation.

The dispute soon migrated to New York. Both parties filed dueling lawsuits — Lively reiterated her harassment claims and added accusations of “unlawful, retaliatory astroturfing,” while Baldoni countered with his own legal action.

More than a year later, neither side is backing down. With a May trial date looming, every new filing adds fuel to what has become one of Hollywood’s most closely watched legal confrontations.

Settlement Talks Collapse After Six Hours

In what was described as a last-ditch effort before trial, Lively and Baldoni spent six hours inside a New York City courtroom in March 2026 attempting to negotiate a resolution.

The court-ordered settlement conference saw the two actors and their attorneys occupy separate courtrooms while a judge shuttled between them for private discussions. Neither side found common ground.

  • Lively left without speaking to reporters.
  • Baldoni and his wife Emily declined to give statements.
  • Baldoni’s attorney, Bryan Freedman, told ABC News he believes the case will go to trial, adding: “I’m looking forward to it.”

The failed settlement all but guarantees that the most intimate details of this dispute will be aired in open court. So what’s happening in the lead-up?

Baldoni Wins Pretrial Delay Over “Document Dump”

Just days after the settlement conference collapsed, Baldoni scored a procedural victory. A federal judge granted his request for a one-week extension on pretrial filings, pushing the deadline from April 3 to April 10 and the final pretrial conference from April 21 to April 28.

The reason? Baldoni’s legal team argued that Lively’s lawyers had flooded the court with an overwhelming amount of evidence:

  • Over 1,000 potential exhibits filed on March 20
  • More than 40 potential witnesses identified for trial
  • A jury instruction document spanning 85 pages and 13 causes of action

“It is difficult to imagine that even half that number would be put before a jury,” Baldoni’s attorneys argued. “Many are lengthy exhibits, consisting of numerous text messages or pages, many of which are obviously irrelevant or inadmissible under multiple rules.”

Despite the delay in pretrial procedures, there is no indication yet that the May 18 trial start date will be affected.

Wayfarer’s Defamation Case Moves Forward

In a parallel legal front that could have major implications for Lively, federal judge Lewis Liman ruled that Baldoni’s production company, Wayfarer Studios, can proceed with a defamation lawsuit against former publicist Stephanie Jones.

Baldoni’s attorney Bryan Freedman called the ruling a vindication: “The court rejected Stephanie Jones’ effort to dismiss the case and will allow Wayfarer’s claims, including defamation, to be tested on a full evidentiary record.”

According to Page Six, this development could expose private communications among several high-profile figures, potentially reshaping the public narrative that has surrounded the case. Baldoni’s legal team has expressed confidence that a fuller evidentiary record will reveal how certain damaging narratives were constructed.

What This Means for Both Sides

With the May 18 trial approaching, both Lively and Baldoni face significant stakes:

For Blake Lively:

  • Her original accusations of sexual harassment and reputational sabotage will be tested before a jury.
  • The Wayfarer defamation case against Jones could pull in communications that expose behind-the-scenes strategy.
  • Friend and A Simple Favor director Paul Feig has publicly stated that Lively is “great” and “soldiering on” amid the legal drama.

For Justin Baldoni:

  • The pretrial delay gives his team additional time to prepare against the volume of evidence.
  • The Wayfarer ruling is a meaningful win, opening up discovery that his side believes will be favorable.
  • His legal team has also dragged Taylor Swift into the battle, arguing in filings that Lively followed a “Taylor Swift playbook” — a claim that has drawn additional media scrutiny.

Meanwhile, It Ends With Us author Colleen Hoover broke her silence on the legal drama, sharing that she remains proud of the project despite the ongoing turmoil between its stars.

The Road to May 18

Here’s where the timeline stands heading into April:

  1. April 10 — New deadline for pretrial motions and filings
  2. April 28 — Final pretrial conference
  3. May 18 — Trial expected to begin in Manhattan federal court

Lively has defended her request for a jury trial, and with settlement talks dead, all roads now point toward a courtroom showdown. The discovery process in the Wayfarer-Jones defamation case could surface communications that reshape public understanding of what happened on and off the It Ends With Us set.

This case has already redefined how Hollywood power dynamics, publicity campaigns, and personal grievances collide in the legal system. The question now is simple: when the jury finally hears both sides, whose version of events will hold up?
