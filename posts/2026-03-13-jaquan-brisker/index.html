Jaquan Brisker Free Agency: Where Will the Former Bears Safety Land?

March 13, 2026 08:14 Trend Insights

Jaquan Brisker Remains One of the Best Players Still Available in Free Agency

The first wave of 2026 NFL free agency has come and gone, and one name keeps surfacing in trade rumors and landing-spot speculation: Jaquan Brisker. The hard-hitting safety spent the first four years of his career with the Chicago Bears, but the relationship appears to be over. With the Bears pivoting to other options and multiple contenders circling, Brisker’s next move could reshape a secondary across the league.

So why hasn’t one of the NFL’s most talented young safeties found a home yet — and who’s most likely to sign him?

Key Takeaways:

  • The Bears have all but confirmed they are moving on from Brisker after signing safety Coby Bryant to a 3-year, $40 million deal
  • Brisker posted career-best numbers in 2025: 93 tackles, 1 sack, and 8 passes defended across all 17 games
  • The Eagles, Steelers, and 49ers are the most frequently linked suitors
  • A concussion history (diagnosed in each of his first three NFL seasons) is suppressing his market value
  • Spotrac projects his market value at approximately $10–11 million annually

The Bears Have Moved On

For months, the question in Chicago was whether the Bears would re-sign Brisker or let him walk. That question now has a definitive answer.

Chicago signed free agent safety Coby Bryant to a three-year, $40 million contract, a move that effectively slammed the door on a Brisker return. Former Bears safety Kevin Byard also departed, agreeing to a one-year, $9 million deal with the New England Patriots.

Chicago Tribune insider Brad Biggs was blunt about Brisker’s future: “I think I’ve been pretty adamant all along that I don’t think there’s a future with the organization for Jaquan Brisker. I think they’re moving on there.”

The Bears now appear focused on addressing other roster needs — offensive line, wide receiver, and cornerback — leaving Brisker to find his next chapter elsewhere.

A Career Year Heading Into Free Agency

Before his departure, Brisker reminded everyone what he’s capable of. His 2025 season was statistically the best of his career:

It was the first time Brisker played all 17 games in a season, a significant milestone given his injury history. The 26-year-old, originally selected by the Bears in the second round (48th overall) of the 2022 NFL Draft out of Penn State, showed he could be a full-time starter when healthy.

His rookie season in 2022 was equally impressive: 104 combined tackles, four sacks, an interception, and a forced fumble across 15 games, earning him a spot on Pro Football Focus’s All-Rookie Team.

But there’s one factor that keeps coming up in every discussion about his value — and it’s the reason he’s still unsigned.

The Concussion Concern

Brisker has been diagnosed with a concussion in three of his four NFL seasons, a troubling pattern for any player but especially for a safety who makes his living as a downhill enforcer.

  • 2022: Possible concussion during a Week 11 matchup at Atlanta; the Bears were criticized for initially failing to follow concussion protocol
  • 2023: Concussion suffered during a late-game two-point conversion attempt against the Raiders; missed two games
  • 2024: The most severe incident — a collision with Panthers tight end Tommy Tremble on October 6 resulted in a vestibular concussion that kept Brisker out for over four months. He was placed on injured reserve and had to retrain his nervous system through specialized rehabilitation exercises

After the 2024 concussion, Brisker told reporters he planned to “play the same way” despite the accumulating head injuries. That fearless mentality is part of what makes him elite, but it’s also what makes teams hesitate to commit significant guaranteed money.

The result? A player with top-15 safety talent sitting on the open market while lesser players have already been signed.

Top Landing Spots: Eagles, Steelers, and 49ers

Three teams stand out as the most logical fits for Brisker. Here’s how each stacks up:

Philadelphia Eagles

The Eagles may be the best schematic fit of any team. Starting safety Reed Blankenship signed with the Houston Texans, creating a clear opening. Defensive coordinator Vic Fangio runs a system that demands a physical, versatile safety — someone who can blitz, play in the box against the run, and drop into coverage. That’s Brisker’s exact skill set.

Eagles fans have been vocal on social media, urging general manager Howie Roseman to make the call. Philadelphia has the cap space and the need — the question is whether they’re willing to invest in a player with Brisker’s medical history.

Pittsburgh Steelers

The hometown angle is hard to ignore. Brisker grew up in Pittsburgh and is considered one of the best football players to come out of the region. The Steelers need a free safety to anchor the back end of their defense, especially with Jalen Ramsey expected to slide into a nickel role.

Spotrac projects it would take approximately a three-year, $30 million deal to bring Brisker home. For a franchise that prides itself on defense, pairing him with Minkah Fitzpatrick could give Pittsburgh one of the league’s most formidable safety tandems.

San Francisco 49ers

The 49ers have been mentioned as a potential suitor, but finances may be the obstacle. With significant needs at other positions and limited cap flexibility, San Francisco would likely need Brisker to accept a deal well below his projected $11 million annual market value. It’s possible, but only if no other team meets his asking price.

What This Means for the Safety Market

Brisker’s situation highlights a broader trend in the 2026 safety market. Teams are increasingly cautious about investing top dollar in the position, especially when injury risks are involved. The fact that Sports Illustrated listed him among the five best players still available in free agency speaks to both his talent and the league’s hesitancy.

For Brisker, the calculus is straightforward: take a prove-it deal with a contender and bet on himself, or wait for the right long-term commitment. At just 26 years old, he has time on his side — but the concussion history means every team’s medical staff will have the final say.

The Bottom Line

Jaquan Brisker is too talented to stay unsigned for long. A former All-Rookie selection who just posted career-best numbers across a full 17-game season, he represents the kind of impact safety that can transform a defense. The Eagles need him, the Steelers want the hometown story, and the 49ers are lurking if the price drops.

The only question remaining: which team is willing to look past the medical history and invest in one of the most dynamic safeties in football? If Brisker stays healthy, whoever signs him will have gotten one of the best bargains of the 2026 offseason.
