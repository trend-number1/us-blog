AFG vs IND: Essential Guide to the 3rd ODI Showdown

What if a so-called “dead rubber” was actually the most revealing match of the series? That is exactly the question hovering over the AFG vs IND third ODI in Chennai, where India can complete a clean sweep — and Afghanistan can finally land a punch on a side that has steamrolled them across two matches.

Quick Summary

  • India lead the three-match ODI series 2-0 after wins in Dharamsala and Lucknow.
  • The 3rd ODI is being played at the M. A. Chidambaram Stadium, Chennai on June 20, 2026.
  • Shubman Gill has scores of 84* and 154 so far; Gurnoor Brar has six wickets in two games.
  • Returning to the XI: Nitish Kumar Reddy, Prasidh Krishna, and Harshit Rana (back from a knee injury).
  • Afghanistan won the toss in the 3rd ODI and opted to bat first.

The matchup matters far beyond the scorecard. It is a live audition for India’s middle order ahead of a packed white-ball calendar, and a stress test for an Afghanistan side still searching for its first ODI win against a top-ranked opponent in this cycle.

How the AFG vs IND Series Has Unfolded

India’s dominance in this series has been almost surgical. In the 1st ODI at Dharamsala, a rain-hit game, India chased down a middling 195 with little fuss. In the 2nd ODI at Lucknow, the Men in Blue piled up 402 and won by a thumping 170 runs.

The numbers tell a brutal story for Afghanistan: two matches, two heavy defeats, and a captain in Shubman Gill who has looked impossible to dismiss.

  • 1st ODI (Dharamsala): India won, chasing 195 in a rain-affected game.
  • 2nd ODI (Lucknow): India 402; won by 170 runs.
  • 3rd ODI (Chennai): Afghanistan opted to bat first after winning the toss.

The pattern is clear — whenever the ball has stayed dry, India have controlled both ends. And that is before we get to the individual storylines that make this 3rd ODI so compelling.

If the first two matches were about India flexing, the next section is about the men driving that engine.

Shubman Gill: A Captain Quietly Building a Case

You could argue Shubman Gill is the most in-form ODI batter on the planet right now, and the AFG vs IND series has only sharpened the case.

  • 1st ODI: 84 not out in a chase.
  • 2nd ODI: 154, anchoring India’s 402.
  • Combined: a 224-run third-wicket stand with Ishan Kishan in Lucknow.

That partnership matters because it answered a quiet question about India’s middle order. Kishan, often debated as a top-order specialist, has slotted in at No. 4 with intent. The two stitched together one of the largest stands of the series, turning a strong start into a record total.

Why this is bigger than runs: Gill is captaining a side that is rotating heavily, and his scores keep removing pressure from a constantly changing XI. In a series where India’s selectors have used the matches as a workshop, Gill’s bat has been the one stable variable.

That stability is exactly what allows India to take the risks we will look at next.

The Returning Stars and India’s Selection Headache

India enter the 3rd ODI with a genuine selection headache — the good kind. According to ESPN’s match-day update, Nitish Kumar Reddy, Prasidh Krishna, and Akash Deep Dubey return for the Chennai clash.

Reddy had missed the previous match due to thigh soreness and has been declared fit. Harshit Rana is back in the squad after recovering from the knee injury he picked up in the build-up to the T20 World Cup 2026. Both inclusions deepen an already crowded all-rounder and pace pool.

Gurnoor Brar deserves a paragraph of his own. The newcomer “beanpole speedster” has taken three wickets in each of the first two ODIs, while Arshdeep Singh chipped in with two and three wickets across the two games. That is six wickets each from two bowlers in two matches — the kind of breakthrough form that turns a series into a coronation.

So how does Afghanistan respond? That is the real question of the day.

What Afghanistan Must Do — and the Misconceptions to Drop

There is a common misconception worth addressing: that Afghanistan are “punching above their weight” simply by sharing a field with India. That framing is outdated.

The Afghanistan cricket team has been a true story of underdogs who battled all odds to achieve their cricketing destiny, and they have earned full Test status alongside Ireland. They are no longer a novelty side — they are a Test-playing nation looking to break a specific psychological barrier against India in ODIs.

Three things have to change for Afghanistan in Chennai:

  1. Front-load wickets. India’s openers have not been threatened in the first two games. Afghanistan opting to bat first today is a tactical reset — put runs on the board and force India to chase under Chennai conditions.
  2. Slow Gill down. Spin in the middle overs has to bite. Gill has scored freely against pace; the off-spin and leg-spin options need to dictate tempo.
  3. Build partnerships of their own. India’s 224-run stand in Lucknow was the difference. Afghanistan have not produced a partnership remotely close to that in the series.

The toss decision suggests Afghanistan know exactly what they need to do. Whether they can execute on a Chennai surface is another matter entirely.

Which brings us to what this match actually means — for both teams, and for you as a viewer.

What This Means For You: How and Why to Watch

Even as a “dead rubber on paper,” the 3rd ODI is a crucial clash in terms of choices India still has to make ahead of bigger assignments. For fans, that means every selection is a story.

Where to watch (AFG vs IND 3rd ODI):

  • Multiple outlets — including NDTV Profit, Business Standard, The Indian Express, and India TV News — have published live streaming and TV broadcast details for the match.
  • The match is being played at the M. A. Chidambaram Stadium in Chennai.
  • Live commentary and ball-by-ball updates are running on India Today and India TV News.

Actionable viewing tips:

  • Watch the powerplay closely. That is where the first two ODIs were effectively decided.
  • Track Gurnoor Brar’s first spell. A third consecutive three-wicket haul would cement his case as a long-term selection.
  • Note who bats at No. 4 for India. That slot is the clearest signal of India’s middle-order plans.

A clean sweep would be India’s headline. A spirited Afghanistan total — or, better yet, a defendable one — would be the more interesting story.

A Final Question Worth Sitting With

If India do complete a 3-0 sweep, the headline writes itself. But the more interesting question is this: does Afghanistan exit this series closer to bridging the gap, or further away than when they arrived?

That answer will not be settled in Chennai alone. But the AFG vs IND 3rd ODI is where we get the clearest snapshot yet — and possibly the most honest one. Tune in, watch the small moments, and decide for yourself.
