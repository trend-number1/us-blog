Movies Dominating 2026: Super Mario Galaxy Crosses $250M and What's Next

April 12, 2026 18:03 Trend Insights

Have you checked the box office numbers lately? The Super Mario Galaxy Movie just became the first film of 2026 to cross the $250 million mark domestically — and it did it in just 10 days.

While Nintendo’s latest blockbuster dominates theaters, a wave of controversial biopics, surprising rom-coms, and stacked streaming lineups are reshaping what moviegoers are watching this spring. Here’s everything you need to know about the movies making headlines right now.

Key Takeaways:

  • The Super Mario Galaxy Movie crossed $250M domestically in its first 10 days, outgrossing multiple Harry Potter films
  • The upcoming Michael Jackson biopic is already generating significant controversy
  • Rom-com You, Me & Tuscany is projected to outperform Anyone But You’s opening weekend
  • The Mandalorian and Grogu has the shortest Star Wars movie runtime in 18 years
  • Streaming platforms are loaded with must-watch picks from Outcome to Thrash

Super Mario Galaxy Movie Shatters Box Office Records

Nintendo and Illumination have struck gold again. During its sophomore weekend, the Super Mario Galaxy Movie rocketed past a major milestone, becoming the first 2026 release to cross $250 million at the North American box office — accomplishing the feat in a mere 10 days.

To put that in perspective, the film has already outgrossed multiple Harry Potter movies in domestic earnings. The sequel builds on the massive success of 2023’s The Super Mario Bros. Movie, which itself earned over $574 million domestically.

Critics have been less enthusiastic than audiences, with reviews describing the film as “likely to sell toys and make kids happy, but a mess.” Still, the box office numbers tell a different story — audiences are showing up in droves regardless of critical reception.

The film was packed with beloved Nintendo characters that fans are already clamoring to see in their own spinoff films. Whether the franchise continues to expand remains to be seen, but the financial case is undeniable.

So what else is pulling audiences into theaters and onto their couches this April?

The Michael Jackson Movie Sparks Early Controversy

One of the most talked-about upcoming releases isn’t even in theaters yet. The Michael Jackson biopic is already generating intense debate, with USA Today reporting on why the film has become “extremely controversial.”

Details about the film’s first audience reactions are expected to drop online soon, adding fuel to an already heated conversation. Biopics of controversial public figures have always walked a tightrope between celebration and accountability, and this project appears to be no exception.

The film joins a growing list of music biopics that have dominated the box office in recent years, following the success of films about Freddie Mercury, Elton John, and Bob Marley. But the unique complexities surrounding Jackson’s legacy make this one of the most closely watched releases of the year.

Whether the controversy helps or hurts the film’s commercial prospects will be one of the biggest stories in entertainment over the coming months.

Rom-Coms Make a Comeback With You, Me & Tuscany

The romantic comedy genre continues its unlikely renaissance. The new film You, Me & Tuscany is projected to outperform the opening weekend of the 2023 smash hit Anyone But You, which earned over $220 million worldwide on a modest budget.

Despite mixed reviews from critics, audiences are responding enthusiastically. Viewers have been calling the film “charming and funny,” praising its standout cast and stunning Italian scenery.

But rom-coms aren’t the only genre seeing a revival right now.

Star Wars, Horror, and Franchise Updates

Several major franchise films are making waves this spring:

  • The Mandalorian and Grogu has officially revealed its runtime, making it the shortest Star Wars movie in 18 years. The Star Wars franchise has become increasingly divisive over the past decade — even Mark Hamill’s own children reportedly have split opinions on the original trilogy.

  • Blumhouse’s The Mummy continues breaking new ground in the monster franchise, setting a runtime record previously held by Brendan Fraser’s 1999 classic.

  • Faces of Death, a reboot of the banned original, features a killer inspired by the first film’s murders. The team behind the film purposely hid the killer’s motives and backstory to maximize suspense. The original Faces of Death was banned in multiple countries, and this reboot aims to be equally provocative.

  • A new Shrek installment is reportedly in development, adding to the nostalgia-driven sequel wave.

  • Universal is moving forward with another installment of a hit crime comedy franchise after a 10-year gap, with original stars potentially returning.

These franchise moves reflect Hollywood’s ongoing strategy of leveraging established IP, but the results vary widely in quality and audience reception.

  2. Thrash — A hurricane-gone-wrong thriller with sharks; the destruction “looks real”

For sci-fi fans, the genre continues to benefit from Steven Spielberg’s legacy. Multiple outlets have highlighted how the sci-fi blockbuster wouldn’t exist in its current form without Spielberg’s groundbreaking contributions to the genre.

What This Means for You

The 2026 movie landscape is defined by a few clear trends:

  • Video game adaptations are the new superhero movies. The Super Mario Galaxy Movie’s $250M haul in 10 days proves that gaming IP has become Hollywood’s most reliable cash cow. Expect more studios to greenlight game-based films.

  • Don’t trust critics alone. Films like You, Me & Tuscany and the Super Mario sequel show a widening gap between critical scores and audience enjoyment. Check audience reviews before skipping a film based on critic ratings.

  • Streaming is more competitive than ever. With every major platform releasing high-quality content simultaneously, the best strategy is to rotate free trials or focus on the platforms that match your taste.

  • Franchise fatigue is selective. Audiences aren’t tired of franchises — they’re tired of bad franchises. Strong entries like the Mario sequel continue to break records while weaker offerings struggle.

The rest of 2026 promises even more blockbuster showdowns, controversial releases, and streaming surprises. Whether you’re heading to the theater this weekend or queuing up your watchlist at home, there’s no shortage of compelling movies demanding your attention.

What’s the one movie you’re most excited about this year? Share your pick — and don’t sleep on The Christophers if you haven’t seen it yet.
