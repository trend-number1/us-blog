McNeese Basketball Returns to March Madness: Cowboys Eye Another 5-12 Upset

Key Takeaways:

  • 12-seed McNeese faces 5-seed Vanderbilt on March 19 in Oklahoma City
  • The Cowboys upset 5-seed Clemson in last year’s first round as a 12-seed
  • Viral student manager Amir “Aura” Khan and his boombox are back for another March run
  • This is a classic 5-12 matchup — historically one of the most upset-prone seeds in the tournament

What are the odds that lightning strikes twice in the same spot? McNeese basketball is betting on exactly that as the Cowboys return to the NCAA Tournament with the same script that made them America’s Cinderella last March.

McNeese vs. Vanderbilt: A Familiar 5-12 Script

The McNeese Cowboys are back in March Madness, and the setup feels almost eerily familiar. Once again seeded 12th, they face a 5-seed from a major conference — this time the Vanderbilt Commodores — in the first round of the 2026 NCAA Tournament.

The game tips off at 3:15 p.m. ET on March 19 in Oklahoma City, an early contest on a packed Day 1 of first-round action.

Last year, McNeese was in nearly the identical position. As a 12-seed, the Cowboys stunned 5-seed Clemson in the first round before bowing out in the second round. That upset catapulted a small program from Lake Charles, Louisiana, into the national spotlight — and created an internet sensation in the process.

The 5-12 matchup is historically the most upset-friendly line in the bracket. Since the tournament expanded to 64 teams, 12-seeds have one of the highest upset rates of any seed matchup. McNeese proved they belong in that conversation last March. Can they do it again?

The Return of Amir “Aura” Khan

You can’t tell the story of McNeese basketball without talking about Amir Khan, the student manager whose pregame routine became one of the defining images of last year’s tournament.

Khan earned the nickname “Aura” for a reason. Before games, he carries a boombox and walks alongside the McNeese players as they stretch, warm up, and get hyped. It’s part performance, part team ritual — and it’s completely genuine.

When McNeese knocked off Clemson last March, clips of Khan’s boombox routine flooded social media. The vibes were undeniable:

  • Viral clips spread across every major platform within hours of the Clemson upset
  • Khan was featured in CBS’s One Shining Moment video — the iconic post-tournament montage celebrating March Madness highlights
  • He landed NIL deals, including a partnership with Buffalo Wild Wings
  • His pregame energy became synonymous with McNeese’s underdog identity

“Social media is very much a vibes-based platform,” and Khan’s vibes met the moment perfectly last March. Now he’s back, and the internet is already buzzing about a potential sequel.

For a student manager — not a player — to become one of the faces of March Madness speaks to what makes the tournament special. It’s not always about the blue bloods. Sometimes it’s about a guy with a boombox and a team with nothing to lose.

Vanderbilt enters the tournament as the higher seed, but the Commodores have their own storylines. Guard Chandler Bing — yes, that’s his real name — used to watch Kevin Durant play in Oklahoma City as a kid. Now he’s making his own NCAA Tournament debut in that same city.

McNeese, led by head coach Will Wade (a Nashville native with deep ties to the region), brings tournament experience that can’t be understated. This team has been here before. They know what the bright lights feel like, and they’ve proven they can perform under pressure.

Why 12-Seeds Keep Pulling Upsets

The 5-12 game isn’t a fluke — it’s a feature of March Madness. Here’s why these upsets happen so consistently:

  1. Motivation gap — 12-seeds play with house money while 5-seeds feel pressure to advance
  2. Mid-major peak — Conference tournament champions from smaller leagues often hit their stride in March
  3. Style mismatch — Smaller programs frequently play at tempos that disrupt power-conference teams
  4. Experience factor — Teams like McNeese, with returning tournament players, carry a psychological edge

McNeese checks every one of those boxes. The Cowboys are riding a wave of confidence from last year’s run, their roster features players who’ve tasted March magic, and they have the kind of team chemistry — amplified by Khan’s energy — that makes them dangerous.

Some analysts, including ESPN’s Jay Bilas, have already weighed in on this matchup, with several national pundits giving McNeese a legitimate shot at the upset. The New York Times even ran a piece exploring whether McNeese could push past the first round and into the Sweet Sixteen this year.

What This Means for March Madness Fans

If you’re filling out a bracket, the McNeese-Vanderbilt game is the kind of contest you need to watch closely before locking in your pick. Here’s what to consider:

  • Don’t dismiss the 12-seed. McNeese isn’t just a feel-good story — they’re a program that has proven it can win in March.
  • Watch for the energy shift. When Khan hits the court with that boombox during warmups, it sets a tone. If McNeese feeds off that early, Vanderbilt could be in trouble.
  • Second-half resilience matters. Last year, McNeese showed they could close out a game against a power-conference opponent. That experience is invaluable.

For casual fans, this game has everything: an underdog narrative, a viral personality, and the always-electric atmosphere of Day 1 in March. Tune in at 3:15 p.m. ET and you might witness another McNeese moment that ends up on this year’s One Shining Moment reel.

The Bigger Picture: McNeese’s Rise

What’s happening at McNeese is bigger than one tournament game. The program is building something sustainable. Back-to-back NCAA Tournament appearances signal that this isn’t a one-hit wonder — it’s a program on the rise.

The Cowboys have turned the Southland Conference into a launching pad, and the national exposure from last year’s run — fueled by Khan’s viral fame — has done wonders for recruiting and visibility. Every March Madness moment is a marketing opportunity, and McNeese has capitalized better than almost any mid-major in recent memory.

Whether McNeese pulls off the upset against Vanderbilt or not, the Cowboys have already cemented their place in the March Madness conversation. But if they do win? Expect Amir “Aura” Khan and that boombox to break the internet all over again.

Will the Cowboys write another chapter in their Cinderella story, or will Vanderbilt’s talent prove too much? That’s the beauty of March — we won’t know until the final buzzer sounds in Oklahoma City.
