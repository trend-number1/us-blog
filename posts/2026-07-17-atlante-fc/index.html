Atlante FC Returns: The Historic Liga MX Comeback

July 17, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

Imagine winning championship after championship for over a decade, yet never being allowed to move up. That was the frustrating reality for Atlante FC — until now.

After 12 long years in Mexico’s second tier, Atlante FC is officially back in Liga MX for the Apertura 2026. One of the oldest and most storied clubs in Mexican soccer is opening a brand-new chapter, and fans across North America are paying attention.

Quick Summary: Atlante FC, founded in 1916, has returned to Liga MX after 12 years away. The historic “Potros” — three-time league champions and two-time Concacaf Champions’ Cup winners — secured their spot under legendary coach Miguel Herrera, and are already drawing crowds in international friendlies across the United States.

Let’s break down why this comeback matters and what it means for soccer fans on both sides of the border.

Why the Atlante FC Return Is a Big Deal

Atlante FC isn’t just any club. Founded on April 18, 1916, in the Colonia Roma neighborhood of Mexico City, it stands as one of the oldest clubs in the entire country. Its official identity celebrates “110 years of history” — a pedigree few clubs anywhere can match.

The team began life under a different name entirely. It was first called Sinaloa, then Lusitania, before finally settling on Atlante in 1921. The iconic blue-and-maroon (azul y grana) colors arrived to stay, worn by a club proud to challenge the establishment.

Over its long history, Atlante has assembled an impressive trophy cabinet:

  • Three Liga MX titles (1946–47, 1992–93, and Apertura 2007)
  • Two Concacaf Champions’ Cups
  • A fourth-place finish representing Mexico at the 2009 FIFA Club World Cup

That last achievement is remarkable. Reaching the semifinals of a global tournament against the best clubs on the planet is a feat most teams never experience.

So how did a club with this résumé end up stuck in the second division for over a decade? The answer reveals a lot about modern Mexican soccer.

The 12-Year Wait: A Broken Ladder

Atlante’s last top-flight campaign was Clausura 2014, when it was relegated after finishing with one of the league’s worst relegation coefficients. From there, the club dropped into what was then Liga de Ascenso, later rebranded as Liga de Expansión MX.

Here’s the twist that makes their story so unusual.

Atlante dominated the second tier — winning multiple championships and consistently competing at the top of the table. But there was a catch: promotion and relegation had been suspended. All those trophies came with no reward. The “elevator” back to Liga MX simply didn’t exist anymore.

This is a common misconception worth clearing up. Many fans assume relegation systems are universal and automatic. In reality, Liga MX froze promotion for years, meaning even the best second-division club couldn’t earn its way up on the field.

So how did Atlante finally break through? Not by playing — but by purchasing.

How Atlante Bought Its Way Back

Rather than earning promotion on the pitch, Atlante secured its return by acquiring Mazatlán FC’s Liga MX membership certificate. In modern Mexican soccer, a top-flight “spot” is effectively a tradable asset, and Atlante used that mechanism to reclaim its place.

The club also brought in serious leadership for the new era. Atlante is now guided by legendary coach Miguel Herrera, a well-known figure in Mexican soccer with deep experience at both club and national-team levels.

The relocation history adds another layer. Atlante built deep roots in Mexico City before moving first to Querétaro and later to Cancún — a club that has literally traveled the country.

With the membership secured and a big-name coach in place, the next question is what fans will actually see on the field. And that story is already unfolding — in the United States.

Atlante FC in the U.S.: Friendlies and Leagues Cup

For American soccer fans, Atlante’s return is more than a Mexican headline. The club has been active across the U.S. in the lead-up to the season.

In early July 2026, Atlante played New Mexico United in an international friendly that ended in an entertaining 3-3 draw, with United’s Starr earning praise across local coverage. The match drew attention from outlets including KRQE and the Albuquerque Journal.

Atlante has also been confirmed for the Leagues Cup 2026 field, the cross-border competition featuring Liga MX and MLS clubs. That means more chances for U.S.-based fans to catch the historic Potros in person.

Recent competitive results show a team finding its rhythm. In a July 16 clash, Necaxa and Atlante drew 1-1, with Necaxa advancing on penalties (4-2) — a tight contest that underscored Atlante’s competitiveness against established Liga MX opposition.

Upcoming fixtures include a matchup against Real Salt Lake at America First Field, giving West Coast and Mountain-region fans another opportunity to watch.

What This Means for You

If you’re a soccer fan in the United States, Atlante’s comeback is a gift. You now have another historic, tradition-rich Liga MX club playing friendlies and Leagues Cup matches on American soil — often at accessible venues.

Here’s actionable advice:

  1. Check Leagues Cup 2026 schedules for Atlante fixtures near you.
  2. Follow the club’s official channels for ticket presales and match updates.
  3. Watch the Necaxa and Real Salt Lake matchups to gauge how the squad stacks up early in the season.

For neutral fans, Atlante offers a compelling underdog narrative: a 110-year-old institution reclaiming its place at the top after more than a decade in limbo.

The bigger story here is about resilience. Atlante spent 12 years winning without reward, then found a path back through the business side of the sport. Whether they can add a fourth Liga MX title to their legacy remains to be seen.

So here’s the question worth pondering: In an era where a top-flight spot can be bought rather than earned, does Atlante’s return represent the triumph of a historic club — or a sign of how much modern soccer has changed?

Either way, the Potros are back. And the next chapter starts now.
