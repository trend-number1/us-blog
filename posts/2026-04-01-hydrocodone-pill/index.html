Hydrocodone Pills Found on Tiger Woods: What the Arrest Affidavit Reveals

April 1, 2026 00:03 Trend Insights

Did you know that hydrocodone is one of the most commonly prescribed — and most commonly abused — opioids in the United States? The drug is back in the national spotlight after newly released court documents revealed that two hydrocodone pills were found in Tiger Woods’ pocket during his DUI arrest last week in Martin County, Florida.

Key Takeaways

  • A probable cause affidavit released on March 31 confirms deputies found two white hydrocodone pills on Tiger Woods after a rollover crash on March 27.
  • Woods told deputies he takes “a few” prescription medications and had taken them that morning.
  • He passed a breathalyzer test but failed field sobriety exercises and refused a urine test.
  • Woods faces misdemeanor charges for DUI, property damage, and refusal to submit to a lawful test.
  • This marks his second DUI incident, following a 2017 arrest also linked to prescription drugs.

What the Hydrocodone Pill Affidavit Actually Says

The Martin County Sheriff’s Office released a probable cause affidavit on Tuesday, March 31, painting a detailed picture of what happened on South Beach Road near Jupiter Island on the afternoon of March 27.

According to the document, Tiger Woods, 50, was driving a black Land Rover northbound behind a white Ford F-150 towing a pressure cleaner trailer. When the truck slowed and signaled a right turn, Woods crossed over double solid lines to pass. He clipped the rear of the trailer, causing his SUV to flip onto its driver’s side.

After the crash, the truck driver helped Woods out of his vehicle. Deputies noted that Woods appeared lethargic and moved slowly but was described as “extremely alert” during the investigation.

A search following his arrest turned up two white pills in his left pants pocket, identified by their imprint as hydrocodone — a prescription opioid used to treat severe, chronic pain. When asked about prescription medication use, Woods told the deputy, “I take a few.”

So what else did investigators observe at the scene?

Signs of Impairment: Bloodshot Eyes, Sweating, and Failed Sobriety Tests

While Woods passed a breathalyzer test — confirming he had not been drinking alcohol — multiple signs of impairment were documented in the affidavit:

  • Bloodshot, glassy eyes with extremely dilated pupils
  • Profuse sweating throughout the encounter
  • Hiccups that persisted during the investigation
  • Failed several field sobriety exercises
  • Difficulty with coordination tests, which Woods attributed to multiple back and knee surgeries

Woods also admitted to being distracted by both his phone and the radio when the crash occurred, according to reporting from TMZ Sports. He told deputies he did not notice the vehicle ahead of him had slowed down.

Under a recently strengthened Florida law, Woods was asked to submit to a urine test but refused. That refusal now carries its own misdemeanor charge. In total, he faces three misdemeanor counts: driving under the influence, property damage, and refusal to submit to a lawful test.

Neither Woods nor the other driver reported injuries at the scene — but the legal and reputational damage may be far more lasting.

Tiger Woods’ Troubling History Behind the Wheel

This is not the first time Tiger Woods has made headlines for an incident involving a vehicle and prescription drugs. Here’s a timeline of his most significant incidents:

The 2017 arrest was a turning point that led to public scrutiny of Woods’ use of prescription painkillers. At the time, he blamed the episode on an unexpected reaction to prescribed medications. Now, nearly a decade later, the parallels are striking.

The 82-time PGA Tour winner was in the midst of yet another comeback attempt after years of injuries and car accidents — a comeback that now faces its most serious off-course obstacle yet.

Hydrocodone in America: A Broader Crisis

The Tiger Woods case puts a human face on a much larger issue. Hydrocodone remains one of the most widely prescribed opioids in the country, and its misuse continues to fuel concern among public health officials and law enforcement.

Consider these recent developments:

DEA supply adjustments for 2026. The Drug Enforcement Administration announced in January that it would make small reductions in hydrocodone production quotas this year, while cutting oxycodone supply by over 6%. The agency received more than 5,000 public comments opposing further opioid supply cuts, with many citing existing shortages at pharmacies and hospitals.

Pharmacy theft on the rise. In January 2026, suspects smashed through the front glass of Belmont Pharmacy in Mississippi and escaped with an estimated 5,000 hydrocodone pills in under four minutes. Two individuals entered the store while a third waited in a getaway vehicle. The case remains under active investigation.

These stories underscore a painful contradiction: legitimate patients struggle to access medications they need, while the same drugs continue to be diverted and misused.

What This Means for You

Whether you follow golf, follow the opioid crisis, or simply saw the headlines, this story carries lessons worth considering:

  • Prescription medications can impair driving just like alcohol. Hydrocodone is a Schedule II controlled substance. Operating a vehicle while under its influence is illegal, regardless of whether you have a valid prescription.
  • Florida’s DUI laws have gotten stricter. Refusing a chemical test now carries its own misdemeanor charge — a provision that directly applies to the Woods case.
  • Celebrity cases raise awareness but rarely change policy. Woods’ 2017 DUI prompted a wave of coverage about prescription drug impairment. The fact that a nearly identical incident has occurred suggests awareness alone isn’t enough.

If you or someone you know takes prescription opioids, the safest approach is simple: never drive after taking them unless your physician has specifically confirmed it is safe to do so.

What Comes Next for Tiger Woods

Tiger Woods’ legal team has not yet issued a detailed public statement regarding the affidavit’s findings. The misdemeanor charges he faces could carry penalties including fines, license suspension, probation, or even jail time — particularly given his prior 2017 DUI.

For the golf world, the timing couldn’t be worse. Woods was attempting yet another competitive return, and the 2026 Masters at Augusta National is just days away. Whether he will compete — or whether sponsors and the PGA Tour will respond — remains an open question.

One thing is clear: the intersection of prescription opioids, chronic pain management, and public safety is a conversation that isn’t going away. If anything, this latest chapter in Tiger Woods’ turbulent story only makes it more urgent.
