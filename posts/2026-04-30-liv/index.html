LIV Golf's Stunning Collapse: Saudi Funding Pulled After 2026

April 30, 2026 06:03 Trend Insights

What if the most disruptive force in professional golf history vanished in a single news cycle? That’s exactly what happened on April 29, 2026, when multiple outlets confirmed Saudi Arabia is officially pulling the plug on LIV Golf after this season.

Quick Summary: Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund (PIF) will end LIV Golf funding after the 2026 season, according to reports from the Wall Street Journal, Reuters, Bloomberg, and Forbes. The June Louisiana event has already been postponed, and players like Phil Mickelson reportedly won’t be welcomed back to the PGA Tour.

The announcement caps a whirlwind month that began with cancelled events, escalated with course-condition complaints, and ended with the effective dismantling of the PGA Tour’s most aggressive rival in decades.

The News That Shook Pro Golf

On Wednesday, April 29, 2026, the Wall Street Journal reported that the Saudi Public Investment Fund will withdraw its backing of LIV Golf after the current season concludes. Reuters, Bloomberg, Forbes, and the New York Post all confirmed the reporting within hours.

Golfweek went a step further, reporting that LIV Golf will officially announce the end of Saudi funding. The league had been bankrolled by PIF since its 2022 launch — a financial firehose that allowed it to lure stars like Bryson DeChambeau, Jon Rahm, and Cameron Smith with reportedly nine-figure contracts.

Just weeks earlier, LIV Golf CEO Scott O’Neil had pushed back hard against this exact narrative. At the Masters, O’Neil told reporters the circuit was “fully funded at least through this year” and that the season would continue “exactly as planned, uninterrupted and at full throttle.”

That confidence didn’t hold for long.

The Louisiana Cancellation Was the First Crack

Days before the funding bombshell, WDSU New Orleans reported that the LIV Golf Louisiana event scheduled for June 2026 — set to debut at City Park — would be postponed. Sources told the station that the state of Louisiana is postponing the event until LIV can restructure financially and find additional sources of funding.

The BBC confirmed the postponement on April 28, 2026. LIV publicly said it looks forward to continuing its “strong momentum” despite the cancellation, but the optics were brutal: a Saudi-backed event being held back by a U.S. state over financial concerns.

Did this US state just take a quiet shot at LIV Golf? Today’s Golfer asked exactly that question — and the answer, increasingly, looked like yes.

Why Saudi Arabia Is Pulling the Plug

LIV Golf’s value proposition was always financial muscle. PIF spent billions building rosters, paying appearance fees, and propping up team franchises. So why walk away now?

While the published reports don’t disclose a single internal reason, the public timeline tells a story:

  • Cancelled and questioned events: The Louisiana postponement followed Bryson DeChambeau publicly calling out the Mexico City course conditions in mid-April, which Fox News tied directly to “funding concerns.”
  • Player departures: Brooks Koepka returned to the PGA Tour ahead of the 2026 season. Patrick Reed exited unexpectedly. Pat Perez, Hudson Swafford, and Kevin Na were quietly reinstated to the PGA Tour.
  • Roster instability: Phil Mickelson missed LIV’s first two 2026 events citing a family health matter, and Lee Westwood was sidelined with a wrist injury.

The Athletic also reported that DeChambeau used his time at the Masters to discuss a possible return to the PGA Tour — an extraordinary signal from one of LIV’s biggest stars.

The Player Fallout: No Easy Way Back

If LIV does shut down, where do its stars go? For some, the door appears closed.

Yahoo Sports reported on April 29, 2026 that the PGA Tour won’t welcome back Phil Mickelson if LIV Golf shuts down. That’s a striking line in the sand for a six-time major champion who became the face of the breakaway league.

DeChambeau, by contrast, addressed the speculation differently, pledging: “As long as LIV is here, I would figure out a” way to stay — though his Masters-week conversations suggest he’s preparing for either outcome.

The bigger insight here: the financial leverage that built LIV is the same leverage that will determine where its stars land. Without PIF money, contracts that locked players in suddenly look very different.

What’s Still Happening This Season

Despite the looming shutdown, LIV is treating 2026 as a normal year — at least publicly. The league announced a new broadcast partnership with Sony Pictures Networks India for the 2026 season on April 29, the same day the funding news broke. The team formerly known as Smash GC rebranded as OKGC, formally aligning with Oklahoma as its home market — the first U.S.-aligned franchise in LIV’s history.

The 2026 season opened in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, with new faces including reigning NCAA champion Michael La Sasso, who made his first LIV media appearance there. Jon Rahm has already won LIV Golf Mexico City with a closing 7-under 64.

So the on-course product continues. The off-course question is whether anything beyond 2026 actually exists.

What This Means for You as a Fan

Three practical takeaways if you follow professional golf:

  1. Watch the contract clauses, not the press releases. LIV’s public messaging and the reported funding decision are now diverging openly. Court filings and player movements will tell the real story.
  2. Expect a reshuffled major-championship landscape. If top LIV players migrate back to the PGA Tour or DP World Tour, world rankings, FedEx Cup standings, and major qualifying will all shift quickly.
  3. Don’t count Phil Mickelson out — but don’t count on him at the PGA Tour either. The Yahoo Sports report suggests his path forward may be neither tour as we know it.

A common misconception worth correcting: the PGA Tour and PIF “merger” announced in 2023 was a framework agreement, not a completed deal. It was never fully consummated, and the funding withdrawal effectively ends the most realistic version of that scenario.

The Bigger Picture: A Three-Year Disruption

LIV launched in June 2022. If it folds after the 2026 season, that’s roughly four years of operation — enough to permanently reshape player contracts, broadcast deals, and tour governance, but not enough to establish itself as a lasting institution.

Compare that to the PGA Tour, which has operated continuously since 1929. The lesson? Capital can buy disruption, but only sustained competitive viability can buy permanence.

For Saudi Arabia, the calculus appears to have shifted from sports-washing through golf to redirecting resources elsewhere. For players, the bill is coming due. For fans, the next eight months of LIV competition may be the last eight months of LIV competition.

The Question Worth Asking

If LIV Golf does shut down at the end of 2026, will professional golf return to the pre-2022 status quo — or has the disruption permanently changed how players are paid, where they play, and who controls the sport?

The answer probably isn’t “back to normal.” Player compensation has already ratcheted up across the PGA Tour in response to LIV’s pressure. Signature events, elevated purses, and equity-style player ownership models all trace back to this rivalry.

So even if the league disappears, its fingerprints won’t.

What do you think — should the PGA Tour welcome back LIV defectors, or close the door for good? The next chapter of professional golf may depend on how that question gets answered.
