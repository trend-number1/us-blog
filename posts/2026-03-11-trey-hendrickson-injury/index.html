Trey Hendrickson Injury Update: How a Core Muscle Surgery Is Reshaping NFL Free Agency

March 11, 2026 22:31 Trend Insights

The most dominant pass rusher of the last three NFL seasons is sitting at home without a team, and a single surgery is the reason why.

Trey Hendrickson, the four-time Pro Bowl defensive end, entered 2026 free agency expecting a bidding war. Instead, he got silence, lowball offers, and a gap between his asking price and reality that could be as wide as $10 million per year. The culprit? A core muscle injury that cut his 2025 season to just seven games and forced him under the knife in December.

Key Takeaways

  • Hendrickson played only 7 games in 2025 before season-ending core muscle surgery
  • He is seeking a deal averaging $30M+ per year, but teams are offering significantly less
  • At least 6 NFL teams have expressed interest, including the Eagles, Cowboys, Bears, and Bills
  • A decision is expected by mid-March, with the Colts emerging as a dark-horse favorite
  • Despite the injury, he is projected to be fully healthy for the 2026 season

Trey Hendrickson’s Injury: What Happened and Why It Matters

Hendrickson’s 2025 campaign started like every other season in his prime. Through his first several games with the Cincinnati Bengals, he was wreaking havoc on opposing offensive lines, continuing the form that earned him Pro Bowl nods in four consecutive seasons.

Then the core muscle issue appeared.

Core muscle injuries, sometimes called “sports hernias,” affect the lower abdominal wall and the muscles that connect to the pelvis. For a pass rusher whose entire game depends on explosive hip rotation and lower-body torque, this type of injury is especially devastating.

Hendrickson tried to play through it, but by early December, the pain was too much. He underwent surgery on December 10, 2025, officially ending his season at just seven games – his lowest total since 2018, when he was a backup with the New Orleans Saints.

The good news? Medical reports indicate he is expected to be fully ready for the 2026 season. But in the world of NFL free agency, “expected” is not the same as “guaranteed.”

So how much is this uncertainty costing him? The answer might shock you.

The $10 Million Gap: Why Hendrickson’s Market Has Stalled

Here is where things get fascinating. Hendrickson and his agents believe he belongs in elite company. Currently, eight NFL pass rushers have contracts averaging $30 million or more per year. Hendrickson’s camp believes his production absolutely warrants a seat at that table.

The numbers back them up – at least on paper. Hendrickson has compiled 81 career sacks over 117 games, including a monster 2024 season where he led the NFL with 17.5 sacks and won the Deacon Jones Award. He has earned four consecutive Pro Bowl selections and racked up 161 solo tackles along the way.

Those are elite numbers by any measure. But teams are reportedly offering deals well below his asking price – creating a gap that could be as high as $10 million per year between what Hendrickson wants and what teams are willing to pay.

Why the disconnect? Three factors are converging against him:

  1. Age: Hendrickson turned 31 in December. Edge rushers historically decline sharply after 32.
  2. Injury risk: Core muscle surgery requires significant recovery time, and there are lingering concerns about durability in his first full season back.
  3. Guaranteed money: Hendrickson reportedly wants guarantees extending past the first year, which teams are reluctant to provide given factors one and two.

The Bengals declined to use their franchise tag on him, which would have paid him approximately $24 million for one season. That decision sent a clear message: even Cincinnati, the team that knows him best, was not willing to commit that kind of guaranteed money.

But here is the twist that changes everything.

Which Teams Are in the Hendrickson Sweepstakes?

Despite the concerns, Hendrickson remains one of the most talented pass rushers available. At least six NFL franchises have been linked to the veteran:

  • Philadelphia Eagles – Defending champions looking to bolster an already fearsome defensive line
  • Dallas Cowboys – Desperate for pass-rush help after a disappointing 2025
  • Chicago Bears – Young team with significant cap space ready to add veteran talent
  • Buffalo Bills – Perennial contenders seeking the missing piece on their defensive front
  • New England Patriots – Rebuilding but willing to spend on proven talent
  • Los Angeles Rams – Need a replacement after losing key defensive pieces

However, the most intriguing prediction comes from ESPN’s Dan Graziano, who projects Hendrickson landing with the Indianapolis Colts on a three-year, $84 million deal with $42 million guaranteed. That works out to $28 million per year – below his asking price but above what most teams have offered.

The Colts make sense for several reasons. They have significant cap space, a young quarterback in Anthony Richardson who needs defensive support, and a scheme that could maximize Hendrickson’s skill set while managing his workload.

But here is the detail most people are missing about this situation.

The Hidden Factor: Pass Rusher Market Inflation

What makes Hendrickson’s situation truly unprecedented is the broader context of the pass-rusher market. Over the past two years, top edge rusher contracts have escalated dramatically, with eight players now earning $30 million or more per year. The market has inflated significantly, pushing even mid-tier deals well above what would have been considered elite money just a couple of seasons ago.

This inflation is what gives Hendrickson’s camp confidence. Even at 31, even coming off surgery, the market itself is pushing values upward. A deal that looked expensive two years ago now looks like a bargain.

Consider this comparison:

Hendrickson’s projected deal sits comfortably between the premium tier and the discount bin. The real question is not whether he will get paid – it is whether he will get paid what he believes he deserves.

What This Means for NFL Fans and Fantasy Players

If you are a fan of one of the six teams in the Hendrickson sweepstakes, here is what you should know:

For Eagles/Bills/Rams fans: Adding Hendrickson would give your team a proven pass rusher who can immediately contribute 10+ sacks in a full healthy season. The injury risk is real but manageable with proper rotation.

For fantasy football players: Hendrickson’s landing spot will dramatically affect his value. A team with a strong secondary (like Philadelphia) would give him more one-on-one matchups and boost his sack numbers. A team that asks him to do everything (like Chicago) might wear him down.

For Bengals fans: Losing Hendrickson is painful, but the franchise tag decision suggests Cincinnati has a plan – likely redirecting funds toward keeping their offensive weapons around Joe Burrow.

The bottom line is this: Trey Hendrickson’s core muscle injury has created the most fascinating negotiation standoff of the 2026 NFL offseason. A decision is reportedly expected by mid-March, and when it comes, it will set the market for pass rushers for years to come.

The question every general manager is asking themselves right now: Is the risk of paying a 31-year-old coming off surgery worth the reward of adding a four-time Pro Bowler?

What would you do if you were running an NFL front office – take the gamble on Hendrickson, or play it safe?
