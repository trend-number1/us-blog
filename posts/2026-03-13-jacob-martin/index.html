Jacob Martin Signs with Titans: $11M Deal Reunites Edge Rusher with Robert Saleh

March 13, 2026 00:38 Trend Insights

Jacob Martin Cashes In: Titans Land Edge Rusher on Two-Year Deal

The Tennessee Titans continue their aggressive 2026 free agency spending spree. On March 12, the team agreed to sign outside linebacker Jacob Martin to a two-year deal worth up to $11 million, according to NFL insider Jordan Schultz.

The signing reunites Martin with Titans head coach Robert Saleh, who previously coached him during their time together with the New York Jets in 2022.

Key Takeaways:

  • Jacob Martin signs a 2-year, $11M deal with Tennessee
  • Career-high 5.5 sacks and 18 QB hits with Washington in 2025
  • Martin becomes the fourth ex-Saleh-era Jets D-lineman on the Titans
  • Titans paying well above his projected $2.8M annual market value
  • Martin will be the eighth NFL team in his career

From Career Year in Washington to Nashville

Martin’s 2025 season with the Washington Commanders was the best of his career. Playing on a one-year, $2.585 million contract, the 30-year-old edge rusher delivered across the board:

Stat2025 Season (Career High)
Games played17 (all games)
Starts14
Tackles39
Sacks5.5
QB hits18
Forced fumbles1

Every one of those numbers represented a career high for Martin. Washington used him as a starter in front of all-time great Von Miller, who despite playing behind Martin in the rotation, still led the team with nine sacks.

The Commanders have since moved on, signing Odafe Oweh and K’Lavon Chaisson to bolster their front while Miller remains a free agent.

So where does Martin fit in Nashville? The answer has everything to do with Saleh’s defensive rebuild.

Saleh’s Jets Reunion in Tennessee

Martin’s signing extends a fascinating pattern. He is now the fourth former Saleh-era Jets defensive lineman to land in Tennessee:

  • Jermaine Johnson — acquired via trade (Titans sent T’Vondre Sweat)
  • John Franklin-Myers — signed a 3-year, $63 million deal
  • Solomon Thomas — acquired in a low-level pick-swap trade
  • Jacob Martin — signed to a 2-year, $11 million contract

All four were teammates on the 2022 Jets. Martin’s time in New York was brief — the team traded him to the Denver Broncos at the 2022 deadline when Denver needed edge depth after its Bradley Chubb trade.

The Titans also hired Aaron Whitecotton as their defensive line coach — another Jets connection. Saleh has made clear his desire to completely overhaul Tennessee’s defense, and he’s doing it with players he knows and trusts.

A Journeyman Who Finally Found His Groove

Martin’s NFL journey reads like a road map of the league. Originally selected in the sixth round of the 2018 draft by the Seattle Seahawks, he has bounced through an extraordinary number of stops:

  1. Seattle Seahawks (2018)
  2. Houston Texans (2019–2021)
  3. New York Jets (2022)
  4. Denver Broncos (2022)
  5. Indianapolis Colts (2023)
  6. Chicago Bears (2024)
  7. Washington Commanders (2025)
  8. Tennessee Titans (2026)

The Titans are Martin’s eighth NFL team — a remarkable number for any player, let alone one coming off a career year. He’s not just a defensive contributor either; over the last four seasons, Martin has played almost as many special teams snaps as defensive ones, making him a versatile roster piece.

What’s striking about the contract is how much it exceeds expectations. Martin’s projected market value was approximately $2.8 million per year, according to analysts. The Titans are paying him nearly double that, signaling genuine confidence in his ability to replicate or exceed his 2025 production.

What Martin Means for Tennessee’s Defense

With the Titans having entered free agency with league-leading cap space, they’ve been the most active team on the market. Martin slots into a defensive front alongside Johnson, rising linebacker Cedric Gray, and the newly signed Franklin-Myers.

Here’s what makes the signing smart beyond the numbers:

  • Immediate starter — Martin projects into a heavy role from day one
  • Proven in Saleh’s scheme — familiarity with the 4-3 defensive system
  • Salary-friendly — at $5.5M per year, he leaves room for additional moves
  • Durability — played all 17 games in 2025 with 14 starts
  • Versatility — contributes on both defense and special teams

For a team that needed edge rushing desperately, finding a player with Martin’s recent production at this price point represents a significant win.

The Bigger Picture: Titans’ Free Agency Strategy

Tennessee’s approach this offseason has been unmistakable — spend aggressively, build around players who understand the system, and create a defensive identity from scratch. Martin’s signing is another box checked in that process.

The Titans aren’t just collecting talent. They’re rebuilding institutional knowledge by bringing in players who already speak Saleh’s defensive language. That shortcut in communication and scheme comprehension could pay dividends early in the 2026 season when chemistry typically takes time to develop.

Can a defense built almost entirely from free agency and trades gel quickly enough to compete? The Titans are betting $11 million on Martin that the answer is yes.
