LJ Cryer's Surprising Rise: How an Undrafted Guard Is Making Waves with the Warriors

April 10, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

What happens when an undrafted guard from the Houston Cougars lands on one of the NBA’s most storied franchises — and actually starts making noise? That’s exactly the story of LJ Cryer, who has quietly carved out a meaningful role with the Golden State Warriors during the 2025–26 season.

Key Takeaways

  • LJ Cryer went undrafted in the 2025 NBA Draft but signed with the Golden State Warriors
  • He was waived during the preseason, then brought back and has since earned consistent minutes
  • Cryer returned to action on April 10 after missing two games due to illness
  • With Stephen Curry sidelined by a knee injury, Cryer’s role has grown significantly
  • He currently plays on a two-way contract, making him ineligible for the play-in tournament unless converted to a standard deal

LJ Cryer’s Path from Undrafted to the NBA

Not hearing your name called on draft night is a gut punch for any basketball player. For LJ Cryer, a standout guard at the Houston Cougars, the 2025 NBA Draft came and went without a selection. But that didn’t stop him.

Cryer signed with the Golden State Warriors during the offseason, joining a franchise known for developing talent and trusting young players with real minutes. His journey, however, wasn’t a straight line. The Warriors waived Cryer during the preseason as part of a broader roster shakeup that also saw veteran sharpshooter Seth Curry released.

That move was described as a strategic roster adjustment — Golden State’s front office was looking to optimize cap space and maintain flexibility while balancing championship aspirations with developmental priorities. Cryer’s athleticism and defensive potential were noted, but he had limited chances to showcase his skills before being let go.

Then came the callback. The Warriors made another roster tweak, releasing Jackson Rowe and clearing the way to convert a two-way slot. Cryer was brought back, and this time he seized the opportunity.

Stepping Up While Curry Is Sidelined

The timing of Cryer’s emergence couldn’t be more critical. With Stephen Curry sidelined due to a knee injury, the Warriors have needed backcourt production from unexpected sources. Cryer has been one of several young players answering the call.

According to reports, the Warriors have gotten significant contributions from LJ Cryer, Nate Williams, Malevy Leons, and Omer Yurtseven in recent weeks. It’s a testament to the organization’s depth and willingness to trust players who might not have been in the rotation under normal circumstances.

Cryer’s recent highlights tell the story:

  • April 2: Nailed a three-pointer from behind the arc in a game against the Cleveland Cavaliers — a contest that featured a chaotic fourth quarter
  • April 8: Fought off a defender for an impressive and-1 finish, showcasing his toughness and ability to finish through contact

These aren’t just garbage-time minutes. Cryer is contributing in competitive, meaningful moments for a Warriors team still fighting for postseason positioning.

The Illness Scare and His Return

Just as Cryer was building momentum, an illness knocked him out of commission. He was ruled out of Tuesday’s game on April 8 and listed as day-to-day ahead of the April 9 matchup against the Lakers.

The good news came quickly. By Wednesday evening, Cryer was listed as probable for Thursday’s game, and on April 10, it was confirmed: Cryer will play Thursday against the Lakers.

His return is especially important given Curry’s ongoing absence. The Warriors need every capable guard they can get, and Cryer’s two-game absence — while brief — highlighted just how much the team has come to rely on his contributions off the bench.

The Two-Way Contract Dilemma

Here’s where things get complicated — and potentially consequential. Cryer is currently on a two-way contract with the Warriors. Under NBA rules, players on two-way deals are not eligible for the play-in tournament unless their contract is converted to a standard deal.

This creates a genuine decision point for Golden State. If the Warriors believe Cryer can contribute in the postseason — and his recent play suggests he can — they’ll need to act before the play-in begins. Converting his contract would mean a deeper financial commitment but would keep a proven contributor available when it matters most.

It’s worth noting that Nate Williams, Malevy Leons, and Omer Yurtseven face the same eligibility question. The Warriors may have to make tough choices about which two-way players earn that conversion.

What This Means for the Warriors — and for You

LJ Cryer’s story is one of the most compelling underdog narratives in the NBA right now. An undrafted player, waived during the preseason, who fought his way back and is now a rotation piece for the Golden State Warriors while a future Hall of Famer sits out with an injury.

For fantasy basketball managers, Cryer is worth monitoring closely. His value spikes whenever Curry is out, and his ability to score from three-point range and finish through contact makes him a viable streaming option in deeper leagues.

For Warriors fans, the question is straightforward: has Cryer done enough to earn a standard contract and a spot in the play-in tournament? The next few games will likely determine that answer.

And for anyone who loves basketball, Cryer’s journey is a reminder that the path to the NBA isn’t always a straight line. Sometimes the players who make the biggest impact are the ones who had to fight hardest just to be on the roster.

Will the Warriors convert LJ Cryer’s two-way contract before the play-in tournament begins? With Curry’s status uncertain and Cryer’s recent contributions speaking for themselves, it might be a question Golden State can’t afford to leave unanswered.
