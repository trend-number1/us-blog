How Powerball Actually Works (And Common Misconceptions)

Plenty of casual players misunderstand the game’s basic mechanics. Let’s clear things up.

The rules in plain English

  • Pick five numbers from 1 to 69 for the white balls
  • Pick one number from 1 to 26 for the red Powerball
  • Tickets cost $2 per play ($3 in Idaho and Montana, where Power Play is bundled in)
  • Add the Power Play multiplier for an extra $1 to boost non-jackpot prizes by 2X, 3X, 4X, 5X, or 10X
  • Drawings happen three times a week: Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday at 10:59 p.m. ET

The jackpot is won by matching all five white balls in any order plus the red Powerball. There are 9 total ways to win a prize, not just one.

Misconception #1: “You have to be a U.S. citizen to play.”

False. You do not need to be a U.S. citizen or resident to play Powerball. Any visitor to a participating jurisdiction can buy a ticket from an authorized retailer, as long as they meet the legal age requirement — typically 18 years old.

Misconception #2: “Powerball is sold everywhere in the U.S.”

Also false. Powerball is played in 45 states, Washington D.C., Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. Five states do not participate: Alabama, Alaska, Hawaii, Nevada, and Utah.

Misconception #3: “The 10X multiplier is always available.”

Not quite. The 10X multiplier is only in play when the advertised jackpot annuity is $150 million or less. Once jackpots climb higher, that top multiplier drops out of the pool — something even regular players often miss.

Knowing the mechanics is one thing. Knowing what to do if your numbers actually match is another.

What This Means For You

Let’s get practical. Most ticket holders lose — that’s math. But millions of non-jackpot winners still collect prizes every drawing. The lotto.net reporting shows 381,457 winners in a recent draw alone, across all nine prize tiers.

Here’s how to handle your ticket smartly:

  1. Check the numbers within 24 hours. Prizes go unclaimed every year because players forget.
  2. Sign the back of your ticket immediately. It’s a bearer instrument — whoever signs it claims it.
  3. Don’t post a photo of a winning ticket online. The serial numbers can be read and disputed.
  4. If you hit the jackpot, choose carefully. Winners can take an annuity paid in 30 graduated payments over 29 years, or a lump-sum cash payment. Both are advertised before federal and jurisdictional taxes.
  5. Consult a financial advisor and lawyer before claiming large prizes. Rules on anonymity, taxes, and trusts vary significantly by state.

For small wins, most states let you redeem at the retailer up to a limit (often $600). Anything larger usually requires a trip to a regional lottery office.

When Is the Next Powerball Drawing?

The next Powerball drawing is Saturday, April 25, 2026, at 10:59 p.m. ET. Sales cutoffs vary by state — for example, New York cuts off at 10 p.m., New Jersey at 9:45 p.m., and Connecticut at 10 p.m.

A few smart tips for the next draw:

  • Set a budget before you buy. Lottery play is entertainment, not investment.
  • Consider joining a workplace pool. More tickets = better odds, but always put agreements in writing.
  • Don’t chase losses. Odds don’t improve because you bought yesterday.
