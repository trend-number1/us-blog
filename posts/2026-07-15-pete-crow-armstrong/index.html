Pete Crow-Armstrong: The Surprising MVP Chase Behind the Cubs Star

July 15, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

What if the second-best player in the National League right now isn’t a household name yet? Pete Crow-Armstrong put together a June so dominant that only two players in major-league history — Babe Ruth and Lou Gehrig — have ever matched his monthly output. At the All-Star break, the Chicago Cubs center fielder trails only Shohei Ohtani in the WAR rankings.

That’s not hype. That’s the resume of a 24-year-old who just became the face of a 150-year-old franchise.

Quick Summary: Pete Crow-Armstrong (nicknamed “PCA”) is a Gold Glove center fielder for the Chicago Cubs. In 2026 he transformed elite defense and speed into a full-blown MVP campaign, signed a six-year, $115 million extension, and earned an All-Star reserve spot — all while learning to handle the pressure that comes with sudden stardom.

Why Pete Crow-Armstrong Is Suddenly Everywhere

For years, Pete Crow-Armstrong was known for two things: blazing speed and highlight-reel defense in center field. The bat was the question mark. Not anymore.

Over one recent 34-game stretch, Crow-Armstrong slashed .367/.451/.734 with 13 home runs and 9 stolen bases, according to ESPN. That is not a hot streak — that is one of the best runs anyone in the majors produced all season.

The timing mattered as much as the numbers. He came through repeatedly in high-leverage moments, helping push the Cubs toward the top of the National League standings. ESPN notes he currently leads all NL hitters in both Win Probability Added and Championship Probability Added — two metrics built specifically to measure how much a player directly contributes to winning.

Translation: nobody in the National League has done more to win actual games.

So how rare is this kind of season? The historical company tells the story.

A Season in Historic Company

The comparisons attached to Crow-Armstrong this year are almost absurd. Consider what the record books say:

  • His June production matched the monthly output of just two players in MLB history: Babe Ruth and Lou Gehrig.
  • Only three players in the Cubs’ 150-year history have posted multiple seasons with 20 home runs and 20 stolen bases — Ryne Sandberg, Sammy Sosa, and Crow-Armstrong.
  • As MLB Network pointed out, the first 385 games of his career look nearly identical to Barry Bonds’ resume at the same point.

Even rival voices are taking notice. Alex Rodriguez recently called Crow-Armstrong “the Kylian Mbappé of MLB” — a nod to the electric, generational energy the star brings to the field.

Here’s the insight worth pausing on at the midpoint of this story: elite defenders who become elite hitters are among the rarest players in baseball. A center fielder who saves runs and leads the league in win contribution is exactly the profile that MVP voters — and franchises — build a decade around.

Which is precisely what the Cubs did.

The $115 Million Bet on the Future

Chicago didn’t wait around. Before this breakout fully crystallized, the Cubs and Crow-Armstrong agreed to a six-year, $115 million extension, keeping him under contract through the 2032 season.

The structure rewards exactly the trajectory he’s now on. Here’s how the deal breaks down:

Total value6 years, $115 million

MVP escalators2031 and 2032 salaries increase up to $2M per top-MVP finish

The MVP escalators are the fascinating part. His 2031 salary rises by $2 million for each MVP won in 2027–2030 (with sliding bonuses of $1M for 2nd/3rd, $750K for 4th/5th, and $500K for 6th–10th finishes), and his 2032 salary carries the same structure for MVP finishes through 2031.

In other words, the better he performs, the more the contract pays him — a bet both sides seem happy to make.

But there’s a common misconception worth clearing up before we go further.

The Misconception: “He’s Already a Finished Product”

It’s easy to assume a player this good has it all figured out. He doesn’t — and that’s part of the story.

Crow-Armstrong has spoken openly about learning to handle failure as a central part of his All-Star season. Baseball is a sport where the best hitters fail seven times out of ten, and PCA’s emotional, high-intensity style cuts both ways.

That intensity spilled over recently. During a 9-8, 10-inning loss to the rival White Sox, Crow-Armstrong nearly made an incredible catch on a deep fly ball off the bat of Miguel Vargas, narrowly missing before slamming hard into the outfield wall. Afterward, in a heated exchange with a taunting fan, he used vulgar language — a moment caught on camera that spread quickly online.

He addressed it directly the next day. “I think I just regret my choice of words the most,” he told reporters, acknowledging the kids who might see the clip on social media. He didn’t regret firing back at a taunt, but wished he’d handled it differently.

It’s a reminder that stardom at 24 comes with a learning curve that plays out in public.

What This Means For You

If you’re a baseball fan — or just someone who likes catching a rising star before the rest of the world does — a few takeaways stand out:

  1. Watch him now. Seasons compared to Ruth, Gehrig, and Bonds don’t come around often. This is the kind of run people reference for decades.
  2. Wrigley in 2027 could be must-see. Crow-Armstrong has pondered participating in the 2027 Home Run Derby at Wrigley Field, which would put his power on display at the Cubs’ iconic home.
  3. The MVP race is live. He’s the closest player to catching Shohei Ohtani in the NL, per ESPN’s Awards Watch. That gap could close down the stretch.

There’s also a bigger backdrop. MLB and the players’ union have begun negotiating the next collective bargaining agreement, with the threat of an owners’ lockout looming and proposed changes to the amateur draft. Those changes could reshape the path for the next generation of players trying to become “the next PCA.”

Interestingly, despite all the on-field brilliance, Crow-Armstrong fell short in All-Star fan voting and enters as a reserve at Citizens Bank Park in Philadelphia. Winning on the field while losing the popularity contest — for now.

So here’s the question worth sitting with: when a player is producing at a Ruth-and-Gehrig level yet still can’t win a fan vote, what does that say about how we notice greatness while it’s happening? Keep your eyes on Pete Crow-Armstrong — you may be watching a first-ballot story unfold in real time.
