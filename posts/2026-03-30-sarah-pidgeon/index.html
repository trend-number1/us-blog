Sarah Pidgeon's Meteoric Rise: From Broadway to TV's Most Talked-About Role

March 30, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

Have you ever watched a performance so captivating that you forgot the person on screen wasn’t the real historical figure? That’s exactly what Sarah Pidgeon has achieved in FX’s Love Story, her portrayal of style icon Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy leaving audiences emotionally wrecked and critics scrambling for superlatives.

Key Takeaways

  • Sarah Pidgeon stars as Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy in Ryan Murphy’s FX series Love Story, which premiered in February 2026
  • The emotional finale depicted the fatal 1999 plane crash that killed JFK Jr., Carolyn, and her sister Lauren Bessette
  • Pidgeon, a Carnegie Mellon graduate, earned a Tony nomination for Stereophonic on Broadway
  • Her next project: Honeymoon With Harry alongside Kevin Costner and Jake Gyllenhaal, shooting April 2026 in Queensland

Sarah Pidgeon Transforms Into Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy

The 29-year-old actress took on what many considered an impossible task — embodying one of the most iconic, photographed, and fiercely private women of the 1990s. In an interview with Vogue, Pidgeon opened up about the immense preparation required to become Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy, a former Calvin Klein executive whose style and mystique have endured for decades.

The FX series, produced by Ryan Murphy, premiered in February 2026 and chronicled the relationship between JFK Jr. and Bessette from their first meeting in 1992 through their marriage four years later. Paul Anthony Kelly portrays John F. Kennedy Jr. opposite Pidgeon’s Carolyn.

Not everyone was initially convinced. Before the premiere, the show faced sharp criticism over its wardrobe choices — a significant concern given that Bessette-Kennedy’s minimalist style was practically a character in its own right. Stylist Liz Teich commented on Instagram that “the wardrobe is a main character in this story” and urged the production to “step it up.” Bessette-Kennedy’s former hair colorist, Brad Johns, told Vogue that Pidgeon’s blonde wig color was “too 2024” and that “no one would believe that Carolyn in the ’90s would ever have that color from me.”

Pidgeon’s response? She told E! News that viewers should wait to watch the series before making final judgments. “I can’t wait for them to see it,” she said, adding, “I feel very honored to take on this role and learn about such an incredible woman who’s endured.”

That confidence paid off — by the time the finale aired, audiences were fully invested.

The Love Story Finale That Left Viewers Sobbing

The series finale delivered the moment everyone knew was coming but no one was ready for: the depiction of the fatal 1999 plane crash.

In the emotional episode, Pidgeon’s Carolyn and Kelly’s JFK Jr. headed to Martha’s Vineyard alongside Carolyn’s sister Lauren Bessette, played by Sydney Lemmon. As Kennedy Jr. helmed the small aircraft, panic began to set in behind the controls. In the scene’s final moments, Carolyn attempted to calm her husband, repeating, “It’s OK. Just breathe.”

The actual crash into the Atlantic Ocean — which killed all three passengers — was not explicitly shown on screen. Instead, the trio’s deaths occurred roughly halfway through the episode, with the remaining runtime documenting their family members’ grief. The series closed with Carolyn and Lauren’s mother, Ann Freeman (played by Constance Zimmer), reading a eulogy as the late fashion executive was shown in a tender beach scene with her partner.

Social media erupted with emotional responses:

  • “I got so attached to the characters for the past few weeks that I forgot it’s based on a true story and how it ends. Now I’m a sobbing mess,” one fan wrote on X.
  • Another simply posted, “Damn, this hurts.”

The restrained approach to depicting the tragedy proved more devastating than any graphic recreation could have been. But how did Pidgeon prepare for a role this emotionally demanding? Her career trajectory offers some answers.

From Stereophonic to Stardom: Pidgeon’s Career Path

Sarah Pidgeon’s rise didn’t happen overnight. A Carnegie Mellon graduate, she built her résumé steadily before landing the kinds of roles that demand attention.

Her Broadway debut in Stereophonic — the Tony Awards–sweeping play — was itself a slow-burn success story. Pidgeon first auditioned just before the pandemic, after which the production was paused. When her chance came again in 2023, she had, in her own words, grown considerably.

“Every character I’ve played, I’m always ‘Team Fill-in-the-Blank.’ Even if they’re extremely flawed, there’s a way to justify what they did,” she told ELLE. Playing Diana, a member of a 1970s band navigating a complicated relationship with her bandmate, taught her about the complexity of dependency. “I did judge Diana a bit for being in this relationship and finding it so difficult to advocate for herself,” she admitted. “It’s like, ‘Just get out, leave him,’ but it’s so much more complicated than that.”

That ability to find humanity in complex women is precisely what made her casting as Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy so inspired. As she told The Hollywood Reporter, playing the style icon actually taught her to speak her own mind — a quality Bessette-Kennedy was known for behind closed doors.

What’s Next: Honeymoon With Harry and Beyond

Pidgeon isn’t slowing down. According to Deadline, she has been cast in Honeymoon With Harry, an upcoming film alongside Kevin Costner and Jake Gyllenhaal. The production is set to begin shooting in April 2026 in Queensland, Australia.

The 29-year-old also made a splash at the 2026 Vanity Fair Oscar Party at LACMA in Los Angeles, rubbing shoulders with over 250 celebrities at one of Hollywood’s most exclusive post-Academy Awards gatherings.

Her spring 2026 wardrobe has even caught fashion media attention, with Who What Wear declaring her style “so CBK coded” — a testament to how thoroughly she has absorbed the Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy aesthetic both on and off screen.

What This Means for You

If you haven’t watched Love Story yet, you’re missing one of 2026’s most emotionally powerful television performances. The series is available on FX and Hulu, and Pidgeon’s work as Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy is the kind of performance that lingers long after the credits roll.

For anyone interested in the intersection of fashion, history, and compelling drama, this series delivers on all fronts. And with Honeymoon With Harry on the horizon, Sarah Pidgeon is proving that she’s not a one-role wonder — she’s a generational talent whose best work may still be ahead of her.

The question is: with Broadway credentials, a critically acclaimed limited series, and a major film on the way, is Sarah Pidgeon on track to become one of Hollywood’s defining actresses of the decade? Based on everything we’ve seen so far, the answer seems increasingly clear.
