Ty Simpson's Surprising $6.5M Gamble Before 2026 NFL Draft

April 24, 2026 00:03 Trend Insights

Ty Simpson walked away from $6.5 million in guaranteed NIL money to chase an NFL dream that, as of draft night, still has scouts arguing over whether he belongs in Round 1 at all.

That’s the tension hanging over Pittsburgh tonight. The former Alabama quarterback is one of the most polarizing prospects in the 2026 NFL Draft — a player some executives view as the most pro-ready passer in the class, and others can’t find a first-round grade on. The gap between those two views could cost him tens of millions of dollars.

Quick Summary: Ty Simpson turned down a $6.5M NIL offer from Miami to enter the 2026 NFL Draft. He’s widely considered QB2 behind Heisman winner Fernando Mendoza, but ESPN’s Matt Miller reports teams are struggling to commit to a first-round grade. A last-minute visit with the Arizona Cardinals could reshape Round 1.

Who Is Ty Simpson? The Alabama QB Nobody Can Agree On

Simpson spent three years as a backup at Alabama before finally taking over as the starter in 2025. That short résumé as a starter is a big reason his evaluation is so split.

Heading into the 2026 NFL Draft, he’s viewed as the No. 2 quarterback in the class, sitting behind Heisman Trophy winner Fernando Mendoza. But some analysts argue Simpson is actually the more pro-ready of the two, with a higher NFL ceiling.

Others aren’t so sure. ESPN’s Matt Miller wrote this month that he’s “still struggling to find a team for Alabama quarterback Ty Simpson in Round 1,” adding that league sources keep suggesting someone will take him — just not agreeing on who.

That uncertainty sets up one of the most unusual storylines of draft night.

The $6.5 Million Decision That Almost Changed Everything

Here’s the part that might be the most revealing: Simpson nearly didn’t enter this draft at all.

On David Pollack’s See Ball, Get Ball podcast, Simpson confirmed he turned down a $6.5 million NIL offer from the Miami Hurricanes to transfer and play in college in 2026. It would have made him the highest-paid player in college football.

“I think the last offer was definitely one that I just had to sit down and consider, because it would have been life-changing money,” Simpson said. “It was something to where I was like, I can’t just ignore this. I have to sit down and think about it.”

He went to his former head coach Nick Saban for advice. According to Simpson, Saban reframed the whole question:

  • Take the money out of it
  • Take the draft rounds out of it
  • What do you actually want to do next year?

Simpson’s answer was immediate: “I want to play professional football.” Miami eventually pivoted to former Duke quarterback Darrian Mensah through the transfer portal.

NIL Money vs. First-Round Rookie Contract

So how does the money Simpson walked away from compare to what’s actually at stake tonight?

PathGuaranteed MoneyTimeline
Miami NIL offer (declined)$6.5 millionOne college season
Expected late first-round NFL pick~$20 million4-year guaranteed contract
Second-round slideSignificantly less than $20M, less guarantee4 years

According to NBC Sports, a late first-round selection comes with a guaranteed four-year contract worth around $20 million. That math only works, of course, if Simpson actually goes in Round 1 — and that’s where it gets complicated.

Why Teams Are So Split On His First-Round Grade

The skepticism around Simpson isn’t about talent in the abstract. It’s about fit, risk, and whether anyone is willing to use a premium pick on a one-year starter.

Recent reports indicate that the Cleveland Browns, New York Jets, and Pittsburgh Steelers are not planning to grab Simpson with their top picks tonight. Some NFL executives reportedly don’t even view Simpson as the undisputed QB2 of this class.

ESPN’s Ben Solak floated the idea earlier this week that the Arizona Cardinals could trade back into Round 1 to draft Simpson late. But Matt Miller’s sources weren’t convinced:

“Why give up draft picks to jump into Round 1 when the Jets didn’t like him enough at No. 2 or No. 16 to take him?”

That one quote captures the entire Ty Simpson problem. Teams keep waiting for someone else to fall in love first.

The Arizona Cardinals Wild Card

Arizona suddenly looms as the team that could change everything. The Cardinals released former No. 1 overall pick Kyler Murray last month, and Murray has since joined the Minnesota Vikings.

That leaves a massive void. Murray departed Arizona ranking:

  • Third in franchise history in passing yards (20,460)
  • Third in franchise history in passing touchdowns (121)
  • Second in franchise history in completions (1,974)

The Cardinals currently hold the No. 3 overall pick. Simpson visited Arizona on the final day prospects were allowed to meet with teams — a notable last-minute stop reported by ESPN’s Field Yates.

Whether Arizona takes him at No. 3, trades back into the first round, or lets him slide to Day 2 is arguably the biggest unresolved question heading into tonight.

What Simpson Is Saying About His Critics

While analysts debate, Simpson has stayed unusually direct. He’s pushed back publicly against what he’s called “puzzling” criticism, and he’s leaned into his faith as the anchor of his confidence.

He’s also one of the invited prospects scheduled to attend the draft in person in Pittsburgh — a group that typically only includes players expected to go in Round 1. Matt Miller has publicly suggested Simpson might want to reconsider attending to avoid the awkward optics of a long green-room wait.

That’s the real stakes of draft night for Simpson: not just the money, but the live-television spectacle of being the player whose fall everyone is watching.

What This Means For You

If you’re a casual NFL fan tuning into the 2026 Draft tonight, here’s what to actually watch for:

  1. Watch Arizona at No. 3. If the Cardinals pass, it gets interesting fast.
  2. Watch for a trade back into Round 1. Arizona or another QB-needy team jumping up late is the most-talked-about scenario.
  3. Watch the Jets and Steelers early. Reports suggest they’re not taking him — but draft-night leaks sometimes exist to create leverage.
  4. Pay attention if Simpson slides past pick 22. A Day 2 fall would be one of the biggest stories of the entire draft.

The common misconception about Simpson is that he’s a “safe” QB2. He isn’t. He’s a high-variance prospect whose evaluation gap between optimists and skeptics is wider than almost anyone else in this class.

The Bigger Question

Ty Simpson’s situation is almost a perfect case study of the modern college-to-pro decision. He turned down generational college money to bet on himself — and that bet’s actual payoff won’t be known until his name (eventually) gets called tonight.

If he goes in Round 1, his gamble pays off roughly 3x over. If he slides to Day 2, that $6.5 million NIL offer suddenly looks like the path not taken.

Would you have taken the guaranteed $6.5 million — or bet on a pro career like Simpson did? Tonight’s draft is going to give us a real answer about whether self-belief was the right call.
