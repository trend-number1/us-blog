Kennedy Chandler's Surprising NBA Comeback: From G League Assists Leader to Jazz Starter

April 11, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

What does it take to go from averaging 7.8 minutes per game as a rookie to dropping a career-high 31 points in just three seasons? For Kennedy Chandler, the answer was a detour through the G League — and a relentless refusal to let his NBA dream die.

Key Takeaways:

  • Kennedy Chandler signed two consecutive 10-day contracts with the Utah Jazz after dominating the G League
  • He averaged 12.0 points, 6.2 assists, and 2.8 rebounds in 30.1 minutes across nine games with Utah
  • Chandler exploded for a career-high 31 points, 8 assists, and 7 rebounds in the Jazz’s season finale
  • The former 38th overall pick in 2022 is now a serious contender for a full-time NBA roster spot

Kennedy Chandler’s Road Back to the NBA

The 23-year-old point guard’s NBA journey has been anything but linear. Selected as the 38th overall pick in the 2022 NBA Draft by the Memphis Grizzlies, Chandler’s rookie season offered only a brief glimpse of his potential. In 33 regular season games during the 2022-23 campaign, he averaged just 2.2 points, 1.6 assists, and 1.1 rebounds in 7.8 minutes per game, shooting 42.2% from the field.

Those limited minutes barely scratched the surface of what Chandler could do. After his rookie year, he hadn’t appeared in an NBA game — until the Utah Jazz came calling in March 2026.

What changed? The G League happened.

Dominating the G League With the Delaware Blue Coats

Chandler didn’t just survive in the G League — he ran it. Playing for the Delaware Blue Coats this season, he emerged as one of the most productive players in the entire league. His numbers over 14 games were impossible to ignore:

That 9.6 assists per game made him the NBA G League assists leader — a stat that turned heads across the league. He wasn’t just scoring; he was controlling games and dictating tempo, exactly the kind of skill set that translates to the NBA level.

According to ESPN’s Shams Charania, agent Ryan Davis of WME Basketball confirmed the Jazz signing, noting that “Chandler returns to the NBA after playing his 2022-23 rookie campaign in Memphis as a second-round pick.” So how has that transition gone?

Making Every Minute Count in Utah

The Jazz signed Chandler to his first 10-day contract around March 21, 2026, and he wasted no time making an impression. In his Jazz debut, he posted 19 points, 5 rebounds, and 4 assists, prompting immediate praise from head coach Will Hardy.

“I thought he handled himself great,” Hardy said after the game. “It’s not easy to do what he just did in terms of stepping in. I thought he played great.”

Over his first six appearances during the initial 10-day deal, Chandler’s production was consistent and impressive:

  • 14.2 points per game on 43.1% shooting
  • 6.8 assists per game
  • 3.2 rebounds per game
  • 36.4% from three-point range (1.3 threes per game)
  • 1.0 steals in 32.3 minutes per night

The Jazz were dealing with significant backcourt injuries — Isaiah Collier, Keyonte George, and Elijah Harkless were all sidelined with hamstring issues — and Chandler stepped directly into the starting role. Rather than viewing this as a fluke of circumstance, Chandler treated it as a live audition.

His second 10-day contract followed quickly, as reported by Michael Scotto of USA Today. Over nine total games, Chandler settled into averages of 12.0 points, 6.2 assists, and 2.8 rebounds in 30.1 minutes. He scored in double figures in seven of nine games and dished out five or more assists in seven contests, including a nine-assist performance against the Toronto Raptors on March 23.

A Career Night to Remember

The real exclamation point came in the Jazz’s season finale against the New Orleans Pelicans. Given 43 minutes — the most playing time of his Jazz stint — Chandler erupted for career highs across the board:

  • 31 points on 61.1% shooting from the field
  • 8 assists
  • 7 rebounds
  • 66.7% from three-point range

Despite the Jazz losing to the Pelicans, Chandler’s performance was the kind of statement game that front offices remember. For a player who averaged just 2.2 points as a rookie, putting up 31 in a meaningful NBA game signaled a complete transformation.

The connection to his former Grizzlies teammates also played a role in his comfort level. Chandler reunited with Jaren Jackson Jr., Vince Williams Jr., and John Konchar in Utah, all of whom were with him during his rookie year in Memphis.

“It’s been great just seeing guys here,” Chandler said. “Like Jaren and Vince and John Konchar. I was with them my rookie year, so just seeing them — they give me the positivity to just go out there and be yourself. So those guys have given me confidence.”

What This Means for Chandler’s Future — and Yours

At 21-49, the Jazz weren’t chasing a playoff spot. This was about evaluation, and Chandler passed every test. The real question now is whether Utah offers him a longer-term deal heading into next season.

Here’s what works in Chandler’s favor:

  1. Proven production — double-figure scoring in seven of nine games
  2. Playmaking ability — 6.2 assists per game shows legitimate floor general skills
  3. Efficiency — 43.1% from the field and 36.4% from deep over his first six games
  4. Age — at just 23, he’s still in his development window
  5. G League dominance — his assists title shows he can carry an offense

For basketball fans and fantasy managers alike, Kennedy Chandler is a name to watch heading into the 2026-27 season. His trajectory from forgotten second-round pick to G League assists leader to legitimate NBA contributor is the kind of development story the league thrives on.

The Jazz have already shown they’re willing to invest playing time in young talent. Chandler’s job now is to ensure those 10-day contracts turn into something permanent. After a 31-point, 8-assist, 7-rebound finale, he’s made his case loud and clear.

Could Kennedy Chandler become the Jazz’s starting point guard of the future? The numbers say the conversation is worth having — and the 2026-27 season may provide the definitive answer.
