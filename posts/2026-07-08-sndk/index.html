SNDK Stock Slides as AI Memory Momentum Cools

July 8, 2026 00:03 Trend Insights

A memory stock that helped power the AI trade just dropped 14% in a single day. That is the kind of move that makes traders wonder whether the party is winding down.

SNDK stock — the ticker for Sandisk Corporation (NASDAQ:SNDK) — has become one of the most-watched names in the semiconductor selloff this week. On Tuesday, July 7, chip stocks broadly declined and SanDisk fell more than 8%, extending a rough stretch for the memory group.

So is the AI memory boom actually cracking, or is this a healthy pause in a longer run? Let’s dig into what the latest sources actually say.

Quick Summary

  • SanDisk (SNDK) dropped over 8% Tuesday, part of a broad memory-chip selloff.
  • The trigger: Samsung Electronics’ Q2 earnings failed to impress investors.
  • On July 3, Kioxia and SanDisk began production of 10th-generation 3D flash memory in Japan.
  • Their joint venture was recently extended through December 2034.
  • Analysts are split — some now call SanDisk “fully valued” as AI memory momentum cools.

Why SNDK Stock Fell This Week

The selloff wasn’t isolated to SanDisk. According to reporting from Yahoo Finance and Stocktwits, Micron (MU), SanDisk (SNDK), and Western Digital (WDC) all fell together premarket on July 7.

The catalyst was Samsung Electronics. Its Q2 earnings failed to impress investors, and the disappointment rippled straight through the memory-chip complex.

The shock was big enough overseas that Samsung and SK Hynix briefly dragged South Korea’s Kospi index into a brief bear market, per Stocktwits. When the two largest memory makers on earth stumble, U.S.-listed peers rarely escape unscathed.

Here’s the striking part: just one day earlier, on July 6, Yahoo Finance reported that memory stocks — including SNDK, WDC, and MU — had rebounded overnight after an earlier Anthropic-driven selloff, with traders eyeing the Samsung results ahead.

So within roughly 48 hours, the memory group went from relief rally to renewed decline. That whiplash tells you something important about the current tape, which we’ll unpack next.

The AI Memory Trade Is Getting Volatile

The single most important insight this week is not the direction of the move — it’s the speed of the reversals.

Memory names have posted extraordinary gains during the AI buildout. One 24/7 Wall St. summary noted a stock that traded at $45 a year ago now changes hands near $1,729 — a 3,718% gain. Micron alone was quoted around $1,061 in one recent session.

When gains stack up that fast, sentiment turns fragile. A single earnings miss can undo days of buying.

That fragility is exactly why Morningstar warned investors to brace for a reckoning in AI stocks, with memory-chip names sitting on some of the biggest gains, according to 24/7 Wall St. It doesn’t mean the trade is over — it means the easy money may be behind it.

Consider the key drivers pulling SNDK in opposite directions:

  • Bullish: Surging global demand for NAND flash, especially for AI-driven applications.
  • Bullish: A newly extended manufacturing partnership locking in capacity through 2034.
  • Bearish: Weak Samsung earnings signaling softer near-term memory pricing.
  • Bearish: Valuation concerns after a massive run-up.

If the near-term picture is murky, the long-term production story is far more concrete — and that’s where SanDisk’s Kioxia deal comes in.

SanDisk and Kioxia: The 2034 Bet

On July 3, Kioxia and SanDisk announced the start of production for their 10th-generation 3D flash memory technology at the Fab2 (K2) facility in Kitakami, Japan.

This milestone follows the plant’s opening in September 2025. It’s part of a multi-year effort to expand manufacturing capacity to meet rising global demand for NAND flash — particularly for AI workloads.

The K2 facility isn’t ordinary. According to Insider Monkey, it uses advanced CBA technology, earthquake-resistant architecture, and artificial intelligence to maximize manufacturing efficiency and energy savings.

By scaling both 8th- and 10th-generation products, the companies aim to deliver high-performance, high-capacity, and low-power storage. That combination is precisely what AI data centers are hungry for.

Crucially, the launch reinforces a long-standing joint venture that was recently extended through December 2034. Continued investment in the Kitakami plant is also framed as strengthening U.S.-Japan manufacturing relations.

Here’s the takeaway most headlines miss: while traders obsess over a single day’s price swing, SanDisk is committing to a manufacturing roadmap that stretches nearly a decade out. Which raises the real question — is the stock cheap or expensive right now?

Is SNDK Stock Undervalued or Fully Valued?

This is where the sources genuinely disagree, and that disagreement is worth understanding.

On one hand, Insider Monkey lists Sandisk Corporation (NASDAQ:SNDK) as one of the 10 Most Undervalued NASDAQ Stocks to Buy Right Now. That framing leans on the company’s NAND flash technology and its expanding production capacity.

On the other hand, a July 7 Yahoo Finance piece argued that “Sandisk (SNDK) Looks Fully Valued As AI Memory Momentum Cools.” Same company, same week, opposite conclusions.

Bull Case (SNDK)Bear Case (SNDK)

10th-gen 3D NAND production now liveSamsung’s weak Q2 dragged the sector
JV secured through December 2034Fell 8%+ Tuesday amid broad chip decline
AI storage demand risingMorningstar warns of an AI-stock reckoning

A common misconception is that undervalued and overvalued calls can’t both be reasonable. They can — they simply price in different time horizons. The bull case rests on 2034-era capacity; the bear case reflects near-term memory pricing after Samsung’s miss.

What This Means For You

SanDisk makes data storage devices built on NAND flash — solid-state drives, embedded products, removable cards, USB drives, and wafers and components, per Insider Monkey. In other words, its fortunes track the broader memory cycle closely.

If you follow AI memory stocks, three practical points stand out:

  1. Expect volatility. Reversals within 48 hours are now normal for this group.
  2. Separate the trade from the business. A bad Samsung quarter is not the same as SanDisk halting its 10th-gen production.
  3. Watch the sector, not just the ticker. MU, WDC, and SNDK are moving together on the same macro headlines.

None of this is investment advice — it’s a map of what the latest reporting actually confirms.

So here’s the question worth sitting with: when a stock can be labeled both “most undervalued” and “fully valued” in the same week, are you watching a bubble deflate — or a durable AI-storage story just catching its breath? Keep an eye on Samsung’s next results, because the memory tape may hinge on them.
