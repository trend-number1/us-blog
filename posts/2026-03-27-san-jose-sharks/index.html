San Jose Sharks Playoff Hopes Fade as Five-Game Losing Streak Bites Hard

After spending most of the 2025–26 season firmly in the Western Conference playoff conversation, the San Jose Sharks are watching their postseason dreams slip away at the worst possible time. A brutal five-game losing streak has left the team scrambling with just weeks remaining in the regular season.

Key Takeaways

  • The Sharks sit at 32-31-6 (70 points), five points behind Nashville for the second wild card spot.
  • A five-game winless skid has derailed a season that exceeded preseason expectations.
  • Key injuries to Tyler Toffoli, Ryan Reaves, and others have thinned the lineup at the worst time.
  • With only 14 games remaining, every point matters — and the margin for error is nearly gone.

So how did a team that surprised the hockey world end up on the outside looking in?

A Season That Defied Expectations — Until Now

Not many predicted, back in the summer, that the San Jose Sharks would be anywhere near playoff contention this season. The franchise has been in rebuild mode, and expectations were modest at best. Yet head coach Ryan Warsofsky guided a young, hungry roster into legitimate postseason territory for most of the campaign.

The Sharks were scrappy, competitive, and found ways to win close games. Young players stepped up, and the team’s identity started to crystallize around effort and accountability. As San Jose Hockey Now put it: “Remember, this has been a successful season for the San Jose Sharks.”

But hockey is a game of momentum, and momentum has turned violently against them. The current five-game losing streak includes a demoralizing 6-3 loss to the Nashville Predators on March 25, a game where the Preds’ Filip Forsberg posted three points and Nashville extended its own winning streak to five games.

The contrast couldn’t be sharper: Nashville surging, San Jose sinking — and the two teams are direct rivals for the same wild card berth.

The Injury Factor: Too Many Key Players Missing

Timing is everything, and the Sharks’ injury report reads like a cautionary tale about the grind of an 82-game season.

Here’s a look at the recent injury situation:

  • Tyler Toffoli (lower-body) — may not join the team’s upcoming road trip
  • Ryan Reaves (undisclosed) — out week-to-week
  • Igor Chernyshov (concussion) — recently returned to the lineup
  • Yaroslav Askarov (lower-body) — back in net and making key saves
  • Vincent Desharnais (upper-body) — also returning

The return of Chernyshov and Askarov is welcome news. Askarov, in particular, showed his quality with an impressive save against Pius Suter during the Sharks’ road game at St. Louis on March 26. But Toffoli’s uncertain availability is a significant blow — the veteran forward is one of the team’s most reliable offensive threats.

Meanwhile, the loss of enforcer Ryan Reaves strips the lineup of its physical edge, something that matters enormously during the dog days of a playoff push.

The math is unforgiving. Five points is a significant gap with only 14 games to play. The Sharks would essentially need to go on a heater — winning 9 or 10 of their remaining contests — while simultaneously hoping Nashville stumbles. The probability isn’t zero, but it’s uncomfortably close.

Even the optimists at Fear the Fin have acknowledged the reality, titling their latest update “Sharks Playoff/Lottery Hopes” — a nod to the fact that draft lottery positioning is now a legitimate consideration alongside the playoff push.

Bright Spots Worth Celebrating

Despite the losing streak, there are genuine positives to take from this season.

Alex Wennberg continues to be a steady presence. He scored against the St. Louis Blues on March 26 and was named the Sharks’ King Clancy Memorial Trophy nominee — an award recognizing leadership and humanitarian contributions. That nomination speaks volumes about the culture being built inside the locker room.

Collin Graf has emerged as an NHL impact player, a development that San Jose Hockey Now describes as “a tribute to every level of San Jose Sharks’ scouting and development and coaching.” His growth represents the kind of internal development that sustains franchises long-term.

And Will Smith showed his scoring touch with two goals in the Nashville game, even in a loss. For a young player, that kind of production in meaningful games is invaluable experience.

The goaltending situation also appears to be stabilizing. Laurent Brossoit, originally recalled on March 13, was reassigned to the AHL San Jose Barracuda after serving as backup for four games. With Askarov back healthy and performing well, the crease has a clearer hierarchy heading into April.

What’s Left to Play For

Even if the playoff dream officially dies, this season hasn’t been wasted. Far from it.

Here’s what the Sharks can still accomplish:

  1. Develop young talent in high-pressure situations — Players like Smith, Graf, and Chernyshov are getting playoff-race experience that money can’t buy.
  2. Solidify the team culture — Warsofsky has built something real. The “culture” word gets thrown around loosely, but the Sharks’ competitiveness this season proves the foundation is solid.
  3. Improve draft lottery positioning — If the playoffs slip away, a higher pick in the 2026 NHL Draft could accelerate the rebuild.
  4. Evaluate the roster for the offseason — These final 14 games are an audition for every player on the bubble.

The upcoming schedule features home games against Toronto (April 2), Nashville (April 4), and Vancouver (April 11) — all meaningful matchups. The Nashville game, in particular, could serve as either a statement or a farewell to playoff contention.

What This Means for Sharks Fans

If you’re a San Jose Sharks fan, this stretch stings. Watching a team that overachieved for months stumble at the finish line is a particular kind of sports pain. But context matters.

This franchise went to the Stanley Cup Final in 2016 against the Pittsburgh Penguins and has been searching for that level again ever since. The 2025–26 season, regardless of how it ends, represents real progress. A team that was expected to be at the bottom of the standings spent the vast majority of the year in the playoff picture. That’s not failure — that’s a foundation.

The question now is whether the front office uses the offseason to add the missing pieces that could push this group over the line in 2026–27. The core is young, the coaching is strong, and the culture inside the SAP Center — affectionately known as “The Shark Tank” — is heading in the right direction.

Will the Sharks use this painful late-season collapse as fuel for a stronger push next year, or will it linger as a missed opportunity? That’s the question that will define the franchise’s next chapter. One thing is certain: San Jose hockey is relevant again, and that alone is worth watching.
