Pat Summitt Documentary 'Breaking Glass' Premieres on Hulu During March Madness

March 19, 2026 22:08 Trend Insights

Pat Summitt won 1,098 games and never had a losing season in 38 years of coaching. Now, during the very tournament she helped make legendary, her full story is finally getting the documentary it deserves.

“Breaking Glass: The Pat Summitt Story” will premiere on Hulu on March 25 — right in the heart of Women’s March Madness. The timing is no accident. Few names in the history of women’s college basketball carry the weight that Summitt’s does, and this film aims to show the world why.

Quick Summary:

  • “Breaking Glass: The Pat Summitt Story” premieres on Hulu on March 25
  • Executive produced by Robin Roberts, directed by Emmy-winner Dawn Porter
  • Features interviews with Billie Jean King, Peyton Manning, Dawn Staley, and Tamika Catchings
  • Includes never-before-seen archival footage from Summitt’s personal VHS tapes and recordings
  • Also airs on Disney+ (March 29) and ESPN2 (April 5)

A Friendship That Made This Film Possible

This isn’t just another sports documentary. It’s deeply personal — born from a decades-long friendship between Summitt and executive producer Robin Roberts.

Roberts first met Summitt in 1987, when she was a sports anchor at a Nashville TV station and Summitt had just won her first national championship with the Lady Vols. From that moment, their careers launched in parallel — Summitt building the greatest dynasty in women’s basketball while Roberts rose to become co-anchor of “Good Morning America.”

Their bond endured through the hardest moments of both their lives: Roberts’ cancer battles and Summitt’s devastating early-onset Alzheimer’s diagnosis in 2011.

“Robin was one of my mom’s closest friends, and there is nobody my family trusts more to lead this project,” Summitt’s son Tyler said.

Tyler described Roberts as a mother figure who remained close to the family even after Summitt’s death. “Robin sent gifts to our house when our kids were born. Our kids call her Aunt Robin,” he shared with Knox News.

That level of trust and intimacy is what separates this film from previous efforts to tell Summitt’s story.

What ‘Breaking Glass’ Covers

The documentary is directed by Dawn Porter, an Emmy-award-winning filmmaker, and is presented with the intimacy of a biopic rather than a traditional sports film.

What makes it unique is how Summitt’s story is told — mostly in her own voice. The filmmakers drew from:

  • Personal VHS tapes from Summitt’s private collection
  • Voice recordings never previously released
  • Private photographs spanning her entire life
  • Never-before-seen archival footage

These personal materials are supplemented with original interviews from some of the biggest names in sports:

IntervieweeConnection to Summitt

The film traces Summitt’s entire life — from her upbringing on a Tennessee dairy farm through her record-setting coaching career to her courageous public battle with Alzheimer’s disease.

Summitt passed away in 2016 at the age of 64, five years after her diagnosis. But as this documentary demonstrates, her impact hasn’t faded.

Pat Summitt’s Legacy by the Numbers

For anyone unfamiliar with just how dominant Summitt was, the statistics are staggering.

During her 38 seasons as head coach of the Tennessee Lady Vols, Summitt built a program that redefined what was possible in women’s college basketball:

  • 8 national championships with the Lady Vols
  • 1,098 career wins — the most in Division I basketball history at the time of her retirement
  • Zero losing seasons across nearly four decades
  • 100% graduation rate for players who completed their eligibility
  • 18 Final Four appearances

That last statistic — the graduation rate — mattered as much to Summitt as the wins. She was famous for holding players to the highest academic standards, personally checking grade reports and sitting in the front row of the classroom when a player was struggling.

Her influence extends far beyond Tennessee. Coaches like Dawn Staley, who played under Summitt and now leads South Carolina’s dominant program, carry her philosophy forward. The coaching tree she built continues to shape women’s basketball at every level.

The Pat Summitt Award

Summitt’s legacy is also honored annually through the Pat Summitt Award, given by the NCAA to coaches who demonstrate excellence both on and off the court. The 2026 recipient is Dr. Nikki Franke, Temple University’s fencing coach, honored for her legendary 50-year career — a testament to the cross-sport impact of Summitt’s name.

Why This Documentary Matters Now

The timing of “Breaking Glass” is significant for several reasons.

Women’s college basketball is experiencing a renaissance. Programs like Vanderbilt, led by coach Shea Ralph, are sparking revivals across the country. Television ratings for the women’s tournament have shattered records in recent years. The sport Summitt spent her life building is finally getting the mainstream attention she always fought for.

Releasing the documentary during March Madness ensures the largest possible audience will see it — many of whom may be too young to remember Summitt coaching on the sidelines in her signature power suits.

What This Means for You

Whether you’re a lifelong Tennessee fan or someone just discovering women’s basketball through the current March Madness bracket, “Breaking Glass” offers something powerful.

This isn’t just a story about basketball. It’s about a woman who grew up on a farm, walked into a sport that barely existed as a profession, and built it into what it is today through sheer force of will and an uncompromising standard of excellence.

Summitt’s niece continues the family legacy in coaching in Cheatham County, Tennessee. Tyler Summitt carries her memory. And every time a women’s basketball game fills an arena or trends on social media, Pat Summitt’s fingerprints are there.

“I’m honestly grateful to Robin Roberts for making this happen,” Tyler said. “There’s nobody better to tell my mom’s story.”

Mark your calendar for March 25. And as you watch the Women’s March Madness tournament unfold this month, ask yourself: would any of this be possible without Pat Summitt?
