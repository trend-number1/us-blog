Cruz Azul’s Dramatic 2026 Campaign: Champions Cup Clash and Liga MX Rivalry

Nine-time Liga MX champions and six-time Concacaf winners — and yet Cruz Azul find themselves staring down a 3-0 deficit heading into the second leg of their Champions Cup quarterfinal against LAFC. Can one of Mexican soccer’s true giants mount another legendary comeback?

Key Takeaways

  • LAFC defeated Cruz Azul 3-0 in the first leg of the Concacaf Champions Cup quarterfinals, powered by a David Martínez brace
  • Cruz Azul simultaneously faces Club América in a pivotal Liga MX Clausura 2026 clash
  • The club previously dominated earlier Champions Cup rounds, advancing 8-0 on aggregate and 4-3 on aggregate in prior matchups
  • Son Heung-min’s presence at LAFC adds a global spotlight to the quarterfinal series

Cruz Azul in the Concacaf Champions Cup: A Tale of Two Rounds

Cruz Azul entered the 2026 Concacaf Champions Cup as one of the tournament favorites, and their early-round performances justified the hype. In the Round of 16, the club advanced past CF Monterrey, and across earlier rounds posted dominant aggregate victories — including a stunning 8-0 aggregate win and a hard-fought 4-3 aggregate triumph in separate rounds.

But the quarterfinals have told a different story. On April 8, LAFC dismantled Cruz Azul 3-0 in the first leg at home. David Martínez was the standout performer, scoring twice to put the MLS side firmly in control of the tie. The result sent shockwaves through the tournament bracket, as few expected Cruz Azul to be so thoroughly outplayed.

The second leg still awaits, but Cruz Azul now face the daunting task of overcoming a three-goal deficit — a challenge that would test even the most storied club. So what went wrong in Los Angeles?

LAFC’s Masterclass: How Son Heung-min and David Martínez Changed the Series

One of the most compelling storylines heading into the LAFC-Cruz Azul quarterfinal was the presence of Son Heung-min at the MLS club. As World Soccer Talk noted, the match represented a “crucial test” for Son ahead of the 2026 World Cup, adding enormous international interest to what was already a marquee continental fixture.

While Son’s individual performance drew global eyeballs, it was David Martínez who stole the headlines with his two-goal performance. His brace gave LAFC a commanding 3-0 lead and left Cruz Azul with everything to do in the return leg.

Cruz Azul’s Champions Cup pedigree — six continental titles — means they cannot be written off. But the scale of the first-leg defeat makes the return fixture one of the most anticipated matches of the entire tournament. Can their history fuel a miraculous turnaround?

América vs Cruz Azul: Liga MX’s Biggest Rivalry Takes Center Stage

As if the Champions Cup pressure weren’t enough, Cruz Azul also faced Club América on April 11 in one of the most heated rivalries in all of Mexican soccer. The Clásico Joven is more than a match — it is a cultural event that divides Mexico City and captivates millions of fans across the Americas.

The Liga MX Clausura 2026 regular season is winding down, making every point crucial for both clubs. For Cruz Azul, the challenge of balancing continental and domestic ambitions is nothing new, but the timing could hardly be more demanding:

  • April 8: Champions Cup quarterfinal first leg vs LAFC (lost 3-0)
  • April 11: Liga MX clash vs Club América
  • Upcoming: Champions Cup quarterfinal second leg vs LAFC

This condensed schedule forces Cruz Azul to manage squad rotation and player fitness at a critical juncture of the season. The club’s coaching staff faces difficult decisions about where to prioritize their strongest lineup.

The stakes in Liga MX are clear — a poor finish in the Clausura could mean missing out on the Liguilla playoffs entirely, which would compound the disappointment of a potential Champions Cup exit.

Cruz Azul’s 2026 Season by the Numbers

Cruz Azul’s campaign has been a mix of brilliance and vulnerability. Their earlier Champions Cup dominance stands in sharp contrast to the LAFC result, painting a picture of a team capable of extremes.

Champions Cup Performance:

  • Round of 16: Advanced past CF Monterrey
  • Earlier rounds: 8-0 aggregate victory (dominant); 4-3 aggregate victory (resilient)
  • Quarterfinals: Trailing 0-3 after first leg vs LAFC
  • Historical pedigree: 6 Concacaf titles, 9 Liga MX titles

A common misconception about Cruz Azul is that their “cursed” reputation — a reference to decades without a Liga MX title before their recent championships — still defines them. In reality, the modern Cruz Azul squad is a legitimate force across multiple competitions, with the depth and talent to compete on two fronts simultaneously.

The real question is whether the psychological blow of the LAFC defeat will bleed into their Liga MX form, or whether it will sharpen their focus for the challenges ahead.

What This Means for North American Soccer

The Cruz Azul storyline carries significance well beyond one club’s fortunes. The LAFC result in the Champions Cup reflects the growing competitiveness of MLS against Liga MX sides in continental competition. For years, Mexican clubs dominated the Concacaf Champions League (now Champions Cup), but results like the 3-0 quarterfinal drubbing suggest the balance of power may be shifting.

Key factors driving this shift:

  1. MLS roster investment — clubs like LAFC are signing world-class talent (Son Heung-min being a prime example)
  2. Home-field advantage — MLS teams are leveraging their passionate fanbases in continental play
  3. Tactical evolution — MLS coaching has improved dramatically, closing the gap with Liga MX’s tactical sophistication
  4. Schedule congestion — Liga MX clubs juggling domestic and continental duties face fitness and rotation challenges

For fans, the rivalry between MLS and Liga MX is what makes the Champions Cup compelling. Cruz Azul’s second leg represents a chance to reassert Mexican dominance — or confirm that the continental landscape has fundamentally changed.

Looking Ahead: Can Cruz Azul Rewrite the Script?

Cruz Azul’s 2026 season stands at a crossroads. A Champions Cup comeback from 3-0 down would rank among the greatest results in the club’s storied history. A quiet exit, combined with a mediocre Liga MX finish, could define the season as a disappointment despite those dominant early rounds.

What makes Cruz Azul fascinating is their refusal to be predictable. This is a club that won an 8-0 aggregate in one round and lost 3-0 in the next. A club that contests Mexico’s fiercest derby one day and a continental quarterfinal the next.

With the second leg against LAFC still to come and the Liga MX Clausura reaching its decisive phase, every match from here on carries season-defining weight.

The question for Cruz Azul fans is simple but profound: Is this team built for a historic comeback, or has the 2026 campaign already reached its ceiling? The answer could reshape how we think about North American club soccer for years to come.
