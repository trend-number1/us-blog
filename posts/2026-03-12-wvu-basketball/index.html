WVU Basketball Big 12 Tournament: Mountaineers' NCAA Hopes on the Line

March 12, 2026 08:24 Trend Insights

WVU basketball needs a miracle run — and they know it. The seventh-seeded West Virginia Mountaineers enter the Big 12 Tournament at 18-13, sitting firmly on the outside of the NCAA Tournament bubble. Their only path to March Madness likely runs through Kansas City.

Key Takeaways

  • WVU enters the Big 12 Tournament as the 7th seed with an 18-13 record
  • The Mountaineers are off Joe Lunardi’s NCAA Tournament bubble
  • Key wins this season: Kansas, BYU, and Pitt (71-49)
  • Senior guard Honor Huff earned All-Big 12 Honorable Mention
  • WVU women’s basketball won the Big 12 title and may host NCAA games

WVU Basketball’s Roller-Coaster Season Under Ross Hodge

First-year head coach Ross Hodge has navigated a season full of extremes. The highs have been exhilarating — none more than a 71-49 demolition of rival Pitt that Hodge says he’ll “never forget for as long as I live.”

The Mountaineers also picked up statement victories against Kansas and BYU, a team that features potential top-two 2026 NBA Draft picks Darryn Peterson and A.J. Dybantsa. Those wins showed WVU can compete with anyone in the conference.

But the lows have been costly. Losses to Utah, Baylor, Kansas State, and Oklahoma State drained whatever NCAA Tournament case the Mountaineers were building. After a 77-62 Senior Day win over UCF, WVU climbed to No. 56 in the KenPom rankings — but that still wasn’t enough to crack ESPN bracketologist Joe Lunardi’s bubble.

“Obviously, we let a couple get away,” forward Chance Moore admitted. “The one thing about this group is we always fight to the end.”

That fighting spirit is exactly what WVU needs now.

Big 12 Tournament — WVU’s Last Stand

The Mountaineers opened Big 12 Tournament play Wednesday night at the T-Mobile Center in Kansas City. As the 7th seed, WVU faced the winner of the BYU-Kansas State first-round matchup.

For a team that likely needs to win the entire tournament to earn an automatic NCAA bid, every game is an elimination game. The pressure is immense, but this roster has shown it can rise to the occasion.

Moore reflected on the journey: “It was a crazy journey starting this summer. We were on that turf, and all of the practices and all of the hills we had to run. It was all meant for us to get better by 1% everyday.”

Most of this roster is experiencing high-major basketball for the first time. Regardless of what happens in Kansas City, the foundation Hodge is building is clear.

Honor Huff’s Breakout and the Season’s Bright Spots

Senior guard Honor Huff capped off his regular season with an All-Big 12 Honorable Mention selection. His consistency throughout a turbulent season gave the Mountaineers a reliable scoring threat.

The Pitt win stands out as the signature moment. A 22-point victory over a heated rival — in Hodge’s very first matchup against the Panthers — set the tone for what this program could become. Wins against ranked opponents like Kansas and BYU further validated the Mountaineers’ potential.

But the real story may be happening on the women’s side.

WVU Women’s Basketball — The Bigger Success Story

While the men fight for survival, the WVU women’s basketball program is thriving. The Mountaineers won the Big 12 regular season title and have climbed in the AP Poll as a result.

According to West Virginia University Athletics, the women’s team is on the “precipice of hosting NCAA Tournament games” — a historic achievement for the program. While the men need a tournament title to dance, the women are preparing to welcome teams to Morgantown as a host school.

It’s a remarkable contrast that highlights the depth of WVU athletics and the growing strength of women’s college basketball.

What This Means For WVU Fans

The men’s path is narrow but not impossible. College basketball is built on improbable runs, and a team with wins over Kansas and BYU has the talent to surprise. The key question is whether the Mountaineers can string together four or five consecutive wins when it matters most.

For the women’s program, the immediate future is bright. Hosting NCAA Tournament games would be a program-defining moment and a validation of everything the coaching staff has built.

Can WVU’s men pull off the Big 12 Tournament upset run they need? And how far can the women go in March Madness? The next week will define the Mountaineers’ 2026 season.
