Suns vs Spurs: Ultimate Season Series Breakdown and What It Means for the Western Conference

March 20, 2026 08:31 Trend Insights

Devin Booker averaged 33.9 points per game over his last seven appearances heading into the final Suns vs Spurs matchup of the season — and yet Phoenix’s defense has been so porous that putting up 30-plus still might not be enough.

The Phoenix Suns and San Antonio Spurs wrapped up their four-game regular-season series on March 19 at Frost Bank Center, capping a rivalry that has produced dramatic swings, monster stat lines, and serious playoff implications for both Western Conference squads.

Key Takeaways

  • The Suns won their early-season meetings against the Spurs, including snapping San Antonio’s historic 5-0 start
  • Phoenix’s offense has surged recently (7th in PPG over last seven games) but defense has cratered to 23rd in rating
  • Victor Wembanyama and De’Aaron Fox anchor a Spurs offense ranked 4th in the NBA
  • Dillon Brooks’ absence has been a major defensive blow for Phoenix

Suns vs Spurs Season Series: A Rivalry Renewed

The 2025-26 season has injected fresh energy into this Western Conference matchup. Both rosters look markedly different from years past, and the results have reflected that unpredictability.

Early in the season, Phoenix handed San Antonio its first loss in franchise-best fashion. The Spurs entered that game at 5-0, the best start in franchise history, but the Suns dominated with a 130-118 victory at Footprint Center. Phoenix was just 3-4 at the time, making the upset all the more surprising.

The Suns took round two on November 23 as well, winning 111-102 before a sellout crowd of 17,071 at Mortgage Matchup Center. Dillon Brooks paced Phoenix with 25 points, while Booker added 24. De’Aaron Fox led San Antonio with a game-high 26 points, but it was not enough.

By the time the teams met for their fourth and final contest on March 19 at Frost Bank Center, the stakes had shifted considerably — and so had both rosters’ health situations.

The Booker and Green Scoring Explosion

Phoenix’s offensive surge heading into the final matchup was hard to ignore.

Over his last seven games, Devin Booker put up 33.9 points per game, scoring 28 or more in six of those contests. He finished with exactly 27 in the seventh — meaning he did not dip below 27 points once in that entire stretch.

Jalen Green was equally electric. Across a six-game sample before a nine-point showing against the Minnesota Timberwolves on March 18, Green averaged 27.5 points per game, scoring 22 or more in five of those six outings.

Together, Booker and Green have transformed the Suns’ offense:

  • Season averages: 26th in PPG (112.4), 17th in offensive rating (113.9)
  • Last seven games: 7th in PPG (116.0), 8th in offensive rating (120.3)

That is a massive jump — the kind that can flip a team’s postseason trajectory if sustained. But there is a catch that should worry every Phoenix fan heading into the playoff stretch.

Phoenix’s Defensive Crisis Without Brooks

The offensive numbers tell one side of the story. The defense tells a far more troubling one.

With Dillon Brooks sidelined, the Suns have lost their most tenacious perimeter defender. The impact has been immediate and measurable:

A defensive rating of 119.2 over any meaningful stretch is alarming. It means Phoenix has been allowing opponents to score at a pace that only the league’s worst defenses can relate to. When Brooks was healthy earlier in the season, he was the difference-maker — his 25-point, high-energy performance in the November 23 win over San Antonio showed just how much he brings on both ends of the floor.

Without him, the Suns have been forced into a strategy of simply outscoring opponents. It worked often enough during the regular season, but against a Spurs team with the 4th-ranked offense in the NBA, that approach is a gamble.

Wembanyama, Fox, and San Antonio’s Offensive Firepower

San Antonio’s transformation into an elite offensive team has been one of the season’s best stories.

Victor Wembanyama entered the season averaging eye-popping numbers — at one point posting 30.2 points and 14.6 rebounds through the Spurs’ unbeaten start. While he has had quieter nights (he was held to nine points on 4-of-14 shooting with six turnovers in the early-season loss to Phoenix), his mere presence on the court warps defenses.

De’Aaron Fox has been the catalyst San Antonio needed as a secondary star. Fox scored a game-high 26 points in the November loss to the Suns, going 8-of-8 from the free-throw line in the first half alone. His speed and finishing ability give the Spurs a backcourt weapon that forces opponents to pick their poison between him and Wembanyama.

The supporting cast has stepped up as well:

  • Stephon Castle emerged as a reliable scorer, posting 26 points in the early-season loss to Phoenix
  • Keldon Johnson provided 19 points off the bench in that same game
  • Dylan Harper, the No. 2 overall pick, has shown flashes despite missing time with a calf injury sustained while defending a dunk attempt

The depth of San Antonio’s scoring threats is what makes their 4th-ranked offense so difficult to game-plan against.

What the Season Series Means for the Playoff Picture

The Suns’ early dominance in the head-to-head series might have masked a deeper issue. Winning individual games through sheer firepower is viable in the regular season — but the playoffs demand more.

For the Suns, the path forward is clear: get Dillon Brooks back healthy and find a way to stabilize a defense that has been hemorrhaging points. Booker and Green can carry the scoring load, but a team allowing opponents to score at a 119.2 clip will not survive a seven-game series against any legitimate contender.

For the Spurs, the season series losses to Phoenix should not cause panic. Wembanyama’s growth trajectory, Fox’s playmaking, and a roster built for both the present and the future give San Antonio legitimate reasons for optimism. The 5-0 franchise-best start showed what this group is capable of when healthy.

What This Means for You

If you are a fan of either team — or just someone tracking the Western Conference playoff race — this season series has offered a clear takeaway: both teams can score with anyone in the league, but neither has the defensive consistency to guarantee deep postseason runs.

The Suns’ window with Booker in his prime makes every game feel urgent. The Spurs’ window with Wembanyama is just opening, making patience a luxury San Antonio can actually afford.

As the regular season winds down and seeding battles intensify, the lessons from this four-game series will linger. Can Phoenix find the defensive balance it desperately needs before the playoffs? And can San Antonio’s young core turn regular-season offensive brilliance into postseason success?

The answers will shape the Western Conference for years to come.
