Utah Mammoth Lock In Nick Schmaltz with Massive $64 Million Extension

March 13, 2026 12:49 Trend Insights

The Utah Mammoth just made one of the biggest moves of the NHL season, and it signals exactly where this franchise is headed. By locking in Nick Schmaltz to an eight-year, $64 million contract extension, the Mammoth have removed one of the league’s top pending free agents from the open market and planted a flag for long-term competitiveness in Salt Lake City.

Key Takeaways:

  • Nick Schmaltz signs an 8-year extension worth $64 million ($8 million AAV)
  • The Mammoth hold 73 points through 65 games in their second season since relocating from Arizona
  • GM Bill Armstrong continues an aggressive roster-building approach
  • Utah plans to be aggressive in the summer trade market, particularly targeting centers
  • Defenseman Mikhail Sergachev returns to the lineup after missing over a week

The Schmaltz Deal: Breaking Down the Numbers

The contract, announced on Wednesday, March 11, carries a cap hit of $8.0 million per season over eight years. For a forward who was set to become an unrestricted free agent this summer, the deal represents a significant commitment from both sides.

Here is how the Schmaltz extension stacks up in context:

The $8 million AAV places Schmaltz among the higher-paid forwards in the league, but given the rising salary cap and his production, the Mammoth clearly view it as a cornerstone investment. By acting before free agency, Armstrong avoided a bidding war that could have driven the price even higher.

But how does this fit into the broader picture for a team still finding its identity in a new market?

Utah’s Second Season: Building Momentum in Salt Lake City

In just their second year since relocating from Arizona, the Mammoth are putting together a competitive campaign. With 73 points through 65 games, Utah is well-positioned in the Central Division and fighting for a playoff spot.

The franchise’s move to Salt Lake City has brought renewed energy, and the Schmaltz signing sends a clear message to the fanbase: this team is building something that lasts. Under GM Bill Armstrong, the Mammoth have “consistently been aggressive in terms of their roster decisions,” according to Yardbarker’s team coverage.

That aggression is not limited to re-signing their own players. Reports from Daily Faceoff indicate that the Mammoth will be aggressive in the summer, with a particular focus on acquiring centers to strengthen the roster’s spine. The Schmaltz extension locks in a key piece while freeing Armstrong to focus trade capital elsewhere.

The pipeline keeps flowing on the defensive end too. Mikhail Sergachev, the 27-year-old blueliner who has posted 42 points (10 goals, 32 assists) in 61 games, returned to the lineup on Thursday, March 12, against the Chicago Blackhawks after missing over a week. Sergachev reclaimed his spot on the top powerplay unit, giving the Mammoth a significant boost heading into the final stretch of the regular season.

So if the roster is coming together, why are there bumps along the way?

Recent Struggles: The Wild Loss and Blackhawks Rivalry

Not everything has been smooth for the Mammoth. On Tuesday, March 10, Utah traveled to face the Minnesota Wild at Grand Casino Arena and was dealt a 5-0 shutout loss. Kirill Kaprizov scored a highlight-reel goal, and Matt Boldy added to the tally as the Wild dominated from start to finish.

The loss snapped a three-game winning streak for Utah and exposed some defensive vulnerabilities. The Deseret News post-game analysis pointed to breakdowns in coverage and a lack of offensive zone time as the primary issues.

Adding to the frustration, the Chicago Blackhawks have been a thorn in Utah’s side. Chicago completed a sweep of the season series, including a 3-2 overtime win on Monday, March 9, at United Center where Frank Nazar buried his ninth goal of the season in extra time. The Mammoth faced the Blackhawks for the third time in less than two weeks on Thursday, March 12, at the Delta Center, with Chicago seeking to complete a four-game season sweep.

These losses sting, but the broader context matters. With 73 points and 17 games remaining, the Mammoth are in the thick of the playoff race. Short-term setbacks do not erase the long-term trajectory.

Speaking of the long term, what is the front office planning for the offseason?

Summer Plans: Aggressive Moves Ahead

The Schmaltz extension is just one piece of a larger puzzle. According to Daily Faceoff, the Mammoth are expected to be one of the most active teams during the offseason, with center depth being the top priority.

The Blues reportedly explored a deal that would have sent Robert Thomas to Utah, but the two sides were “unable to make a deal” for undisclosed reasons, according to Bleedin’ Blue. That willingness to swing for high-caliber talent, even if individual trades do not materialize, speaks to the front office’s mentality.

Key offseason priorities for the Mammoth include:

  • Acquiring a top-six center to complement the forward group
  • Maintaining defensive depth with Sergachev anchoring the blue line
  • Capitalizing on the rising salary cap to add premium talent
  • Building on the Salt Lake City market, which has embraced the team in its second season

The Bobby Brink trade, which sent the forward to the Wild, has already “paid off” for Minnesota according to Hockey Wilderness. Utah will need to ensure its own moves yield similar returns.

What This Means for Utah Mammoth Fans

For fans who watched the franchise relocate from the desert to the mountains, the Schmaltz extension is validation. An $8 million AAV commitment over eight years does not happen unless the organization believes it is building a contender.

The immediate focus is securing a playoff berth. With 73 points and the Sergachev return bolstering the lineup, the Mammoth have the pieces to make a push. But the bigger story is the summer. If Armstrong can add a legitimate center through trade or free agency, the Mammoth could enter the 2026-27 season as a genuine threat in the Central Division.

The Utah Mammoth are no longer just a relocated franchise searching for stability. They are a team making bold, expensive bets on the future. The question now is whether the on-ice results will match the front office ambition.

Will the Mammoth’s aggressive spending translate into playoff success this spring, or will the real payoff come in 2026-27 and beyond?
