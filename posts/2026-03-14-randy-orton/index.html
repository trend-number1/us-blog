Randy Orton Turns Heel: Viper Bloodies Cody Rhodes in Shocking WrestleMania 42 Contract Signing

March 14, 2026 12:38 Trend Insights

There is something uniquely chilling about watching a friendship die on live television. On the March 13 episode of WWE SmackDown, Randy Orton did exactly what millions of fans feared and many more secretly hoped for – he turned on Cody Rhodes during the WrestleMania 42 contract signing, leaving the American Nightmare bloodied in the ring.

The attack was sudden, methodical, and unmistakably Orton at his most dangerous. Just like that, the Viper shed his skin and reminded the wrestling world why he has been one of the most feared competitors in WWE history for over two decades.

Key Takeaways:

  • Randy Orton turned heel on the March 13 WWE SmackDown, attacking Cody Rhodes during their WrestleMania 42 contract signing
  • Orton bloodied Rhodes in a vicious assault at the Mortgage Matchup Center in Phoenix, Arizona
  • The heel turn sets up a deeply personal match at WrestleMania 42
  • Cody Rhodes had previously stated he believed he had “the upper hand” and was in the prime of his career
  • Reports suggest earlier creative plans for the WrestleMania match were scrapped in favor of this new direction

The Contract Signing That Changed Everything

Contract signings in WWE have a long and storied history of ending in chaos. Tables get flipped, chairs get thrown, and alliances crumble under the weight of WrestleMania-sized stakes. The March 13 SmackDown segment in Phoenix, Arizona followed that tradition – but with a brutality that felt different.

The scene at the Mortgage Matchup Center began with the expected tension. Both men sat across from each other, the contract for their WrestleMania 42 match laid out between them. For weeks, the relationship between Orton and Rhodes had been simmering with unspoken friction, a slow build that had fans speculating about when – not if – things would explode.

Rhodes, ever the confident champion, had been vocal in the lead-up. He told media that he believed he had “the upper hand” heading into WrestleMania and that he was performing in the prime of his career. It was the kind of self-assured statement that would have been unremarkable from most competitors. But directed at Randy Orton, it may have been the spark that lit the fuse.

The Attack: Calculated and Vicious

What separated this heel turn from the typical SmackDown brawl was its precision. Orton did not snap in a moment of rage. He waited. He signed the contract. And then, with the cold detachment that has defined his most menacing runs, he struck.

The assault left Cody Rhodes bloodied – a visual that WWE does not deploy lightly in the current era. The image of Rhodes, face covered in crimson, slumped in the ring while Orton stood over him, was designed to accomplish one thing: make this personal.

And it worked. In a matter of minutes, a WrestleMania match that had been building on mutual respect and competitive tension was transformed into something far more volatile. This is no longer about who is the better wrestler. This is about betrayal, ego, and the kind of deep-seated animosity that produces classic WrestleMania moments.

Why the Heel Turn Makes Perfect Sense

For those who have followed Randy Orton’s career, the heel turn should not come as a complete surprise. Orton has always been at his most compelling as a villain. The Legend Killer, the Apex Predator, the man who punted legends in the skull – these are the versions of Orton that defined eras in WWE.

His recent babyface run was effective, but there was always an undercurrent of something darker lurking beneath the surface. Consider the timeline:

  • Orton and Rhodes share a deep history dating back to Legacy and their intertwined family legacies in professional wrestling
  • Rhodes’s rising confidence and declarations about being in his prime may have triggered Orton’s competitive jealousy
  • The WrestleMania stage demands high-stakes storytelling, and a straightforward respectful match would not have delivered the same emotional punch

The heel turn allows WWE to tap into the richest vein of their shared history. These are two men who know each other’s weaknesses intimately, and now one of them has shown he is willing to exploit that knowledge without mercy.

Scrapped Plans and a New Creative Direction

Reports have emerged suggesting that earlier creative plans for the WrestleMania 42 match were scrapped in favor of this new direction. While the specifics of those original plans remain unclear, the pivot to a heel Orton vs. a bloodied, vengeful Rhodes tells a far more compelling story.

The decision to abandon the initial approach speaks to WWE’s willingness to adapt when a better narrative presents itself. A Rhodes vs. Orton match built on mutual admiration would have been good. A Rhodes vs. Orton match built on betrayal and blood has the potential to be great.

Here is how the creative shift changes the dynamics of the feud:

Rhodes’s Confidence: Fuel for the Fire

In the days before the contract signing, Cody Rhodes projected supreme confidence. His claim that he had “the upper hand” was not empty bravado – it was rooted in his current standing as one of WWE’s most dominant performers.

Rhodes has been operating at a level that few in WWE can match. His in-ring work has been consistently excellent, his promos have carried genuine emotional weight, and his connection with the audience remains one of the strongest in the company. He genuinely believes he is in the best stretch of his career.

But confidence can become a blind spot. And Randy Orton, who has spent more than twenty years studying opponents for precisely those blind spots, exploited it perfectly. Rhodes was so focused on the WrestleMania match ahead that he did not see the threat sitting right across from him.

That miscalculation cost him blood on Friday night. The question now is whether it will cost him more at WrestleMania.

What This Means for WrestleMania 42

The road to WrestleMania 42 just became significantly more unpredictable. With Orton now operating as a full heel, the creative possibilities expand dramatically:

  • Rhodes will be chasing revenge, giving him an underdog emotional edge despite being the established star
  • Orton’s mind games will likely escalate in the coming weeks, targeting Rhodes’s family legacy and personal vulnerabilities
  • The match itself could feature a no-holds-barred stipulation or other modifications that reflect the intensity of the feud
  • Phoenix, Arizona provides a massive WrestleMania stage for what is shaping up to be one of the most personal matches on the card

Rhodes himself had boldly predicted that WrestleMania 42 would deliver the best bell-to-bell action fans have ever seen. After what happened on SmackDown, that prediction feels less like hype and more like a promise forged in genuine anger.

The Bigger Picture: Orton’s Legacy Play

At this stage of his career, Randy Orton does not need championships to validate his legacy. What he needs are moments – the kind of defining segments and matches that cement a performer’s place in wrestling history.

Turning on Cody Rhodes at a WrestleMania contract signing is precisely that kind of moment. It instantly becomes one of the most talked-about heel turns in recent WWE history, and it positions the WrestleMania match as must-see television.

Orton understands something fundamental about professional wrestling: the villain makes the hero. By becoming the most dangerous version of himself, he elevates not just his own performance but the entire WrestleMania narrative. Rhodes does not just need to win a match anymore. He needs to overcome a betrayal.

Looking Ahead

The WWE SmackDown contract signing on March 13 will be remembered as the night Randy Orton reminded the world what he is capable of. The blood, the calculated violence, the cold expression on Orton’s face as he stood over a fallen Rhodes – these images will define the WrestleMania 42 build from this point forward.

Cody Rhodes entered the Mortgage Matchup Center believing he was in control. He left it bloodied and betrayed by a man he trusted. The road to WrestleMania just got a lot darker, a lot more personal, and a lot more dangerous.

One thing is certain: when Orton and Rhodes finally step into the ring at WrestleMania 42, there will be nothing left unsaid and nothing held back. The Viper has struck, and the American Nightmare now has every reason to make this the fight of his life.
