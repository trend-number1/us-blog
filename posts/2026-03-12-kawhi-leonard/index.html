Kawhi Leonard Fuels Clippers' Stunning Turnaround After Disastrous Start

March 12, 2026 Trend Insights

Few players in NBA history have carried a team from the edge of irrelevance back into the playoff picture the way Kawhi Leonard is doing right now. After the LA Clippers lost 21 of their first 27 games this season, most analysts had already written their obituary. Leonard had other plans.

Key Takeaways

  • Kawhi Leonard is averaging 28.0 points, 6.4 rebounds, 3.7 assists, and 2.0 steals per game on elite shooting splits
  • The Clippers clawed back from a 6-21 start to reach .500 at 32-32, surpassing the Warriors for 8th in the Western Conference
  • An NBA investigation into a $28 million endorsement deal with Aspiration has raised salary cap circumvention questions
  • Despite the turnaround, Leonard insists there is “nothing to be happy about” — the job is far from finished

Kawhi Leonard’s Monster Season by the Numbers

Leonard’s individual performance this season has been nothing short of extraordinary. At 34 years old, the two-time Finals MVP is putting up numbers that rival his peak seasons in San Antonio and Toronto.

Here’s what his stat line looks like:

  • 28.0 points per game — among the top scorers in the league
  • 6.4 rebounds per game — contributing on the glass
  • 3.7 assists per game — facilitating more than ever
  • 2.0 steals per game — still one of the premier defenders in the NBA
  • 49.4% from the field — ruthless efficiency
  • 38.1% from three-point range — stretching defenses
  • 90.5% from the free-throw line — near-automatic at the stripe

Those shooting splits put him in rare company. A player averaging 28 points on 49/38/90 shooting is performing at an MVP-caliber level, and Leonard is doing it while anchoring one of the best defenses in the second half of the season.

His recent performances tell the story even more vividly. Against the New York Knicks, Leonard poured in 29 points on 10-for-19 shooting, including 21 first-half points, as the Clippers cruised to a 126-118 victory. Against the Orlando Magic, he exploded for 37 points on 14-for-25 shooting, adding 8 rebounds, 4 assists, 3 steals, and 2 blocks in a complete two-way masterclass.

But perhaps the most telling moment came after that Knicks win, when a reporter asked Leonard about the team’s turnaround. His response was vintage Kawhi — blunt, unemotional, and laser-focused.

“Nothing to be happy about.”

That five-word answer encapsulates everything about Leonard’s mentality. He has been to the mountaintop before, winning the 2019 NBA championship with the Toronto Raptors and earning Finals MVP honors. Regular-season milestones don’t move the needle for him.

The Clippers’ Remarkable Turnaround

The story of the 2025-26 Clippers will be studied for years. Losing 21 of your first 27 games typically signals a franchise headed for the lottery, not a playoff push. Yet here they are, having climbed all the way back to .500 at 32-32 and surpassing the Golden State Warriors for 8th place in the Western Conference.

Several factors have fueled this resurgence:

  • Leonard’s health and consistency — after dealing with left ankle soreness earlier in the season, Leonard has strung together an impressive run of availability and dominance
  • Roster recalibration — the mid-season trades of James Harden and Ivica Zubac signaled a shift in the team’s identity, centering everything around Leonard
  • Defensive improvement — with Leonard’s elite perimeter defense setting the tone, the Clippers have become significantly harder to score against
  • Renewed chemistry — removing high-usage players allowed the supporting cast to find their roles and rhythm

The trade deadline moves were bold. Parting with Harden, a future Hall of Famer, and Zubac, a reliable center, could have fractured the locker room. Instead, it clarified the team’s mission: this is Kawhi’s team, and everyone else is here to support his chase for another ring.

It’s worth noting that the Clippers still owe an unprotected first-round pick to Oklahoma City from the Paul George trade, which makes their draft position consequential. Tanking was never truly an option — every loss made that pick more valuable to OKC and more costly for LA.

The $28 Million Question: NBA Investigation

While Leonard’s on-court dominance has been the headline, a significant off-court controversy is developing in the background. The NBA is investigating the Clippers over a $28 million endorsement deal between Leonard and Aspiration, a sustainability-focused financial company.

The core allegation centers on potential salary cap circumvention. The investigation is examining whether the endorsement deal, which critics have characterized as a “no-show” arrangement, was effectively a way for the Clippers to funnel additional compensation to Leonard outside of the salary cap structure.

The timeline matters here. Leonard arrived in Los Angeles after winning the 2019 NBA championship with Toronto, and the circumstances surrounding his free agency decision have come under renewed scrutiny.

The Clippers have firmly denied the allegations, calling Aspiration’s claims “absurd.” If the NBA finds evidence of cap circumvention, the penalties could be severe — ranging from fines and loss of draft picks to voiding portions of Leonard’s contract.

For now, Leonard has remained focused on basketball, and the investigation has not visibly affected his play. But it adds an undeniable layer of uncertainty to the Clippers’ future.

Trade Rumors and What Comes Next

Even as Leonard leads this improbable turnaround, trade rumors continue to swirl. Reports have linked Leonard to a potential deal with the Detroit Pistons in a four-player trade scenario, though nothing has materialized.

The question facing the Clippers’ front office is straightforward but difficult: Is this run sustainable, or is Leonard’s brilliance masking deeper roster issues?

The Clippers are essentially betting everything on Kawhi Leonard’s health and brilliance carrying them through a loaded Western Conference. It’s a high-risk, high-reward proposition — but then again, Leonard has delivered in these situations before.

The Bigger Picture

What makes Leonard’s season so compelling is the contrast between his quiet demeanor and his thunderous impact. He doesn’t give fiery postgame speeches or engage in social media battles. He shows up, dominates, and goes home.

His “nothing to be happy about” response after beating the Knicks wasn’t pessimism — it was a reminder of his championship standard. For a player who has won titles with two different franchises, reaching .500 in March is a waypoint, not a destination.

The next few weeks will be critical. Can the Clippers maintain their torrid second-half pace and lock in a playoff spot? Will the NBA investigation produce findings that shake the franchise? And can Leonard’s body hold up through a grueling stretch run?

One thing is certain: as long as Kawhi Leonard is healthy and on the court, the LA Clippers are dangerous. Every team in the Western Conference knows it. And if you’ve learned anything from Leonard’s career, it’s this — never count him out.

What do you think — can the Clippers make a deep playoff run, or is this turnaround destined to fall short? The answer may come down to one man’s health and will.
