IPL Standings 2026: Surprising Shake-Up at the Top

April 26, 2026 00:03 Trend Insights

Did you know Punjab Kings, a franchise that has never lifted the trophy, is the only unbeaten team six matches into IPL 2026? The IPL standings this season are rewriting expectations, and the defending champions are scrambling to catch up.

Quick Summary: Punjab Kings top the IPL 2026 points table with 11 points and a net run rate of +1.420. Royal Challengers Bengaluru moved to second after a five-wicket win over Gujarat Titans, powered by Virat Kohli’s 81. Sunrisers Hyderabad climbed into the top three after Match 31, while Mumbai Indians slipped to eighth.

The 19th edition of the Indian Premier League has expanded to 84 matches, with each of the 10 teams playing 16 round-robin games before the four-match playoff phase. That’s a denser, more punishing schedule than any previous season — and the standings reflect it.

Punjab Kings Lead an Unbeaten Charge

Punjab Kings have been the most consistent side in IPL 2026, remaining unbeaten in their first six matches. Their +1.420 net run rate is the strongest in the league, and they sit on 11 points heading into the next stretch.

That kind of dominance from a Punjab side is historically rare. The franchise reached the final last season but came up short against Royal Challengers Bengaluru. This year, they’ve flipped the script.

Why their lead matters: With 16 league games per team, an unbeaten start through six fixtures provides crucial cushion. Even two or three losses now wouldn’t dislodge them from the top tier of the IPL points table.

The key question heading into May: can any team actually catch them?

RCB’s Comeback and Kohli’s Masterclass

Royal Challengers Bengaluru, the defending champions, started slowly but have surged. After Match 34 at the M. Chinnaswamy Stadium, RCB beat Gujarat Titans by five wickets to climb to second place in the IPL standings.

The win marked RCB’s fifth of the season, taking their tally to 10 points from seven matches. They are now level on points with Rajasthan Royals but edge ahead on net run rate.

Chasing a target of 206 runs, Bengaluru completed it in 18.5 overs with five wickets in hand. The chase was anchored by:

  • Virat Kohli: 81 runs off 44 balls, including eight boundaries and four sixes
  • Devdutt Padikkal: 55 off 27 balls, with six sixes and two fours
  • A decisive 115-run second-wicket partnership built in just 59 balls

That partnership shifted momentum so completely that GT’s earlier 128-run opening stand — built on Sai Sudharsan’s century off 57 balls (the fastest ever to 2,000 IPL runs) — wasn’t enough.

So if Punjab is leading and RCB is rising, where does that leave everyone else?

The Middle and Bottom of the Table

The middle of the IPL 2026 points table is where the real chaos lives. Sunrisers Hyderabad climbed into the top three after a dominant win over Delhi Capitals in Match 31. Meanwhile, Chennai Super Kings moved up to fifth after Match 33, while Mumbai Indians slipped to eighth — a stunning slide for a five-time champion.

Delhi Capitals, despite a strong squad, are stuck in an indifferent campaign and are now relying on their home stretch to revive their playoff hopes.

A common misconception is that net run rate only matters at the end of the season. In reality, it’s already separating teams tied on points right now — RCB ahead of Rajasthan, Punjab’s lead cushioned by their +1.420.

How the Points System Actually Works

Plenty of fans follow the IPL points table without knowing the underlying math. Here’s the simple version:

  1. Win = 2 points
  2. Loss = 0 points
  3. Tie / No Result = 1 point each
  4. Tiebreaker = Net Run Rate (NRR)

NRR is calculated as the difference between runs scored per over faced and runs conceded per over bowled across the season. It’s why blowout wins matter even when the two points are already secured.

With 84 total matches this year — up from previous seasons — the NRR cushion teams build early can decide playoff spots in May.

What This Means For You as a Fan

If you’re tracking the IPL 2026 standings for fantasy leagues, betting markets, or just bragging rights, three actionable takeaways:

  • Don’t write off Punjab Kings. Their NRR provides a real margin even if they hit a rough patch.
  • Watch RCB’s away form. Their last home game in Bengaluru is done — the road tests reveal whether they can defend the title.
  • Mumbai’s slide is real. Eighth place isn’t a fluke at this stage; the squad has structural issues to solve.

Are you ready to follow one of the most competitive IPL seasons in years?

Final Thoughts

The 2026 IPL standings tell a story no one predicted in March: an unbeaten Punjab Kings, a defending champion clawing back, a former dynasty stumbling. With 16 league games per team and net run rate as the silent kingmaker, every match between now and the playoffs will reshape the table.

So here’s the thought-provoking question: if Punjab Kings finish the league phase unbeaten — or close to it — would that make them favorites against a battle-hardened RCB in the playoffs, or would experience win out when it matters most?
