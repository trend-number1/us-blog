Andrew Rannells: The Surprising Second Act of Broadway’s Brightest Star

What does it take to go from singing “I Believe” on a Tony stage to playing a stepdad in a Hulu crime comedy — and starring in a brand-new HBO Max drama in the same season? Just ask Andrew Rannells, who in June 2026 has somehow become the most quietly omnipresent performer in American entertainment.

Quick Summary: Andrew Rannells, 47, is currently starring in All In on Broadway, returning for season 2 of Hulu’s Deli Boys, and earning rave reviews opposite Allison Janney in HBO Max’s Miss You, Love You. He recently opened up about life as a stepdad to partner Tuc Watkins’ twins and the lasting impact of HBO’s Girls.

If you’ve felt like Andrew Rannells is suddenly everywhere this spring, that’s because he is. And the reasons behind his hot streak say something interesting about how stage actors are quietly winning streaming in 2026.

Who Is Andrew Rannells? The Broadway Origin Story

Andrew Scott Rannells was born August 23, 1978, in Omaha, Nebraska, the fourth of five siblings. He attended Creighton Preparatory School, an all-boys Roman Catholic school, before leaving the Catholic Church after graduation — a biographical detail that would later resonate with one of his most iconic roles.

That role, of course, was Elder Kevin Price in the 2011 Broadway production of The Book of Mormon. The performance earned him a Tony nomination for Best Actor in a Musical and a 2012 Grammy Award for Best Musical Theater Album. He’d later earn a second Tony nomination — Best Featured Actor in a Musical — for his 2017 turn as Whizzer in the Broadway revival of Falsettos.

His Broadway résumé reads like a tour of the modern American musical:

  • Hairspray (2005)
  • Jersey Boys (2009)
  • The Book of Mormon (2011)
  • Hedwig and the Angry Inch (2014)
  • Hamilton (2015)
  • Falsettos (2016)
  • The Boys in the Band (2018)
  • Gutenberg! The Musical! (2023)

He was also nominated for a Laurence Olivier Award for the off–West End production of Tammy Faye. Not many performers can claim that range across two continents — and that’s before you even get to the screen work.

So how did a guy from Omaha end up dominating both Broadway and prestige TV in the same week? Let’s look at what’s actually on his 2026 schedule.

The 2026 Hot Streak: Three Projects, One Star

Andrew Rannells isn’t just having a moment — he’s having a deliberately engineered career peak. Here’s what’s airing, streaming, and selling out right now:

According to Variety’s review of Miss You, Love You, both Allison Janney and Andrew Rannells shine in the new HBO Max series. Decider weighed in days later with their classic “Stream It or Skip It” verdict — a sign of just how much buzz the show is generating.

Meanwhile, BroadwayWorld released a fresh clip from Deli Boys season 2 on June 7, showing Rannells stepping into character. And Playbill confirmed he’ll be appearing alongside Lea Salonga and Alex Newell at a Broadway fan convention in Las Vegas.

Three high-profile projects, one Broadway run, and a fan convention — all in a single month. That’s not luck. That’s craft, timing, and the kind of relationship-building that comes from two decades in the business.

But here’s where it gets personal — and unexpectedly moving.

The Personal Story Fans Are Talking About

In a recent appearance on Today with Jenna & Friends, Rannells opened up about something that surprised even him: becoming a stepdad.

The actor has been linked to partner Tuc Watkins — best known for Desperate Housewives and One Life to Live — since 2019. Watkins has twins, Catchen and Curtis (now 13), and Rannells admits parenting wasn’t part of his original plan.

“My boyfriend has two kids. They were 6 years old when we started dating and that was not on my radar, actually,” Rannells told Today. “It’s been really unexpected. And there’s no good way to prepare for it.”

In a separate exclusive with People.com, Rannells described his morning routine in vivid detail: doggie Prozac, show tunes, and kids who are “horrified” by his outfits. It’s the kind of self-deprecating warmth that’s made him a fan favorite well beyond the theater crowd.

Back in 2023, he’d told PEOPLE that LGBTQ+ progress had left him genuinely uncertain about parenthood. “It’s very tricky now that we have the option to have kids. It was the same thing with marriage. When that became possible, everyone was like, ‘You gonna get married?’ And I was like, ‘Well, I don’t know.’”

His sister Natalie put it more bluntly: “Welcome to my world.”

That honesty — about ambivalence, about being caught off guard by domesticity — is precisely what makes audiences trust him. And it’s a reminder that the most interesting actors are often the ones still figuring it out in public.

The Girls Legacy and a Surprising Inspiration

Speaking of figuring things out in public: Rannells played Elijah on HBO’s Girls from 2012 to 2017, and the role still defines a generation’s view of him.

In a recent Fathom Journal interview, he reflected on the impact of the Lena Dunham–led series. And in late May, The Daily Beast reported that the show’s star revealed a CNN anchor ex-boyfriend who helped inspire the show — a juicy footnote that’s kept Girls in the cultural conversation nearly a decade after it ended.

A few quick facts Rannells shared in his Us Weekly “25 Things You Don’t Know About Me” feature reveal a performer who hasn’t lost his sense of play:

  1. He once sang songs from The Book of Mormon at Maude Apatow’s Sweet 16
  2. His favorite Book of Mormon song to perform is “I Believe”
  3. His go-to home dish is turkey meatballs — the first thing he learned to cook that the twins actually liked
  4. He does not read his own reviews, a lesson he learned early
  5. He learned to blow-dry his hair into a “solid pompadour” during Hairspray

Important note on misinformation: A story circulating from MediaMass claiming Rannells was retiring from acting at 47 has been explicitly flagged as false by the site itself, with an update dated June 8, 2026. With three active projects, the actor is clearly nowhere near hanging it up.

What This Means For You

Why does any of this matter beyond the gossip pages? Because Andrew Rannells’ 2026 is a masterclass in career longevity — and there’s something actionable in it.

He didn’t abandon theater when TV came calling. He didn’t ditch TV when streaming exploded. He didn’t pivot away from comedy when prestige drama got hot. He stacked, layered, and showed up consistently for two full decades.

The result: at 47, he’s juggling Broadway, network promo, prestige streaming, and a personal life he talks about with disarming honesty. That’s the kind of cross-platform presence that used to take a publicist a decade to manufacture.

For anyone watching the entertainment industry, the lesson is simple — range and reliability beat virality every time.

So here’s the question worth sitting with: when audiences flock to a performer who’s been quietly excellent for 20 years, are we finally getting tired of overnight stars? Or is Andrew Rannells just the rare exception who makes the slow path look easy?

Either way, if you haven’t caught Miss You, Love You on HBO Max or Deli Boys season 2 on Hulu yet — now’s the moment. The Broadway tickets, unfortunately, are a tougher get.
