Broncos Depth Chart 2026: Blockbuster Trade and Free Agency Reshape Denver's Roster

March 18, 2026 00:39 Trend Insights

The Denver Broncos are not the same team they were two weeks ago. After a blockbuster trade for a star wide receiver and an aggressive first wave of NFL free agency, the Broncos depth chart has undergone a transformation that signals one thing clearly: Denver is done waiting and ready to compete right now.

Key Takeaway:

  • The Broncos completed a blockbuster trade for a star wide receiver on March 17, dramatically upgrading their passing attack
  • Denver’s first wave of free agency brought significant roster changes across multiple position groups
  • Running back J.K. Dobbins was re-signed, solidifying the backfield alongside RJ Harvey
  • The 2025 season exposed serious depth issues at wide receiver after injuries to Troy Franklin and Pat Bryant
  • Denver’s projected depth chart for 2026 reflects a franchise shifting from rebuild to contention mode

A Blockbuster Trade Changes Everything

The biggest headline rocking the Broncos depth chart came on March 17, when Denver completed a blockbuster trade to acquire a star wide receiver. The move was reported by Broncos Wire and immediately reshuffled the entire offensive pecking order in Denver.

This trade didn’t happen in a vacuum. The Broncos’ wide receiver corps was exposed during the 2025 season when injuries struck at the worst possible time. Both Troy Franklin and Pat Bryant went down with injuries, leaving Denver scrambling to fill gaps in their receiving depth chart. The lack of a true number-one target was painfully apparent, and the front office clearly took that lesson to heart heading into the offseason.

By making this aggressive move before the 2026 NFL Draft, the Broncos have signaled that they’re not content to wait for developmental prospects to mature. They want a proven weapon now — and they went out and got one.

So what does Denver’s offense look like with this new addition at the top of the depth chart?

Offense: The Projected Broncos Depth Chart

The first wave of free agency and the blockbuster trade have reshaped Denver’s offensive roster from top to bottom. Here’s how the key position groups stack up heading into the spring:

Quarterback

The quarterback room remains the foundation of everything Denver is building. The 2025 season established the starter as the franchise signal-caller going forward, and the front office has spent this offseason surrounding him with better talent. The depth chart at QB is settled, which allows the coaching staff to focus resources on other positions.

Wide Receiver

This is where the most dramatic changes have occurred. The Broncos depth chart at wide receiver looked like a weakness entering the offseason — now it looks like a potential strength. The newly acquired star receiver immediately slots in as the WR1, pushing the rest of the depth chart into more favorable roles.

PositionBefore Free AgencyAfter Moves
WR1Uncertain — Franklin/Bryant returning from injuryNew blockbuster trade acquisition
WR2Troy Franklin (injury concern)Troy Franklin (healthy, reduced pressure)
WR3Pat Bryant (injury concern)Pat Bryant (depth role, eased workload)
DepthThin — 2025 exposed lack of optionsSignificantly improved

The key insight here is that this trade doesn’t just add a WR1 — it improves every position beneath it. Franklin and Bryant no longer need to carry the load as primary targets. They can develop at their own pace while the new star commands defensive attention on the outside.

Running Back

The Broncos locked down their backfield early in free agency by re-signing J.K. Dobbins on March 11. Dobbins provides a proven, veteran presence at the position, giving Denver a reliable option on early downs and in short-yardage situations.

Behind Dobbins, RJ Harvey has generated real optimism in the Denver Broncos backfield. As reported by multiple outlets in mid-March, Harvey provides the kind of explosive, versatile skill set that modern NFL offenses need. The combination of Dobbins’ consistency and Harvey’s upside gives Denver a genuine one-two punch at running back.

  • J.K. Dobbins — Re-signed; lead back, early-down and short-yardage specialist
  • RJ Harvey — Dynamic change-of-pace back; explosive playmaking ability
  • Depth pieces — To be addressed through the draft or remaining free agency

This is a running back room that went from a question mark to a position of relative stability in just a few days of free agency.

Defense: Filling the Gaps

While the offensive moves have grabbed the biggest headlines, the Broncos’ defensive depth chart has also been a focus during this free agency window. Denver’s defense was competitive in 2025, but the front office identified areas that needed reinforcement.

The first wave of free agency typically sets the tone for a team’s defensive identity, and the Broncos have been active in targeting players who fit their scheme. Depth along the defensive line and in the secondary were known priorities entering the offseason.

What makes Denver’s approach notable is the balance between youth and experience. The Broncos aren’t loading up exclusively on expensive veterans or gambling entirely on unproven talent. They’re building a roster with a healthy mix of both — a strategy that tends to produce more sustainable results than going all-in on one approach.

How Denver’s Depth Chart Compares to the AFC West

The Broncos don’t exist in a vacuum. Every move they make has to be evaluated against what the Kansas City Chiefs, Las Vegas Raiders, and Los Angeles Chargers are doing. Here’s a snapshot of how the AFC West is shaping up:

TeamOffseason ApproachBiggest Move
Denver BroncosAggressive — blockbuster trade + active free agencyStar WR acquisition via trade
Kansas City ChiefsTargeted additions around championship corePotential Tyreek Hill reunion
Las Vegas RaidersRebuilding — seeking foundational piecesRoster-wide turnover
Los Angeles ChargersStrategic upgrades around Jim Harbaugh’s systemComplementary signings

Denver’s moves stand out for their boldness. While the Raiders are still finding their identity and the Chargers are making incremental improvements, the Broncos are swinging for the fences. Whether that aggression translates to wins depends on health, chemistry, and the continued development of their young quarterback.

What This Means for Broncos Fans

The 2025 season taught Denver a painful lesson: talent on paper means nothing if it can’t stay on the field. The injuries to Franklin and Bryant exposed just how thin the roster was at key positions, and the front office has responded with urgency.

Here’s what fans should watch for as the depth chart continues to evolve:

  1. NFL Draft (April) — Denver still has draft capital to address remaining needs, particularly along the offensive line and in the secondary
  2. OTAs and minicamp — The first chance to see the new WR1 integrated into the offense
  3. Training camp battles — The running back competition between Dobbins and Harvey could be one of the most compelling storylines in camp
  4. Second wave of free agency — Bargain signings in April and May often produce surprising contributors

The Broncos depth chart is a living document that will continue to change between now and September. But the foundation has been laid, and it’s a dramatically better foundation than what Denver had just two weeks ago.

The question every Broncos fan should be asking isn’t whether the roster has improved — that much is obvious. The real question is whether these moves are enough to dethrone Kansas City in the AFC West. What’s your take: are the Broncos a legitimate contender, or still a year away?
