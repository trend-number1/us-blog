De'Aaron Fox's Surprising New Role With the Spurs: What It Means for San Antonio

April 2, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

When a franchise point guard who averaged over 25 points per game for multiple seasons suddenly finds himself in a secondary offensive role, it raises eyebrows across the league. That’s exactly what’s happening with De’Aaron Fox on the San Antonio Spurs — and surprisingly, it might be working.

Key Takeaways

  • Fox is averaging 17.9 points per game since the beginning of March 2026, adjusting to a complementary role alongside Victor Wembanyama
  • He has been named a finalist for an NBA award honoring high character and leadership
  • The Fox-LaVine trade’s impact on both the Spurs and Sacramento Kings is still being debated
  • Despite a rough outing against Chicago (7 points on 3-8 shooting), the Spurs are surging heading into the final stretch

De’Aaron Fox and the Spurs’ Winning Formula

The San Antonio Spurs have been on a tear, and the arrival of De’Aaron Fox has been a significant part of the equation — even if his stat line doesn’t always scream superstar.

Fox came to San Antonio via the blockbuster Fox-LaVine trade that reshaped both rosters. In Sacramento, Fox was the guy. He was the engine of every offensive set, the closer in crunch time, and the face of the franchise. In San Antonio, that spotlight belongs to Victor Wembanyama.

The adjustment hasn’t been seamless. Fox has been relegated to a secondary role in the Spurs’ offensive scheme, according to multiple reports. But here’s the thing — the Spurs are winning, and Fox seems to be embracing the shift rather than fighting it.

His March numbers tell the story: 17.9 points per game heading into the final days of the month. That’s a far cry from his Sacramento peak, but it’s efficient, purposeful basketball from a veteran who understands what this team needs.

So what does Fox’s new reality actually look like on the court?

A Rough Night Against Chicago — But Context Matters

Fox’s most recent outing on March 30 was one to forget on the surface. Against the Chicago Bulls, he posted just seven points on 3-of-8 shooting (0-for-3 from three-point range, 1-of-2 from the free-throw line), adding two rebounds, six assists, and two steals across 23 minutes.

The Spurs won 129-114 anyway.

Wembanyama and Stephon Castle carried the load in what amounted to a routine 15-point blowout. Fox’s poor shooting didn’t have much impact on the outcome, which is both a relief and a reality check.

That seven-point output tied his second-worst scoring mark since the All-Star break. But context matters here:

  • His March average of 17.9 PPG suggests this was an outlier, not a trend
  • The blowout nature of the game meant reduced minutes (just 23)
  • His six assists and two steals still contributed to the winning effort
  • The Spurs’ depth means Fox doesn’t need to force shots in comfortable wins

One bad game doesn’t define a player’s value. But it does raise questions about how Fox fits when the stakes are highest — in the playoffs, when every possession matters.

The Fox-LaVine Trade: Did It Actually Work?

The trade that sent De’Aaron Fox to San Antonio and Zach LaVine to Sacramento remains one of the most debated moves of the season. Sacramento media has been asking the question directly: did the Fox-LaVine trade actually help the Kings?

Here’s how the two sides stack up since the deal:

FactorSpurs (with Fox)Kings (with LaVine)
Primary benefitVeteran guard alongside WembanyamaScoring punch, salary flexibility
Fox/LaVine roleSecondary playmakerPrimary scorer
Team trajectorySurging toward playoffsRebuilding timeline unclear
ChemistryFox adapting to new hierarchyLaVine fitting into new system

For the Spurs, the answer seems clearer. Fox brings playoff experience, defensive intensity, and leadership — intangibles that don’t always show up in box scores. His willingness to defer to Wembanyama shows maturity that few star players demonstrate after being traded.

The Kings’ side of the equation is murkier and will take more time to evaluate fully.

Award Recognition: Fox’s Character Shines Through

Perhaps the most telling development came this week: De’Aaron Fox and teammate Harrison Barnes were both named finalists for NBA awards honoring high character. The recognition highlights what Fox brings beyond his on-court production.

Being nominated for a character-based award in your first partial season with a new team speaks volumes. It suggests that Fox has:

  • Embraced the locker room rather than sulking about his diminished role
  • Mentored younger players like Stephon Castle and No. 2 overall pick Dylan Harper
  • Set the right example for a young franchise building toward championship contention

Wembanyama is the generational talent. Castle and Harper represent the future backcourt. But Fox is the veteran glue holding it together — and the NBA has noticed.

This kind of leadership is exactly what the Spurs lacked in previous seasons when their young core was still learning how to win.

The Bigger Picture: Fox’s Season Arc

Fox’s journey this season has been anything but straightforward. He missed the season opener due to a strained right hamstring sustained during training camp. That absence gave Harper and Castle extended backcourt minutes alongside Devin Vassell, and the Spurs looked dominant — Wembanyama dropped 40 points in a blowout of the Dallas Mavericks on opening night.

When Fox returned, the question became: how do you integrate a star point guard into a team that proved it could win without him?

The answer, several months in, is that Fox has accepted a role transformation rather than demanding the ball. His assist numbers remain solid, his defense is engaged, and his scoring comes within the flow of the offense rather than through isolation dominance.

MetricFox (Sacramento era)Fox (Spurs, March 2026)
Points per game25+17.9
Primary roleLead scorer, primary ball handlerSecondary playmaker
Team successInconsistent playoff appearancesSurging toward deep playoff run
Offensive hierarchy#1 optionBehind Wembanyama

The numbers are down. The wins are up. For a player entering the prime years of his career, that trade-off isn’t always easy to accept — but Fox appears to be making it work.

What This Means for the Spurs’ Playoff Push

As San Antonio enters the final stretch of the regular season, Fox’s role will be tested in a new way. Regular-season blowouts are forgiving. Playoff basketball is not.

The Spurs need Fox to be the player who can take over when Wembanyama is doubled or in foul trouble. They need his speed in transition, his ability to collapse defenses, and his experience in high-pressure moments.

A few things to watch:

  1. Scoring consistency — Can Fox push back above 20 PPG when games tighten?
  2. Late-game execution — Will the Spurs trust Fox with the ball in clutch situations?
  3. Defensive engagement — His steals and active hands are valuable in a playoff setting
  4. Chemistry with Wembanyama — The pick-and-roll potential between these two is elite

The surging Spurs have the pieces. The question is whether Fox can elevate from a secondary regular-season role to a co-star in the postseason.

One thing is clear: De’Aaron Fox didn’t come to San Antonio to collect a paycheck. His award nomination, his willingness to adapt, and his steady March production all point to a player who is invested in winning, even if it means sacrificing individual glory.

What do you think — can Fox reclaim his All-Star form when the playoffs begin, or has his role permanently shifted? The answer could determine how far these Spurs go this spring.
