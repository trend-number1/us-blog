Rocket Launch Today: Your Ultimate Guide to This Week's Space Missions

March 23, 2026 00:03 Trend Insights

A SpaceX Falcon 9 just lit up the Florida sky on March 22, and at least four more launches are lined up before the month ends. If you’ve been searching “rocket launch today,” you’re in the right place.

Quick Summary

  • March 22: SpaceX launched 29 Starlink satellites from Cape Canaveral (completed)
  • March 24: Starlink Group 17-17 scheduled from Vandenberg, CA
  • March 26: Starlink Group 10-44 from Cape Canaveral, FL
  • March 29: Atlas V carrying Amazon Kuiper satellites + SpaceX Transporter 16 rideshare
  • Also this week: Roscosmos Progress 94 cargo spacecraft docking to the ISS

Rocket Launch Schedule: What’s Flying This Week

The final week of March 2026 is packed. SpaceX alone has three Falcon 9 flights on the calendar, and United Launch Alliance is preparing a significant Atlas V mission for Amazon’s satellite internet constellation.

The March 22 Starlink launch carried 29 Starlink v2 Mini satellites into low-Earth orbit. The Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station at 10:47 a.m. EDT (14:47 UTC), with the booster designated B1078 completing another successful landing.

But the real story is what comes next.

SpaceX Dominates the Launch Pad — Again

SpaceX’s Starlink constellation continues its relentless expansion. The March 24 mission from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California will deploy 25 more Starlink satellites, while the March 26 flight from Cape Canaveral adds another 29.

That’s 83 Starlink satellites in a single week across three launches.

This pace is nothing new for SpaceX. The company has been averaging multiple launches per week throughout 2026, building toward a mega-constellation designed to provide space-based broadband internet across the globe.

The GPS Satellite Switch

In a notable development this month, the U.S. Space Force moved a GPS satellite launch from ULA’s Vulcan rocket to a SpaceX Falcon 9 due to a technical glitch with the Vulcan. This shift underscores both the reliability of the Falcon 9 platform and the growing confidence government agencies place in SpaceX for critical national security missions.

Meanwhile, Rocket Lab successfully launched a private Japanese “Strix” satellite on March 20, proving that the small-launch market remains competitive alongside SpaceX’s dominance.

Amazon’s Atlas V Launch: A Different Kind of Space Race

The most anticipated mission of the week may be the March 29 Atlas V 551 launch carrying Amazon’s Leo satellites as part of the company’s Project Kuiper — Amazon’s answer to SpaceX’s Starlink.

Scheduled for liftoff at 7:53 AM UTC from Cape Canaveral, this is the fifth Amazon Leo launch (LA-05) aboard a United Launch Alliance Atlas V. Amazon has committed to deploying a constellation of 3,236 satellites to deliver affordable broadband worldwide.

The competition between these two programs is driving unprecedented launch frequency — and that benefits everyone who wants better internet coverage, especially in rural and underserved areas.

How to Watch: Viewing Tips and Livestream Links

Whether you’re in Florida, California, or watching from your couch, here’s how to catch the action.

Best Viewing Spots in Florida

For Cape Canaveral launches, Florida Today recommends these locations:

  • Playalinda Beach — closest public beach to the launch pads
  • Jetty Park in Port Canaveral — great unobstructed views
  • Max Brewer Bridge in Titusville — popular with photographers
  • Space View Park in Titusville — dedicated rocket-watching spot
  • Cocoa Beach Pier — combine a beach day with a launch

For the March 26 Starlink launch from Cape Canaveral, check the exact launch time (currently 11:22 AM UTC / 7:22 AM EDT) and arrive at least an hour early for a good spot.

California Launches

Vandenberg Space Force Base launches on March 24 can be visible along the central California coast. The rocket’s trajectory often creates stunning visual effects visible from Los Angeles to the San Francisco Bay Area, especially for evening or predawn launches.

Livestreams

  • SpaceX: Streams all launches on their website and YouTube, typically starting 15 minutes before liftoff
  • NASA+: The agency’s free, ad-free streaming service covers ISS docking events and select missions — no subscription required
  • NASA App: Available on all major platforms with live ISS video feeds

NASA is currently streaming coverage of the Roscosmos Progress 94 cargo spacecraft docking to the International Space Station, available on Amazon Prime, YouTube, and NASA+.

Beyond This Week: Missions to Watch in 2026

The launch tempo isn’t slowing down. Several landmark missions are coming up on the 2026 space calendar:

  • Isar Aerospace Spectrum — The second test flight of this European launch vehicle will carry five cubesats for ESA’s “Boost!” program, including satellites from TU Berlin, University of Maribor, and TU Wien
  • ESA LEO-PNT — Two “Pathfinder A” satellites built by Thales Alenia Space will demonstrate low-Earth orbit navigation to complement Europe’s Galileo system at 510 km altitude
  • Artemis II — NASA’s first crewed mission of the Artemis program will send astronauts Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Hammock Koch, and Jeremy Hansen on a lunar flyby

The European launches are particularly significant. The Isar Spectrum flight represents a new generation of European commercial launch vehicles, while the LEO-PNT mission could reshape satellite navigation by combining LEO and higher-orbit constellations.

What This Means for You

The sheer volume of rocket launches in 2026 signals a fundamental shift. Space is no longer the exclusive domain of government agencies — it’s becoming infrastructure. Every Starlink batch, every Kuiper deployment, and every small-sat rideshare launch is building the backbone of future connectivity.

If you’re near the Florida Space Coast or central California, you have front-row seats to history happening multiple times a week. If not, livestreams make every launch accessible from anywhere.

Want to stay on top of launch schedules? Sites like SpaceLaunchSchedule.com, SpaceLaunchNow.me, and RocketLaunch.Live offer real-time tracking, calendar subscriptions, and instant notifications so you never miss a liftoff.

What’s the most exciting mission on your radar this year — the relentless Starlink expansion, Amazon’s Kuiper challenge, or NASA’s Artemis II crewed lunar flyby?
