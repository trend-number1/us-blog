Ryan McMahon's Alarming Slump: What's Next for the Yankees Third Baseman?

April 9, 2026 12:03 Trend Insights

When the New York Yankees traded for Ryan McMahon at the 2025 deadline, they believed they were locking down elite defense at third base for years to come. Barely into the 2026 season, McMahon is hitting .087 slugging through his first 23 at-bats — and the conversation has already shifted from swing fixes to potential replacements.

Key Takeaways

  • McMahon is 2-for-23 with 11 strikeouts to start 2026, a 40% whiff rate far above his 32.3% career mark
  • Top prospect George Lombard Jr. could be called up before the All-Star break, per MLB insider Jim Bowden
  • The Yankees may also explore trades involving outfield prospects Jasson Dominguez or Spencer Jones
  • McMahon is in the fifth year of a six-year, $70 million contract

Ryan McMahon’s Early-Season Numbers Paint a Grim Picture

The Yankees acquired McMahon from the Colorado Rockies last July, sending pitching prospects Griffin Herring and Josh Grosz the other way. At the time, McMahon was a National League All-Star and ranked in the 91st percentile with 4 outs above average at the hot corner — a legitimate Gold Glove-caliber defender.

His bat was always the question mark. McMahon hit .214 with 20 home runs and 53 RBI last season, respectable power numbers but a thin batting average. The Yankees’ hitting staff spent the offseason working with him to iron out swing mechanics that manager Aaron Boone acknowledged “get him in trouble a little bit.”

Those fixes haven’t taken hold yet. Through early April, McMahon’s stat line is alarming:

Batting Average.087~.240
Slugging %.087~.430

McMahon’s 32.3% career strikeout rate was already the highest among qualified hitters last season. Bumping that to 40% — nearly half his at-bats ending in a whiff — is unsustainable for any lineup spot.

He finally snapped an 0-for-22 skid with a single in the sixth inning of the Yankees’ 7-6 loss to Miami on Sunday, then drew a walk in the eighth. Small steps, but Boone was looking for silver linings.

“I like the ball he hit to left and his at-bats were better than we saw in Seattle,” Boone said. “I thought his intent was good on his swings on a couple of fastballs.”

So is this a slow start that corrects itself, or something deeper?

The Coors Field Factor Still Haunts McMahon

One detail that often gets overlooked: McMahon spent the first eight-plus years of his career playing home games at Coors Field, the most hitter-friendly park in baseball. His career .738 OPS looks solid on paper, but strip away the altitude boost, and the picture dims.

The Yankees knew this going in. They acquired McMahon primarily for his defense, not his bat. But a lineup that already features slumping bats from José Caballero and Austin Wells at the bottom of the order can’t afford a third baseman producing like a pitcher at the plate.

McMahon has shown occasional hard contact — a few hard-hit balls on the West Coast trip and one in Saturday’s win over Miami — but as the New York Post noted, those drives are going into the ground rather than leaving the yard. The power the Yankees hoped he’d flash after his swing overhaul has been largely absent.

The question hanging over the Bronx: how long is long enough to wait?

George Lombard Jr. Looms as the Heir Apparent

The Yankees’ top prospect, George Lombard Jr., didn’t make the Opening Day roster, but his shadow over McMahon’s spot is growing by the day.

MLB insider Jim Bowden of The Athletic has already made a bold prediction: Lombard will be called up before the All-Star break and become the everyday third baseman for the remainder of the season.

Boone himself hasn’t shut the door on that possibility. Asked about Lombard during spring training, the manager said, “I wouldn’t rule it out” when discussing the prospect’s potential mid-season impact.

If Lombard does take over at third, McMahon’s future becomes murky. A few possible scenarios:

  • Utility role — McMahon becomes a bench player who can fill in across the infield
  • Trade chip — The Yankees move McMahon to a team that values his glove and can absorb the remaining contract
  • Platoon arrangement — McMahon splits time with Lombard while the rookie adjusts to big-league pitching

With McMahon still owed significant money through 2027 on his $70 million deal, the financial flexibility of each option matters.

Could a Blockbuster Trade Be the Answer?

If the Yankees decide McMahon isn’t the long-term solution, they have another card to play: their surplus of outfield talent. Both Jasson Dominguez and Spencer Jones are currently at Triple-A Scranton/Wilkes-Barre, blocked from MLB playing time by a crowded outfield headlined by Aaron Judge.

Trading one of those high-upside outfielders for an established third baseman is already being discussed. Among the potential targets floated by analysts:

  • Brett Baty or Mark Vientos (New York Mets) — A crosstown swap would be dramatic but not unprecedented
  • Royce Lewis (Minnesota Twins) — A talented infielder who “could use a change of scenery”
  • Alec Bohm (Philadelphia Phillies) — Frequently mentioned in trade rumors

The calculus is straightforward: do the Yankees want to win a World Series with Aaron Judge in his prime? If so, having a productive third baseman alongside a lineup already anchored by Judge might require sacrificing one promising outfield prospect.

No concrete trade talks have surfaced yet, but as one analysis put it, the Yankees “can only put up with hitting like that for so much longer.”

What This Means for Yankees Fans

The 2026 season is young. McMahon has a track record of producing 20-plus home runs across six full seasons, and his glove remains elite. But the early warning signs — a ballooning strikeout rate, zero extra-base hits, and a swing that hasn’t responded to offseason adjustments — are real.

Here’s the realistic timeline:

  1. Now through April: McMahon gets every chance to right the ship. Boone will mix in rest days (McMahon was already benched on a recent Tuesday) but won’t pull the plug early
  2. May: If the numbers don’t improve, expect Lombard to get a call-up and a trial run
  3. Pre-trade deadline (July): If neither McMahon nor Lombard solves third base, the Yankees explore deals using Dominguez or Jones as currency

The Yankees’ window with Judge is finite. Every lineup spot matters. McMahon was supposed to be the answer at third base — but right now, he looks more like a question mark with a $70 million price tag.

Can McMahon turn it around before the Yankees are forced to make a move, or is the clock already ticking on his time in pinstripes?
