Drew Timme’s Surprising Rise: From Undrafted to Lakers Starter

Gonzaga’s all-time leading scorer couldn’t get drafted. Now Drew Timme is starting games for the Los Angeles Lakers — and making believers out of everyone who counted him out.

Key Takeaways:

  • Drew Timme signed a two-way contract with the Lakers in November 2025 after Deandre Ayton’s injury
  • The 25-year-old averaged 25.5 points, 7.5 rebounds, and 4.0 assists with the South Bay Lakers in the G League
  • As Gonzaga’s all-time leading scorer (2,307 points), Timme went undrafted in 2023 before grinding through the G League
  • He’s now earning starts for the Lakers, including an April 8 matchup against OKC

From March Madness Star to Undrafted Free Agent

Drew Timme wasn’t supposed to end up here. At Gonzaga, the 6-foot-10 big man was a three-time All-American and two-time West Coast Conference Player of the Year. He left as the program’s all-time leading scorer with 2,307 points across four seasons — a resume that screams NBA lottery pick in most eras.

But the 2023 NBA Draft came and went without his name being called.

The knock on Timme was always the same: an old-school post scorer in a league that demands floor spacing. During his freshman year at Gonzaga, he attempted just three shots from beyond the arc — and he’s joked that at least two of those were halfcourt heaves.

So what does a player do when the league tells him he doesn’t fit? He adapts.

“Adapt or Die”: Timme’s Offensive Evolution

Following the Lakers’ 136-108 loss to the San Antonio Spurs — a game where young players and two-way guys got extended run — Timme laid out his philosophy with refreshing honesty.

“The more the league started to change, it’s obviously adapt or die. It’s Darwinism,” he said. “If you wanted something and you want to get to this level, you have to do whatever the trend of the game is. And the trend was obviously more and more threes.”

Timme acknowledged the tension between his elite post game and the league’s demands. “It’s hard when you’re so good at scoring in the post… it’s hard when you’re trying to win, to work on your game as well,” he explained.

That self-awareness has been the engine behind his transformation. As his college career progressed, Timme gradually began shooting more from behind the arc. In the professional ranks, that work has continued to pay dividends.

His path through the G League tells the story:

  • 2023-24: Signed an Exhibit 10 deal with the Milwaukee Bucks, then played for the Wisconsin Herd in the G League
  • Late 2024-25: Signed with the Brooklyn Nets in March, averaging 12.1 points, 7.2 rebounds, and 2.2 assists in 9 games (two starts)
  • 2025-26: Dominated for the South Bay Lakers, putting up 25.5 points, 7.5 rebounds, and 4.0 assists

That South Bay stint is what changed everything.

The Lakers Call-Up: Opportunity Meets Preparation

On November 24, 2025, starting center Deandre Ayton left L.A.’s 108-106 win over the Utah Jazz with a right knee contusion and was ruled out of the next game against the Clippers. The Lakers needed a big man — fast.

They turned to Timme, signing him to a two-way contract and waiving center Christian Koloko to create the roster spot. His agents, Deddrick Faison and Rich Gray, confirmed the deal to ESPN’s Shams Charania.

There was a catch, though. A CBA clause limited Timme’s availability. As ESPN’s Bobby Marks reported, because the Lakers had already played 16 games when they signed him, Timme could only appear in 40 games instead of the typical 50-game limit for two-way players.

For Timme to become a full-time roster member, the Lakers would need to waive either rookie Adou Thiero or Nick Smith Jr. to create a spot and sign him to a standard NBA deal — a decision that speaks to the stakes of every game Timme plays.

G League Playoff Dominance

Even while shuttling between the Lakers and South Bay, Timme proved he belongs at the highest level of G League competition.

In the Western Conference Semifinals against the Rio Grande Valley Vipers, Timme erupted for 26 points on 9-of-13 shooting, adding 8 rebounds and 5 assists across 36 minutes in South Bay’s 112-97 victory. His team-high scoring performance propelled South Bay into the Western Conference Finals.

Rather than rejoining the parent club for its next game, Timme stayed with South Bay for the conference finals matchup against the Stockton Kings. Though South Bay’s season ultimately ended with a loss in the Western Conference Finals, Timme’s playoff performance only strengthened his case for a bigger NBA role.

The numbers tell a compelling story: a player who went from undrafted to the most dominant force in the G League playoffs, all while contributing to an NBA roster when called upon.

The LeBron Factor

Perhaps the most telling endorsement has come from LeBron James himself. During a rehabilitation assignment with the South Bay Lakers, LeBron practiced alongside Timme and was clearly impressed by what he saw. LeBron later publicly praised the young big man — a seal of approval that carries enormous weight in the Lakers organization.

In a moment that went viral in late March, LeBron was spotted wearing Timme’s Lakers practice jersey — a gesture that delighted fans and underscored just how much the veteran superstar respects his teammate’s game and work ethic.

When LeBron James says what all Lakers fans are thinking about a player, the front office listens.

What This Means For You

If you’re a Lakers fan, Drew Timme represents something increasingly rare in professional sports: a player who earned his spot through sheer persistence. No lottery pick hype. No guaranteed contract. Just a former college star who refused to let an undrafted label define his career.

With Timme now starting against OKC on April 8, the question is no longer whether he belongs in the NBA — it’s whether the Lakers will convert his two-way deal into a full roster spot.

The 40-game availability limit creates urgency. Every start, every rotation minute, every productive stretch is an audition — not just for the Lakers, but for the league.

Drew Timme went from three halfcourt heaves at Gonzaga to adapting his entire game for the modern NBA. If that’s not the definition of Darwinism in basketball, what is?

What do you think — should the Lakers convert Timme’s two-way deal into a standard contract?
