Powerball Results: What Happened on April 4

Saturday’s Powerball drawing took place at the Florida Lottery draw studio in Tallahassee at 10:59 PM ET, as scheduled. While the $216 million jackpot (with a cash lump sum of $98.2 million) went unclaimed, the drawing was far from a shutout.

A total of 601,570 tickets won prizes across the nine prize tiers. These ranged from the $4 prize for matching just the Powerball to the $1 million second-tier prize for matching all five white balls without the red Powerball.

Because no one hit the jackpot, the prize pool carries forward. The next estimated Powerball jackpot now stands at $231 million, making Monday’s draw even more enticing for players across the country.

So what are your actual odds, and how does the game work? Let’s break it down.

How Powerball Works: Rules, Costs, and the Power Play Option

Powerball is one of America’s most popular lottery games, available in 45 states, Washington D.C., Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. Here’s how to play:

  • Cost: $2 per play ($3 minimum in Idaho and Montana, where Power Play is bundled)
  • Pick your numbers: Select five white balls from 1 to 69 and one red Powerball from 1 to 26
  • Drawing schedule: Every Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday at 10:59 PM ET
  • Quick Pick option: Let the lottery terminal randomly generate your numbers

The Power Play Multiplier

For an extra $1 per play, the Power Play feature can multiply any non-jackpot prize by 2x, 3x, 4x, 5x, or even 10x. The multiplier is randomly selected before each drawing.

Since the current jackpot exceeds $150 million, the 10x multiplier is not in play for the upcoming drawing. However, the other multipliers can still turn a modest win into a substantial payout.

What many players don’t realize is that these non-jackpot prizes add up significantly. With over 600,000 winners in Saturday’s draw alone, the Power Play option can be a smart addition to your ticket.

Powerball Winning Odds: What the Numbers Actually Tell You

Understanding the odds is crucial before you invest your $2. Powerball offers nine ways to win, and while the jackpot odds are steep, smaller prizes are more attainable than most people think.

Common Misconceptions About Lottery Odds

“You’ll never win anything.” This is the most widespread myth. Saturday’s drawing alone produced more than 601,000 winners. While most won smaller prizes, the sheer volume proves that prize-winning tickets are far from rare.

“Choosing your own numbers gives you better odds.” Whether you pick numbers manually or use Quick Pick, the odds remain identical. Each combination has the same mathematical probability of being drawn.

“The jackpot is what you take home.” The advertised jackpot figure represents the annuity value — paid as 30 graduated payments over 29 years. The cash lump sum is significantly less. Saturday’s $216 million jackpot had a cash value of just $98.2 million, and that’s before federal and state taxes.

Annuity vs. Lump Sum: Which Is Better?

Jackpot winners face a critical decision:

  • Annuity: 30 graduated payments over 29 years. Payments increase over time, providing a hedge against inflation. Total payout equals the advertised jackpot amount.
  • Lump sum: A single, immediate payment at a steep discount. For the rolled-over $231 million jackpot, expect the cash option to be roughly $105 million before taxes.

Most financial advisors note that the right choice depends on your personal financial situation, tax bracket, and investment discipline. Both options are subject to federal and jurisdictional taxes.

What This Means for Monday’s $231 Million Drawing

With no winner on Saturday, the Powerball jackpot has climbed to an estimated $231 million. This represents a $15 million increase from the previous drawing, and ticket sales tend to accelerate as jackpots grow — which in turn pushes the prize even higher.

Key Dates to Remember

  • Next drawing: Monday, April 7, 2026, at 10:59 PM ET
  • Sales cutoff: Varies by state (check your local lottery retailer)
  • Results posted: Shortly after the drawing on official lottery sites

Tips for Playing Smart

  1. Set a budget — Only spend what you can afford to lose. The lottery is entertainment, not an investment strategy.
  2. Consider Power Play — For just $1 more, you can multiply non-jackpot prizes significantly.
  3. Check your tickets — An estimated $2 billion in lottery prizes goes unclaimed every year in the U.S. Don’t let your winning ticket expire.
  4. Join a pool — Office or friend lottery pools let you buy more number combinations without increasing individual spending.
  5. Verify through official sources — Always check results on official lottery websites or authorized retailers.

Where to Check Powerball Winning Numbers

After each drawing, results are available through multiple official channels:

  • Powerball.com — The official Powerball website with results, prize breakdowns, and past winning numbers
  • Your state lottery website — Each participating state posts results independently
  • Authorized retailers — Lottery terminals can scan your ticket to check for winners
  • Official lottery apps — Many state lotteries offer mobile apps with push notifications for drawing results

Historical results are also archived going back to 1992, so you can review past winning number patterns — though remember that each drawing is an independent, random event.

The Bigger Picture: Why Powerball Captures America’s Attention

Saturday’s drawing is a reminder of why Powerball remains a cultural phenomenon. Over 600,000 winners in a single draw, a jackpot rolling past $230 million, and drawings three times a week keep the game consistently in the national conversation.

As the jackpot climbs toward Monday’s $231 million drawing, one question remains: will you check your numbers, or will you be buying a new ticket?

Whether you’re a regular player or thinking about jumping in for the first time, make sure to play responsibly and always verify your results through official channels. The next Powerball drawing is just days away.
