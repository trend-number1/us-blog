Hakimi's Stunning Comeback: PSG Star Returns for Bayern Clash

April 29, 2026 06:03 Trend Insights

What does it take for a footballer to bounce back from a season-threatening injury just months before one of the biggest matches of his career? Achraf Hakimi is about to show us — and the timing could not be more dramatic.

Quick Summary

  • Hakimi has returned to full fitness ahead of PSG’s Champions League semifinal against Bayern Munich.
  • The Morocco captain suffered a severe left ankle sprain after a red-card challenge by Luis Díaz.
  • PSG sit six points clear in Ligue 1 after beating Angers, despite a late red card.
  • Hakimi also faces a separate trial in France over a rape allegation he firmly denies.

The Paris Saint-Germain right-back is back in training, back on the team sheet, and according to club updates, back to full fitness at a moment when PSG need him most. For a player whose 27 goal involvements last season helped fire PSG to their first-ever Champions League title, this comeback is more than a personal victory — it’s a tactical lifeline.

The Injury That Shook PSG

The story really begins earlier this season at the Parc des Princes. During PSG’s Champions League group-stage clash with Bayern Munich, Bayern winger Luis Díaz lunged in on Hakimi with a sliding tackle that caused him to roll his ankle.

Díaz was originally shown a yellow card. After a VAR review, it was upgraded to red.

Hakimi, visibly in tears, had to be helped off the pitch. PSG’s medical staff later confirmed he had “severely sprained his left ankle” and would be unavailable for several weeks. Initial reports estimated three to five weeks out, with the exact length depending on ligament damage.

That timeline mattered for two reasons: PSG’s European campaign, and the Africa Cup of Nations 2025, hosted by Morocco — where Hakimi captains the national side.

The forward whose challenge caused the injury became the villain. The defender who got hurt became the symbol of PSG’s resilience. And now, he’s back.

A Pattern of Comebacks

This is not Hakimi’s first time staring down a long recovery.

In 2019, while on loan at Borussia Dortmund, he also suffered a serious setback before returning stronger. After this latest injury, Hakimi posted on social media: “Falls are part of the journey, the comeback is what makes the difference.”

That mindset is showing on the pitch again.

According to Morocco World News and Africa Top Sports (both reporting on April 27, 2026), Hakimi has now returned to full fitness ahead of PSG’s Champions League semifinal against Bayern Munich. beIN SPORTS reports that he and teammate Vitinha are training normally with the squad, eyeing a place in the final.

If you wanted a story arc, this is it: injured by a Bayern player, then returning to face Bayern in a knockout tie that could send PSG to back-to-back Champions League finals.

Why Hakimi Matters to PSG — and to Morocco

To understand the stakes, look at the numbers.

Last season, Hakimi made history by becoming the defender with the most goal contributions (27) in a single campaign, according to GIVEMESPORT. That stat alone reframes how we should think about him: not as a traditional full-back, but as an attacking weapon who happens to defend.

Here’s a quick comparison of what’s at stake on the two stages where Hakimi shines:

CompetitionRoleWhy He Matters
PSG (Champions League)Right-back / attacking outletDefending last season’s title; semifinal vs. Bayern
Morocco (AFCON 2025)CaptainHosting the tournament on home soil
Ligue 1Starting right-backPSG six points clear after beating Angers

His value isn’t theoretical. PSG’s recent win over Angers pushed them six points clear at the top of Ligue 1, even after picking up a late red card in the match. That’s the kind of cushion that lets a manager rest key players, but with the Bayern semifinal looming, Hakimi’s availability changes the calculus entirely.

For Morocco, his importance is even more emotional. He has previously told reporters: “The tournament is approaching fast, and it remains our top priority. We have a big responsibility.” AFCON on home soil with a fully fit captain is a story Morocco fans have been waiting for.

The Trial Hanging Over the Comeback

No honest article about Hakimi today can ignore the legal cloud.

Hakimi is set to face trial in France after a woman accused him of raping her at his home in the French capital in 2023, when she was 24. He firmly denies the allegation.

The case has been moving through the French legal system for years. The Nanterre prosecutor’s office began a preliminary investigation in March 2023. French prosecutors have requested that the case be referred to a criminal court, and if found guilty, Hakimi could face up to 15 years in prison — the maximum sentence for non-aggravated rape in France.

Hakimi has publicly addressed the allegations multiple times. In a post on X, he wrote: “Today, a rape accusation is enough to justify a trial. This is as unjust to the innocent as it is to the genuine victims. I calmly await this trial, which will allow the truth to come out publicly.”

His lawyer has said it is “with determination and resolve that we await this trial so that justice may be served.”

No date has been set for the trial.

It’s a heavy backdrop. But for now, the football world is watching what he does on the pitch — and the pitch awaits.

What This Means for You

If you’re a PSG fan, you have your starting right-back back for the biggest match of the European season. If you’re a Morocco fan, your captain is fit and firing weeks before AFCON kicks off on home soil. And if you’re a neutral, you’ve got one of the most compelling personal narratives in modern football: a player coming back from a serious injury inflicted by the very club he is now preparing to face in a Champions League semifinal.

Here are three things worth watching in the next few weeks:

  1. Hakimi’s minutes load — will Luis Enrique start him from the first whistle, or ease him in?
  2. The Bayern semifinal result — does PSG’s full-strength side punch through to another final?
  3. AFCON 2025 readiness — how does Hakimi balance club and country in the run-up?

So here’s the question: in a season where comebacks, controversy, and Champions League dreams collide, which storyline will define Achraf Hakimi’s legacy when the 2025–26 campaign ends — the trophies, the trial, or the return?

Whatever the answer, one thing is clear. The next few matches won’t just be about football. They’ll be about a player who refuses to stay down.
