When Is the NBA Draft? Your 2026 Guide

June 22, 2026 00:02 Trend Insights

For the second straight year, the answer to “when is the NBA Draft” isn’t a single night — it’s two of them.

The 2026 NBA Draft is here, and the biggest annual event on the basketball calendar will reshape the futures of 60 college players, international prospects, and the franchises betting on them. If you’ve been searching for exact dates, start times, and the prospects most likely to hear their names called first, this is your complete guide.

Quick Summary: The 2026 NBA Draft takes place over two nights — Round 1 on Monday, June 23 and Round 2 on Tuesday, June 24 — at Barclays Center in Brooklyn, New York. It airs exclusively on ESPN platforms. AJ Dybantsa, the NCAA scoring champion, is the projected No. 1 pick to the Washington Wizards.

When Is the NBA Draft in 2026?

The 2026 NBA Draft will be held across two days: June 23 and June 24.

The first round takes place Monday, June 23, with all the lottery drama and the headline No. 1 pick. The second round follows on Tuesday, June 24, when teams finalize the back half of the 60-player field.

Both nights are staged at Barclays Center in Brooklyn, New York — the same arena that has hosted the event in recent years.

This two-night format isn’t new. The NBA first split the draft across multiple days in 2024, when the first round was held June 26 at Barclays Center and the second round took place the next day at ESPN’s Seaport District Studios. The league has stuck with the spread-out schedule since.

Now that you know the dates, where can you actually watch it unfold?

How to Watch the 2026 NBA Draft

Here’s a notable shift for 2026: the draft will air exclusively on ESPN platforms on June 23 and June 24.

That means a single broadcast home rather than the split-network coverage of past years. If you have ESPN through cable, a streaming bundle, or ESPN’s direct services, you’re set for both nights.

Quick viewing checklist:

  • Dates: Monday, June 23 (Round 1) and Tuesday, June 24 (Round 2)
  • Location: Barclays Center, Brooklyn, New York
  • Where to watch: ESPN platforms (exclusive)
  • Total picks: 60 across two rounds

A quick tip: if you only care about the lottery and the top of the board, Round 1 on June 23 is the night to clear your schedule. The franchise-altering selections almost always happen there.

So who is actually expected to come off the board first? That’s where the intrigue really begins.

Who Is the Projected No. 1 Pick?

The name dominating mock drafts is AJ Dybantsa.

Dybantsa is the NCAA scoring champion, and he’s widely projected to go No. 1 to the Washington Wizards — a franchise that could instantly inject life into its offense by adding him. According to USA TODAY’s reporting, the Wizards are weighing two players in particular: Dybantsa and Darryn Peterson, both of whom have reportedly already worked out for Washington.

Dybantsa, for his part, isn’t lacking confidence.

“I’m super confident in myself being the No. 1 pick. But you never know. There’s been crazy stuff that happens on draft night,” Dybantsa told USA TODAY Sports.

That last line is the key insight most casual fans miss: mock drafts are projections, not guarantees. Front offices finalize decisions only after the combine and weeks of pre-draft workouts — and trades can scramble the order in minutes. The “lock” at No. 1 in June can look very different once teams are actually on the clock.

Even with Dybantsa as the favorite, the rest of the lottery remains genuinely unsettled, with reporting suggesting real intrigue among the top guard prospects.

What the Draft Means for Contending Teams

The draft isn’t only about the top pick — it’s where smart franchises find value deeper in the order.

Consider the Milwaukee Bucks, who hold the No. 10 pick in 2026. The central question for a team like Milwaukee is whether a mid-lottery selection can become a genuine star, or simply a useful rotation piece. That gamble is the heart of draft strategy.

A Brief History — and a Common Misconception

A common misconception is that the NBA Draft has always looked like the glitzy, two-night television event it is today. It hasn’t.

The draft dates back to 1947 and has evolved dramatically — from the old territorial-pick era to the modern lottery system that now sets the order for non-playoff teams. That evolution is exactly why a single pick can carry so much weight.

Recent history makes the stakes obvious. Top selections like Victor Wembanyama have reshaped franchises almost overnight, while all-time greats such as Michael Jordan and LeBron James entered the league through this same process. Every draft is a chance to find the next generational name.

What This Means for You

If you’re planning your viewing, the takeaway is simple:

  1. Block off Monday, June 23 for the first round and the No. 1 pick.
  2. Tune in to ESPN — it’s the exclusive home this year.
  3. Watch AJ Dybantsa at the top, but don’t treat any mock draft as final.
  4. Follow your team’s pick, wherever it lands — value runs deep in this class.

The 2026 NBA Draft is more than a roll call of names. It’s the moment 30 franchises commit to a vision of their future, and where the next Wembanyama could be waiting.

So here’s the question worth asking as the clock ticks down to June 23: will your team play it safe with the obvious pick — or take the swing that defines the next decade?
