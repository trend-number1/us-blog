Sal Stewart's Stunning Transformation Has the Reds Dreaming Big for 2026

March 27, 2026 06:03 Trend Insights

A 22-year-old drops 26 pounds in one offseason, mashes his way through spring training with a 1.066 OPS, and earns the cleanup spot on Opening Day — that’s the Sal Stewart story heading into 2026, and Cincinnati hasn’t been this excited about a young hitter in years.

Key Takeaways

  • Sal Stewart lost 26 pounds this offseason to prepare for his first full MLB season
  • He slashed an impressive 1.066 OPS with 3 home runs and 4 stolen bases in Cactus League play
  • Manager Terry Francona has slotted him into the cleanup spot on Opening Day
  • Stewart is ranked as baseball’s No. 22 overall prospect and Cincinnati’s top prospect
  • His 2025 debut produced 5 home runs in just 58 plate appearances

Sal Stewart’s 26-Pound Offseason Overhaul

Body transformations in baseball aren’t new. But the intent behind Stewart’s matters.

According to MLB.com’s Mark Sheldon, the right-handed slugger dropped 26 pounds over the winter — a deliberate move to prepare for the grind of a 162-game schedule. At just 22 years old, Stewart understood that talent alone wouldn’t carry him through his first full Major League campaign.

The results showed up immediately in Arizona. Across 54 Cactus League plate appearances, Stewart posted:

  • .333 batting average
  • 1.066 OPS
  • 3 home runs
  • 4 stolen bases

That last number is especially intriguing. The added speed from his leaner frame opens up a dimension to his game that the Reds didn’t necessarily expect. A first baseman who can hit for power and swipe bags is a rare commodity.

But Stewart didn’t just work on his body. He also dedicated the offseason to learning first base, a position transition that could define his career trajectory. Through 11 games at first, he’s posted a .988 fielding percentage with 73 putouts — solid numbers for a player still getting comfortable at a new position.

So what does this mean for the Reds’ lineup construction?

Francona’s Cleanup Hitter: A Vote of Confidence

Terry Francona doesn’t hand out lineup spots as gifts. The man has 25 years of managerial experience and a decade of playing in the big leagues. When he slots a 22-year-old into the cleanup spot, it’s a statement.

And that’s exactly what he’s done with Sal Stewart.

According to CBS Sports, Francona has been moving Stewart up the batting order throughout the final week of exhibition play, using him in the cleanup spot against both left-handed and right-handed pitching. On Opening Day, Stewart is projected to start at first base and bat cleanup against Red Sox lefty Garrett Crochet.

ESPN’s Alden Gonzalez reported that Francona has “continually called Stewart one of the most advanced” young hitters he’s seen — a remarkable endorsement from a manager who has witnessed generations of talent cycle through Major League clubhouses.

The Reds have also added a safety net. The February 3 signing of Eugenio Suárez gives Cincinnati another experienced bat, which should take pressure off Stewart to single-handedly replace the offense the club was missing in 2025. The organization plans to rotate Stewart, Suárez, and Spencer Steer through the designated hitter spot, keeping all three fresh throughout the season.

That depth is precisely why Stewart can swing freely — which leads to the big question everyone’s asking.

Can Sal Stewart Win NL Rookie of the Year?

The case is more legitimate than you might think.

MLB.com’s Mark Sheldon put it plainly: “If Stewart can build from his small sample of last season, he could be an NL Rookie of the Year candidate.” That small sample — 5 home runs in 58 plate appearances during September 2025 — was enough to make evaluators take notice.

But there are real concerns to address. Stewart’s strikeout rate jumped 10 percentage points during his initial cup of coffee in the Majors last season. That’s not unusual for a young hitter adjusting to big-league velocity and pitch mix, but it’s the kind of trend that needs to reverse for Stewart to sustain success over a full season.

Here’s what works in his favor:

  1. The weight loss — A leaner Stewart means quicker bat speed and better pitch recognition
  2. Francona’s trust — Hitting cleanup from Day 1 means maximum RBI opportunities
  3. Lineup protection — Suárez behind him prevents pitchers from pitching around him
  4. Youth and ceiling — At 22, he’s still developing; his best baseball is ahead of him
  5. Positional value — A power-hitting first baseman with speed is a unicorn profile

The Reds are clearly going all-in on Stewart’s readiness. He’s one of four players making their first Opening Day starts at Great American Ball Park this season, per the Dayton Daily News — a sign of the franchise’s broader youth movement.

What This Means for Fantasy Managers

If you play fantasy baseball, Sal Stewart might be the biggest sleeper in your draft.

His current average draft position (ADP) suggests he’s being significantly undervalued relative to his projected role. Consider what you’re getting: a cleanup hitter in a lineup playing 81 home games at Great American Ball Park — one of the most hitter-friendly parks in baseball.

Stewart offers high value as a primary run producer in the middle of the Reds lineup. His combination of raw power, improving plate discipline, and a locked-in everyday role makes him an attractive target for fantasy managers seeking an edge in the home run and RBI categories.

The stolen base potential adds another layer. Four steals in spring training might seem small, but for a first baseman, that kind of athleticism translates to category flexibility that most players at his position simply can’t provide.

One word of caution: that strikeout rate. If it stays elevated, it could cap his batting average upside. Monitor his first two weeks closely — if the whiff rate comes down, you might have the steal of your draft on your roster.

The Bigger Picture for Cincinnati

The Cincinnati Reds haven’t made the postseason since 2020, and the franchise is banking on its young core to change that. Sal Stewart sits at the center of that bet.

He’s baseball’s No. 22 overall prospect for a reason — the combination of right-handed power, positional versatility, and now a transformed physique makes him the kind of player contending teams are built around.

Reds broadcaster reactions have been overwhelmingly positive. Sports Illustrated noted that the broadcast team has had a “strong reaction” to what Stewart is doing at the plate this spring, and Sporting News called him “a Cincinnati Reds perfect fit.”

The 2026 season will answer the question that’s been hovering over Stewart since he was drafted: Can he translate elite minor-league tools into sustained Major League production?

With 26 fewer pounds, a cleanup spot in the order, and the full confidence of a Hall of Fame-caliber manager, Sal Stewart has never been better positioned to answer with a resounding yes.

What’s your prediction — will Sal Stewart crack 20 home runs in his rookie season? The tools are there. Now it’s just about the reps.
