Eduardo Camavinga Transfer Saga: Real Madrid’s Midfielder at a Crossroads

Eduardo Camavinga was supposed to be the future of Real Madrid’s midfield. At just 23 years old, the French international has already racked up 216 appearances for Los Blancos — yet his name is now dominating transfer headlines for all the wrong reasons.

Key Takeaways

  • Real Madrid are open to listening to offers for Camavinga, but are not actively pushing him out
  • Liverpool, Manchester United, and Chelsea have all expressed interest
  • Camavinga himself reportedly wants to stay and fight for his place
  • Any transfer would cost upwards of €50 million
  • Fabrizio Romano confirms no club-to-club talks have taken place yet

Eduardo Camavinga’s Struggles at the Bernabeu

The 2025/26 season has been a frustrating one for Camavinga. Plagued by injury problems for two consecutive seasons, the midfielder has started just 12 of Real Madrid’s 21 La Liga matches this campaign.

When he has played, the results have been underwhelming. A particularly poor performance against Mallorca recently drew heavy criticism from Spanish media outlets, with AS reporting that the club is “seriously considering” placing him on the transfer market this summer.

Real Madrid’s interim setup under Alvaro Arbeloa has also raised questions about Camavinga’s role. Ahead of the Champions League quarterfinal clash with Bayern Munich, reports from Yahoo Sports indicated that Camavinga was predicted to remain on the bench, with Arbeloa reportedly considering starting a Real Madrid academy product over the 23-year-old.

His career numbers tell a story of a talented player who hasn’t quite reached his ceiling: six goals and 11 assists in 216 appearances since his €40 million move from Rennes in 2021.

So what happens next for one of football’s most talked-about midfielders?

The Transfer Rumors: Who Wants Camavinga?

The Premier League appears to be the most likely destination if Camavinga does leave Madrid. Here’s a breakdown of the clubs reportedly in the mix:

Liverpool appear to be the frontrunners. According to TEAMtalk, intermediaries are actively working on a deal that could see Camavinga move to Anfield while Alexis Mac Allister heads in the opposite direction to Real Madrid, who have expressed interest in the Argentine international.

Manchester United’s interest is newer but real. TEAMtalk’s transfer insider Graeme Bailey reported on March 18 that INEOS registered “early interest” in the Frenchman, with intermediaries beginning to sound out top clubs about a potential move.

The price tag? ESPN’s report, citing Spanish outlet Marca, states that any deal would cost upwards of €50 million — a significant markup on the €40 million Real Madrid paid Rennes five years ago.

But before anyone gets too excited, there’s a critical wrinkle in this story.

What Fabrizio Romano Really Said

Transfer journalist Fabrizio Romano, widely regarded as the most reliable source in football transfers, dropped a major update on April 13 that cooled some of the speculation.

Speaking on his YouTube channel, Romano laid out several key points:

  • Real Madrid are not actively trying to sell Camavinga
  • His agents are not offering him to other clubs
  • Camavinga is not showing a desire to leave
  • He wants to fight for his place and loves the club

“Camavinga wants to fight for his place at Real Madrid. Camavinga loves Real Madrid, so he still wants to find a way to continue at Real Madrid, also in the future,” Romano stated.

However, Romano added a crucial caveat: Camavinga is “not an absolutely untouchable player.” If a big proposal arrives and the player is open to it, Real Madrid would be willing to negotiate.

This was echoed by Florian Plettenberg of Sky Sports Germany, who reported that Camavinga has “no intention” of leaving at this time. Plettenberg added that the midfielder has shown no signs of “dissatisfaction” in the locker room and that there have been “no talks” with other clubs.

The picture that emerges is nuanced: Real Madrid won’t block a departure, but they’re not engineering one either.

Common Misconceptions About the Camavinga Situation

There’s a lot of noise around this story, so let’s separate fact from fiction:

  1. “Real Madrid are desperate to sell” — False. Multiple sources confirm they are not actively pushing Camavinga out or offering him to clubs.

  2. “Camavinga wants to leave” — False. Both Romano and Plettenberg report he wants to stay and compete for his spot.

  3. “A deal is close with Liverpool” — Premature. While intermediaries are working on a potential Mac Allister swap framework, no formal negotiations have begun between clubs.

  4. “He’s only a midfielder” — Not quite. Reports note Camavinga is also capable of playing as a left-back, adding tactical versatility that could appeal to Premier League managers.

The reality sits somewhere in the middle: Camavinga’s situation is fluid, and a lot depends on how the rest of the season plays out and what kind of offers materialize this summer.

What This Means for the Summer Transfer Window

The Camavinga saga is shaping up to be one of the defining storylines of the 2026 summer transfer window. Here’s why it matters beyond just one player:

For Real Madrid, a Camavinga sale could fund moves for their own midfield targets. The club has been linked with several Premier League midfielders including Enzo Fernandez, Rodri, and Sandro Tonali, suggesting they see room for an upgrade regardless of Camavinga’s decision.

For Liverpool, landing Camavinga would represent a statement signing — a 23-year-old with Champions League pedigree and years of top-level experience. The Mac Allister swap angle adds intrigue, as it could allow both clubs to reshuffle without massive cash outlays.

For Manchester United, Camavinga fits the profile of the young, technically gifted midfielder that INEOS have been targeting as part of their rebuild. At €50 million-plus, he would be a significant investment but one with considerable upside.

For Camavinga himself, the coming weeks are pivotal. Strong performances in the Champions League knockout rounds against Bayern Munich could silence critics and solidify his position at Madrid. Poor showings — or continued benchings — could accelerate the exit narrative.

The Bottom Line

Eduardo Camavinga’s story is far from over. At 23, he remains one of the most talented midfielders in European football, even if his current form doesn’t reflect his potential. The transfer speculation is real but premature — no club has entered formal negotiations, and the player himself wants to prove he belongs at the Bernabeu.

The summer window will be the ultimate test. Will Real Madrid receive an offer too good to refuse? Will Camavinga’s patience wear thin if playing time doesn’t improve? Or will he do what he says he wants to do — fight his way back into the starting XI?

One thing is certain: every Premier League sporting director with €50 million to spare will be watching Real Madrid’s Champions League campaign very closely.
