Carson Wentz Returns to Vikings: What It Means for Minnesota's Stacked QB Room

March 19, 2026 22:07 Trend Insights

What do you do when you already have two quarterbacks and sign a third? If you’re the Minnesota Vikings, you’re apparently building the most intriguing QB room in the NFL.

The Vikings announced on March 19 that they are re-signing Carson Wentz on a one-year free agent deal, reuniting with the veteran just months after trading for him before the 2025 season. This move comes on the heels of Minnesota’s blockbuster signing of Kyler Murray — making it three former first-round quarterbacks under one roof.

Quick Summary:

  • Carson Wentz signs a one-year deal to return to the Minnesota Vikings
  • He joins a QB room with newly signed Kyler Murray and incumbent J.J. McCarthy
  • Wentz started 5 games for Minnesota in 2025 before a shoulder injury ended his season
  • The signing could open the door for a potential J.J. McCarthy trade
  • The New York Jets had interest in Wentz, but he chose to stay in Minnesota

Carson Wentz’s 2025 Season With the Vikings

Wentz’s first stint in Minnesota was brief but meaningful enough to earn a return ticket.

The Vikings traded for him before the 2025 season, and he was thrust into action after J.J. McCarthy went down with a high-ankle sprain. In his five starts, Wentz threw for 1,216 yards, completing 65.1% of his passes with 6 touchdowns and 5 interceptions, posting an 85.8 passer rating.

He went 2–3 as a starter, including a notable comeback win in London. His total QBR of 40.9 placed him near the bottom of the league — but context matters. Wentz was signed late in August and had minimal time to learn the playbook before being called into action.

His season ended during a Thursday Night Football game against the Los Angeles Chargers when he suffered a left shoulder injury that required mid-season surgery.

Despite the mixed numbers, the Vikings clearly saw enough to bring him back. So what changed between then and now?

Why the Vikings Want Three Quarterbacks

On paper, re-signing Wentz when you’ve already added Kyler Murray looks like overkill. But there’s a method to this madness.

Kyler Murray arrives in Minnesota after spending his first seven seasons with the Arizona Cardinals, where he racked up impressive career numbers:

StatMurray’s Career (Cardinals)
Passing Yards20,460
Passing Touchdowns121
Games Played (2025)5
2025 Stats962 yards, 6 TD, 3 INT

Murray was limited to just five games in 2025, though the previous year he played all 17 games for the first time since 2020.

Murray wouldn’t have signed with the Vikings without the belief that he would be the starter. And at this stage of his career, Wentz isn’t good enough to challenge Murray for the QB1 spot.

But J.J. McCarthy? That’s where things get interesting.

The J.J. McCarthy Trade Scenario

The addition of Wentz doesn’t just add depth — it potentially unlocks a trade chip.

With Murray locked in as the likely starter and Wentz as a capable veteran backup, the Vikings no longer need McCarthy as their insurance policy. According to Sporting News, the signing “opens up other doors.”

Here’s how the scenarios could play out:

  • Scenario 1: McCarthy competes with Murray in the preseason, loses the job, and serves as QB2 with Wentz as QB3
  • Scenario 2: The Vikings listen to trade offers for McCarthy, knowing Wentz provides adequate backup depth
  • Scenario 3: Minnesota trades Wentz for a small return if they feel confident in the Murray-McCarthy combination

The third-year quarterback will likely at least nominally “compete” with Murray during training camp and preseason. But everyone in the building knows this is Murray’s job to lose.

For McCarthy, the writing may be on the wall. Having three former first-round picks in one QB room is unsustainable — and typically signals that at least one is on the way out.

The Jets Connection That Didn’t Happen

Wentz’s return to Minnesota wasn’t a foregone conclusion. The New York Jets were heavily interested in signing him as their QB2.

After trading for Geno Smith from the Raiders, Jets insiders reported that Wentz was “squarely in the mix” to back him up. Frank Reich, the Jets’ new offensive coordinator, has a deep history with Wentz dating back to their time together in Philadelphia.

  • Reich was Wentz’s offensive coordinator with the Eagles from 2016 to 2017
  • Wentz’s best season came under Reich in Year 2: 11-2 record, 3,296 yards, 33 touchdowns, 7 interceptions
  • Wentz was an MVP frontrunner that year before tearing his ACL in Week 13

“Frank Reich loves Carson Wentz,” Connor Hughes of SNY reported. “He wants Carson Wentz in his room.”

However, multiple sources indicated that Wentz had options beyond the Jets. According to Zack Rosenblatt of The Athletic, a deal with New York became “unlikely” as other opportunities materialized.

Ultimately, Wentz chose familiarity. He knows the Vikings’ offense, has built rapport with Minnesota’s key playmakers, and can step in at a moment’s notice if needed.

What This Means for the 2026 NFL Season

The Vikings’ quarterback room is now one of the most fascinating in football. Three former first-round picks, three very different career trajectories, and only one starting spot.

Here’s how the depth chart likely shakes out:

PositionQuarterbackRole
QB1Kyler MurrayStarter — signed as the franchise’s new face
QB2J.J. McCarthyBackup / potential trade candidate
QB3Carson WentzVeteran insurance with system knowledge

For the Vikings, this is a luxury problem. Having too many capable quarterbacks is far better than the alternative — as they learned in 2025 when McCarthy’s injury left them scrambling.

CBS Sports’ Jonathan Jones noted back in November that Minnesota “didn’t add a veteran backup at the trade deadline so they could look to re-sign Carson Wentz.” That patience has now paid off.

The practical reality is clear: Wentz knows the offense, comes cheap on a one-year deal, and provides the kind of experienced depth that championship-caliber teams need. Re-signing him was the safe, smart move.

But the ripple effects on McCarthy’s future could end up being the bigger story. Will the Vikings cash in on their young quarterback while his value is high, or keep all three arms in case of emergency?

The answer to that question could define Minnesota’s 2026 season before it even begins. What do you think — should the Vikings trade McCarthy now, or is having three quality quarterbacks the ultimate insurance policy?
