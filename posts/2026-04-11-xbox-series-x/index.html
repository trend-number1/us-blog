Xbox Series X in 2026: Essential Guide to the Latest Games, Deals, and Performance Updates

April 11, 2026 18:03 Trend Insights

Is the Xbox Series X still worth your attention in 2026? With blockbuster launches hitting Game Pass, significant price cuts, and some surprising performance revelations, Microsoft’s flagship console is making headlines again — and not all of them are what you’d expect.

Key Takeaways

  • A new Xbox deal saves buyers up to $250 on the Xbox Series X
  • Hades 2 arrives on Xbox and Game Pass on April 14, with cloud support
  • Crimson Desert runs well on Series X but struggles significantly on Series S
  • Multiple titles now confirmed at 120 FPS on Xbox Series X
  • PS5 vs Xbox Series sales data for February 2026 reveals the ongoing console race

Xbox Series X Deals: Save Up to $250 Right Now

If you’ve been waiting for the right moment to pick up an Xbox Series X, that moment may have arrived. According to ComicBook.com, a new Xbox deal can save users up to $250 on the Xbox Series X, making it one of the most aggressive price drops the console has seen since launch.

This kind of discount puts the Series X in a highly competitive price bracket, especially considering what you get out of the box:

  • Custom AMD Zen 2 processor with 8 cores and 16 threads
  • Custom RDNA 2 GPU with hardware-accelerated ray tracing
  • Up to 4K resolution at 60 FPS, with select titles hitting 120 FPS
  • Full backwards compatibility with Xbox One, Xbox 360, and select original Xbox titles

For anyone building a living room gaming setup on a budget, this deal makes the Series X hard to ignore. But the hardware is only part of the story — what really matters is the games.

Game Pass Is Stacking Wins in April 2026

Microsoft’s Game Pass subscription continues to be the Xbox ecosystem’s strongest selling point, and April 2026 is shaping up to be one of its best months yet.

The headline addition is Hades 2, Supergiant Games’ highly anticipated sequel. After a successful run on Nintendo Switch and PC, Hades 2 finally arrives on Xbox on April 14. The game will be available on Game Pass from day one, complete with Xbox Cloud Gaming support — meaning you can play it on phones, tablets, and browsers without downloading a thing.

Release times have already been confirmed by Supergiant Games, so Xbox players can plan their launch-day sessions accordingly.

Beyond Hades 2, here’s what’s making waves on Game Pass this spring:

  • Starfield received a major update with a paid story expansion and a free overhaul of its space travel system, both going live simultaneously
  • Stalker 2: Heart of Chornobyl announced its first DLC, titled Cost of Hope, arriving this summer
  • State of Decay 3 developer Undead Labs will hold a series of playtests beginning next month
  • A new Serious Sam entry gives the FPS franchise a co-op roguelite twist

Polygon recently highlighted the 25 best games on Game Pass right now, underscoring just how deep the library has become. Whether you’re into RPGs, shooters, or indie darlings, there’s something worth playing.

But not every Game Pass experience is created equal — and that leads us to a growing conversation about the Series S.

Crimson Desert Exposes the Xbox Series X vs Series S Divide

One of the most talked-about releases this month is Crimson Desert, the ambitious RPG from Pearl Abyss. Digital Foundry’s extensive tech review of the Xbox versions tells two very different stories depending on which console you own.

On Xbox Series X, the game has seen meaningful improvements through post-launch updates. The experience is solid, delivering the visual fidelity and frame rates you’d expect from the more powerful hardware.

On Xbox Series S, however, Digital Foundry was blunt: “The Series S version can’t be readily recommended.” The performance mode was described as “reminiscent of a Switch port” — a damning comparison for a current-gen console.

FeatureXbox Series XXbox Series S
Visual QualityStrong, improved by updatesSignificantly compromised
Performance ModeStable, recommended“Reminiscent of a Switch port”
RecommendationYesNot readily recommended

This isn’t the first time the Series S has struggled with demanding titles, and it raises an important question: as games become more ambitious, is the performance gap between the two consoles becoming too wide?

For developers, supporting two vastly different hardware profiles within the same generation adds complexity. For consumers, it means the $250 savings on a Series S might cost you in game quality.

120 FPS Gaming and New Titles Pushing the Hardware

On the brighter side, the Xbox Series X continues to flex its technical muscles. Mouse P.I. For Hire, the stylish black-and-white rubber-hose-animated FPS from Fumi Games, has confirmed 120 FPS on both Xbox Series X and Series S ahead of its launch next week. It’s a reminder that when developers optimize for the hardware, both consoles can deliver smooth experiences.

Meanwhile, the Invincible VS open beta is live right now on Xbox Series X and S. This free 3v3 fighting game based on the popular comic and animated series offers 10 playable fighters and is available to download from the Xbox Store this weekend.

Other noteworthy developments include:

  • Fable from Playground Games is shaping up to be a strong entry, with previews generating excitement
  • A deep dive into Gears of War: E-Day is scheduled to stream soon
  • Microsoft revealed at GDC that its big-screen Xbox UI is coming to all Windows 11 devices
  • Microsoft is also testing redesigned achievements, giving Xbox Insiders more control over their achievement lists

That last point addresses one of the most requested features since the launch of the current-gen consoles. Achievement hunters, your patience may finally be paying off.

The Console Sales Race: Where Xbox Stands in 2026

According to VGChartz’s latest sales comparison, PS5 and Xbox Series vs PS4 and Xbox One sales data through February 2026 continues to paint a picture of a competitive but Sony-led generation. The current-gen consoles (launched November 2020) are now well into their fifth year, and installed base numbers matter more than ever as publishers decide where to allocate development resources.

Microsoft’s strategy has clearly shifted away from pure console sales toward an ecosystem play. The company is no longer promoting Xbox by telling people what an Xbox is — instead, the focus is on Game Pass, cloud gaming, and cross-platform availability. The narrative superhero game Marvel Rivals, for instance, will soon be available on all platforms, reflecting this broader approach.

Even hardware modders are finding creative uses for the console. Tom’s Hardware recently featured an Xbox Series X transformed into a gaming PC using 3D-printed components, a low-profile RTX 5060, and an Intel NUC 12 Extreme board — a testament to the console’s unique tower design lending itself to unconventional projects.

What This Means for You

Whether you’re a current Xbox Series X owner or considering jumping in, here’s the practical takeaway:

If you’re buying now: The $250 savings deal makes this the best time to purchase a Series X. Pair it with a Game Pass subscription and you have immediate access to hundreds of games, including day-one releases like Hades 2.

If you own a Series X: April is a great month. Hades 2 on Game Pass, the Invincible VS beta, Starfield updates, and Mouse P.I. For Hire all land within days of each other.

If you own a Series S: Keep an eye on Digital Foundry reviews before buying demanding titles like Crimson Desert. The performance gap is real and growing for graphically intensive games, though well-optimized titles still run beautifully.

If you’re platform-agnostic: Microsoft’s push toward Windows 11 integration and cloud gaming means the Xbox ecosystem increasingly doesn’t require an Xbox console at all.

The Xbox Series X in 2026 isn’t just about hardware specs — it’s about an evolving ecosystem where Game Pass, cloud gaming, and cross-platform play are redefining what it means to be an Xbox gamer. With deals this aggressive and a game lineup this strong, the question isn’t whether the Series X is relevant. It’s whether you can afford to ignore what Microsoft is building.

What’s the one Game Pass title you’re most excited about this spring? The lineup has never been deeper.
