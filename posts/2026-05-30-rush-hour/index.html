Rush Hour 4 Revival: The Surprising Trump Connection

May 30, 2026 14:46 Trend Insights

Eighteen years after the last installment hit theaters, Rush Hour 4 is finally on the way — but the story of how it got greenlit is almost stranger than the buddy-cop comedy itself. The sequel reportedly exists because the President of the United States personally picked up the phone to lobby for it.

Quick Summary: Paramount Pictures will distribute Rush Hour 4, with original director Brett Ratner returning and Jackie Chan and Chris Tucker expected to reprise their roles. The revival reportedly came after President Donald Trump pressed Paramount Skydance shareholder Larry Ellison to bring the franchise back. Salary negotiations with the two stars — initially offered around $8 million each — are ongoing.

How Rush Hour 4 Got Greenlit

According to reporting from Puck’s Matthew Belloni, Semafor, Deadline, and Variety, the path to Rush Hour 4 began with an unusual phone call. President Donald Trump, 79, reportedly lobbied billionaire Larry Ellison — the largest shareholder in Paramount Skydance and the father of CEO David Ellison — on behalf of director Brett Ratner.

Trump’s pitch was nostalgic rather than financial. Per Semafor, the president “appears to want to revive the raucous comedies and action movies of the late 1980s to late 1990s,” citing his affection for the 1988 Jean-Claude Van Damme film Bloodsport as another example. A person familiar with the conversations told the outlet that Trump specifically pressed Ellison to revive Rush Hour.

The lobbying worked. Just one day after the Semafor report surfaced, Variety confirmed that Paramount Pictures had agreed to distribute the fourth installment, with Ratner attached to direct his first feature film in eight years. The film is being released by Paramount on behalf of Warner Bros. Discovery, which originally distributed the trilogy through New Line Cinema.

So why didn’t another studio jump in first? Warner Bros. and New Line let Ratner shop the project around back in 2024. Reportedly, Lionsgate considered it but didn’t want Ratner directing. No other studio bit — until Paramount Skydance under David Ellison stepped up.

The Brett Ratner Question

Ratner’s return is the most controversial element of the deal. He directed the original 1998 Rush Hour along with its two sequels, but his career stalled after 2017, when multiple women — including actresses Olivia Munn and Natasha Henstridge — accused him of sexual misconduct or harassment amid Hollywood’s #MeToo reckoning. Ratner denied all wrongdoing, filed a defamation lawsuit, and later dropped it.

He has reportedly grown close to the Trump family in the years since. According to MovieWeb, Ratner directed a documentary about Melania Trump for Amazon MGM, titled Melania, which TMZ describes as a “massive success.” That documentary appears to have served as his soft re-entry to the industry.

The question facing audiences: will the public’s appetite for a 1990s-style buddy-cop comedy override lingering reservations about its director? Paramount appears to be betting yes.

The Money Problem: Chan and Tucker’s Pay Dispute

Even with the green light, Rush Hour 4 has hit a very modern snag — the stars want more money. According to Puck, Jackie Chan, 71, and Chris Tucker were each offered approximately $8 million to return. That’s a steep pay cut from Rush Hour 3 in 2007, when Chan reportedly earned around $20 million and Tucker took home an even higher payday.

The initial $8 million offer was rejected, according to Puck. But before fans panic, TMZ pushed back hard on the doomsday framing. A source with direct knowledge told the outlet that negotiations are ongoing, this is “a completely normal part of the movie-making business,” and confidence remains high that a deal will be reached.

Crucially, TMZ reports that production timelines have not slipped. The film was always scheduled to start shooting in late summer or early fall, and that schedule is intact. The project was officially greenlit in November 2025, shortly after Trump first publicly mentioned missing old-school buddy-cop comedies.

Chan himself first signaled this was possible back in 2022, confirming the team was “talking about” ideas and that he was in touch with an unnamed director. Three years later, those conversations have finally produced a real movie.

What This Means for Hollywood

The Rush Hour 4 saga is more than nostalgia — it’s a data point about how big franchise decisions get made in 2026. A few takeaways worth noting:

  • Political influence is now an open variable in greenlight decisions. A sitting president lobbied a studio shareholder, and the project moved forward.
  • The “legacy sequel” model keeps winning. Studios are still betting that audiences who loved a film 20+ years ago will show up for a fourth installment.
  • Star salaries are compressing. A 60% pay cut from a previous installment — even for proven box-office draws — reflects how studios are tightening budgets even on tentpole revivals.
  • Reputational risk is now negotiable. Directors sidelined post-#MeToo are finding paths back, especially with political backing.

Common misconception: many fans assume Rush Hour 4 has been “stuck in development hell” for purely creative reasons. The reality, based on the latest reporting, is more about studio politics, distribution rights between Warner Bros. and Paramount, and now compensation talks — not script problems.

What’s Next for Rush Hour 4

Per TMZ’s reporting, cameras are still expected to roll in late summer or early fall of 2026. Paramount is handling release on behalf of Warner Bros., Ratner is locked in to direct, and final deals with Chan and Tucker are the last major piece of the puzzle.

For fans of the original trilogy, the comeback they’ve waited nearly two decades for is closer than ever. For the wider industry, the deal is a reminder that nostalgia, money, and politics all sit at the same table when a major franchise gets revived.

What do you think — is a fourth Rush Hour what audiences actually want in 2026, or has the buddy-cop genre moved on? Whatever side you fall on, the wheels are now in motion, and we’ll know by next summer whether the gamble pays off.
