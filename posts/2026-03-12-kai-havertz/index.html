Kai Havertz Haunts Former Club: Arsenal's Penalty Hero Secures 1-1 Draw at Leverkusen

March 12, 2026 04:48 Trend Insights

Kai Havertz stepped up when it mattered most. Returning to the BayArena where his career began, the 26-year-old Arsenal forward converted a late penalty to secure a crucial 1-1 draw against Bayer Leverkusen in the Champions League Round of 16 first leg.

Key Takeaways

  • Bayer Leverkusen 1-1 Arsenal — Kai Havertz scored a late penalty to level for the Gunners
  • Robert Andrich headed Leverkusen ahead from a corner kick
  • Noni Madueke won the penalty after being fouled by Malik Tillman in the 87th minute
  • Havertz made just his 10th appearance of the season after two surgeries in seven months
  • Arsenal won all 8 league phase matches — the only team with a perfect record

Havertz’s Remarkable Comeback — From “Worst Pain” to Match Hero

The story of Kai Havertz’s 2025-26 season reads more like a medical drama than a football highlight reel. According to BBC Sport, ESPN, and Sky Sports, the German international has endured a nightmare stretch of injuries:

  • February 2025: Tore his hamstring during Arsenal’s Dubai training camp — required surgery
  • August 2025: Suffered a knee injury on the opening day of the Premier League season at Manchester United — required a second surgery
  • Missed 26 matches between August and December
  • February 2026: Another setback with a muscular issue

“I never felt that pain before in my life,” Havertz told reporters before the match. “Having two surgeries one after another is not easy. But I think I’m professional enough to know that’s also part of football sometimes.”

His 62-minute appearance in Saturday’s win over Mansfield was only his fourth start of the season. Wednesday’s match at Leverkusen was just his second European appearance this campaign.

Yet when Arsenal needed someone to step up from the penalty spot, it was Havertz — not a regular starter, not fully match-fit — who delivered.

The Match — How Arsenal Survived a Difficult Night in Germany

Arsenal arrived in Leverkusen as heavy favorites. The Gunners had won all eight league phase matches to top the table, the only team in the competition with a perfect record. Leverkusen, by contrast, finished 16th in the league phase and currently sit sixth in the Bundesliga.

But form doesn’t always translate to results.

Leverkusen took the lead through Robert Andrich, who rose unchallenged at the far post to head in from a corner. The Independent described it as “all too easy” — a dose of Arsenal’s own set-piece medicine, as The Telegraph noted.

Arsenal struggled to find their rhythm for much of the match. The Athletic noted that Bukayo Saka had a difficult evening, while the defense looked uncharacteristically vulnerable. Gabriel Martinelli delivered a dangerous cross that Jurrien Timber headed over from close range, but clear chances were scarce.

Everything changed in the 87th minute.

The Penalty That Changed Everything

Substitute Noni Madueke drove into the box, skipping between two defenders. Malik Tillman clipped his foot, and referee Umut Meler pointed to the spot. VAR confirmed the decision — from most angles it looked soft, but one replay clearly showed Tillman catching the top of Madueke’s foot.

Havertz, the man who came through Leverkusen’s academy and scored 46 goals in 150 appearances before leaving for Chelsea in 2020, stepped up to take it. He made no mistake.

What This Means For Arsenal’s Quadruple Bid

Arsenal are chasing an unprecedented quadruple this season. The 1-1 draw means they take an away goal into the second leg at the Emirates, where they’ll be heavy favorites to advance.

The winners of this tie will face either Bodo/Glimt or Sporting CP in the quarterfinals — a favorable draw that keeps Arsenal on the easier side of the bracket.

But the schedule is relentless. The second leg falls just five days before the Carabao Cup final against Manchester City. Managing fitness — especially for returning players like Havertz — will be crucial.

As Havertz himself said: “I’m confident. I’m feeling good, I’m feeling better. I have a new hunger.”

The Bigger Picture — Havertz’s Arsenal Journey

There’s a common misconception that Havertz has been a disappointment at Arsenal. His first season included stretches of inconsistency, and this season has been ravaged by injury. But the numbers tell a different story.

Before his hamstring injury in February 2025, Havertz was on track for his highest-scoring season at the club. Mikel Arteta’s trust in the German has never wavered — The Athletic specifically highlighted the bond between manager and player.

Now 26, with the worst of his injury nightmare behind him, Havertz has “a lot of years hopefully in front of me,” as he put it. If Wednesday’s penalty was any indication, the best may be yet to come.

Can Havertz stay fit long enough to be the difference-maker in Arsenal’s quadruple chase? The next few weeks will tell us everything.
