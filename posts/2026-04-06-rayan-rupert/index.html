Rayan Rupert's Surprising Rise: From Blazers Cast-Off to Grizzlies Starter

April 6, 2026 06:03 Trend Insights

What happens when an NBA team gives up on a 21-year-old too soon? The Memphis Grizzlies might have found the answer — and the Portland Trail Blazers might be regretting the question.

Rayan Rupert, the French-born guard-forward who was waived by Portland in February 2026, has quietly transformed into a legitimate rotation piece for Memphis. In just five starts with the Grizzlies, he’s averaging 10.4 points and 5.8 rebounds in 26.8 minutes per game — numbers that dwarf anything he produced in Portland.

Key Takeaways

  • Rupert was waived by the Trail Blazers on February 20, 2026 to make room for Sidy Cissoko
  • The Grizzlies signed him to a 10-day contract, then upgraded to a two-way deal for the rest of the season
  • In five games with Memphis, he’s averaging 10.4 points and 5.8 rebounds — a massive leap from his 2.9 points in Portland
  • At just 21 years old, Rupert is showing flashes of the talent that made him a 2023 second-round pick

Rayan Rupert’s Journey: Portland to Memphis

Rupert was selected as the 43rd overall pick in the 2023 NBA Draft by the Portland Trail Blazers. Over his first two and a half seasons in Portland, the 6'7" wing averaged a modest 3.2 points in 12.0 minutes per game. This season specifically, he put up 2.9 points and 1.8 rebounds across 48 appearances for the Blazers.

The numbers weren’t eye-catching, but context matters. Limited minutes and an inconsistent role made it nearly impossible for a young developmental player to find rhythm.

Everything changed on February 20, 2026, when Portland made a roster decision. The Trail Blazers converted Sidy Cissoko’s two-way contract to a standard NBA deal, and to create the necessary roster spot, they waived Rupert.

It was a business move. But for Rupert, it was a fresh start.

The Grizzlies Take a Chance

Memphis didn’t hesitate. The Grizzlies signed Rupert to a 10-day contract almost immediately after his release. The fit was apparent from the start — a long, versatile wing with defensive instincts was exactly what Memphis needed for their stretch run.

The 10-day deal was essentially an audition. Rupert passed it convincingly.

Less than 24 hours after that initial contract expired, the Grizzlies re-signed him to a two-way deal for the remainder of the 2025-26 season, as reported by ESPN’s Shams Charania. The move signaled that Memphis saw real value in the young Frenchman.

Here’s where things get interesting. Look at the statistical transformation:

The difference is staggering. With more than double the minutes and a defined role, Rupert has looked like a completely different player.

Making the Most of the Starting Lineup

In his five starts for Memphis, Rupert has averaged 7.0 points, 5.0 rebounds, 1.4 assists, and 1.4 steals across 31.6 minutes per game. Those steal numbers are particularly notable — they suggest the kind of active, disruptive defense that coaches covet in a wing player of his size.

On April 5, Rupert was back in the starting lineup against the Toronto Raptors after being held out of the previous two games. The Grizzlies have had to carefully manage his availability due to the restrictions of his two-way contract.

As a two-way player, Rupert faces eligibility limits on how many games he can suit up for with the NBA roster. Memphis has been strategic about when to deploy him:

  • Inactive for Wednesday’s game against the Knicks (April 1)
  • Inactive for Monday’s game against the Suns
  • Starting against Toronto on April 4
  • After Wednesday’s game against the Knicks, he’ll be eligible to play in four of Memphis’ final six games

This careful rationing tells you everything about how the Grizzlies view Rupert. They’re not burning his eligibility on meaningless minutes — they’re saving him for games where he can make the biggest impact.

What This Means for Rupert’s Future

The bigger picture here extends well beyond the 2025-26 season. Rupert is only 21 years old, and he’s demonstrating that his struggles in Portland may have been more about circumstance than ability.

Consider the common misconception about young players who don’t produce immediately: that they simply aren’t good enough. Rupert’s case challenges that narrative directly. The same player who averaged 2.9 points in Portland is suddenly a double-digit scorer in Memphis. The talent didn’t materialize overnight — it was always there, waiting for the right opportunity.

For fantasy basketball managers, Rupert represents a fascinating late-season pickup. His two-way contract limitations make him an inconsistent option week-to-week, but in games where he’s active, he’s been a reliable contributor across multiple categories.

For the Trail Blazers, this has to sting. Portland is in a rebuilding phase, and watching a 21-year-old wing blossom on another team — especially one they drafted — is the kind of developmental miss that haunts front offices.

  • Defensive versatility at 6'7" with active hands (1.4 steals per start)
  • Rebounding ability that outpaces his position average
  • Scoring efficiency that improves dramatically with consistent minutes
  • Youth — at 21, his ceiling remains undefined

The Broader NBA Lesson

Rupert’s story fits a pattern the NBA sees every season. Young players get lost in the shuffle on teams with crowded rosters, only to find their footing elsewhere. The key ingredients are usually the same: opportunity, confidence, and a coaching staff willing to invest minutes in development.

Memphis, despite not having a great 2025-26 campaign — including a tough 136-120 loss to the Miami Heat earlier in the season — has been willing to experiment. That willingness gave Rupert the runway he needed.

With the regular season winding down and only a handful of games left, every remaining appearance for Rupert is an audition — not just for Memphis, but for the entire league. A strong finish could position him for a more permanent NBA role heading into 2026-27, whether that’s with the Grizzlies or another team willing to bet on his upside.

What to Watch Next

The immediate question is straightforward: can Rupert sustain this level of production? Five games is a small sample, and the Grizzlies’ two-way restrictions mean we won’t get many more data points this season.

But the trajectory is undeniable. From waived to starting in the span of weeks, Rayan Rupert has turned what could have been a career setback into the most compelling chapter of his young NBA journey.

If you’re a Blazers fan, you might want to look away. If you’re a Grizzlies fan — or just someone who loves a good underdog story — Rupert’s final stretch of the season is must-watch basketball. The question isn’t whether he belongs in the NBA anymore. It’s how high his ceiling can go.
